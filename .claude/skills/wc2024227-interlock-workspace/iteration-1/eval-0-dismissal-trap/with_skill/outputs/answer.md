## The short answer

**It is not bad for you at all, and you must not ask him to change it.**

Both statements were **true on 13 February 2025**. You had been dismissed — employment ceased on notice given 9 October 2024, taking effect 8 October 2024 — and on the day Dr Krishnaiah wrote that report you had an unfair dismissal application on foot in this Commission (TD/2024/110, stamped 25 October 2024) and you had not yet returned to work. You went back **eleven days later**, on 24 February 2025.

He is not wrong. **He is out of date.** Those are completely different problems and they have opposite remedies. You do not correct a doctor who was right; you brief him on what has happened since.

And there is a harder point underneath it. A4 is in the record **because you put it there** — it is *your* exhibit, cited in *your* Form 24 Notice to Admit Facts at ¶¶38–39. Going back now to a treating psychiatrist and asking him to alter a report that is already an exhibit, already quoted verbatim in the Regulator's filed Response, and already relied on in your own Notice, is the single most damaging thing available to you in this matter. It would put your credit in issue where it currently is not.

## 3. Correcting the premise: A4 is *your* exhibit

You said "the Regulator has that report as Exhibit A4." That is not quite the position, and the difference matters.

**Form 24 – Notice to Admit Facts** (pages 1–6 of `2026-02-18_Form24_Response_and_email_communication.pdf`) is **your** document. Item **39** of it reads:

> *"Dr. Krishnaiah opined that premature exposure to the workplace is likely to result in 'significant deterioration in my mental health' (**Exhibit A4, p. 5**)."*

"A" is your exhibit series. The Regulator holds A4 because you served it and asked them to admit facts out of it. You cannot now treat it as their document, and you cannot quietly swap it for a different version.

⚠ **Small quoting error to note, in your own Notice.** Item 39 renders the quote as *"significant deterioration in **my** mental health."* The report reads *"in **his** mental health."* The Regulator's Response quoted it back correctly. It is trivial, but it is the kind of thing the other side reads aloud, and it is in a document under your name. Do not repeat it in submissions.

## 5. So how bad is it, honestly?

On the dismissal wording: **a non-issue, and mildly helpful.** It dates the report, it explains why he assessed complete incapacity in February 2025, and it corroborates that you were out of the workplace at that time.

But since you have asked me to look at A4, the real exposure in that document is elsewhere, and you should know what it is before 12 August. Ranked:

**(a) The perception vocabulary — this is the genuine risk.** A4 contains *"perceived unfair treatment"*, *"his **perceived discrimination** and unfair treatment in the workplace have eroded self-esteem"*, and *"becomes consumed by issues he **perceives** as unjust."* Section 32(5)(b) excludes injury arising from the worker's *expectation or perception* of reasonable management action. SOFC ¶6(d)(ii) already recites that limb as part of the onus **you** bear. The Regulator has not pleaded it as a positive case — do not hand it to them, and make sure the 12 August report does not either. For anything now admitted (the 7-hour break, Form 24 ¶1 and ¶3) or found (Review Decision 69983), the correct word is not "perceived".

**(b) The competing-cause list.** Answer 2 names financial stress, relationship strain, legal proceedings, loss of routine and limited union support as *"non-medical factors influencing capacity to work"*. Answer 6(b) names *"relationship breakdown, **job loss**, and **bereavement**"*. Bereavement appears nowhere in your pleadings. This is a s 32(1) apportionment attack waiting to be made. The answer is not to hide it — it is that the test is **"a significant contributing factor"**, not the major or only one, and that "job loss" is the employer's own act, later reversed, so it belongs on their side of the ledger, not in a list of life events.

**(c) The February 2025 "complete incapacity" opinion.** *"Mr Shepherd has complete incapacity to undertake duties required for his own occupation"* — written eleven days before he went back to work. That reads as a failed prediction unless the report is dated properly. It is not a failed prediction: what changed on 24 February 2025 was that the **employment was restored**, and he returned on a reduced pattern. Make sure Dr Krishnaiah understands that sequence, because he will be asked about it.

**(d) "No formal cognitive assessment was conducted"** while recording concentration and working-memory difficulty. That is a gap you can close on 12 August.

**(e) The footer.** *"Not for medico-legal use"*, on every page. A4 is a clinical/insurer document. It has real value for **provenance** — collateral history from your mother obtained independently, observed signs (sweating, shaking, hesitant speech), the medication escalation to a *"treatment resistant"* state, and the unprompted causal sequence *"these issues began… when a new manager was appointed. After raising concerns about rostering, fatigue management, and workplace policies, he experienced changes in work conditions including shorter breaks, more night shifts"*. But it should not be leaned on as an **opinion** on causation. That is what the 12 August report is for.

## 6. Should you ask him to correct it? No. Here is why, plainly

**1. There is nothing to correct.** A statement that was true when written does not become false because circumstances changed. Amending it would not be a correction; it would be a retrospective alteration of a historical clinical record.

**2. It is already in the pleadings, verbatim, on both sides.** Your Notice ¶¶38–39 cites it. Their Response rows 35–36 quote it. A different version of A4 appearing now would be immediately visible, and the first question would be who asked for the change and why.

**3. It puts your credit in issue where it currently is not.** *Briginshaw* raises the standard of persuasion for serious allegations, and your case depends on the Commission accepting your account of a fourteen-month sequence. A request to a treating specialist to revise a served exhibit is the kind of fact that gets put to you in cross-examination and answered badly no matter what you say. You would be trading a non-problem for a credit problem.

**4. It is unfair to him and it exposes him.** He may be required to give evidence. A doctor who has been asked by his patient to change a report is a doctor whose independence is now a topic.

**5. You risk him changing more than you asked.** Reopen the document and he may revisit the incapacity opinion, the competing-cause list, or the predisposition paragraph. Every one of those is more dangerous than the word "former".

**6. Your own pleading already says the same thing.** Form 9A ¶3(b). You cannot sanitise A4 without contradicting yourself.

## 7. What to do instead — before 12 August

The appointment is in four days. `CLAUDE.md` already records the two-report structure: **Report A** for Metro South (current capacity, restrictions, adjustments — silent on aetiology) and **Report B** for the appeal (causation and chronology), from one shared attendance. The bundle at `drafts/PSYCHIATRIST_BUNDLE_12AUG.md` is sound. Add or confirm these five things:

**1. Write to the practice about the footer, today.** Confirm in writing at booking that the 12 August report **is for use in a Queensland Industrial Relations Commission proceeding**. If the standard "not for medico-legal use" footer lands on it, the report is compromised on its face before anyone reads a word.

**2. Bring him up to date in writing, on one page. Do not frame it as a correction.** Something close to:

> *"Doctor, your February 2025 report was accurate on the day you wrote it — I had been dismissed on 8 October 2024 and my reinstatement application was on foot. Since then: the dismissal was reversed and I returned to work on 24 February 2025; I worked until 14 June 2026 on a reduced pattern of about six shifts a fortnight; I remain employed; I have been held out of the workplace since 26 June 2026 and unpaid since 13 July 2026, having been certified fit with restrictions on 3 July 2026."*

That gives him what he needs for a fresh report without touching the old one. It also tells him, correctly, that **of the three "multiple life stressors" he listed in February 2025, one — "job loss" — was the employer's act and was overturned.**

**3. Fix the vocabulary in advance.** Ask that the new report avoid "perceived" and "perception" for anything now admitted or found, and that the statutory phrase used is **"a significant contributing factor"** — never "major". That test was repealed in 2019 and the injury is 18 June 2024.

**4. Set the causation boundary at 24 October 2024.** Anything after that date — the dismissal, the appeal, the 2026 exclusion, the loss of wages — goes under **prognosis and recovery environment**, not causation. If causation is attached to 2026 conduct, the Regulator gets to run it as management action under s 32(5)(a), which is the exclusion the whole appeal turns on.

**5. Ask for formal cognitive testing on the day**, and ask him to address competing causes expressly rather than ignore them. A report that names the alternatives and weighs them survives an opposing IME. One that pretends they do not exist does not.

⚠ And tell him plainly that a report served in this appeal is **disclosable** — everything recorded travels with it. That is a reason to be precise, not a reason to be incomplete.

**(b) The bundle's "Finding C" on Review Decision 69983 is characterised a little too strongly.** At p 16 the reviewing officer writes that *"the work capacity certificate of Dr Hawes dated 1 July 2024 indicated your mechanism of injury was consistent with his clinical findings and there was **no pre-existing factor or condition**"*, and that *"in a conversation with WorkCover on 2 September 2024, Dr Hawes stated that **work events were the sole cause**"*. The unreasonable-management-action finding at p 26 **is** the delegate's own: *"I find the rostering of these two shifts amounted to unreasonable management action given that it was in direct contradiction to the award and the 8-hour agreement."* Present them that way to the psychiatrist, or the distinction gets made for you in cross-examination. Also worth remembering when using that decision: the same officer used ***Delaney*** globally to defeat you at p 27. *Delaney* is the Respondent's authority. One mechanism with one proven instance is ***Mahaffey***.
