## The short answer

**It is not bad for you at all, and you must not ask him to correct it.**

Both halves of the question rest on a premise that the file does not support. The words are
not errors — they were **true on the day he wrote them** — and A4 is not the Regulator's
exhibit. Asking him to "correct" a true statement four days before he writes the report that
matters is the single most damaging thing you could do to this appeal.

## 3. What "correcting" it would actually cost you — four things, all bad

1. **It deletes your best answer to the worst sentence in the report.** Q6(b) lists *"multiple
   life stressors, including relationship breakdown, **job loss**, and bereavement"* — three
   apparently non-work causes in your own treating psychiatrist's words, in the Regulator's
   hands. The answer that guts it is that **"job loss" was not a life event: it was the
   employer's own act**, effected by an abandonment letter on a stated ground you contested at
   the time, **and subsequently overturned**. One of the three "life stressors" belongs on
   MSH's side of the ledger. **That answer only exists because the dismissal was real.** Talk
   the doctor out of the dismissal and you hand the Regulator a genuine third-party stressor
   for free.
2. **It hands them a coaching point they do not currently have.** A treating doctor who amends
   a factual statement about his patient's employment, after the document is in a proceeding,
   at the patient's request, is the easiest cross-examination in the world: *who asked you to
   change it, when, and what were you told?* His file is producible. The standing rule in this
   matter (`RFMI-maximising-the-request.md`) is blunt: **"an independently reached opinion is
   unanswerable; a coached one is worthless."**
3. **It creates a real inconsistency where none exists.** Right now the 2025 report and the
   employment record agree. A "correction" puts a document on the file that contradicts MSH's
   own 21 Feb 2025 and 8 Apr 2025 correspondence.
4. **It breaks the standing rule the repo adopted after this exact mistake.** From
   `confirmed-record.md`: *"before characterising ANY statement in a source document as an
   error, search the corpus and the file names for the fact it asserts. The filename is
   evidence. **A document is presumed right until the record says otherwise.**"* That rule was
   written because of this report.
