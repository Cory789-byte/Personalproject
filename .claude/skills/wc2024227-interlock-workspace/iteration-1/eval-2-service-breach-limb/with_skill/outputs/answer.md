# The answer

**I'm not going to draft that paragraph, and I want to be direct about why: it is already in your filed pleading, and in the form you've described it fails on the words of the rule itself.**

Three separate things in your sentence don't survive contact with the documents. Each one is fixable, and underneath them there is a real point worth running — just not this one, and not in the Statement of Facts and Contentions.

---

## 1. There *was* a Form 29. It just wasn't served on you.

The Respondent's Response (rendered page 8, row 23) reads:

> "With respect to paragraph 23, denies as untrue the fact that the Respondent obtained the Appellant's full medical history and says that the Respondent obtained records in a date range in line with **Form 29 - Notice of non-party disclosure, signed by a Senior Registry Officer on 4 July 2025**, and the Respondent received the medical records some time before 16 July 2025."

Row 24 repeats it: the Form 29 "was signed by a Senior Registry Officer on 4 July 2025".

So a Form 29 was issued out of the Registry. If you plead "without ever issuing a Form 29", the Respondent answers it in one line with their own Response and a Registry-stamped document, and every other limb in your pleading is read with more suspicion afterwards. That is a very expensive way to lose a point you didn't need to make.

## 2. What they actually admit is narrower than "they admit it" — and it comes attached to a defence.

Response row 25, verbatim from the rendered page:

> "With respect to paragraph 25, **admit the fact** and says that the Respondent **did not serve a Form 29 – Notice of Non-Party Disclosure on the appellant prior to obtaining the records** and says **there was no requirement to do so pursuant to section 64D** *Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules* 2011. The Respondent **denies as untrue that the Respondent breached Rule 64E**."

Two things follow.

**First, the admission is of the fact of non-service only.** They have never admitted a breach. They have pleaded a legal answer in the same sentence as the admission. "They admit it" is true of the fact and false of the wrong.

**Second, their legal answer is textually correct on r 64D.** Rule 64D(1)(a), read verbatim from ATT27 today, requires service of a copy of the notice on:

> "a person, **other than a party** and the non-party, about whom information is sought by the notice"

You are the Appellant. You are a party. The rule carves you out in terms. A limb pleaded as "breach of r 64D / mandatory Form 29 not served on me" therefore fails on the rule's own words, and it fails in front of Dwyer without the Respondent having to do any work.

And the r 64E allegation is wrong in a different way. Rule 64E is not a service duty at all — it is the *objection right* (64E(1) for people who were served, 64E(2) for people who weren't). There is no such thing as breaching it. Pleading "breach of r 64E" tells the Commission you have read the subdivision inaccurately, at the exact moment you are asking it to accept that the other side read it inaccurately.

## 3. You have already pleaded this. It is in the filed Amended Form 9A at ¶3(c).

> "(c) During proceedings, the Respondent unilaterally obtained the Appellant's primary medical records without issuing the mandatory Form 29 Notice (Rule 64C), depriving the Appellant of statutory objection rights (Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 25)."

So the request isn't really "draft me a paragraph" — it's "expand a paragraph that's already filed." Three observations about it:

- **The cross-reference is one of the few that is actually right.** Response rows track Notice paragraphs 1:1 up to row 25 (drift starts at row 26). So Notice ¶25 → Response row 25. The tag is sound. The law around it is not.
- **"without issuing the mandatory Form 29 Notice" is contradicted by rows 23 and 24 of the very document cited two lines later.** The pleading cites the Response as an admission while contradicting the Response on the page above.
- **It sits under the heading "Subsequent Aggravating Conduct (Post-Injury Unreasonableness)".** That is a structural problem independent of the rules. The injury onset you plead is 18 June 2024. Conduct by the *Regulator* in *litigation* in July 2025 cannot be management action that caused an injury thirteen months earlier, and the Regulator is not your employer. Placing it there invites the answer that you don't understand what s 32 is about.

There is also a cost you should price in. The Appeal Guide, Part 4.6, is blunt:

> "**You will be bound by the content of your Statements of Facts and Contentions.** ... You will not be allowed to depart from the statement filed. You will not be able to amend the statement without the approval of the Commission."

Your current SOFC was already filed "pursuant to leave of the Commission". Leave to amend is finite political capital in this matter. Spending it to *strengthen* a limb that fails on the rule's own words is the worst available use of it. The house rule applies here exactly: **a limb that fails on a rule's own words gets deleted, not softened.**

## What is actually available

Four points, in descending order of strength. None of them is "they broke the service rules and the evidence is tainted."

### A. r 64B(2) — a threshold prohibition, untouched by any carve-out. *This is the best point.*

> "The party **may not** require production of a document if there is available to the party **another reasonably simple and inexpensive way** of proving the matter sought to be proved by the document."

You were a party. You were legally represented (Saines). The documents were **your own GP records**. Asking for them, or obtaining them by consent, or getting them through ordinary disclosure, was plainly another reasonably simple and inexpensive way. On that reading the notice should never have issued at all.

This is not a service argument. The r 64D(1)(a) carve-out does nothing to it. And note what the Respondent's own Response row 27 gives you: they served the records **on your solicitors** on 22 July 2025 — which is itself an acknowledgement that the ordinary route was available and was in fact used, after the fact.

### B. r 64B(1)(a) — "directly relevant".

The notice may only require a document "directly relevant to a matter in issue in the proceeding". Response row 26 answers your ¶¶26–29 with:

> "denies the fact that details contained in the records are not relevant because, **read as a whole**, the medical records are relevant in that they tend to prove or disprove an allegation in the proceeding."

"Read as a whole" is not the test. The test is document-by-document direct relevance. That is a clean, small, winnable point, and it is the one that actually addresses your real grievance — that they took the lot.

### C. r 64D(5) — and the document you don't have.

> "The party must write the name and address of anyone who must be served under this rule on the notice and on all copies of the notice."

**The 4 July 2025 Form 29 is not in this repo.** I checked: the only Form 29 documents held are your own (`documents/filings/2026-04-22_Form29_Notice_NonParty_Disclosure_sealed.pdf` and the service copy to Matheson). Ask Matheson for a copy of the medical-records Form 29 and its r 64D(5) endorsements. You are asking for a document filed in your own proceeding; it is an unremarkable request. Until it is inspected, nothing about how that notice was framed should be asserted in a filing.

### D. r 24(1) — arguable, and never yet put to them.

> "**24 Who is to be served** (1) The party by or for whom a document is filed or issued in a proceeding must serve the document on each other party to the proceeding. (2) Subrule (1) does not apply if— (a) the court, commission, or registrar otherwise directs; or (b) the Act or these rules provide otherwise."

The Form 29 was *issued in the proceeding* (Registry-signed, 4 July 2025, on their own pleading). r 24(1) would then require service on each other party — you. And r 64D(1)(a)'s carve-out is naturally read as avoiding duplication of a duty parties already have under r 24, not as creating an exemption from it.

**But treat this as arguable, not as a win.** Their answer is r 24(2)(b): that Subdivision 7A "provides otherwise" and is an exhaustive code for who gets served with a non-party notice, supported by r 64C(2) ("the party may serve the non-party only after the party has served all other persons who are required to be served under rule 64D"). That reading requires treating silence about parties as exclusion of them — but it is a real answer and it may well succeed.

Its one genuine virtue: **Response ¶25 addresses only r 64D and never mentions r 24.** The point has never been put to them. That is worth something, but it is worth it in correspondence and, if necessary, in an interlocutory application — not as a pleaded contention you are bound by.

## The vehicle: an objection under r 64E(2), not a paragraph in the SOFC

The rule that was written for someone in your position is r 64E(2):

> "Also, **another person who would be affected by the notice and who has not been served** may object to the production of some or all of the documents mentioned in the notice **at any time with the leave of the industrial tribunal**."

And r 64E(4) supplies the grounds, which map onto your actual complaint far better than a service breach does:

- **(b)** lack of relevance to the proceeding
- **(d)** privilege
- **(e)** the confidential nature of the documents or their contents
- **(f)** the effect production would have on any person
- **(g)** if the objector was not served with the notice — the fact that the objector should have been served

Note that **(g) is a ground of objection, not a cause of action.** That is precisely where non-service belongs in this scheme, and it is why pleading it as a breach in the SOFC is the wrong shape as well as the wrong law.

### Two honest limits before you commit to this route

**The stay is worth less than it looks.** r 64F says service of an objection "operates as a stay of the notice" — but this notice is spent. Production happened before 16 July 2025 and the records were served on your solicitors on 22 July 2025. Staying a notice that has already been complied with achieves nothing by itself. What you would be seeking is an order under r 64G(2), which is open-ended ("any order it considers appropriate including, but not limited to" — lifting the stay, varying the notice, setting aside the notice), and then, on the back of a set-aside, orders as to return, destruction and exclusion from evidence. That is an argument, not a certainty. Say so to yourself before you file it.

**Do not cite s 580 as a medical-confidentiality provision.** I read it today. s 580(1)–(2) applies only to records relating to "a person's trade secrets" or "the financial position of a party or witness". It does not cover medical records. The only part of s 580 that helps is **s 580(5)(b)** — a discretionary direction that "evidence given, records tendered or things exhibited ... be withheld from release or search". Cite that, and cite it as a discretion, not as an entitlement.

### Sequencing

Timing matters, and there is a live diary. The 7 August 64G mention is procedural machinery on a different notice — your own, against MSH — and the standing posture there is ordinary, restrained, and volunteers nothing. **Do not raise the GP records at that mention.** Deal with them separately: first the document request to Matheson for the 4 July 2025 Form 29, then r 64B(2) and relevance in correspondence, then leave under r 64E(2) if the correspondence doesn't resolve it.

## What I'd actually recommend on the pleading

**Delete ¶3(c) from the Amended Form 9A when you next have leave open — don't rewrite it.**

The reason is not squeamishness, it's arithmetic. Your case is one mechanism — responsibility imposed and the means to discharge it withheld — with one admitted instance: the seven-hour break of 17–19 March 2024, which the Regulator concedes at SOFC ¶22(a) as "a 7-hour break rather than an 8-hour break". Seven is less than eight on everybody's case. That is the only strand in fourteen months where the employer's own rule produces the answer by subtraction and where your own conduct is not in issue at any point.

Every additional limb dilutes it, and a limb that is *wrong on the face of the rule* dilutes it twice: once by taking up space, and once by giving Dwyer a reason to read the strong limb with the same scepticism the weak one earned.

### Three other things in that same filed document, while you have it open

You didn't ask, but you'll want these before any further amendment, because leave is best spent once:

1. **¶2(d) — "The Regulator's own Independent Review Office (IRO)".** There is no such body. It was the Regulator's own **Review Unit** — which is the better fact anyway, because it is *their* unit. And the tag "(Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 37)" does not say what the pleading says it says. Notice ¶37 is answered at Response row 34, which reads: "admits the contents of the Respondent's review unit decision but says that this fact is not relevant because the appeal is a hearing de novo and it is for the Commission to determine." That is an admission of the *contents of a document*, not an admission of unreasonable management action. There is no admission anywhere of the UMA finding.
2. **Contention 2 — "The Respondent bears the onus under *Prizeman*".** Wrong twice. The onus is on the appellant, including on showing the injury was not connected to reasonable management action (Appeal Guide, Part 7.3). And *Prizeman* is about the reality of the employer's conduct versus the worker's perception of it — which is a point *for* you, on a different issue.
3. **"composite, hostile course of conduct" (Contention 2(a)).** A composite course is *Delaney* territory and *Delaney* is the Respondent's authority. One mechanism with one proven instance is *Mahaffey*. They sound identical and produce opposite results. "Hostile" also edges toward the s 32(5)(b) door — injuries connected to a worker's *expectation or perception* of reasonable management action — which the Regulator has not pleaded and should not be handed.

Also note that the tags at ¶1.2 and ¶1.4 need re-checking against the rendered Response and the row map before they go anywhere: Response row numbers stop matching Notice paragraph numbers after row 26, and "Admitted Fact" is overstated wherever the Respondent admitted only that *a document says something* without admitting its accuracy — which is what rows 30, 31, 33, 35 and 36 do.

## Bottom line

They admitted not serving you. They did not admit doing anything wrong, and on r 64D they are right — you are a party and the rule carves parties out. A Form 29 did issue, Registry-signed on 4 July 2025, so the "no Form 29 at all" version is contradicted by the same page you'd be citing.

The grievance underneath your question is legitimate: they took your entire GP record without telling you, and you never got the chance to object. **r 64B(2) and r 64B(1)(a) are how you say that in a way that can't be answered in one line** — the records were your own and there was a simpler way to get them, and "read as a whole" is not the relevance test. The vehicle is a r 64E(2) objection with leave, not a contention in the SOFC.

Next concrete step: **write to Matheson asking for a copy of the Form 29 signed by the Senior Registry Officer on 4 July 2025, including its r 64D(5) endorsements.** Nothing further should be asserted about how that notice was framed until it has been read on the page. Say when you want that letter drafted.
