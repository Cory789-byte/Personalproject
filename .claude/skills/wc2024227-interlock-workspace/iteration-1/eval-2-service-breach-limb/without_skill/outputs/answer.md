## 1. Before the draft — one thing you need to know

**Your facts are right. The legal label you want to put on them is the one thing in this matter that has already been checked against the legislation and found not to work.**

Two separate propositions:

- **"The Regulator obtained my GP records under a Form 29 that was never served on me, and it admits that."** ✅ **True, admitted, and already pleaded.** Form 24 Para 25 admits it. Your affidavit swears it at ¶44. Nobody is going to make you prove it.
- **"That was a breach of the rules."** ⛔ **This fails, and it fails on the text.** `confirmed-record.md` records the *Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011* Subdivision 7A checked verbatim against the authorised source (sl-2011-0237, checked 17 June 2026):

  > **r 64D(1)(a)** requires a copy to be served on *"a person, **other than a party** and the non-party, about whom information is sought"*.

  You were a party. The rule carves parties out. And **r 64E is an objection right, not a service duty** — so "breach of r 64E", which is how the point was put in the Form 24 at ¶25, has no rule behind it at all.

The Regulator saw this coming. Its Response to ¶25 admits the non-service **and in the same breath says there was "no requirement to do so pursuant to section 64D"**. That is the whole of their answer, and on r 64D it is correct.

`FORM24-VERIFICATION-5AUG.md` puts it bluntly: *"Never plead a service breach or 'unlawfully obtained'."*

**So: if you file a paragraph that says "the Respondent breached the rules by failing to serve me", you hand the Regulator a paragraph it can answer in one line by quoting the rule at you.** In a hearing where your credibility as a self-represented litigant is an asset you have been carefully building — Dwyer only started engaging yesterday once he saw you knew your case — that is an expensive thing to give away for nothing.

There are three further reasons not to file it as a breach:

**(a) You already ran this and withdrew it.** The Form 4 of 9 February 2026 sought exclusion of *"unlawfully obtained medical records"*. You **recalled that application at a conference**. `confirmed-record.md` marks it CLOSED — *"Nothing in it is to be re-run, re-argued or carried forward."* Pleading an illegality limb now invites the answer *"the Appellant withdrew that very application"*.

**(b) Timing.** Yesterday's mention was a necessity audit. Dwyer read the pleaded items out one by one asking what evidence exists for each, signalled the scope was over the top, and you sensibly gave away the pay limb yourself. Turning up a fortnight later seeking leave to **add** a limb — a procedural grievance with no bearing on s 32 — reads against everything you achieved in that room.

**(c) The privacy complaint is banked, deliberately.** You offered to release the medical-information-handling complaint at Calderbank ¶6.2(d). It sits on the reserved track, sequenced behind settlement (discipline rule 8). Pleading it into the appeal spends it and complicates the release architecture.

## 2. What is already in your SOFC

The Amended Form 9A filed 7 April 2026 already pleads it — Part B, subsequent aggravating conduct:

> *"(c) medical records obtained without a Form 29 (Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 25)."*

The Regulator's answer, SOFC ¶25 (13 May 2026):

> *"occurred AFTER the WC application (1 July 2024) and POST-DATE any injury… NOT RELEVANT."*

So the existing plea is **thin** — one clause — and it is **unanswered on the merits**, because they met it with a relevance objection rather than a denial.

That is the real problem to fix, and it is fixable without alleging a breach.

## 3. The paragraph — recommended version

This **replaces** the existing one-clause plea. It pleads every fact you have, reserves everything you might want later, and alleges no breach of any rule.

> **3(c) Medical records obtained without notice to the Appellant.**
> On or about 4 July 2025 the Respondent caused a Notice of Non-Party Disclosure (Form 29) to be issued by the Industrial Registry in this proceeding, by which it required production of the Appellant's medical records from a non-party medical practice. No copy of that Notice was served on the Appellant, or on his then solicitors, at any time before the records were produced. The Respondent admits that it did not serve the Notice on the Appellant (Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 25). The Appellant was accordingly not on notice of the Notice, of its scope, or of the documents it required, and had no opportunity to be heard as to that scope before production occurred. The material produced in answer to the Notice comprised the whole of the Appellant's general practice record, and included documents created for purposes unconnected with this proceeding and entries which are not directly relevant to any matter in issue in the appeal. The Appellant does not by this paragraph seek any relief in respect of the manner in which the Notice was issued or served. He pleads these matters as facts, and reserves his objection to the relevance and admissibility of so much of that material as is not directly relevant to a matter in issue, and in particular to any reliance by the Respondent upon entries predating 2024 as evidencing a pre-existing condition.

**Why each sentence is there:**

| Sentence | Doing what |
|---|---|
| "On or about 4 July 2025… issued by the Industrial Registry" | Their own ¶23 says the Form 29 was signed by a Senior Registry Officer on 4 July 2025. Fixing it as **issued in the proceeding** is what keeps the r 24(1) argument alive if you ever want it. |
| "or on his then solicitors" | You confirmed on 17 July 2026 that Saines was never served either. That is a fact they have never answered and it widens the point. |
| "(Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 25)" | Pins it to the admission. |
| "had no opportunity to be heard as to that scope" | This is the **fairness** framing — not a rules breach. A tribunal can act on unfairness without you having to identify a broken rule. |
| "the whole of the Appellant's general practice record… not directly relevant" | ⭐ **This is the sentence that matters.** r 64B(1)(a) permits a notice to require only documents *directly relevant* to a matter in issue. They took the lot. Their Response ¶26 says the records *"read as a whole … are relevant"* — which is not the test and never was. |
| "does not by this paragraph seek any relief in respect of the manner…" | Kills the strike-out application before it is written. It is a fact, not a cause of action. |
| "reserves his objection to relevance and admissibility… entries predating 2024" | ⭐⭐ **This is the whole reason to plead it at all.** See §4. |

## 4. Where this point actually pays — and it is not in a breach allegation

Read `confirmed-record.md` on the current state of the records:

> *"**Production is spent** (records produced and sealed) — a r 64E(2) production objection is pointless. **Use is live** — the remedy is a **relevance / admissibility objection at the hearing**."*

You accepted the s 580 sealing. Sealing restricts **publication**; it does not stop the Regulator **relying** on the material, and they are relying on it — the amended SOFC ¶8 does not admit your clean baseline *"because medical records identify a past medical history of anxiety"*, and Form 24 ¶35 asserts an anxiety and ADHD history from 26 October 2022.

That is the live fight, and it is the one that decides s 32(1) causation. Note what it collides with: **Review Decision 69983, Finding C — *"there was no pre-existing factor or condition"*** — the Regulator's own delegate, and the Regulator has admitted the contents of that decision at Form 24 ¶37.

So the sequence you want at hearing is:

1. The records came in **without you ever seeing the notice or its scope** (admitted).
2. They came in **whole**, not confined to what was directly relevant (r 64B(1)(a) is your yardstick — you are not alleging a breach, you are measuring relevance).
3. The Regulator now asks the Commission to rely on **pre-2024 entries** from that unbounded collection to contradict its own delegate's express finding.
4. The Commission should not receive, or should give no weight to, that material.

**That argument does not need a rules breach. It needs the facts in §3 and a well-timed objection.** And it is far stronger than a service complaint, because it goes to something the Commission has to decide anyway.

**Practical step, worth more than the pleading:** you still do not hold the medical-records Form 29 itself. Your 24 July 2026 request to Matheson asked for exactly that and is unanswered. **r 64D(5)** requires the party to write on the notice the name and address of everyone required to be served — so the notice on its face records who was served and what was required. Get it. If disclosure is canvassed at the next listing, the unanswered 24 July request is a clean, neutral thing to raise.

## 5. If you want the breach limb anyway

I would not file this, and I have told you why. But if you instruct that it goes in, do not run it on r 64D or r 64E — run it on the two rules the Regulator has **never addressed**. Their ¶25 answers r 64D and nothing else.

> **[Part C, additional contention].** Further and in the alternative, the Appellant contends that the Notice of Non-Party Disclosure issued on or about 4 July 2025 was a document issued in the proceeding within the meaning of r 24(1) of the *Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011*, and that the Respondent was accordingly required to serve it on the Appellant as the other party to the proceeding. The Appellant contends that r 64D(1)(a) does not displace that requirement: the exclusion of a party from the class of persons to be served under r 64D(1)(a) operates to avoid duplication of the obligation already imposed by r 24(1), and not to create an exemption from it. The Appellant further contends that the Notice ought not have been issued, r 64B(2) providing that a party may not require production where there is available to the party another reasonably simple and inexpensive way of proving the matter sought to be proved, the Appellant then being a party to the proceeding, legally represented, and the documents sought being his own medical records. The Appellant further contends that the documents produced exceeded the scope which r 64B(1)(a) permitted the Notice to require, being the entirety of the Appellant's general practice record rather than documents directly relevant to a matter in issue. The Appellant contends that so much of that material as was not within the proper scope of the Notice ought not be received in evidence, or ought be given no weight, in the exercise of the Commission's discretion.

**Health warnings on that paragraph:**

- ⚠ **The r 24(1) point is arguable, not certain.** The repo's own stress test says so. Their answer will be **r 24(2)(b)** — that Subdivision 7A "provides otherwise" and is an exhaustive code for who gets served with a non-party notice, supported by r 64C(2) ("only after the party has served all other persons who are required to be served under rule 64D"). That reading treats silence about parties as excluding them. **It is a real answer and it may win.** Its only virtue is that it has never been put to them.
- ⛔ **The words "unlawful", "unlawfully obtained", "improper" and "breach" appear nowhere in that draft, deliberately.** Keep it that way. It is a scope-and-authority argument, not an illegality argument. `confirmed-record.md`: *"Do not overstate it as illegality."*
- ⭐ **r 64B(2) is the strongest of the three** and the one they have never touched — you were a party, represented, and it was your own record. There was an obviously simpler way to get it: ask you. That is also the rule Dwyer applied out loud to *your* Form 29 yesterday, item by item. It cuts both ways, and you should expect to be asked why it applies to them and not to you. The answer is that your items are third-party records you cannot obtain, and theirs were your own records held by your own doctor.

## 7. My recommendation, in one line

**File §3. Do not file §5.** §3 costs you nothing, keeps every fact on the record, is unanswerable because it is admitted, and sets up the only version of this point that can actually change the outcome — the objection to the Regulator relying on pre-2024 entries from an unbounded collection to contradict its own delegate's finding that there was no pre-existing condition. §5 spends a banked complaint, re-opens an application you withdrew, and hands them a one-line answer out of the rulebook.
