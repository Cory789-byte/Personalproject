# Is "Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 37" right for the unreasonable-management-action finding?

**Short answer: no. Do not file anything else that carries that tag.** The paragraph number points
at the wrong fact, and the words "Admitted Fact" claim something the Regulator expressly refused to
admit. The underlying finding is real and it is useful — but it is not an admitted fact, and it is
not at Notice ¶37.

## 1. What the Amended Form 9A actually says

Filed 7 April 2026, Part B, Stressor 3, sub-paragraph (d), verbatim from the filed PDF:

> **(d) Admitted Unreasonableness:** The Regulator's own Independent Review Office (IRO) formally
> found that this 7-hour rest break constituted unreasonable management action (**Admitted Fact:
> Form 24, Para 37**).

Three things are asserted there: that the finding exists; that it is about the 7-hour break; and that
it is an *admitted fact* at Form 24 ¶37. Only the first is right.

---

## 2. What Form 24 Notice ¶37 actually says

Read from the rendered page (Notice page 4 of 6), verbatim:

> **37.** In the Review Decision (**Exhibit B1**, p. 16), the Regulator stated: *"I am satisfied your
> employment was a significant contributing factor to the psychological injury"*.

**Notice ¶37 is the causation sentence.** It is not about unreasonable management action. The words
"unreasonable", "management action", "rostering" and "7-hour" do not appear in it. So the citation
points at the wrong fact — it would support "employment was a significant contributing factor",
which is a different (and also valuable) proposition.

---

## 3. Where the unreasonable-management-action proposition actually sits — and it is refused

The UMA characterisation was put at **Notice ¶2**, and the Respondent's answer is a flat
non-admission. Read from the rendered Response (page 7 of 13, row 2), verbatim:

> **2.** With respect to paragraph 2, **does not admit the fact contained therein** because the appeal
> is a hearing de novo and **it is for the Commission to determine if this fact amounted to
> unreasonable management action.**

That is the single most important line for your question. **There is no admission anywhere in the
Form 24 that the rostering amounted to unreasonable management action.** Tagging it "Admitted Fact"
is the opposite of the record.

---

## 4. And even ¶37 is not an "admitted fact"

Response row 34 answers Notice ¶37. Verbatim from the rendered page (Response page 9 of 13):

> **34.** With respect to paragraph 37, **admits the contents** of the Respondent's review unit
> decision **but says that this fact is not relevant** because the appeal is a hearing de novo and it
> is for the Commission to determine. action

(The trailing stray word "action" is in the original — a drafting artefact on their side.)

That is an admission that **a document says what it says**. It is not an admission that the statement
is true, and relevance is expressly reserved. In your own file's taxonomy this is a Type B admission,
not Type A. Compare the Type A form used elsewhere in the same document — row 1: *"admits the facts
contained therein"*; row 3: *"admits the facts contained therein"*. Row 34 does not use that form.

---

## 5. The numbering trap — a separate problem sitting underneath

The Notice paragraph numbers and the Response row numbers **drift apart**. Rows 1–25 answer Notice
¶¶1–25 one-to-one; row 26 answers Notice ¶¶26–29; thereafter row N answers Notice ¶(N+3); row 39
answers Notice ¶¶42–45. I confirmed this on the rendered pages — row 34 opens *"With respect to
paragraph 37…"*, row 39 opens *"With respect to paragraphs 42 to 45…"*.

So a bare **"Form 24, Para 37"** is ambiguous on its face: it could mean Notice ¶37 or Response row
37 (which answers Notice ¶40, the payroll instruction — an entirely different subject). Your filings
have consistently used **Notice** numbering, so the number is at least internally consistent. But the
safe citation form, and the one you should use from here, is:

> **Form 24, Notice ¶37 / Response row 34**

## 6. What the Review Officer actually found, and where

The finding is genuine. It is just not worded the way the 9A words it, and the pinpoints in
circulation need fixing.

From Review Decision 69983 dated 24 October 2024, at **document page 26 of 28**, verbatim:

> "Based on this, **I find the rostering of these two shifts amounted to unreasonable management
> action given that it was in direct contradiction to the award and the 8-hour agreement.**"

And in the Conclusion, at **page 27 of 28**:

> "• **factor 4 amounted to unreasonable management action.**"

Three corrections follow:

**(a) It is the *rostering of the two shifts*, not "this 7-hour rest break".** A small difference in
words, but you are quoting a finding — quote it as made.

**(b) There is no "Independent Review Office (IRO)".** The decision is signed **Ms Victoria Squires,
Senior Reviewing Officer, Review Unit**, Workers' Compensation Regulator. Inventing an "Independent
Review Office" both misnames the body and, ironically, weakens your point — the force of the finding
comes precisely from the fact that it was made by *the Respondent's own delegate*, not by anything
independent. Fix this wherever it appears.

**(c) The Notice's own pinpoint "Exhibit B1, p. 16" is off by one.** The quoted causation sentence
— *"Having regard to the medical evidence, I am satisfied your employment was a significant
contributing factor to the psychological injury"* — is at **p. 17 of 28**. Page 16 ends with a
different sentence. That is the Regulator's error in your Notice, but if you re-cite the passage,
cite p. 17.

## 7. Why this matters more than tidiness — three real risks

**Risk 1 — a correction from Matheson at your strongest point.** Stressor 3 is the anchor of the
appeal. A pleading that labels a refused characterisation an "Admitted Fact" invites a one-line
reply that reads: *"paragraph 2 of the Appellant's own notice was expressly not admitted."* That
correction costs credibility exactly where you can least afford it, and it is trivially easy for them
to make.

**Risk 2 — it cannot bear the weight in any event.** The appeal is a hearing **de novo** under s
550(4) WCRA. Even a fully admitted Review Officer finding would not bind Commissioner Dwyer. Running
it as an admitted fact edges toward an estoppel argument that is unavailable and that the Respondent
has pre-emptively answered in rows 2, 34 and 44 (each reserving relevance on the de novo hearing).

**Risk 3 — the decision as a whole is against you.** Every time you cite Review Decision 69983, the
Commission reads its conclusion. At p. 27 Squires applied *Delaney* global evaluation and concluded:

> "since two out of the three causative factors amounted to reasonable management action taken in a
> reasonable way, this means your psychological injury mainly arose out of such management action…
> I find overall your psychological injury arose out of reasonable management action taken in a
> reasonable way and **section 32(5) is consequently enlivened**."

That paragraph is the Regulator's best material. Citing the decision as though it is on your side —
rather than citing one finding within a decision that rejected the claim — hands them the platform to
read the rest of it. The correct posture is to cite the specific finding at the specific page, and to
meet *Delaney* head-on with the single-unreasonable-stressor line (*Mahaffey*), which you already
plead at Contention 2(b).

## 8. Is there anything left of the argument? Yes — as argument, not as admission

Response row 34 says the Regulator *"admits the contents of the Respondent's review unit decision"*
— not "admits the contents of the passage quoted at paragraph 37". Read literally, that admits the
contents of the whole document, which includes the finding at p. 26. That is a genuinely available
submission and worth keeping.

But keep it in the right register:

- It is a **construction argument** about the scope of row 34, not something apparent on the face of
  an admission.
- Rows 2 and 34 will be read together. A tribunal will not readily treat row 34 as conceding what
  row 2 expressly refused.
- Even at its highest it is Type B: it proves the document says it, not that it is true.

So the submission is *"their own delegate found this, and they admit their document contains it"* —
never *"the fact is admitted"*.

## 9. Replacement wording for Stressor 3(d)

If you amend, this version is accurate on every limb, pre-empts the objection, and reads stronger
than what is there now:

> **(d) The Respondent's own review.** In Review Decision 69983 dated 24 October 2024, Ms Victoria
> Squires, Senior Reviewing Officer of the Respondent's Review Unit, found that *"the rostering of
> these two shifts amounted to unreasonable management action given that it was in direct
> contradiction to the award and the 8-hour agreement"* (p. 26; and see the Conclusion, p. 27). The
> Respondent admits the contents of that decision (Form 24, Notice ¶37 / Response row 34), reserving
> its relevance on the hearing de novo. The Appellant relies on the finding as persuasive material
> identifying the matters in issue, not as binding: the characterisation is for the Commission (Form
> 24, Response row 2).

The three propositions that *are* Type A admitted facts, and which carry Stressor 3 without any of
this risk, are:

| Proposition | Anchor | Response wording |
|---|---|---|
| The break between the 17 and 18 March 2024 shifts was 7 hours | Notice ¶1 / Response row 1 | *"admits the facts contained therein"* |
| The applicable minimum is 10 hours, or 8 by written agreement | Notice ¶3 / Response row 3 | *"admits the facts contained therein"* |
| On the Respondent's own case the operative minimum is 8 hours | Notice ¶4 / Response row 4 | *"denies the fact because in June 2020, the Appellant signed an agreement allowing an 8 hour break between shifts"* |

**7 < 8 on every version of the facts either side puts.** The shortfall is uncontested by anyone.
That is arithmetic, not characterisation, and it needs no admission of unreasonableness at all —
which is exactly why it is the safest anchor in the case.

⚠ Related tightening while you are in there: the same Stressor 3 pleads *"the Award and the FRMS
mandate a minimum 10-hour rest break"*. Response row 3 admits **10 hours *or* 8 by written
agreement**, and row 4 asserts the 8-hour agreement. Pleading a flat 10-hour mandate overstates the
admission and is answerable. Plead the 7 < 8 bind instead — the concession is stronger than the
overstatement.

## 11. Bottom line

1. **The number is wrong for the proposition.** Notice ¶37 is the significant-contributing-factor
   sentence, not the UMA finding.
2. **The label is wrong.** Notice ¶37 / Response row 34 admits *contents*, with relevance expressly
   reserved. It is not an admitted fact.
3. **The UMA proposition is at Notice ¶2 and was expressly not admitted.** There is no admission of
   unreasonable management action anywhere in the Form 24.
4. **The finding itself is real** — Squires, Review Decision 69983, p. 26 — and remains valuable as
   *persuasive material from the Respondent's own delegate*. Plead it that way.
5. **Fix the body name.** It is the Regulator's Review Unit (Senior Reviewing Officer Victoria
   Squires), not an "Independent Review Office".
6. **Your load-bearing facts do not need any of this.** Notice ¶¶1, 3 and 4 give you 7 < 8 on
   admissions and on the Respondent's own denial. Lead with that.

**Do not file anything carrying the "Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 37" tag for the unreasonable-
management-action proposition.** Correct the Form 20 ¶41 / checklist conflict first, since that is
the live document most likely to go out next.

### Caveats on this advice

- I read the Form 24 by **rendering the pages to image**, per the source-integrity rule in
  `CLAUDE.md` — the text layer of that PDF drops exhibit references, dates and quoted phrases, so any
  analysis built from `pdftotext` output on it is unreliable.
- The Form 24 Notice as held in the repo is **unsigned and undated** (signature, print name, title of
  office and date all blank on Notice page 6). The Respondent's paragraph-by-paragraph Response of 18
  February 2026 puts service beyond doubt, but the executed copy should be located.
- I have not verified **Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011, rr 41, 49, 108 and 113** (cited
  on the face of the form) against the legislation in this pass. Before any submission on the *effect*
  of the admissions — deemed admission by silence, withdrawal by leave — verify those rules from
  sl-2011-0237.
- Reminder from the form's own face: these admissions operate **"for this proceeding only"**. They do
  not travel to the employment track, an AD Act complaint, or anything before the Chief Executive.
