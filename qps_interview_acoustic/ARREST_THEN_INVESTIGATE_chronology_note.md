## Three steps

1. **What it proves.** At 6:49 am, after the arrest and at the end of the interview, the officer described
   whether Cory was controlling as a matter still under investigation, not a finding. It is recorded, so it
   is not open to dispute.
2. **What it does not prove:**
   - **That charging was improper.** An arrest needs only a reasonable suspicion (Police Powers and
     Responsibilities Act 2000 (Qld) s 365, arrest without warrant: verify against the current reprint), and
     questioning after arrest is the ordinary course.
   - **That the charge depends on "control".** The order's conditions were good behaviour and no domestic
     violence (`CHARGES_SUMMARY.md`), and the particulars may rest on other conduct. Confirm the particulars.
   - **That it is an "admission" in the legal sense.** An admission is a statement by a party against its
     own interest. A police officer's words are a witness's prior statement. They can be put to him in
     cross-examination (Evidence Act 1977 (Qld): prior statements, verify the sections), but they do not
     bind the prosecution.
3. **The available inference.** If the charge was laid on 24 Feb, the order of events is:
   - control described as under investigation at 6:49 am;
   - the earlier order's basis not reinvestigated that evening;
   - that order struck out the next day;
   - the complainant's first sworn statement after that.

   The inference is that the charge preceded the evidence on the contested characterisation. That is
   chronology. Its weight depends on the charge date and the particulars.

## The prosecution's best reply (stress test)

- "Investigating" meant the matter as a whole; post-charge statements are routine.
- The breach is proved by the conduct, not by a finding of "control".
- The officer's words are not an admission.

**Answer:** none of these touches the chronology. The use is the sequence, put to the officer, not a label.

## Cross-examination sequence (closed questions; only once the charge date and particulars are confirmed)

2. At the end of the interview, at 6:49 am, he asked whether you really thought he wanted to control the
   person he was trying to get away from?
3. You told him that was what you were investigating?
4. So at 6:49 am, whether he was controlling was still under investigation?
5. The complainant's first sworn statement was taken on 25 February 2025? *(QP2500332406)*
6. *(If confirmed)* The charge had already been laid by then?
7. *(If confirmed)* On the evening of 24 February you were present when Constable Davies said police do not
   reinvestigate the earlier order? *(Exhibit #4 [12:57–13:14])*

Stop on the answer to 4 or 6. Do not ask why.
