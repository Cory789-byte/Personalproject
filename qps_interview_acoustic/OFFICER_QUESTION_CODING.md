# What the officers said, coded turn by turn (interview of 24 Feb 2025)

> ⛔ QPS track / personal file only (discipline rule 10). **No transcript text is in this file or anywhere in
> the repo.** The speaker-labelled transcript is held outside git (Drive / OneDrive). Script:
> `officer_question_coding.py` (times and codes only); output: `officer_question_coding_output.txt`.
> Coded by one reader (Claude) and not independently double-coded.

**What was tested.** Cory's account (8 Oct 2026) was that:
- every officer statement was accusatory;
- they sped up to press and accuse;
- they never listened;
- guilt was presumed throughout;
- they did not follow a PEACE model.

**Method.**
- **Coding.** Each of the 179 officer turns after the caution was read in context and given one code from a
  fixed rubric (in the script header).
  - Question types follow Griffiths & Milne 2006 and Oxburgh, Myklebust & Grant 2010.
  - Accusatory forms follow Kassin & Gudjonsson 2004 and Kassin, Goldstein & Savitsky 2003.
- **Uptake.** Each turn was also coded for how it treated what Cory had just said: it explored his point,
  used his words to contest him, or did not engage the point.
- **Borderline turns went the officers' way.** Echoes and chronology summaries were coded neutral, and one
  fragment that is probably Cory's speech was excluded.
- **Timing and level** come from the acoustic segments, aligned to the transcript's turn sequence.

## Headline

| Cory's claim | What the coding shows | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Every statement was accusatory | 53% of substantive turns were accusatory, 7% control or redirect, 40% neutral | **Not literally.** In substance it holds for the second half (69%) |
| They accused more as it went on | 32% in minutes 6–30, then 69% in minutes 30–60 (peak 76% at 30–40) | **Holds** |
| They got faster as they pressed | Officers' median reply gap was 1.64 s and 1.11 s in the two least accusatory windows, and 0.63–1.00 s in the three most accusatory | **Holds by phase.** Turn by turn it is only a lean (p ≈ 0.1) |
| They never listened | 30% of turns explored his point, 23% used his words against him, 47% did not engage it. **None of the 10 evidence offers was taken up when he made it** | **Not literally:** they heard him (word uptake 4.8 times chance). **What they did with it was selective** |
| Guilt was presumed | 7 direct accusations (2 of lying), 24 repeats of questions he had answered, 10 opinion-of-conduct questions | **Holds for the second half** |
| No PEACE model | PEACE's structure was present. Its principles (open mind, fair testing, following the account) were mostly absent after 30 minutes | **Holds for the principles,** not the structure |

\* Gaps are for the coded officer turns matched to the audio, so they differ slightly from the all-turn
figures in `OFFICERS_TONE_OVER_TIME.md` (20–30: 1.35 s; 50–61: 0.76 s). The pattern is the same.

**How the interview ran:**
- **Opening.** It began in a PEACE shape. Cory gave a free account from 6:46 for about five minutes, with
  only a procedural reminder and one acknowledgement from the officers.
- **First half.** The first half mixed element questions with real clarification. There was a run of ten
  neutral questions about where he had been living (20:17–21:27).
- **Second half.** From the 30-minute mark it turned: three in four turns were accusatory, and the officers'
  replies came fastest.
- **Delivery.** Their voices got quieter over the same hour (`OFFICERS_TONE_OVER_TIME.md`), and accusatory
  turns were no louder than neutral ones (within 1 dB). The pressure was in what was said and how fast, not
  in volume.

**Turn by turn,** with turns aligned to the audio:
- Accusatory turns followed Cory's last word after a median 0.70 s, neutral turns after 1.05 s (one-sided
  permutation p = 0.095).
- In the second half the figures were 0.65 v 0.95 s. In the first half they were 1.18 v 1.05 s, the other
  way round.
- A cruder nearest-time match gave 0.50 v 0.92 s (p = 0.007), but it matched fewer turns and is less
  reliable.
- **Conclusion:** the claim that accusatory turns were faster than neutral ones, turn by turn, is not
  established. That the most accusatory phases were the fastest is.

This matches the research: open questions produce the most information, and accusation produces the least
(Griffiths & Milne 2006; Walsh & Bull 2010). The interview's accusatory turn cost it information.

## 6. What the officers did that PEACE asks for

- **Opening:** the caution and rights were given and the nature of the investigation stated. His
  understanding was asked about, though a one-word answer to an explain-in-your-own-words question was
  accepted. A free account was invited and taken without questions.
- **Clarification:** stretches of real clarification (20:17–21:27; 32:14–32:47; 43:11–43:14; 44:51–45:14;
  55:10–55:45; 58:14–58:22).
- **The phone:** a download offered, with reassurance about context (61:26–61:50).
- **Closure:** formal closing questions (truth, free will, add or alter, treatment), a statement offered, an
  audio copy promised, and a route to complain mentioned.

What PEACE also asks for, and what was mostly missing after 30 minutes:
- an open mind;
- fair testing of the account;
- following up the interviewee's own leads, including the evidence he offered.

## 7. What the literature says about this pattern

- **Question types.**
  - Open and probing questions are "productive". Leading, multiple and opinion questions are not (Griffiths
    & Milne 2006; Oxburgh, Myklebust & Grant 2010).
  - Interviews run with PEACE skills produce fuller accounts (Walsh & Bull 2010).
- **Presumed guilt.**
  - Interviewers who expect guilt ask more guilt-presumptive questions, use more techniques and press harder
    (Kassin, Goldstein & Savitsky 2003).
  - Guilt-presumptive questions make the interviewee look more defensive to observers (Hill, Memon &
    McGeorge 2008).
- **Not accepting denials.**
  - Refusing to accept denials and repeating the accusation are core steps of accusatorial interrogation
    (Inbau, Reid, Buckley & Jayne; Kassin & Gudjonsson 2004).
  - Across studies, accusatorial methods raise false confessions as well as true ones; information-gathering
    raises true ones only (Meissner et al. 2014).
- **Selective uptake.**
  - The pattern above is what investigation research calls confirmation bias, or tunnel vision: information
    that fits the hypothesis is picked up, and information pointing elsewhere is discounted or not pursued
    (Findley & Scott 2006; Ask & Granhag 2005; O'Brien 2009; Rassin, Eerland & Kuijpers 2010).
  - The codes describe what was said and done, not why.
- **Calm delivery.** Accusatorial training teaches a calm, confident manner while asserting guilt (Inbau et
  al.). Rising accusation alongside a falling voice level fits that pattern.

## 8. Calibration

- **Not oppression in the legal sense.** The leading English example of an oppressive interview, R v Paris
  (1993) 97 Cr App R 99, involved some 300 denials over about 13 hours of questioning, of a man with a mental
  age of 11. This interview ran about an hour, rights were given, and voice levels fell. What the coding
  shows is an accusatory, confirmation-led interview, not an unlawful one.
- **Not confirmed: QPS's own interviewing model.** Public sources did not show whether QPS formally uses
  PEACE. The comparison here is against the research benchmark.

## 9. Limits

- **One coder.** Inter-rater reliability is unknown. The rubric and every code are in the script, so the
  coding can be re-checked line by line.
- **Machine transcript.** Nine turns are flagged as doubtful in transcription or speaker attribution.
- **The officers are not split.** The transcript gives them one lane.
- **Timing.** The transcript and audio align to about ±2 s, so turn-level timing is weak evidence and window
  timing is stronger.
- **Content cannot show intent.**

**Using it.** The countable facts are stronger than the absolutes:
- the accusatory share doubled at the 30-minute mark;
- 24 repeated questions;
- 7 direct accusations;
- none of 10 evidence offers taken up when made.

"Every single thing" and "never listened" are not accurate as stated, and the free account, the
clarification stretches and the friend inquiry would be put against them.
