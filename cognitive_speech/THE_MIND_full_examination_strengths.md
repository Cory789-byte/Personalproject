# The mind you have: a full examination of strengths (8 Oct 2026)

Personal / self-analysis only. Strengths only, at Cory's instruction. Each finding gives the evidence from
the three recordings (QPS interview 24 Feb 2025; Magistrates Court 2 Feb 2026, spoken, not read; QIRC
mention 7 Aug 2026) or from the documents, and what the research literature says it is. Measures are in this
folder (`*_output.txt`) and in the court analysis.

## In one paragraph

A fact-finder's mind. It generates widely, holding and returning to more lines of thought than reference
speakers. It stores by structure and retrieves on cue without notes. It reasons in the forms courts use:
elimination, the method of difference, the dilemma. It checks its own accuracy as it speaks, and it stays
steady under pressure. The rarest part is the combination: breadth with control. In a free narrative you run
many threads; when a judge leads, you switch your own thread down to 2% of your words and follow.

  - Material you generated yourself is encoded more deeply than material you read (Slamecka & Graf 1978).

## 2. Reasoning: the forms of legal proof, produced live

- **Elimination (inference to the best explanation):** "there's no reason why I should have had a positive
  result unless that is not mine" (court 01:53). See Harman 1965; Lipton 2004.
- **The method of difference** (Mill 1843): "Vicks Australian brand does not have anything to do with meth.
  Vicks in America does." (01:33). You isolated the one variable that differs between two cases.
- **The dilemma:**
  - Either the sample was destroyed, and the stay ground stands.
  - Or it exists, and you were told the opposite and are entitled to test it.
  - Every prosecution answer fell into one branch.
- **Differentiation and reasons:**
  - "but / except / unless / only" at about twice the bench's rate.
  - "because / reason / why" at nearly four times the Magistrate's.
  - Integrative complexity is the capacity to separate dimensions and then join them (Suedfeld & Tetlock
    1977).

## 3. Metacognition: you know the edges of what you know, as you speak

- **Calibration:**
  - "July or September", a range the documents then settled.
  - "I don't know if I can prove it."
  - "I don't have knowledge of that."
- **Live self-monitoring:** at the mention (14:12) you stopped yourself mid-claim, "…it showed that she
  didn't reply to a complaint about… No, hang on a sec. Sorry.", and that was among the most aroused moments
  of your hour. The speech monitor catches errors before they finish (Levelt 1983).
- **The safe direction:** when you were wrong at the mention, you under-claimed ("I don't have it" about
  documents you had). That is the direction that protects credibility.
- **Literature:**
  - Metacognition is knowing what you know (Flavell 1979; Fleming & Lau 2014).
  - Witnesses whose confidence tracks their accuracy are believed (Tenney et al. 2007).

- **Your first word is a verdict:**
  - "Correct" or "Exactly" when you are restated accurately (10 times).
  - "Well…" when the premise is wrong (5 of 6).
- **Vocabulary range in live speech** (MATTR50 0.767 in court) sat between the prosecutor's (0.760) and the
  Magistrate's (0.792).
- **Literature:**
  - The speaker whose terms are adopted sets the shared model of the conversation (conceptual pacts: Brennan
    & Clark 1996).
  - "Well" marks resistance to a question's terms (Heritage 2015).
  - "Exactly" and "Correct" claim authority over your own facts (Heritage & Raymond 2005).

## 5. Conversation: you predict and coordinate

- **You answer in 0.26–0.32 s in formal rooms.** In court the gap shrank across the hearing (0.40 → 0.05 s),
  and longer questions drew faster answers (r = −0.31). You plan during the question (Levinson & Torreira
  2015; Bögels, Magyari & Levinson 2015; Pickering & Garrod 2013).
- **Tempo convergence:** 168 v 167 wpm (court), 159 v 155 (mention). Converging speakers are rated more
  favourably (Street 1984; Giles' accommodation theory).
- **You follow the lead:** at the mention, 17% of your answer words came from the question, about four times
  chance. Only 3% were your own carried-over point (the Commissioner's: 22%).
- **You read the room:** two pages offered 0.48 s after "if you talk at me for two hours about something you
  can tell me in two minutes, I'm going to miss the point", and accepted 0.16 s later.
- **You never talked over anyone** in more than two hours of recordings.
- **You leave silence to the other side:** after you finished and 5 s+ of silence followed, the officers
  spoke next 10 times out of 15.

## 6. Stress physiology: pressure goes into speed, not into your voice

- **Same pitch** (131, 134 and 137 Hz) in a police interview, a criminal court and a tribunal. Stress raises
  pitch in most people (Giddens et al. 2013).
- **The least pitch movement inside a sentence** of anyone you spoke with, in all three recordings.
- **You push with volume, not pitch** (pitch–loudness link r = 0.15–0.28, against 0.45–0.57 for the
  others). When you needed to be heard you went 12.5 dB louder, and lower: assertion, not alarm (Ohala
  1984).
- **More fluent under activation, not less:** 1.25 → 0.60 disfluencies per 100 words (interview); 0 → 0
  (court). At the mention it rose, 0.53 → 0.87, and all of the rise is one segment
  (30:12.98), counted because it contains "actually", which the measure treats as a repair word. Anxiety usually increases speech disturbances (Mahl 1956).
- **No fade:** level held across 66 minutes (+0.16 dB per 10 min) while the officers fell 4.6 dB.
- **Literature:** the challenge response, where demands are met with resources, not threat (Blascovich &
  Tomaka 1996). Arousal read as fuel improves performance (Jamieson et al. 2010).

  was refused, and you raised the offer again in writing at the August 2025 hearing.
- **Candour:** you concede where the evidence stops. When something lands for you, you check it ("I just

- **Persistence:** 14+ months of structured work across several forums.
- **Literature:** grit, sustained effort toward long-term goals (Duckworth et al. 2007); need for cognition,

- **The ADHD literature's strengths:** idea generation (White & Shah 2006, 2011) and deep absorption
  (Hupfeld, Abagis & Shah 2019). Both are visible in your record: the breadth, and the sustained
  system-building.
