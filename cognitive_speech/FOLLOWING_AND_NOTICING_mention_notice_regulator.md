## 1. The Regulator noticed the structure, and wrote it down on 8 Sep

Its cover letter to the Form 24/25 responses, under the heading "Admissions are for the purposes of
rule 49 only", says:
- Admissions do not accept "any characterisation, conclusion, inference or submission you have drawn
  or may draw".
- "Admissions as to documents are limited to existence and wording." They are not an admission of the
  truth of any statement recorded, "including … any finding recorded in Review Decision 69983".
- The appeal is de novo.

**That is the 7 August line, existence against characterisation, returned in writing.** They saw the
facts were existence facts and answered on exactly that line.

**No sign they saw where it came from.**
- Any careful respondent writes a reservation like that.
- The letter's Word file lists its author as "Peter", so it may not be Matheson's own drafting.

**It undid nothing.**
- The notice asked for no characterisation, so 298 facts were admitted.
- The reservation leaves the dispute where the 9A points it: characterisation.
- Their outlines then argued only characterisation: "human error, and was not intentional or
  repeated"; payroll's advice that it was "better characterised as a rostering practice issue for the
  line manager". "Characterised" is their own word.
- On 12 Oct they declined to put characterisation in writing before the conference (direction 5).

## 5. What the research says

| Finding | Source | Here |
|---|---|---|
| Overhearers understand less than addressees, even hearing the same words. Addressees build understanding with the speaker; overhearers cannot | Schober & Clark 1989; Clark & Schaefer 1989 | Matheson was an unaddressed participant for about 59 of 64 minutes |
| Speakers design talk for the addressee, not the auditor | Bell 1984; Goffman 1981 | His talk was built for you |
| Shorthand is a "conceptual pact" between two people; newcomers need the long version | Clark & Wilkes-Gibbs 1986; Brennan & Clark 1996 | "Unassessed unilateral directives" was your shorthand |
| Judges engage more readily with rule-oriented accounts (rules, documents, duties) than relational ones | Conley & O'Barr 1990 | The 9A moved from the second to the first |
| Speakers overestimate how visible their structure is: tappers expected half their songs to be named; 2.5% were | Newton 1990; Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber 1989 | Your "so" at 29:17; his "after my speech about efficiency" |
| Listeners first interpret through their own frame and correct late | Keysar, Barr, Balin & Brauner 2000 | His frame was efficiency, so the application sounded like drift |
| After long turns, listeners keep the gist, not the wording; working memory holds about four chunks | Sachs 1967; Cowan 2001 | The seven-minute opening |
| People ask "what do you mean?" or similar about once every 84 seconds in everyday talk | Dingemanse et al. 2015 | Zero requests from you or Matheson in 64 minutes: in court the bench asks and parties answer (Atkinson & Drew 1979; Drew & Heritage 1992). Silence is not evidence of understanding |
