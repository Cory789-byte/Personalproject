# FORM 4 — APPLICATION IN EXISTING PROCEEDINGS

**QUEENSLAND INDUSTRIAL RELATIONS COMMISSION** — *Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003 (Qld)* — Matter No: WC/2024/227

**APPLICATION IN EXISTING PROCEEDINGS** *(Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011, r 64G)*

## OVERVIEW
This is an application under r 64G for the Commission to decide MSH's objection to a sealed Notice of Non-Party Disclosure. The Appellant has narrowed the Notice, withdrawn what the record does not need, and seeks production by a single self-executing step — a server-side export of records MSH already holds, bounded by identified custodian, date-range and phrase-family, requiring no review or reading of any email. Where a record is said not to exist, the Appellant asks only that the non-existence be stated on oath after a defined search. The objection therefore reduces to one question: whether that bounded export and produce-or-swear verification should be ordered, or whether a non-party's un-sworn assertion that its emergency-code paging logs and fatigue records "do not exist" should instead be accepted. The grounds below address relevance, possession, proportionality, and each ground of objection in turn; the relief sought is at Part B and in the Draft Order.

## PART C — GROUNDS
1. **The matter in issue.** The Form 29 is directly relevant to the matter in issue in the appeal (the s 32(5)(a) "reasonable management action" defence) as articulated in the Amended Statement of Facts and Contentions filed 7 April 2026 ("the Amended 9A"). The Continuation Schedule to the Form 29 states that matter in issue and ties each Item, by a Relevance Anchor Table, to specified stressors pleaded in the Amended 9A and to the admitted facts in the Respondent's Form 24.

2. **The health dimension.** The documents sought are not collateral to a private dispute; they go to whether the patient-safety and clinical-governance functions of the Switchboard were compromised. As deposed (Affidavit, paras 6–25), the Switchboard is the hospital's communications hub — the point at which emergency codes are activated and urgent pathology results are relayed doctor-to-doctor. The misrouted-call corpus (Item 3(a)), the manager's presence record (Item 9), and the manager's directives (Item 10) bear directly on whether those time-critical clinical and emergency communications were delayed or misdirected. The Commission's evaluation of the s 32(5)(a) defence is, in this matter, inseparable from the patient-safety reality the records will reveal — and that reality is presently visible only from within MSH's own systems.

3. **Possession and control.** As deposed (Affidavit, para 46), each pressed Item is recorded in documents within MSH's exclusive possession or control — its email system, its Active Directory / Entra / Citrix identity-management environment, its Security Access swipe-card system, its meeting and governance records, and its roster and HR systems. The Appellant, as the Switchboard operator, cannot produce them.

4. **No other reasonably simple, inexpensive way (r 64B).** The probative content of the documents is their completeness — the scale, frequency and pattern of the conduct pleaded. The Appellant can produce individual examples (and has annexed them to the Affidavit), but cannot export the complete corpus. There is no other reasonably simple, inexpensive way for the Appellant to prove what the documents show, save by bulk server-side export from MSH's systems, which is achievable at minimal cost and requires no inbox review.

5. **The period is the course of conduct — not a narrow window, and not unlimited.** Because the injury developed cumulatively over the course of management conduct, the documents go to the volume and frequency of that conduct. The Form 29 noticed the email-based Items (3(a), 3(b) and 10) for the period 1 December 2023 to 30 June 2024, and the Appellant presses no period earlier or broader than the Notice; the SPOK paging Item (Item 1) is confined to 17–18 March 2024. Confining disclosure to those two days alone would sample the conduct, not show it; the period the Notice fixes is the appropriate measure of the pleaded course (and see the global evaluation of a composite course in *Delaney v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 11; 178 QGIG 197). The volume of directives from the Line Manager to the Switchboard across the noticed period is sought not by producing the corpus but by an aggregate monthly count (sender, date, recipient-list and subject only — no message bodies: Schedule A, Item 10), which proves the scale without any review of content (Stressor 1(a)).

5A. **The fatigue-governance records (Items 4, 5 and 7) are records MSH's own policy requires it to hold.** The Queensland Health Fatigue Risk Management policy (QH-POL-171) applies, on its terms, to "employees working for Queensland Health"; it integrated and superseded the former medical- and practitioner-only fatigue policies and contains no carve-out for non-clinical, administrative, or Switchboard staff, and the related fatigue-leave policy (QH-POL-135) expressly covers "Administrative stream employees." The policy requires the line manager to ensure fatigue risk in the work area is assessed and recorded on the work health and safety risk register. MSH's objection that fatigue records do not exist because "Fatigue Risk Management System training only applies to health practitioners and clinical assistants" is therefore contradicted by the policy itself; and MSH's separate statement that FRMS was "implemented at Switchboard… after 30 June 2024" concedes the policy applied to the Switchboard. Items 4, 5 and 7 are records the policy required MSH to create and hold for the Switchboard work area. The framework is the post-Neville fatigue regime Queensland Health adopted after the Queensland Ombudsman's 2006 finding that fatigue from long hours contributed to a death (as Queensland Health records in its FRMS Implementation Guideline QH-GDL-401-3.3) — so the employer had documented knowledge of the very hazard (cf. the failure-to-mitigate-a-known-risk line in *Granato v Workers' Compensation Regulator* [2024] QIRC 162).

6. **The documents exist.** The objection cannot rest on non-existence. MSH itself produced documents responsive to Items 6, 11, 12, 13, 15 and 16 with its 5 June 2026 letter — including my myHR leave records and AVAC history for Process Reference 15480560 (Item 11) — which establishes that the classes of record sought are real and retrievable from MSH's systems; and on 11 June 2026 the Respondent disclosed further responsive material drawn from within MSH's systems (the 15–16 May 2024 correspondence falling within the Item 20 window). The Appellant has further annexed individual examples (including the manager's email of 9 May 2024 and the union correspondence of 18–20 May 2024) which demonstrate, on their face, that the classes of document sought are real.

7. **The application is not oppressive — it seeks an export, not a review.** The Appellant does not seek the review, reading, or relevance-assessment of any email by MSH. He seeks only the native electronic export of the messages and records returned by a server-side filter applied to the identified custodians, identified date-ranges and identified phrase-families set out in the Form 29. The filter is self-executing and requires no inbox review of any kind. Each Item is bounded at the system level; the relief is the antithesis of a general search; and it is offered subject to nil-result discharge (the verification affidavit at Part B(b)(ii)). Should MSH assert legal professional privilege over any specific record returned by the filter, it may do so item-by-item, which does not require, and is not a basis for resisting, the export itself. Further, if any question of privacy or confidentiality is raised, the Appellant consents to the export being produced to the Commission for its inspection in the first instance (Part B(d)), with access to the parties as the Commission directs. No question of privacy or privilege can therefore justify refusing the export — at most it governs the manner of access, which is the Commission's to manage.

8. **The verification affidavit is the proportionate course.** Framed as produce-or-swear, the order leaves MSH a single, bounded step: it either produces the documents, or verifies their non-existence on oath after a defined search. Either course resolves the objection without an item-by-item production dispute. The Appellant respectfully submits this is the orderly and proportionate disposition.

8A. **The model-litigant expectation on a government respondent.** In *State of Queensland (Queensland Police Service) v Workers' Compensation Regulator* [2025] QIRC 203 (O'Neill IC), the Commission, in a disclosure dispute, applied the model-litigant principles to a State respondent. While that duty binds the Regulator (and the State) as a party, the Appellant respectfully submits that the same expectation of candour and cooperation should inform how a government entity such as MSH approaches a bounded, self-executing server-side export: it should facilitate, not resist, production, and a bare assertion that its emergency-code paging logs or fatigue records "do not exist" should be tested on oath rather than accepted.

9. **Evidentiary context.** The Respondent itself obtained the Appellant's primary medical records without issuing a Notice of Non-Party Disclosure (Form 29) under r 64C of the *Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011* (the admitted fact characterising this as a breach of r 64E: Form 24, Para 25). The process MSH now resists is the process the Appellant has scrupulously followed and the Respondent did not.

## PART C2 — MSH'S OBJECTIONS ANSWERED (letter of 5 June 2026, K-LM26/729, signed by the Chief Executive)
MSH's objections reduce to three refrains, each of which the Notice already answers — and the letter itself supplies several of the answers the disclosure was designed to obtain.

1. *"We have not received the Form 9A, so we cannot assess relevance."* The Notice does not depend on the supply of the parties' pleadings to a non-party. The Continuation Schedule states the matter in issue and ties each Item, by a Relevance Anchor Table, to a specified Stressor and an admitted Form 24 fact on its face (r 64C). Relevance is demonstrable from the Notice itself; the absence of the Form 9A in MSH's hands is not a ground under r 64E(4).

2. *"Compliance would require eHealth to review and search individual staff email accounts" (Items 3(a), 10).* It would not. The Appellant seeks a server-side export (a self-executing filter bounded by identified custodian, identified date-range and identified phrase-family) and expressly disclaims any inbox review (Notice, Administrative Concessions; Part B(a)). MSH's "expense and inconvenience" objection rests on a characterisation of the request as a manual account-by-account search, which is incorrect. As to Item 3(a) in particular, MSH's own letter records that complaints about Switchboard operational errors "are made directly to the Line Manager of Switch Board and managed solely via email" — i.e. a single identified mailbox, not a hospital-wide trawl.

3. *"The matter in issue relates only to the consecutive shifts of 17–18 March 2024."* It does not. The 7-hour break (Stressor 3) is one of three pleaded stressors; Stressor 1(a) pleads a course of conduct. The Form 29 noticed the email-based Items (3(a), 3(b) and 10) from 1 December 2023 to 30 June 2024; confining disclosure to two days would sample that course, not show it (*Delaney v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 11).

4. *"Item 19 would require searching the emails of ~20,006 staff."* It would not. Item 19 is confined to identified custodians (the Line Manager, the Director, the Logan Hospital health-team mailbox and HR) and is narrowed to 18 May – 18 June 2024 (Schedule A): the very limitation MSH itself proposed in the letter.

5. **The letter supplies the answers, under the Chief Executive's hand.** In responding, MSH has stated: (a) *No remedial change* — "there have been no 'consequential' changes to operating procedures over the period requested" (Item 3(c)): despite the misroute complaints, nothing was changed. (b) *No fatigue framework at the time, and a restructure* — fatigue risk management at the Switchboard "occurred after 30 June 2024 in connection with an organisational change related to the reporting lines for Switchboard" (Item 5): no FRMS framework was in place at the date of injury. (c) *Emergency-code logs not retained* — the SPOK records "do not exist… records predating [the system] upgrade were not retained," while "a spreadsheet of recorded MET calls is available for the period 17–18 March 2024" (Items 1–2): the "spreadsheet" being the call-record spreadsheet the Appellant himself authors and maintains as part of his Switchboard duties (Affidavit, ¶50) — the operator's own record, not the SPOK system-of-record and not an MSH record independent of the Appellant. These are not reasons to refuse production; they are matters directly relevant to the s 32(5)(a) inquiry.

6. **The verification affidavit should formalise these statements.** Where MSH states a record does not exist (Items 1–2, 3(c), 4, 5, 7, 14, 20), the order at Part B(b) requires that to be stated on oath by the Chief Executive or a delegated Director, with the search identified — so the statements made informally in the letter are placed on the evidentiary footing the appeal requires. In particular, the verification should address, on oath: (i) Items 1–2 (SPOK): the date and nature of the system upgrade, and whether the pre-upgrade emergency-code paging records were migrated, archived or destroyed, and the basis on which they were "not retained" — an upgrade not ordinarily entailing the disposal of a hospital's emergency-code logs; (ii) Item 3(c): the "no consequential changes" statement, reconciled with the changes the letter itself records (the SPOK upgrade, the reporting-line restructure and the issue of a Queensland Health device) each occurring after the injury; (iii) Items 4, 5 and 7: what fatigue-management framework governed the Switchboard as at 17–18 March 2024 (see ground 5A); and (iv) Item 20: MSH states PID 24-ESU-1130 "commenced in November 2024", whereas the underlying complaint was lodged on 13 May 2024 (Affidavit, ¶34; the determination of that complaint as a Public Interest Disclosure on 24 December 2024 being admitted at Form 24, Para 20). The verification must identify whether records of the operational and HR response in the 13–31 May 2024 window exist — particularly because the Respondent itself disclosed, on 11 June 2026, correspondence dated 15–16 May 2024 falling within that very window (Affidavit, ¶52). MSH cannot swear that the Item 20 material "does not exist" consistently with that disclosure: the verification must therefore either produce the records, or contradict its own 5 June 2026 letter or the Respondent's own disclosure — the reference number being a later-assigned identifier, not the date the underlying material came into existence.
