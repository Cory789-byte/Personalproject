## PART C — GROUNDS

1. **The matter in issue.** The Form 29 is directly relevant to the matter in issue — the s 32(5)(a) "reasonable management action" defence — as articulated in the Amended 9A. The Continuation Schedule to the Form 29 states that matter in issue and ties each Item, by a Relevance Anchor Table, to specified stressors pleaded in the Amended 9A and to admitted facts in the Respondent's Form 24.

2. **The health dimension.** The documents are not collateral to a private dispute; they go to whether the patient-safety and clinical-governance functions of the Switchboard were compromised. The Switchboard is the hospital's communications hub — where emergency codes are activated and urgent pathology results relayed doctor-to-doctor. The Appellant deposes that he personally handles in the order of 250 to 300 calls per shift, coordinates Code Blue and MET activations through SPOK, fields complaints about switchboard and paging matters, handles after-hours calls, and absorbs roster and procedure changes issued at short notice (Affidavit, paras 2–3). The misrouted-call corpus (Item 3(a)), the manager's presence records (Items 8–9), and the directive corpus (Item 10) bear directly on whether time-critical clinical and emergency communications were delayed or misdirected. The Commission's evaluation of the s 32(5)(a) defence is, in this matter, inseparable from the patient-safety reality the records will reveal — a reality presently visible only from within MSH's own systems.

3. **Possession and control.** Each pressed Item is recorded in documents within MSH's exclusive possession or control — its email system, its Active Directory / Entra / Citrix identity-management environment, its Security Access swipe-card system, its meeting and governance records, and its roster and HR systems. The Appellant, as the Switchboard operator, cannot produce them.

4. **No other reasonably simple, inexpensive way (r 64B).** The probative content is the documents' completeness — the scale, frequency and pattern of the conduct pleaded. The Appellant can produce individual examples (annexed to the Affidavit) but cannot export the complete corpus. There is no other reasonably simple, inexpensive way to prove what the documents show, save by bulk server-side export from MSH's systems, achievable at minimal cost and requiring no inbox review.

5. **The period is the course of conduct.** Because the injury developed cumulatively, the matter the documents go to is the volume and frequency of conduct across the pleaded period. The Appellant presses no period earlier or broader than the Form 29 itself — for the email-based Items, the date range stated in the Notice and quoted by MSH is 1 December 2023 to 30 June 2024. The Appellant seeks neither production beyond the course of conduct, nor a window so narrow that it samples rather than shows it. The full corpus of directives from the Line Manager to the Switchboard across that period is the direct and only measure of how much of the conduct the role endured (Stressor 1(a); and see the global evaluation of a composite course in *Delaney v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 11; 178 QGIG 197).

6. **The documents exist.** The objection cannot rest on non-existence. MSH has itself produced documents responsive to Items 6, 11, 12, 13, 15 and 16 — including the myHR audit trail for leave record 15480560 (Item 11) — which establishes that the classes of record sought are real and retrievable from MSH's systems. The Respondent separately disclosed that same leave record on 11 June 2026, having obtained it from within MSH's systems through conferencing and without itself issuing a Form 29 (Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 25). The Appellant has further annexed individual examples — including the Line Manager's email of 9 May 2024 and the union correspondence of 18–20 May 2024 — which demonstrate, on their face, that the classes of document sought are real.

7. **The application is not oppressive — it seeks an export, not a review.** The Appellant does not seek the review, reading or relevance-assessment of any email by MSH. He seeks only the native electronic export of the messages and records returned by a server-side filter applied to the identified custodians, identified date-ranges and identified phrase-families set out in the Form 29. The filter is self-executing and requires no inbox review. Each Item is bounded at the system level; the relief is the antithesis of a general search; and it is offered subject to nil-result discharge. Should MSH assert legal professional privilege over any specific record returned by the filter, it may do so item-by-item, which is not a basis for resisting the export itself. If any question of privacy or confidentiality is raised, the Appellant consents to the export being produced to the Commission for its inspection in the first instance, with access as the Commission directs.

8. **The verification affidavit is the proportionate course.** Framed as produce-or-swear, the order leaves MSH a single, bounded step: produce, or verify non-existence on oath after a defined search. Either course resolves the objection without an item-by-item production dispute.

9. **Evidentiary context.** The Respondent itself obtained the Appellant's primary medical records without issuing a Form 29 (r 64C) (Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para 25). The process MSH now resists is the process the Appellant has scrupulously followed and the Respondent did not.

## PART C2 — MSH'S OBJECTIONS ANSWERED (letter of 5 June 2026, K-LM26/729, signed by the Chief Executive)
MSH's objections reduce to three refrains, each of which the Notice already answers — and the letter itself supplies several of the answers the disclosure was designed to obtain.

1. *"We have not received the Form 9A, so we cannot assess relevance."* The Notice does not depend on supply of the parties' pleadings to a non-party. The Continuation Schedule states the matter in issue and ties each Item, by a Relevance Anchor Table, to a specified Stressor and an admitted Form 24 fact on its face (r 64C). The absence of the Form 9A in MSH's hands is not a ground under r 64E(4).

2. *"Compliance would require eHealth to review and search individual staff email accounts" (Items 3(a), 8, 10).* It would not. The Appellant seeks a server-side export — a self-executing filter bounded by identified custodian, date-range and phrase-family — and expressly disclaims any inbox review. The "expense and inconvenience" objection rests on an incorrect characterisation of the request as a manual account-by-account search. As to Item 3(a) in particular, MSH's own letter defeats the premise: it states that complaints about Switchboard operational errors "are made directly to the Line Manager of Switch Board and managed solely via email." On MSH's own account the records sit in a single identified mailbox (the Line Manager's), so the export is precisely targeted — not the hospital-wide trawl of "~20,006 staff" the objection invokes.

3. *"The matter in issue relates only to the consecutive shifts of 17–18 March 2024."* It does not. The 7-hour break (Stressor 3) is one of three pleaded stressors; Stressor 1(a) pleads a course of conduct across 2023–2024. Confining disclosure to two days would sample the conduct, not show it (*Delaney v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 11).

4. *"Item 19 would require searching the emails of ~20,006 staff."* It would not. Item 19 is confined to identified custodians — the Line Manager, the Director, the Logan Hospital health-team mailbox and HR — and is narrowed to **18 May – 18 June 2024** (Schedule A): the very limitation MSH itself proposed.

5. *"The Switchboard office does not have its own access-swipe access" (Item 9).* That answers a question not asked. Item 9 does not seek a dedicated reader on the Switchboard office door; it seeks the Line Manager's entry and exit records at the building and floor swipe-card access points that serve the collocated area — records that exist in MSH's Security Access system irrespective of whether the office itself has a dedicated reader, and that go to the Line Manager's "erratic physical presence" (Stressor 1(a)).

6. *FRMS — Items 4 and 5 answer different requests.* MSH objects to both on the footing that "Fatigue Risk Management System training only applies to health practitioners and clinical assistants." That answers Item 5 (training and competency records). It does not answer Item 4, which seeks the **fatigue risk-assessment record** for the pleaded shift pairing — a record the fatigue risk-management framework requires regardless of whether the staff are clinical and regardless of any mandatory-training threshold. MSH has conflated the training (Item 5) with the risk assessment (Item 4); the non-existence asserted for Item 4 should be tested on oath under Part B(b).

7. **The letter supplies the answers, under the Chief Executive's hand.** In responding, MSH has stated: (a) *No remedial change* — "there have been no 'consequential' changes to operating procedures over the period requested" (Item 3(c)); (b) *No fatigue framework at the time, and a restructure* — fatigue risk management at the Switchboard "occurred after 30 June 2024 in connection with an organisational change related to the reporting lines for Switchboard" (Item 5); (c) *Emergency-code logs not retained* — the SPOK records "do not exist… records predating [the system] upgrade were not retained," while "a spreadsheet of recorded MET calls is available for the period 17–18 March 2024" (Items 1–2). These are not reasons to refuse production; they are matters directly relevant to the s 32(5)(a) inquiry.

8. **The verification affidavit should formalise these statements.** Where MSH states a record does not exist or cannot be produced (Items 1–2, 3(c), 4, 5, 7, 14, 20), the order at Part B(b) requires that to be stated on oath by the Chief Executive or a delegated Director, with the search identified. (As to Item 20, MSH states PID 24-ESU-1130 "commenced in November 2024"; the underlying complaint was lodged 13 May 2024 (Form 24, Para 20). The verification can clarify whether records of the operational/HR response to the disclosures of that period exist, irrespective of the internal reference, and whether such records were included in MSH's earlier production.)
