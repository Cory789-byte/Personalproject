## OVERVIEW
This is an application under r 64G for the Commission to decide MSH's objection to a sealed Notice of Non-Party Disclosure. The Appellant has narrowed the Notice, withdrawn what the record does not need, and seeks production by a single self-executing step — a server-side export of records MSH already holds, bounded by identified custodian, date-range and phrase-family, requiring no review or reading of any email. Where a record is said not to exist, the Appellant asks only that the non-existence be stated on oath after a defined search. The objection therefore reduces to one question: whether that bounded export and produce-or-swear verification should be ordered, or whether a non-party's un-sworn assertion that its emergency-code paging logs and fatigue records "do not exist" should instead be accepted. The Appellant does not seek any medical records, the financial or payroll data of other employees, or any patient or other sensitive personal or health information; where any such identifying information appears in the records produced, it may be redacted (Part B(d)).

## PART C — GROUNDS
1. **The matter in issue.** The Form 29 is directly relevant to the matter in issue in the appeal (the s 32(5)(a) "reasonable management action" defence) as articulated in the Amended Statement of Facts and Contentions filed 7 April 2026 ("the Amended 9A"). The Continuation Schedule to the Form 29 states that matter in issue and ties each Item, by a Relevance Anchor Table, to specified stressors pleaded in the Amended 9A and to the admitted facts in the Respondent's Form 24.

2. **The health dimension.** The documents are not collateral to a private dispute; they go to whether the patient-safety and clinical-governance functions of the Switchboard were compromised. As deposed (Affidavit, ¶¶6–25), the Switchboard is the hospital's communications hub — where emergency codes are activated and urgent pathology results relayed doctor-to-doctor. The misrouted-call corpus (Item 3(a)), the manager's presence record (Item 9) and her directives (Item 10) bear directly on whether those time-critical communications were delayed or misdirected — a patient-safety reality visible only from within MSH's own systems, and inseparable from the s 32(5)(a) evaluation.

3. **Possession and control.** As deposed (Affidavit, para 46), each pressed Item is recorded in documents within MSH's exclusive possession or control — its email system, its Active Directory / Entra / Citrix identity-management environment, its Security Access swipe-card system, its meeting and governance records, and its roster and HR systems. The Appellant, as the Switchboard operator, cannot produce them.

4. **No other reasonably simple, inexpensive way (r 64B).** The probative content of the documents is their completeness — the scale, frequency and pattern of the conduct pleaded. The Appellant can produce individual examples (and has annexed them to the Affidavit), but cannot export the complete corpus. There is no other reasonably simple, inexpensive way for the Appellant to prove what the documents show, save by bulk server-side export from MSH's systems, which is achievable at minimal cost and requires no inbox review.

5. **The period is the course of conduct — not a narrow window, and not unlimited.** Because the injury developed cumulatively over the course of management conduct, the documents go to the volume and frequency of that conduct. The Form 29 noticed the email-based Items (3(a), 3(b) and 10) for the period 1 December 2023 to 30 June 2024, and the Appellant presses no period earlier or broader than the Notice; the SPOK paging Item (Item 1) is confined to 17–18 March 2024. Confining disclosure to those two days alone would sample the conduct, not show it; the period the Notice fixes is the appropriate measure of the pleaded course (and see the global evaluation of a composite course in *Delaney v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 11; 178 QGIG 197). The volume of directives from the Line Manager to the Switchboard across the noticed period is sought not by producing the corpus but by an aggregate monthly count (sender, date, recipient-list and subject only — no message bodies: Schedule A, Item 10), which proves the scale without any review of content (Stressor 1(a)).

6. **The fatigue-governance records (Items 4, 5 and 7).** MSH's own letter states that fatigue risk management at the Switchboard was "implemented… after 30 June 2024 in connection with an organisational change related to the reporting lines for Switchboard" — which concedes that a fatigue risk-management framework applies to the Switchboard work area, and locates its implementation there only after the date of injury. That framework reflects the Queensland Health Fatigue Risk Management System (FRMS Implementation Guideline QH-GDL-401-3.3). MSH's objection that fatigue records do not exist because "Fatigue Risk Management System training only applies to health practitioners and clinical assistants" answers a different question: Item 5 concerns training, but Items 4 and 7 concern the fatigue risk-*assessment* and register records. Whether those records exist for the Switchboard as at 17–18 March 2024 is precisely what the verification on oath (Part B(b)) should establish.

7. **The objection cannot rest on non-existence.** MSH itself produced documents responsive to Items 6, 11, 12, 13, 15 and 16 with its 5 June 2026 letter, and the Respondent disclosed further responsive material on 11 June 2026 (the 15–16 May 2024 correspondence within the Item 20 window) — establishing that the classes of record sought are real and retrievable from MSH's systems. The Appellant has also annexed individual examples (the manager's 9 May 2024 email; the 18–20 May 2024 union correspondence) which show the same on their face.

8. **The application is not oppressive — it seeks an export, not a review.** The Appellant seeks only the native electronic export of the records returned by a self-executing server-side filter (the identified custodians, date-ranges and phrase-families in the Form 29); no inbox review, reading or relevance-assessment by MSH is sought or required. The relief is the antithesis of a general search, and is offered subject to nil-result discharge (Part B(b)). Any claim of legal professional privilege may be made item-by-item over a specific record and is no basis for resisting the export itself; and any question of privacy or confidentiality is met by production to the Commission in the first instance (Part B(d)). No question of privilege or privacy justifies refusing the export — at most it governs the manner of access, which is the Commission's to manage.

9. **The verification affidavit is the proportionate course.** Framed as produce-or-swear, the order leaves MSH a single, bounded step: it either produces the documents, or verifies their non-existence on oath after a defined search. Either course resolves the objection without an item-by-item production dispute. The power to make the order arises under r 64G(2), which empowers the Commission to make any order appropriate to its decision of the objection, together with the Commission's general power to give directions for the conduct of the proceeding; the order does no more than ensure the objection — production, or verified non-existence — is resolved on a proper evidentiary footing. For each Item, MSH must therefore either produce the record or swear, after a defined search, that it does not exist; there is no third answer, and an un-sworn assertion of non-existence in correspondence is not a basis on which the objection can be sustained. The Appellant respectfully submits this is the orderly and proportionate disposition.

10. **The model-litigant expectation.** In *State of Queensland (Queensland Police Service) v Workers' Compensation Regulator* [2025] QIRC 203 (O'Neill IC), the Commission applied the model-litigant principles to a State respondent in a disclosure dispute. While that duty binds the Regulator as a party, the same expectation of candour should inform how a government entity such as MSH approaches a bounded, self-executing export: it should facilitate production, and a bare assertion that its emergency-code logs or fatigue records "do not exist" should be tested on oath, not accepted.

11. **Evidentiary context.** The Respondent obtained the Appellant's medical records under a Form 29 never served on him; the Respondent admits the non-service, saying it was not required to serve him as a party (Form 24, Para 25). The Appellant does not agitate the merits of that course here, but the contrast is instructive: he cannot simply obtain MSH's records — he must serve a sealed Notice on MSH and the persons affected (rr 64C, 64D), absorb their objection (r 64E) and bring this application (r 64G); his own records were taken from a third party without the Notice ever reaching him.

## PART C2 — MSH'S OBJECTIONS ANSWERED (letter of 5 June 2026, K-LM26/729, signed by the Chief Executive)
MSH's objections reduce to three refrains, each of which the Notice already answers — and the letter itself supplies several of the answers the disclosure was designed to obtain.

1. *"We have not received the Form 9A, so we cannot assess relevance."* The Notice does not depend on the supply of the parties' pleadings to a non-party. The Continuation Schedule states the matter in issue and ties each Item, by a Relevance Anchor Table, to a specified Stressor and an admitted Form 24 fact on its face (r 64C). Relevance is demonstrable from the Notice itself; the absence of the Form 9A in MSH's hands is not a ground under r 64E(4).

2. *"Compliance would require eHealth to review and search individual staff email accounts" (Items 3(a), 10).* It would not. The Appellant seeks a server-side export (a self-executing filter bounded by identified custodian, identified date-range and identified phrase-family) and expressly disclaims any inbox review (Notice, Administrative Concessions; Part B(a)). MSH's "expense and inconvenience" objection rests on a characterisation of the request as a manual account-by-account search, which is incorrect. As to Item 3(a) in particular, MSH's own letter records that complaints about Switchboard operational errors "are made directly to the Line Manager of Switch Board and managed solely via email" — i.e. a single identified mailbox, not a hospital-wide trawl.

3. *"The matter in issue relates only to the consecutive shifts of 17–18 March 2024."* It does not — Stressor 1(a) pleads a course of conduct, of which the 7-hour break (Stressor 3) is one part. The Notice fixes the email-Items at 1 December 2023 – 30 June 2024; confining disclosure to two days would sample that course, not show it (ground 5; *Delaney v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 11).

4. *"Item 19 would require searching the emails of ~20,006 staff."* It would not. Item 19 is confined to identified custodians (the Line Manager, the Director, the Logan Hospital health-team mailbox and HR) and is narrowed to 18 May – 18 June 2024 (Schedule A), adopting the custodians MSH itself proposed; MSH's suggested 17–18 March 2024 date predates the 18 May 2024 union request and so could not capture the correspondence.

5. *"Item 9 (building access) lacks relevance and particularity, and the Switchboard office has no swipe access."* The Appellant does not seek a switchboard-office swipe. He seeks the Line Manager's own security access-card records (by her card identity) for the collocated office area and the building — records MSH's access-control system holds — confined to the pleaded-conduct windows (Schedule A). Particularity is satisfied (one identified person; identified dates); relevance is direct (whether the manager was on site when a directive issued or a complaint was left unactioned, going to Stressor 1(a)); and any effect on the Line Manager is managed by the Commission-inspection-first safeguard (Part B(d)), which governs the manner of access, not refusal.

6. **The letter supplies the answers, under the Chief Executive's hand.** In responding, MSH has stated: (a) *No remedial change* — MSH states there have been "no 'consequential' changes to operating procedures over the period requested" (Item 3(c)): nothing was changed to remedy the misroute complaints — although operational changes that worsened the position were in fact made during the period (the unilateral "effective immediately" directives altering call-handling, and the restriction of contact-number and directory updates to the new Switchboard Telecommunications Coordinator role: Affidavit ¶¶22, 26–27), so the statement is, on the Appellant's evidence, also incorrect. (b) *No fatigue framework at the time, and a restructure* — fatigue risk management at the Switchboard "occurred after 30 June 2024 in connection with an organisational change related to the reporting lines for Switchboard" (Item 5): no FRMS framework was in place at the date of injury. (c) *Emergency-code logs not retained* — the SPOK records "do not exist… records predating [the system] upgrade were not retained," yet "a spreadsheet of recorded MET calls is available for the period 17–18 March 2024" (Items 1–2). Whether that spreadsheet is a SPOK export, a separate MSH record, or the monthly call-statistics sheet the Appellant himself authors cannot be told from the letter (Schedule C requires the verification to classify it). If it is the Appellant's own sheet, it records call and code *volume* only and does not satisfy Item 1, which seeks the SPOK system-level paging data — which clinician was paged, whether each page was received or acknowledged, and any misdirection, with timestamps — that MSH alone holds (Affidavit, ¶9). If it is a separate MSH or SPOK record, it is a contemporaneous record for the exact pleaded shift and should be produced under Item 1. Either way the matter goes to the s 32(5)(a) inquiry, not to refusing production.

7. **The verification affidavit should formalise these statements.** Where MSH states a record does not exist (Items 1–2, 3(c), 4, 5, 7, 18, 20), the order at Part B(b) requires that to be sworn by the Chief Executive or a delegated Director, after a defined search by a named person and with the required contents set out Item by Item in Schedule C — so the statements made informally in the letter are placed on the evidentiary footing the appeal requires. The Item 20 answer illustrates why a sworn answer matters: MSH says that material "does not exist" because "PID24-ESU-1130" commenced in November 2024, yet the Respondent disclosed correspondence of 15–16 May 2024 — squarely within the Item 20 window — on 11 June 2026 (Affidavit, ¶52). MSH cannot swear non-existence consistently with that disclosure; the reference number is a later-assigned identifier, not the date the material came into existence. So too the Item 18 "already conceded" answer (Form 24, Para 14): only the removal of a page is admitted (Form 24, Para 6), not the fate of the communication book or the 15 November 2023 business-case minutes. Each such answer should be sworn, not asserted.

## SCHEDULE C — REQUIRED CONTENTS OF THE VERIFICATION AFFIDAVIT (Part B(b))
This Schedule states what the verification affidavit must depose to. It is directed only to verifying, after a defined search, what exists and what is to be produced; it is not directed to any wider inquiry into MSH's conduct. So that a statement of non-existence is verified rather than asserted in the abstract, the affidavit must address the following.

**Part 1 — For every Item for which production is sought, or non-existence, destruction or irrelevance is asserted, the affidavit must depose:**

(1) that a search was in fact undertaken (a bare assertion of non-existence, unsupported by a deposed search, being insufficient); the name and position of the person who conducted the search, and whether that person had access to the systems searched; the systems, mailboxes, repositories and custodians searched; the search terms, filters and date-range applied; and the date on which the search was undertaken;

(2) whether the record exists as at the date of the affidavit; if it does not, whether it ever existed; and, if it once existed, when and by what means it ceased to exist (deletion, system migration, archiving, disposal or destruction), on whose authority or instruction, and under what retention policy or schedule;

(3) whether any copy, backup, archive, export, migrated instance, or version held on any superseding system exists, and if so its production;

(4) where any document already before the Commission, or disclosed in this proceeding, appears to fall within the Item, identification of that document and either its production or the precise basis on which it is said to fall outside the Item; and
