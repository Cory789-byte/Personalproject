## OVERVIEW
This is an application under r 64G for the Commission to decide MSH's objection to a sealed Notice of Non-Party Disclosure. The Appellant has narrowed the Notice, withdrawn what the record does not need, and seeks production by a single self-executing step — a server-side export of records MSH already holds, bounded by identified custodian, date-range and phrase-family, requiring no review or reading of any email. Where a record is said not to exist, the Appellant asks only that the non-existence be stated on oath after a defined search. The objection therefore reduces to one question: whether that bounded export and produce-or-swear verification should be ordered, or whether a non-party's un-sworn assertion that its emergency-code paging logs and fatigue records "do not exist" should instead be accepted. The grounds below address relevance, possession, proportionality, and each ground of objection in turn; the relief sought is set out at Part B as the draft orders sought.

## PART C — GROUNDS
1. **The matter in issue.** The Form 29 is directly relevant to the matter in issue in the appeal (the s 32(5)(a) "reasonable management action" defence) as articulated in the Amended Statement of Facts and Contentions filed 7 April 2026 ("the Amended 9A"). The Continuation Schedule to the Form 29 states that matter in issue and ties each Item, by a Relevance Anchor Table, to specified stressors pleaded in the Amended 9A and to the admitted facts in the Respondent's Form 24.

2. **The health dimension.** The documents sought are not collateral to a private dispute; they go to whether the patient-safety and clinical-governance functions of the Switchboard were compromised. As deposed (Affidavit, paras 6–25), the Switchboard is the hospital's communications hub — the point at which emergency codes are activated and urgent pathology results are relayed doctor-to-doctor. The misrouted-call corpus (Item 3(a)), the manager's presence record (Item 9), and the manager's directives (Item 10) bear directly on whether those time-critical clinical and emergency communications were delayed or misdirected. The Commission's evaluation of the s 32(5)(a) defence is, in this matter, inseparable from the patient-safety reality the records will reveal — and that reality is presently visible only from within MSH's own systems.

3. **Possession and control.** As deposed (Affidavit, para 46), each pressed Item is recorded in documents within MSH's exclusive possession or control — its email system, its Active Directory / Entra / Citrix identity-management environment, its Security Access swipe-card system, its meeting and governance records, and its roster and HR systems. The Appellant, as the Switchboard operator, cannot produce them.

4. **No other reasonably simple, inexpensive way (r 64B).** The probative content of the documents is their completeness — the scale, frequency and pattern of the conduct pleaded. The Appellant can produce individual examples (and has annexed them to the Affidavit), but cannot export the complete corpus. There is no other reasonably simple, inexpensive way for the Appellant to prove what the documents show, save by bulk server-side export from MSH's systems, which is achievable at minimal cost and requires no inbox review.

5. **The period is the course of conduct — not a narrow window, and not unlimited.** Because the injury developed cumulatively over the course of management conduct, the documents go to the volume and frequency of that conduct. The Form 29 noticed the email-based Items (3(a), 3(b) and 10) for the period 1 December 2023 to 30 June 2024, and the Appellant presses no period earlier or broader than the Notice; the SPOK paging Item (Item 1) is confined to 17–18 March 2024. Confining disclosure to those two days alone would sample the conduct, not show it; the period the Notice fixes is the appropriate measure of the pleaded course (and see the global evaluation of a composite course in *Delaney v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 11; 178 QGIG 197). The volume of directives from the Line Manager to the Switchboard across the noticed period is sought not by producing the corpus but by an aggregate monthly count (sender, date, recipient-list and subject only — no message bodies: Schedule A, Item 10), which proves the scale without any review of content (Stressor 1(a)).

6. **The fatigue-governance records (Items 4, 5 and 7).** MSH's own letter states that fatigue risk management at the Switchboard was "implemented… after 30 June 2024 in connection with an organisational change related to the reporting lines for Switchboard" — which concedes that a fatigue risk-management framework applies to the Switchboard work area, and locates its implementation there only after the date of injury. That framework reflects the Queensland Health Fatigue Risk Management System (FRMS Implementation Guideline QH-GDL-401-3.3). MSH's objection that fatigue records do not exist because "Fatigue Risk Management System training only applies to health practitioners and clinical assistants" answers a different question: Item 5 concerns training, but Items 4 and 7 concern the fatigue risk-*assessment* and register records. Whether those records exist for the Switchboard as at 17–18 March 2024 is precisely what the verification on oath (Part B(b)) should establish.

7. **The objection cannot rest on non-existence.** MSH itself produced documents responsive to Items 6, 11, 12, 13, 15 and 16 with its 5 June 2026 letter — including my myHR leave records and AVAC history for Process Reference 15480560 (Item 11) — which establishes that the classes of record sought are real and retrievable from MSH's systems; and on 11 June 2026 the Respondent disclosed further responsive material drawn from within MSH's systems (the 15–16 May 2024 correspondence falling within the Item 20 window). The Appellant has further annexed individual examples (including the manager's email of 9 May 2024 and the union correspondence of 18–20 May 2024) which demonstrate, on their face, that the classes of document sought are real.

8. **The application is not oppressive — it seeks an export, not a review.** The Appellant does not seek the review, reading, or relevance-assessment of any email by MSH. He seeks only the native electronic export of the messages and records returned by a server-side filter applied to the identified custodians, identified date-ranges and identified phrase-families set out in the Form 29. The filter is self-executing and requires no inbox review of any kind. Each Item is bounded at the system level; the relief is the antithesis of a general search; and it is offered subject to nil-result discharge (the verification affidavit at Part B(b)(ii)). Should MSH assert legal professional privilege over any specific record returned by the filter, it may do so item-by-item, which does not require, and is not a basis for resisting, the export itself. Further, if any question of privacy or confidentiality is raised, the Appellant consents to the export being produced to the Commission for its inspection in the first instance (Part B(d)), with access to the parties as the Commission directs. No question of privacy or privilege can therefore justify refusing the export — at most it governs the manner of access, which is the Commission's to manage.

9. **The verification affidavit is the proportionate course.** Framed as produce-or-swear, the order leaves MSH a single, bounded step: it either produces the documents, or verifies their non-existence on oath after a defined search. Either course resolves the objection without an item-by-item production dispute. The power to make the order arises under r 64G(2), which empowers the Commission to make any order appropriate to its decision of the objection, together with the Commission's general power to give directions for the conduct of the proceeding; the order does no more than ensure the objection — production, or verified non-existence — is resolved on a proper evidentiary footing. For each Item, MSH must therefore either produce the record or swear, after a defined search, that it does not exist; there is no third answer, and an un-sworn assertion of non-existence in correspondence is not a basis on which the objection can be sustained. The Appellant respectfully submits this is the orderly and proportionate disposition.

10. **The model-litigant expectation on a government respondent.** In *State of Queensland (Queensland Police Service) v Workers' Compensation Regulator* [2025] QIRC 203 (O'Neill IC), the Commission, in a disclosure dispute, applied the model-litigant principles to a State respondent. While that duty binds the Regulator (and the State) as a party, the Appellant respectfully submits that the same expectation of candour and cooperation should inform how a government entity such as MSH approaches a bounded, self-executing server-side export: it should facilitate, not resist, production, and a bare assertion that its emergency-code paging logs or fatigue records "do not exist" should be tested on oath rather than accepted.

11. **Evidentiary context.** The Respondent obtained the Appellant's primary medical records under a Notice of Non-Party Disclosure (Form 29) that was never served on the Appellant; the Respondent admits the non-service and says it was not required to serve him as a party (Form 24, Para 25). The Appellant does not, in this application, agitate the merits of that course. The contrast is nonetheless instructive: the Appellant cannot simply obtain MSH's records — he must serve a sealed Notice on MSH and on the persons affected by it (rr 64C, 64D), absorb their objection (r 64E) and bring this application (r 64G); whereas his own records were obtained from a third party without the Notice ever reaching him.

## PART C2 — MSH'S OBJECTIONS ANSWERED (letter of 5 June 2026, K-LM26/729, signed by the Chief Executive)
MSH's objections reduce to three refrains, each of which the Notice already answers — and the letter itself supplies several of the answers the disclosure was designed to obtain.

1. *"We have not received the Form 9A, so we cannot assess relevance."* The Notice does not depend on the supply of the parties' pleadings to a non-party. The Continuation Schedule states the matter in issue and ties each Item, by a Relevance Anchor Table, to a specified Stressor and an admitted Form 24 fact on its face (r 64C). Relevance is demonstrable from the Notice itself; the absence of the Form 9A in MSH's hands is not a ground under r 64E(4).

2. *"Compliance would require eHealth to review and search individual staff email accounts" (Items 3(a), 10).* It would not. The Appellant seeks a server-side export (a self-executing filter bounded by identified custodian, identified date-range and identified phrase-family) and expressly disclaims any inbox review (Notice, Administrative Concessions; Part B(a)). MSH's "expense and inconvenience" objection rests on a characterisation of the request as a manual account-by-account search, which is incorrect. As to Item 3(a) in particular, MSH's own letter records that complaints about Switchboard operational errors "are made directly to the Line Manager of Switch Board and managed solely via email" — i.e. a single identified mailbox, not a hospital-wide trawl.

3. *"The matter in issue relates only to the consecutive shifts of 17–18 March 2024."* It does not. The 7-hour break (Stressor 3) is one of three pleaded stressors; Stressor 1(a) pleads a course of conduct. The Form 29 noticed the email-based Items (3(a), 3(b) and 10) from 1 December 2023 to 30 June 2024; confining disclosure to two days would sample that course, not show it (*Delaney v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 11).

4. *"Item 19 would require searching the emails of ~20,006 staff."* It would not. Item 19 is confined to identified custodians (the Line Manager, the Director, the Logan Hospital health-team mailbox and HR) and is narrowed to 18 May – 18 June 2024 (Schedule A), adopting the custodians MSH itself proposed; MSH's suggested 17–18 March 2024 date predates the 18 May 2024 union request and so could not capture the correspondence.

5. *"Item 9 (building access) lacks relevance and particularity, and the Switchboard office has no swipe access."* The Appellant does not seek a switchboard-office swipe. He seeks the Line Manager's own security access-card records (by her card identity) for the collocated office area and the building — records MSH's access-control system holds — confined to the pleaded-conduct windows (Schedule A). Particularity is satisfied (one identified person; identified dates); relevance is direct (whether the manager was on site when a directive issued or a complaint was left unactioned, going to Stressor 1(a)); and any effect on the Line Manager is managed by the Commission-inspection-first safeguard and the s 580 order sought (Part B(d)–(e)), which governs the manner of access, not refusal.

6. **The letter supplies the answers, under the Chief Executive's hand.** In responding, MSH has stated: (a) *No remedial change* — "there have been no 'consequential' changes to operating procedures over the period requested" (Item 3(c)): despite the misroute complaints, nothing was changed. (b) *No fatigue framework at the time, and a restructure* — fatigue risk management at the Switchboard "occurred after 30 June 2024 in connection with an organisational change related to the reporting lines for Switchboard" (Item 5): no FRMS framework was in place at the date of injury. (c) *Emergency-code logs not retained* — the SPOK records "do not exist… records predating [the system] upgrade were not retained," while "a spreadsheet of recorded MET calls is available for the period 17–18 March 2024" (Items 1–2). The Appellant cannot tell from MSH's description whether that "spreadsheet" is the monthly call-statistics record he himself authors and maintains (Affidavit, ¶9) or a separate MSH or SPOK extract. If it is a separate MSH record, it is a contemporaneous record for the exact pleaded shift pairing and should be produced under Item 1, not treated as resolved; the verification at Part B(b) should identify what the spreadsheet is, who created and holds it, and the system from which it derives. These are not reasons to refuse production; they are matters directly relevant to the s 32(5)(a) inquiry.

7. **The verification affidavit should formalise these statements.** Where MSH states a record does not exist (Items 1–2, 3(c), 4, 5, 7, 14, 20), the order at Part B(b) requires that to be stated on oath by the Chief Executive or a delegated Director, with the search identified — so the statements made informally in the letter are placed on the evidentiary footing the appeal requires. In particular, the verification should address, on oath: (i) Items 1–2 (SPOK): the date and nature of the system upgrade, and whether the pre-upgrade emergency-code paging records were migrated, archived or destroyed, and the basis on which they were "not retained" — an upgrade not ordinarily entailing the disposal of a hospital's emergency-code logs; (ii) Item 3(c): the "no consequential changes" statement, reconciled with the changes the letter itself records (the SPOK upgrade, the reporting-line restructure and the issue of a Queensland Health device) each occurring after the injury; (iii) Items 4, 5 and 7: what fatigue-management framework governed the Switchboard as at 17–18 March 2024 (see ground 6); and (iv) Item 20: MSH states PID 24-ESU-1130 "commenced in November 2024", whereas the underlying complaint was lodged on 13 May 2024 (Affidavit, ¶34; the determination of that complaint as a Public Interest Disclosure on 24 December 2024 being admitted at Form 24, Para 20). The verification must identify whether records of the operational and HR response in the 13–31 May 2024 window exist — particularly because the Respondent itself disclosed, on 11 June 2026, correspondence dated 15–16 May 2024 falling within that very window (Affidavit, ¶52). MSH cannot swear that the Item 20 material "does not exist" consistently with that disclosure: the verification must therefore either produce the records, or contradict its own 5 June 2026 letter or the Respondent's own disclosure — the reference number being a later-assigned identifier, not the date the underlying material came into existence; and (v) Item 18 (communication book; 15 November 2023 business-case minutes): MSH objects on irrelevance, asserting the destruction "appears to have already been conceded" (Form 24, Para 14, and Form 9A Stressor 1(b)). The admission of the page removal (Form 24, Para 6) does not establish, on oath, the fate of the communication book or the business-case minutes; the verification should state whether those records exist or were destroyed, and if destroyed, when and by whom. The destruction of a work-health-and-safety record (Stressor 1(b)) is itself relevant to the s 32(5)(a) inquiry, and the "already conceded" answer — like the "does not exist" answers at Items 1–2 and 20 — should be sworn, not asserted.
