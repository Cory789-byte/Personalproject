# THE 28-DAY GAP — why the "we needed medical information" defence fails on its own dates
> ⛔ Strategy layer. Banked 4 Aug 2026 (Cory's formulation). The single strongest manner
> argument in the matter. Deploy in the Stage 1/2 discussion, in any s 32(5) submission,
> and in the AD Act limb — NEVER with motive language. The dates carry it alone.

## THE SEQUENCE
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 3 Jul 2026, 2:32pm | ECC provided by the deadline. **Certified fit for work with restrictions.** |
| 3 Jul, within the hour | Told he could not attend. Excluded. |
| 3–31 Jul | **Nothing. 28 days.** No request for medical information, no instrument identified, no return date. |
| 13 Jul | Pay ceases. Leave debited. |
| 15 Jul & 30 Jul | Health Service in writing: "unable to commit to a timeframe" / "unable to guarantee when". |
| 31 Jul, 11:43am | Request for Medical Information issued — **7-day deadline**, plus "I will make a decision regarding your ability to perform your role." |

## THE ARGUMENT (deployable wording, no motive)
1. The stated reason for exclusion was the need for medical clearance.
2. If that were the operative reason, the request for medical information would have been
   made on or about 3 July. It was made on 31 July — **four weeks later**.
3. Across those four weeks he was excluded, unpaid from 13 July, and his leave was debited,
   while the information said to be necessary was not sought at all.
4. **The exclusion therefore cannot be explained by the stated reason.** A reason that is
   not acted on for 28 days is not the reason the action was taken for.

## THE LESS-RESTRICTIVE-MEANS LIMB
Medical information can lawfully be sought from an employee **who is at work**, or at
minimum **who remains on pay**. Nothing in G03, the WHS Act or the agreement requires an
employee to be excluded and unpaid while an employer gathers medical information; no
instrument requiring it has ever been identified. The employer had at least three
available options and took the most restrictive:
  (a) request the information while he continued in the role on the certified restrictions;
  (b) request it while he remained on full pay pending clearance;
  (c) request it with a timeframe proportionate to psychiatric appointment availability.
It also attached a decision-threat to the request, which none of (a)–(c) requires.

## THE ASYMMETRY THAT SEALS IT
The employer twice recorded in writing that it could not commit to any timeframe of its
own (15 Jul, 30 Jul), then imposed seven days on him (31 Jul). No clock binds them;
day-counts bind him.

## WHERE IT LANDS
- **s 32(5) manner** — the substance defence ("we were entitled to seek medical
  information") is legitimate in the abstract and collapses on these dates. The action
  is not shown to be reasonable *in the way it was taken*.
- **Employment track** — exclusion and pay cessation without an identified instrument,
  and without the less-restrictive alternatives being taken or explained.
- **AD Act limb (limitation ~8 Sep)** — reasonable adjustment was available and certified
  (work with restrictions); exclusion without pay is the antithesis of adjustment.
- **cl 10.3 / dispute** — supports the forward-pay limb: the power to stop pay was never
  identified, and the justification offered does not fit the chronology.

## DISCIPLINE
State the dates; state the available alternatives; stop. Never say "the real reason was".
The gap does the work, and a decision-maker who notices it themselves is worth more than
one who is told.
