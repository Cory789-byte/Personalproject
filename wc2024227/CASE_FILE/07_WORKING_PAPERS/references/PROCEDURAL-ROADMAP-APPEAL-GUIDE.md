## ⭐ Guide 7.6.1 vindicates the standing discipline

The Commission's own instruction for evidence-in-chief is **chronological order, with enough
detail to prove the elements of s 32 in issue.** That is precisely the standing rule: **state the
chronology, never the motive.** ⛔ Characterisation is not among the elements of s 32 — it is a
*Briginshaw* liability and an s 32(5)(b) door.

# PART 4 — WITNESSES: THE MECHANICS AND THE MONEY

## 4.1 Compelling attendance

- Not-voluntary witnesses are compelled by an **attendance notice**: **Form 32 – Request for
  Attendance Notice**, plus a completed **Form 32A / 32B / 32C**, approved by a Member or the
  Registrar and returned for service (6.2.2).
- ⭐ **Timing:** *"This is done **once the hearing dates are advised in the Further Directions
  Order** and prior to the hearing"* (7.6.4). ⇒ **The Taylor notice is not due now.** It is a
  post-listing step — but the decision to call her must be made at the **witness-list** stage.

## 4.2 ⚠ THE MONEY — r 62

- *"You are responsible for any allowance for attendance and expenses for the witnesses you intend
  to call (**rule 62 of the Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011**). These expenses are to
  be paid to the witness **a reasonable period before the day** the person is required to attend."*
- ⛔ *"**The Commission may treat the failure to pay expenses or allowances as a lawful excuse for
  the witness to not appear.**"*

⇒ ⛔⛔ **If Ms Taylor is compelled and her expenses are not paid in time, she has a lawful excuse
not to appear — and the strand the Commission itself identified as the route to proving Stressor
1(a) collapses.** Combined with 1.2 (the psychiatrist's fees), **witness costs are now a
critical-path item, not an administrative one.**

## 4.3 ⚠ The obligation to call

**Guide 4.9:** *"**If you fail to call someone who could provide relevant evidence to your matter
it may count against your case.**"* And: the witness list *"should be reflected in the facts set
out in your Statement of Facts and Contentions."*

⇒ The Amended 9A names Taylor, Reese, Hawes, Krishnaiah and others. **Every named person is a
candidate, and silence about a person whose evidence matters is itself a risk.**

# PART 6 — SO: DOES BEING HONEST GAIN THE CASE?

**Yes, in four nameable ways — none of which is "the Commissioner liked me."**

1. ⭐⭐ **It is what makes the uncontradicted evidence stand.** The Regulator does not admit 1(a)
   for *"no particulars"* and pleads no positive contrary case (ledger §1). **A witness who
   overclaims hands them something to contradict. He has not.** Restraint is the mechanism, not
   the manners.
2. ⭐ **The unprompted pay-limb concession is a credibility act, and the transcript preserves it.**
   Conceding a limb against his own interest, before being pushed, in front of the employer.
3. ⭐ **The candid AI answer closed the topic permanently.** A defensive answer would have kept it
   alive into the hearing.
4. **Explaining rather than arguing produced the narrowing** — which reduced the cost exposure and
   the length of the fight.

**⚠ And the honest limit.** Guide 7.3: the onus is on the Appellant, on the balance of
probabilities, *"often referred to as the '**Briginshaw** principle'… satisfied… to a comfortable
degree, based on **clear and cogent evidence**."*

⇒ ⛔ **Honesty buys credibility. It does not buy proof.** The case still requires the chronology in
order, the documents tendered, and **the psychiatrist in the box.** Credibility decides contests
between competing accounts; it does not supply an element that is missing.
