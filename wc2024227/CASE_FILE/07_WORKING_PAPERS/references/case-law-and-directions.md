## 1. The legal battleground: global (Delaney) vs single-stressor (Mahaffey/Carr)

This is the decisive fork in the appeal, and the Review Decision already chose
the side adverse to Cory.

- **Review Decision 69983 (24 Oct 2024)** applied **Delaney v Q-COMP** — a
  *global* evaluation of management action: it found factors 2–3 reasonable,
  factor 4 (the 17–18 March rostering) **unreasonable**, then reasoned that
  because 2 of 3 causative factors were reasonable, the injury "mainly arose
  out of" reasonable management action → s 32(5) enlivened → claim rejected.
- **The de novo appeal frees Dwyer from that reasoning.** On a hearing de novo
  (*Davis v Blackwood* [2014] ICQ 009) the Commission determines the question
  afresh; the review officer's global approach is not binding.
- **The counter-authorities run the other way:**
  - **Workers' Compensation Regulator v Mahaffey [2016] ICQ 10** (Industrial
    Court — higher authority than a QIRC single member): a **single**
    unreasonable management action can suffice; the s 32(5) exclusion does not
    automatically apply merely because most stressors were reasonable.
  - **Carr v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2022] QIRC 059**: of nine
    nominated stressors only one (excessive workload without support) was
    unreasonable — the appeal **succeeded**. The Commission assessed each
    stressor **individually**, not globally.

**Stress-test of the direction:** Cory's strongest position is that the
**admitted, already-found-unreasonable 7-hour break** (Form 24 ¶1 admitted;
Review Decision found factor 4 unreasonable) is, on *Mahaffey/Carr*, capable of
sustaining the claim by itself. The Regulator's defence depends on *Delaney*
global dilution. **The contest is global-vs-individual, and Cory has Industrial
Court authority (Mahaffey > a QIRC review officer) on the individual side.**
Weakness to shore up: *Mahaffey* still requires the unreasonable stressor to be
a **significant** contributor to the injury — which is a *medical-causation*
question (Hawes/Krishnaiah), so the medical case must tie the injury to the
fatigue/rostering strand, not only to the cumulative conduct.

## 2. Onus — Davis v Blackwood [2014] ICQ 009

Appellant bears the onus on the de novo appeal, including excluding s 32(5)(a).
**Stress-test:** this is a burden, but the 64G converts it into the *necessity*
argument — a party who must disprove reasonableness cannot be denied the records
of the conduct, held solely by its author. The onus is why disclosure is
necessary, not merely useful. (Already the spine of the application's Section D.)

## 3. Disclosure authorities (reserve — NOT cited in the no-case-law 64G)

- **Robson v REB Engineering [1997] 2 Qd R 102** — "directly relevant" = tends
  to prove or disprove an allegation in issue (the narrower standard, not
  train-of-inquiry). The 64G states this as rule-text only.
- **Bunney v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2020] QIRC 210** — applied
  Subdivision 7A to a Form 29 objection; made a **partial-production order**
  within 14 days, upheld privilege only on genuine sub-categories, each party's
  own costs. The model for the order sought. Held in reserve as the template.

**Stress-test:** if the Regulator/MSH cite *Bunney* to argue for narrowing,
that *helps* Cory — Schedule A already self-narrows below the Notice, so a
*Bunney*-style partial order is the floor, not the ceiling, of what's sought.

## 5. The patient-safety / PID strand — a NEW evidentiary direction

**The hypothesis (Cory, 13 Jun):** the 13 May 2024 PID complaint was
**safety-grounded** — its closing line: *"The employee is now making decisions
that directly affect service delivery and patient outcomes."* It alleged the
Line Manager worked unsupervised, was absent, failed to log on/off, and
neglected duties. If, when that disclosure was routed (McGinley email, 16 May),
MSH conducted **no patient-safety assessment and made no safety notes** — i.e.
treated a *patient-safety* disclosure purely as an HR/conduct/PID matter — that
is a distinct institutional-response failure, and may be why Matheson disclosed
the McGinley page.

**What is fact vs inference:**
- FACT: the PID was safety-grounded (patient-outcomes wording, on the form).
- FACT: the McGinley 16 May email routed it through **Human Resources**
  (McGinley = A/Senior Consultant HR) and to ESU — a conduct/HR channel.
- INFERENCE (untested): that **no** patient-safety assessment was done. The
  McGinley email shows HR routing; it does not by itself prove the absence of a
  safety assessment elsewhere.
- SPECULATION (several steps out): that Matheson disclosed *because* it shows
  the absence. Plausible but unprovable on current material.

**Why this matters to the appeal (the real payoff):** it feeds the s 32(1)
causation spine and Stressor 1 (cumulative institutional response). A
patient-safety disclosure answered only as an HR/conduct matter, with no safety
assessment, is exactly the "every channel closed; raising concerns made Cory the
problem" pattern. It also sharpens **Item 20**: the application already seeks
"operational or human-resources action … in response to the disclosures." If
production shows **HR/conduct routing but no patient-safety assessment**, the
absence is itself probative — and the **order 6 verification affidavit** would
force MSH to state what safety assessment (if any) was undertaken.

**Stress-test of this direction:**
- *For Cory:* if no safety assessment exists, it converts his "inadequate
  institutional response" theory from assertion to documentary fact, and ties
  the reprisal/PID strand to a concrete governance failure with patient-safety
  weight (which a WHS-specialist counsel — Willson — will recognise as serious).
- *Against:* (1) the absence of a record is hard to prove except via the
  verification affidavit; (2) the PID's *content* names "fraud" — the
  collateral-purpose grenade — so this strand lives in the PID/reprisal track
  and evidence-in-chief, **never the 64G prose** (discipline rules 1–2); (3)
  causation: a safety-assessment failure must still be tied to *Cory's* injury,
  not only to abstract patient risk.

**Could it be why Renee gave the email?** Honest answer: it's one plausible
strand among the §3 motive set, not a distinct better explanation. The simpler
read remains — she disclosed to discharge her continuing duty / protect her
position before the 64G. But your strand adds a layer: the McGinley page is the
document that shows the *routing* of a safety disclosure into an HR/conduct
channel — and that is adverse to MSH on exactly the point your case needs.
Whether she saw that significance or simply disclosed what conferencing gave her
cannot be resolved from the metadata. **Treat as a lead for the Item 20
production + verification affidavit, not as a finding.**

## 7. The "no safety assessment" affidavit scenario — strength + the trap (13 Jun)

**Premise (Cory):** order 6 affidavit confirms no patient-safety assessment / no safety notes were made in response to the 13 May PID; they cannot manufacture a contemporaneous record that does not exist.

**Sound premise — but expect characterisation, not fabrication.** An officer will not swear false; a contemporaneous record cannot be back-dated without detection. BUT the affidavit's WORDING will be chosen as carefully as the 11 June disclosure was. Expect a technically-true reframing, e.g. "the matter was assessed by ESU as a [conduct/PID] complaint" — which converts "no safety assessment" into "assessed through a different channel." The absence is the fact; its CHARACTER is the fight.

**Layered case strength:**
- **Layer 1 (already strong, independent of this):** the admitted 7-hour break, already found unreasonable by the Review Officer, sufficient alone under *Mahaffey*/*Carr*. This is the spine and it does NOT need the safety-assessment finding.
- **Layer 2 (what the finding adds):** corroborates Stressor 1 (cumulative institutional response) — a safety-grounded disclosure handled as conduct-only, no safety assessment = "every channel closed" proven on paper. Makes the *global-reasonableness* defence harder and the *manner/severity* case stronger.
- **Layer 3:** every adverse sworn fact raises the cost of a heard decision (portable findings) vs settlement → leverage.

**What they CAN still do (even unable to manufacture):**
1. Characterise the absence as reasonable ("no formal safety assessment was required; handled appropriately via ESU").
2. Sever relevance — argue the PID-response is the reprisal track, not the s 32(5)(a) reasonableness of the *rostering* action.
3. Contest causation — poor institutional response ≠ significant contributor to the injury vs the admitted stressors.
4. **THE TRAP:** the safety strand is welded to a PID that names "fraud" against Chloe Taylor. Leaning on the PID invites the **collateral-purpose / vexatious framing** — recasting Cory's disclosures as a personal campaign, not protected disclosures. This is the single biggest risk in pulling this thread, and it is why discipline rules 1–2 quarantine "fraud" from the WC track.

**Net:** the case is ALREADY strong on Layer 1 alone (admitted + found-unreasonable single stressor + ICQ authority). The no-safety-assessment finding is a **force multiplier on severity + settlement value, not a silver bullet** — and it must be deployed via the clean institutional-response/governance angle (FRMS/patient-safety), NEVER via the fraud allegation. Strength with the finding: materially raises an already-strong position and pushes settlement value up; does not change that medical causation (injury ↔ the stressors) remains the one element Cory must still prove independently.

## 8. THE STRONGEST POINT — the FRMS vacuum is already HALF-CONCEDED in writing (13 Jun)

**MSH's 5 June letter already admits, in writing, that no fatigue-management framework governed the Switchboard before the injury:**
- Items 4 & 7: "Mandatory Fatigue Risk Management System training only applies to health practitioners and clinical assistants. The Logan Hospital Switchboard staff are **non-clinical staff**, and therefore there is no mandatory requirement … to complete [FRMS] training."
- Item 5: "The implementation of fatigue risk management assessment at Switchboard Logan Hospital occurred **after 30 June 2024**."

**Onset was 18 June 2024.** So MSH has conceded on its own letterhead that there was **no FRMS training, assessment, or framework applying to the Switchboard at the time of the admitted 7-hour break** — the assessment was implemented only *after* the injury. This does not need the verification affidavit; it is already documentary. **Order 6(b) is purpose-built to convert it to sworn evidence** ("whether the searches identified any fatigue-management training requirement, assessment process or other requirement applying to Logan Hospital Switchboard staff before 30 June 2024").

**BUT — MSH's escape hatch is the same sentence: "non-clinical."** Their concession is framed as a *defence*: FRMS did not apply because Switchboard is non-clinical, so there was no breach of any framework. **The real contest is not whether a safety assessment was done (it wasn't) — it is whether life-safety emergency-code coordination (Code Blue / MET) should have been INSIDE the fatigue framework.** That is the battleground, and it is where Willson (WHS specialist) will fight. Cory's answer: the *nature of the work* (coordinating clinical emergencies) and the *Neville* governance rationale make the "non-clinical" classification itself the governance failure — you cannot exempt the people who coordinate Code Blues from fatigue management on a clerical label.

**"Does the Form 29 look like the assessment they never did?" — YES, and it is closing-submission gold (contingent on production).** The continuation schedule IS a structured fatigue-risk analysis: the 16h-wakefulness / 4h-commute / 4h-sleep arithmetic, the FRMS control framework, the Neville governance anchor, risk categorisation. The framing for closing: *"The only systematic fatigue-risk assessment ever performed in relation to these shifts is the one the injured worker performed himself, after the injury, for this appeal."* Powerful — but it is rhetoric that lands only once production + the affidavit confirm the absence, and only via the clean FRMS/governance angle, never the fraud allegation.

**Calibration of "the hearing writes itself":** the LIABILITY/unreasonableness limb is close to that strong — admitted stressor + admitted FRMS vacuum + Mahaffey single-stressor authority. But two things never "write themselves": (1) the **non-clinical characterisation** fight, and (2) **medical causation** (injury ↔ stressor), which is Cory's to prove via expert evidence regardless of how damning the governance picture is. Strong, even very strong — but won at hearing through the characterisation argument and the medical case, not by the absence alone.
