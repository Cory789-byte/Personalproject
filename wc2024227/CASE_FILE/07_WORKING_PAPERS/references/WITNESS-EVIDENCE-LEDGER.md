## 0. THE FOUR TIERS

| Tier | What it is | What it is worth |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | First-hand fact within his own knowledge, **contemporaneously documented** | Highest. Only dislodged by contradiction, and the document corroborates |
| **2** | First-hand fact within his own knowledge, **not documented** | Good. **Stands if uncontradicted** |
| **3** | Observation carrying an inferential step | Weak. The inference is argument, not evidence |
| **4** | Opinion, characterisation, motive | ⛔ **Worth nothing and actively harmful.** Raises his own *Briginshaw* bar and opens s 32(5)(b) |

## 2. ITEM A — "IT LACKED CONSULTATION AND ADVERSELY AFFECTED PATIENTS"

The answer given to *"that's just management action."* **It is two propositions and they price
completely differently.**

### A1 — "It lacked consultation" ⭐⭐⭐ HIGH — his best evidence

- **Tier 1.** He was a Switchboard team member. He can swear: **I was not consulted. No one asked
  me. The directive arrived by email to the group mailbox, expressed to take effect "effective
  immediately."**
- ⭐ **Absence of consultation is a negative he is uniquely placed to prove.** No one can prove
  consultation did not happen *with him* better than he can.
- ⭐ **Documented twice, both pre-injury:**
  - **16 June 2023** — *"No consultation of the team members or the department when making major
    changes such as additional shift… commence/finish [times]."*
  - **15 May 2024** — raised again.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **And then the employer's own file corroborates it: on 21 May 2024 the Director, Ms Reese,
  asked him to identify the directives about which he had consultation concerns.**
  **That is the employer acknowledging an unresolved consultation complaint, from their side of
  the record.** He does not need Ms Taylor to agree with anything.
- **Legally this is the *"taken in a reasonable way"* limb** — s 32(5)(a) protects reasonable
  management action *taken in a reasonable way*. Absence of consultation goes to the manner, not
  the decision. **That is why the answer given in the room was the right one.**

### A2 — "adversely affected patients" ⚠ MEDIUM-HIGH if confined, ⛔ NEGATIVE if overstated

- **What he CAN give (Tier 1, direct observation as the operator):** complaints came to him;
  doctors were paged to the wrong ward or the wrong team; the roster/directory was out of date;
  urgent clinical and pathology relays were delayed; **he identified and worked around it in real
  time.** He was the person through whom it ran.
- ⛔ **What he CANNOT give:** that a patient was harmed, that outcomes were affected, or any
  clinical conclusion. **That is expert evidence and he is not an expert.** Offered from the box it
  will be objected to, and it will cost him more than it gains.
- ⭐ **He does not need it.** **Form 24 ¶8 — the patient-safety criticality of the function — is a
  Type A admission, removed from contest.** He does not have to prove the function mattered. **He
  only has to prove it failed.** That is a far smaller job and it is entirely within his knowledge.
- ⭐ **The safe formulation, which is also the true one:** *the directives bottlenecked the
  emergency communication pathway and produced misroutes and delay, which I dealt with as the
  operator.* Fact, not opinion. Sufficient.

### ⚠ WHAT DWYER *STOPPING* ACTUALLY PRICES

Two readings, and the disciplined one must govern:

- **Optimistic:** the answer was correct and complete, so there was nothing to test.
- ⚠ **Disciplined:** he was hearing a **disclosure** application. He put the Respondent's case in
  six words to see whether the appellant understood the difference between *management action* and
  *management action taken in a reasonable way*. **The topic closed because the test was over —
  not because the point was won.**

⇒ **It prices the witness, not the proposition.** It establishes that the right answer is
available under pressure without preparation. ⛔ **It does not establish that a Commissioner has
accepted it**, and treating it as won would under-prepare the limb that decides the appeal.

⭐ **The bankable value is different and larger: he now knows the exact question the case turns on,
in the bench's own words, months before a hearing.** Most litigants learn that in closing
submissions. **That is the most valuable thing taken out of the room.**

## 3. ITEM B — "SHE WAS NOT AT THE BOARD AND DECIDING ON IT"

### B1 — "She was not there" ⭐⭐ MEDIUM alone · ⭐⭐⭐ HIGH via cross-examination

- ⭐ **This bound is exactly why Items 8 and 9 were pleaded, and exactly why Dwyer's route is not a
  consolation prize.** He cannot prove the negative across the period — **but she has to answer the

⭐⭐ **THE REFRAME THAT MATTERS — and it is not spin, it follows from the pleading:**
The access records would have proved **where she was**. Cross-examination can prove **what she
knew** — and what she knew is the actual issue. **A manager physically present but not consulting
is no less unreasonable than an absent one.** Presence was always a proxy. **¶26 pleads *unilateral
directives without consultation*, not attendance. Dwyer's route goes to the real point; the
documents never did.**

### B2 — "and deciding on it" ⭐⭐⭐ VERY HIGH — the strongest evidence in the strand, already held

- **Tier 1, documentary.** The directives exist, sent from her account to the Switchboard group
  distribution list, expressed *"effective immediately."*
- ⭐⭐⭐ **CS-1 — her own email of 9 May 2024** records that a doctor's hours were **"not provided on

- ⭐ **The mismatch between the cause she identified and the remedy she imposed is on the face of
  her own document.** **This is not his word against hers. It is her document against her

- ⭐ It also disposes of the misroute issue being *"an error of the operators"*: her own email

### B3 — "without checking the roster, without asking us" ⭐⭐⭐ HIGH

- **Tier 2:** *I was not asked. The Switchboard was not asked.*
- **Tier 3 but permissible:** *had we been asked, we could have identified the cause.* He is not

- Corroborated by Reese's 21 May 2024 request (A1).

## 9. ⛔⛔ CORRECTION — A WC HEARING IS *MORE* ORAL THAN THE MENTION, NOT LESS

**The assumption tested (8 Aug):** *"at the hearing Dwyer would already have seen the attached
document, and so would the Respondent and MSH"* — so the parts he moved on from would already be
in front of everyone in writing.

⛔ **That is not how a workers' compensation appeal runs.** From the Commission's own
**Workers' Compensation Appeal Guide, Part 7.6**:

> **"In workers' compensation matters, all witness evidence is provided ORALLY. There are
> generally NO AFFIDAVITS OR WITNESS STATEMENTS. Therefore, the Directions Order only requires
> that you provide a one-page outline of evidence for each of the witnesses to each of the
> parties."**

And **Part 4.10**: the Further Directions Order requires an **"outline of evidence" — one A4 page
per lay witness**, a *brief overview* only, served so there is no *"hearing by ambush."*

### 9.1 WHAT THAT ACTUALLY MEANS

| | 7 August mention | The hearing |
|---|---|---|
| Basis of decision | **Affidavit** — the Form 20 affirmed 18 June 2026, exhibits CS-1 to CS-4 with Form 21 certificates | **Oral evidence from the witness box** |
| What the Commission has read in advance | The Form 20, the Form 29, K-LM26/729 | **A one-page outline per witness.** Nothing more |
| How CS-1 gets before the Commission | Annexed to the affidavit, already read | **Tendered through a witness during oral evidence** |
| Who does the talking | Largely the Commissioner | **Him — in examination-in-chief** |

⭐⭐⭐ **The exact inverse of the assumption.** On 7 August the papers were in front of Dwyer
**because it was an interlocutory application decided on affidavit**. At the hearing, **nobody
reads the affidavit — he says it.**

⛔ **The Form 20 affidavit is evidence on the r 64G application. It is not his evidence at the
hearing.** It does not carry across automatically, and the 7 April 2026 Further Directions Order
makes no direction for hearing evidence.

### 9.2 ⇒ THE CONSEQUENCES, IN ORDER OF IMPORTANCE

**1. ⭐⭐ The narration Dwyer cut short IS the hearing.** What was out of place on a disclosure
application is the main event at a hearing, done properly in chief, with documents tendered as he
goes. **He was not being told the material was unwelcome. He was being told it was not that day.**

**2. ⭐⭐ 7 August was therefore a rehearsal of the exact skill the hearing requires** — oral
delivery, under pressure, in front of the actual decision-maker — and the feedback was that the
right answer is available without preparation. **That is worth far more under this procedure than
it would be in an affidavit jurisdiction.**

**3. ⭐⭐⭐ Dwyer's cross-examination mechanic was not a workaround. It is simply how evidence is
obtained here.** In a forum where all evidence is oral and there are no witness statements,
*"put it to her in cross-examination and the Respondent then has to displace it"* is **ordinary
procedure** — which is why it was said so matter-of-factly.

**4. ⛔⛔ CRITICAL PATH — TAYLOR MUST BE CALLED, OR THE STRAND DIES.**
MSH is **not a party**; Ms Taylor is an MSH employee. Either the Regulator calls her, or he must
list her and secure her attendance. **If neither happens, the strand the Commission said could be

## 10. ⭐⭐ THE AFFIDAVIT DISPROVED ITS OWN NECESSITY — and it was still the right trade

**Cory's observation (8 Aug):** the sworn account of the job arrived early, in a disclosure
application, in front of everyone — *"I guess it also disproved the need."*

**Mechanically, yes. Precisely that.**

### 10.1 THE STRUCTURAL TENSION — it was never avoidable

**r 64B(2)** asks whether there is *"another reasonably simple and inexpensive way"* to obtain the
evidence. **The affidavit is the answer to that question.** By swearing to the job, the conditions,
the directives, the misroutes and the working-around in real time, he demonstrated on oath that a
competent witness exists who can give it directly.

⭐ **The better the affidavit, the weaker the necessity case.** That is inherent to any r 64G
application brought by the person who is himself the primary source — not a drafting error.

**And there was no way around it.** A r 64G application needs evidence; production is not ordered
on assertion. The affidavit had to establish relevance to the pleaded stressors; establishing
relevance requires describing the facts; describing the facts demonstrates he knows them.
⇒ **No version of this application existed that did not partly disprove its own necessity.** The
alternative — a thin affidavit — fails on relevance instead, which is worse.

### 10.2 ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT IT BOUGHT — and this is why the trade was good

**Under Part 7.6 there are no witness statements at a WC hearing.** All evidence is oral.

⇒ **He has obtained, through the side door of an interlocutory application, the one thing the
procedure does not otherwise provide: a sworn, dated, served, unchallenged written account of his
evidence, sitting on the Commission's file.** Most WC appellants never have that.

Four properties, each independently valuable:

1. ⭐⭐ **It is dated 18 June 2026 — and it predates everything contested since**: the 26 June
   exclusion, the 3 July ECC, the RFMI, the 13 July non-payment. **It cannot be characterised as
   constructed in response to any of it.** A witness account written in 2027 for a hearing is
   written by a person with a grievance and a motive. **This one is not, and the date proves it.**
2. **The decision-maker has read it.** The announced departure on 7 August establishes he came
   prepared, having read the Form 20, the Form 29 and K-LM26/729.
3. **It has been served on the Regulator and MSH since June — and not a word of it has been
   contradicted.**
4. ⭐ **It doubles the exhaustion of the "no particulars" non-admission** (§1). They have had the
   particulars in the pleading *and* the sworn account in the affidavit, since June, and have
   pleaded no positive case against either.

**1. Costs on the 64G — low but not nil.** **r 64G(3): each party bears its own costs unless

⭐ **A sworn misstatement about what the other side admitted is exactly what a skilled

⛔ **This escalates the ¶37 fix.** A filed and served affidavit cannot be unfiled. **The only

### 11.4 ⭐⭐⭐ THEIR EXPLANATION FOR THE ABSENCE *IS* THE ABSENCE

### 11.5 HOW IT PLAYS AT THE HEARING — and why the discipline pays off

Guide 7.6.1: he gives evidence first, **tendering documents as he goes.**

1. **His evidence:** *I raised it in writing on 8 April. It was escalated to Human Resources on

2. **He tenders MSH's letter.**

happened; their document says what existed. The inference belongs to the tribunal** — and an
inference the tribunal draws itself cannot be answered.
