# STAGE 1 — THE SCRIPT AND MEETING DISCIPLINE
> ⛔ Strategy layer. Prep for the cl 1.11.2(a) Stage 1 discussion, whoever conducts it
> (Taylor, or an officer HR nominates under Part 5). Support person + union present.
> Banked 3 Aug 2026. Companion: HAZARD-NOMINATION-decision-making.md.

## 0. WHAT STAGE 1 IS, REALISTICALLY
Local-level resolution attempt. Nothing binding gets decided in the room. The realistic
outputs are: (a) things said by them that become record, (b) things asked by you that
become record, (c) possibly one or two easy concessions (E12/E13, the payroll record,
a process name). Win condition: every ask restated calmly, nothing conceded, everything
minuted. The dispute resolves on paper afterwards or proceeds to Stage 2 on 10 Aug.

## 1. OPENING STATEMENT (60 seconds, verbatim-ready)
"Thank you for meeting me. I want this resolved at Stage 1 if it can be. My notice sets
out what the dispute is, so I won't repeat it — I'd like to work through the eight
things I've asked for at Part 4, and hear the Health Service's position on each. My
position on all of them is the same: I am certified fit, I have offered my services in
writing since 3 July, I want to work, and I want the ordinary processes to apply. I'm
not here to make any complaint about any person."

## 2. THE EIGHT ASKS, IN MEETING ORDER (§4(a)–(h))
Work the list; for each, ask, listen, note WHO answered and WHAT was said:
  (a) What is this process called — instrument, steps, decision-maker, end-decision?
  (b) Am I suspended? Under what? With pay?
  (c) Return me to my roster under cl 1.11.4 — or name the instrument in writing.
  (d) The pay: period from 3 July paid, leave re-credited, permanent-line shifts paid
      as they fall until return or a power is named. Which QSuper guidelines?
  (e) E12 and E13 copies.
  (f) The 12-month roster/payroll record.
  (g) Feedback under cl 7.1.11.
  (h) The psychosocial risk assessment under cl 7.2, with me consulted.

## 3. THE HAZARD FRAMING — WHEN (h) COMES UP
Only if the assessment is discussed. Then, verbatim:

"On the assessment — the Health Service's own letter of 7 July identified unresolved
workplace matters involving line management and said it could not yet assess whether
they present psychosocial hazards. Four weeks on, I'd like that assessment done, and I
ask to be consulted in it as clause 7.2.2 provides. When it is scoped, I will be asking
that two hazard categories from the Code of Practice be included: **poor organisational
justice — that is, the decision-making processes that have been applied to my
attendance, leave, pay and return to work** — and **job insecurity**, because a decision
about my ability to perform my role has twice been foreshadowed without an instrument,
a decision-maker, or a date. I can provide the dated instances in writing if that
helps the assessor."

### How this "applies to the entire management of Corporate Services and the line manager"
IT ALREADY DOES — through the process, never the org chart. "The decision-making
processes applied to my matters" captures every decision by anyone in Corporate
Services and line management, because they made all of them. Their own 7 July letter
supplies the only unit-level phrase ever needed: "workplace matters involving line
management" — QUOTE THEIRS, never coin your own. The assessment's scope then reaches
the whole chain by definition, without one name or unit being accused by you.

## 4. THE FOUR QUESTIONS THEY MAY PUT — SCRIPTED ANSWERS
- **"Are you accusing Chloe / Scott / management?"**
  "No. Clause 1.2 of the Code of Conduct says a conflict of interest is not wrongdoing
  in itself, and my notice says I make no allegation against anyone. I'm asking about
  process and authority, not about people."
- **"Shouldn't we wait for your medical information first?"**
  "That's a separate lane and I'm engaging in it — my response is in and the next step
  there is the Health Service confirming which questions it presses. This dispute is
  about attendance, roster, leave and pay under the agreement, and clause 1.11.4 says
  the status quo continues while it's followed."
- **"Can we handle this informally / will you withdraw the notice?"**
  "I'd welcome an informal resolution — resolving it IS the point of Stage 1. The
  notice stays open until what we agree is confirmed in writing; that protects both
  of us."
- **"What's this letter to the Chief Executive about?"**
  "That's correspondence with her office under section 89 and I'd rather let her
  office deal with it. It doesn't ask for anything from this meeting."

## 5. MEETING DISCIPLINE
- Take your own notes: time, attendees, who said what on each of the eight asks.
- Ask at the end: "Can I have the outcomes of today confirmed in writing?" — every
  time, whatever the outcomes are.
- AGREE to anything good immediately (return date, pay fix, assessment scoped) —
  subject to written confirmation. Concede nothing else: no narrowing of the dispute,
  no withdrawal, no new medical undertakings beyond the response already sent, nothing
  signed in the room.
- If it gets warm: "I don't think we'll resolve that limb today — can we note our
  positions and move to the next item?" Never argue a point twice.
- Close: "Thank you. To keep to the clause, if the dispute is still unresolved by
  Monday I'll refer it to Stage 2 as clause 1.11.2(b) provides — I'd much rather we
  resolve it before then."

## 6. AFTER
Same day: write your own file note (who, when, each ask → each answer), save it, send
NO summary email unless theirs is wrong — if their written outcome misstates anything,
correct it once, factually, within 24 hours. Diarise: Mon 10 Aug Stage 2 referral if
unresolved (one line, does not self-execute).
