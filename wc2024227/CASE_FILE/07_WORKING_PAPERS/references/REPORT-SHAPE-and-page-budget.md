## 3. ⭐ OPTION B — THE TWO QUESTIONS TO CUT, AND WHY

### Cut **1.4** — premorbid personality features
It invites a psychiatrist to characterise personality in a document that goes to the Regulator, and
**nothing in the appeal turns on it.** It was included to pre-empt an adverse IME characterisation
— but the Regulator has **no medical evidence at all** and 21 days from 9 September in which to get
some. ⇒ **The risk it guarded against has largely evaporated. Cut it.**

### Cut most of **2.4** — the three ECC stressors
2.4 draws on the **employee capability checklist of 3 July 2026**. That is 2026 capacity material,
and the question asks about **exacerbation**, not onset. ⚠ It is the last remaining foothold for
2026 material in a report that the narrowing was designed to keep 2026 out of.

⭐ **But keep sub-question (ii)** — *being held accountable and blamed for the failures of others* —
and **fold it into 2.3**, where it belongs: the acknowledged rostering error, the leave declined for
an attachment a review found was "in fact… present", and the 25-day payroll correction.

⇒ **Ten questions. Three pages.**

## 4. HOW IT SHOULD BE WRITTEN

Part D of the 17 August letter already sets nearly all of this. It does not need repeating to him —
it needs **not being undone**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Numbering** | Answers under **the same numbers** as the letter — 1.1, 1.2, 1.3(a)… Where a question is not answered, say so under its number and why |
| **Order** | ⭐ **3.1 → 3.2 → 3.3.** The letter sequences the conclusion **last** on purpose, so it rests on the two answers before it. A report that opens with the conclusion is worth much less |
| **Prose, not bullets** | Continuous clinical prose. Bullet points read as assertion; sentences read as reasoning |
| **Reason, don't assert** | Part D(c) — reason from the assumed facts to the conclusion, and say where material is **insufficient** rather than qualify an opinion he would not otherwise give |
| **Provenance** | Part D(d) — state **which sources** the causation opinion rests on and **whether it depends on Cory's account.** ⭐ This single sentence is what answers the employer's question 1(c) and defeats *"he only had the patient's version"* |
| **Completeness** | Part D(e) — state whether anything in the clinical records is inconsistent with or qualifies the opinions, and how it was taken into account |
| **Independence** | Part D(f) — the opinions are his own. And ⛔ he adopts **no** conclusion or finding of any other decision-maker |
| ⛔ **Never** | Any view on whether management action was reasonable. Part D already excludes it. **That is the exclusion the whole appeal turns on and it is not a medical question** |

## 5. ⛔⛔ THE TITLE MUST CHANGE — THIS IS NOW WRONG

Part D(g) of the 17 August letter asks him to style the report:

> *"Psychiatric report — in answer to the request for medical information made by Metro South
> Hospital and Health Service, 31 July 2026"*

⛔ **With 3.4–3.8 removed, that title is false and actively damaging.** A causation report for the
Commission, titled as answering the employer's capacity request, invites the obvious question: why
is an employer-requested capacity report opining on causation?

✅ **New styling — must be said in the covering email:**
> **"Psychiatric report concerning Mr Cory Lea Shepherd"**
> — dated, addressed to no one, identifying him only as the subject.

⭐ The rest of Part D(g) stands: **a standalone report, not a letter to Cory**, so that it reads the
same in every hand it reaches.
