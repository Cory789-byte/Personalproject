# What could actually be asked for — special leave, LSL, ad hoc, and the dates
Researched 5 September 2026 from the instruments, not from memory. INTERNAL.

## 1. SPECIAL LEAVE WITH PAY — no prohibition. It is a discretion, and the decision-maker is the CE.

**EB12 cl 9.12.1** (verified): *"The parties agree the Minister for Employment and Industrial
Relations Directive 12/24: Special Leave applies to all employees covered by this Agreement."*

**Directive 12/24 cl 6.1**: *"A chief executive may approve paid leave for employees for any
purpose, with duration appropriate to the purpose of the leave."*

**cl 6.1(a)**: *"Leave approved under clause 6.1 must not exceed more than five (5) working days
per year per reason unless the chief executive considers that circumstances warrant the granting
of additional paid leave. Any additional leave must be reasonable and proportionate to the
circumstances."*

**cl 6.5** — mandatory considerations: *"In determining an application for leave under clause 6.1
or clause 6.2, a chief executive must consider: (a) the reason the leave is requested; (b) the
duration of the requested leave; ... (d) the impact on the employee if the requested leave is not
approved."*

⇒ Weeks of paid special leave are NOT barred. The default cap is 5 working days per reason per
year, and beyond that the CE must personally form the view that circumstances warrant it.
cl 6.5(d) makes the consequences of refusal a MANDATORY consideration — months unpaid with an LSL
date weeks away is squarely that.
⛔ HR Policy C7 (QH-POL-231) is NOT the authority. It is keyed to superseded Directive 05/17.

## 2. LONG SERVICE LEAVE — the one hard bar, and the governing policy is MISSING.

**IR Act s 95(2)(a)**: entitlement to 8.6667 weeks arises on completing **10 years continuous
service**. There is no provision for taking leave not yet accrued.
**s 97(2)**: employer and employee may agree *when* leave is taken — that is timing of accrued
leave, not early access.
**s 95(3)–(4)**: after 7 years, a proportionate payment arises **only on termination**, and before
10 years only where (b)(i) the employee terminates because of illness, (b)(ii) domestic or other
pressing necessity, or (c) the employer dismisses for illness / a reason other than conduct,
capacity or performance / unfairly.

⇒ Nothing can be taken before the accrual date (Payroll: 29 September 2026) under the Act.
⇒ s 95(4) IS a live route — 7 years passed 25 March 2026 — but it requires the employment to END.
   Do not treat as a plan; record it as an option that exists.

⚠⚠ **EB12 cl 9.10.1: "Long service leave entitlements and conditions are outlined in HR Policy C38
Long Service Leave." C38 IS NOT IN THIS REPO.** Only referenced, never obtained. Any discretionary
early-access, advance, or hardship provision would live there. **This is the gap to close.**

**Continuity is safe on three independent bases** — s 134(3) (absence on approved paid or unpaid
leave does not break continuity), s 134(4) (termination for illness/injury then re-employment),
s 134(5) (re-employment within 3 months). And on the deed's own terms the separation date and the
reinstatement effective date are both 20 September 2024, so there is no gap at all.
⚠ Not broken ≠ counts toward the qualifying period. Whether the unpaid periods COUNT is a C38
question, and it is what moves the 29 September 2026 date.

## 3. AD HOC PAYMENT — wrong instrument. It cannot do this, by its own definition.

**C13 §8**: the process *"enables employees to request a payment that was missed or requires
correction outside of the regular pay day schedule without waiting for the next pay cycle."*
**C13 definition (p 7)**: available where the employee is *"missing pay in the most recent prior pay
period, and seek the payment prior to the next pay day. **Ad hoc payment process is not to be used
for payment of missed pay for extended periods.**"*

⇒ Ad hoc is a TIMING mechanism for money already owed, not a source of money. It worked on
4 September (PRN 24973005) because that was pay already owed. It cannot fund future weeks and it is
expressly excluded for extended periods.

## 4. MOVING THE APPEAL DATES — available, but it does nothing for LSL.

Further Directions Order (3), 19 Aug 2026, can be varied on application; direction 5 contemplates
contacting the Registry. But the QIRC timetable and LSL accrual are unconnected — LSL accrues by
service, not by litigation. Moving dates buys time for evidence, not money.

## 5. THE DEED DOES NOT RESTRICT ANY OF IT

**cl 5**: *"If the Applicant is not fit to return to the workplace on executing this deed, he will
apply for leave using the usual application process."* The deed expressly CONTEMPLATES leave
applications. **cl 14**: ongoing obligations as applied to the Employment continue. Nothing in
cll 1–18 limits an application for leave of any kind.
cll 6 and 7 are open but concern the absenteeism matters and his reserved rights about future
management action — not leave and not the appeal timetable.

## THE ANSWER IN ONE LINE
Nothing stopped him asking. Special leave with pay was always available to ask for and is decided
by the Chief Executive against a mandatory consideration of what refusal does to him; ad hoc was
never capable of it; LSL is barred by statute until the accrual date unless C38 says otherwise;
and moving the QIRC dates was never going to move the money.
