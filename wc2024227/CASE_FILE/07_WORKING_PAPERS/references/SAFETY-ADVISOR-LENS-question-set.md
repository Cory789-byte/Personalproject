## 1. THE METHOD (six habits of high-exposure safety practice)
1. **Exposure profile first** — what the room absorbs (call types, codes, death notifications,
   aggression, single-operator periods) from LOGS, not position descriptions. First question of
   any assessment: "where is the exposure register?" No register = finding #1 before any interview.
2. **Leading indicators from records** — leave clustering, sick trends, turnover, overtime,
   complaint response times. Records can't be socially pressured into silence.
3. **Full-cycle observation** — the 3am state is the true state. Never assess on day shift only.
4. **Welfare conversations built for candour** — private, voluntary, non-attributable; the real
   answer comes at the third question or not at all.
5. **The normalisation check** — silence is itself a hazard indicator; drift ("part of the job",
   reporting stopped because nothing happened) is what kills people in high-exposure rooms.
6. **Report above the assessed chain, with review dates** — findings that land on the assessed
   manager's desk die there; a control nobody re-checks no longer exists.
Doctrine: individual-level responses (resilience training, EAP, "self-care") = admission of
failure under the hierarchy (Reg 36). Redesign the work first.

## 2. WHAT THE LENS SEES IN THE SWITCHBOARD (instant pattern-match)
- Distressing-call exposure, no register, no debrief practice
- After-hours single-operator isolation, no named welfare owner at 3am
- Fatigue managed through workers' own personal leave (matrix row 10 — the classic
  leading-indicator signature)
- Complaints unanswered 6 days = dead reporting channel = normalisation drift in progress
- Changes made without consultation records
Any two together trigger an assessment in high-exposure practice. This room has all five.

## 3. THE QUESTION SET
### Tier 1 — of the system (scoping consultation / Stage 2 asks)
1. Where is the register of distressing-call and aggression exposure, and who reviews it?
2. What happens in the 15 minutes after a distressing call — who checks the operator, recorded where?
3. Who holds welfare responsibility for the lone operator at 3am? Name and position.
4. Twelve-month roster beside the leave records: who manages fatigue — the system or the
   workers' own leave balances?
5. Documented response time to the last five concerns raised by operators in this room?
6. Consultation records for the last three changes made to this room?
7. When did this room's own data last reach a level of the organisation with power to act?

### Tier 2 — answerable from records alone (64G / RTI / dispute §4(f)-shaped)
Leave + sick-leave trends vs hospital average; turnover/vacancy history; overtime and
single-up frequency; complaint logs with response times. A room can stay silent; its
records cannot.

### Tier 3 — the independent assessor's interview themes (proposed at methodology consultation)
- What does a bad shift look like, and what happens afterwards?
- Who would you tell, and what happened the last time you told them?
- ⭐ "What have you stopped reporting because nothing happened last time?" — the single most
  diagnostic question in a normalised environment; the one an in-house process never asks.
