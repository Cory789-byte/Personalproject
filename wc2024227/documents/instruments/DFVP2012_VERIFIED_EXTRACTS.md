## s 22 — Child as aggrieved or respondent (p 32)

> **22(1)** "A person who is a child can be named as the aggrieved or the respondent in an
> application for a domestic violence order, or in a domestic violence order or police
> protection notice."
>
> **22(2)** "However, a child can only be named as the aggrieved or the respondent **if an
> intimate personal relationship or an informal care relationship exists** between the child
> and the other party named in the application, order or notice."
>
> **22(4)** "To remove any doubt, it is declared that a child can not be named as the
> aggrieved or respondent in a police protection direction." *(Note: see s 100C(1)(a).)*

⭐ **Consequence for the 14 Jan 2024 sister matter:** a sibling is a *family* relationship,
not intimate-personal or informal-care. **Neither a DVO nor a PPN could lawfully have been
made against the then-15-year-old.** (PPDs did not exist in Jan 2024; they are barred for
children anyway, s 22(4)/s 100C(1)(a).) Whatever police issued was **necessarily outside the
DFVP Act** — consistent with Cory's account of a good-behaviour direction/caution, most
plausibly under the Youth Justice Act 1992 or informal. The Matheson email's line "no
protection order was made" is not just factually true — it was **legally impossible** for one
to have been made against her.

## s 159 — Prohibition on publication of certain information for proceeding (p 212)

> **159(1)** "A person must not publish—
> (a) information, including a photograph, picture, videotape and any other visual
> representation, **given in evidence in a proceeding under this Act** in a court; or
> (b) information that **identifies, or is likely to lead to the identification of**—
> (i) a person as a **party** to a proceeding under this Act; or
> (ii) a person as a **witness** in a proceeding under this Act (other than a police
> officer); or
> **(iii) a child.**
> Maximum penalty—(a) for an individual—**100 penalty units or 2 years imprisonment**; or
> (b) for a corporation—1,000 penalty units."
>
> **159(2)** exceptions: (a) court expressly authorises; (b) **each person to whom the
> information relates consents**; (c) court notice-board; (d) law reports/judgment sites
> (non-identifying); (e) approved research (non-identifying); (f) accredited media
> (non-identifying); (g) expressly permitted/required under an Act; (h) permitted under
> regulation.
>
> **159(3)** "**publish** means publish **to the public** by television, radio, the internet,
> newspaper, periodical, notice, circular or other form of communication."

⚠️ **Construction notes — use honestly:**
- The **child limb (1)(b)(iii) is textually the widest** — unlike limbs (i)–(ii) it carries
  no express "party/witness to a proceeding" qualifier. The heading ties the section to
  proceedings; the child limb's breadth is arguable both ways. Cite it as the legislature's
  sensitivity setting, not as a present breach.
- **"Publish" means to the public** — inter-party litigation disclosure is NOT publication.
  s 159 is therefore engaged by any public deployment (open hearing, tender without
  restriction), not by the disclosure that has already occurred.
- Penalty is **criminal — 2 years imprisonment** for an individual. This is the strictest
  provision in the stack; precisely why it is deployed as backdrop, never as accusation.

## s 160 — Prohibition on obtaining copies of documents for proceeding (p 213)

> **160(1)** "A person is not entitled to a copy of— (a) any part of the record of a
> proceeding under this Act; or (b) any document used or tendered in a proceeding under this
> Act." Exceptions (160(2)): parties, persons named in an order, court-authorised, etc.

⭐ **Banked for the ex-partner matters:** if MSH or the Regulator ever sought the DFVP court
records of the Feb–Mar 2025 orders, s 160 is the gate — they are not entitled to them.
(Not engaged by the 14 Jan 2024 texts, which are private messages, not court records.)

## s 101 — Police officer may issue police protection notice (p 138)

> **101(1)** grounds: officer "reasonably believes the respondent **has committed domestic
> violence**" (a); natural-justice attempt (b); no existing order/notice either direction
> (c); notice "**necessary or desirable to protect the aggrieved**" (d); custody not required
> (e). Subject to ss 102–103 (supervisor approval etc.).

Child-respondent bar comes from s 22(2), which governs PPNs by s 22(1).
