## 1. Prizeman v Q-COMP [2005] QIC 53; (2005) 180 QGIG 481
**Source:** `Prizeman_v_Q-COMP_2005_QIC_53_(jade).pdf` (Hall P, 14 September 2005)

> And it must be remembered that it is a consequence of s. 34(5)(b) of the WorkCover Queensland Act 1996 that, in determining whether action was reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way by the employer in connection with the worker’s employment, it is the reality of the employer’s conduct and not the employee’s perception of it which must be taken into account.

**Report citation**, from *Carr* [2022] QIRC 59, footnote 55:

> 55 Prizeman v Q–COMP (2005) 180 QGIG 481, 481.

**9A, Part C, paragraph 1:**

> 1. The onus of proof, accepted. The Appellant accepts that it is for him to prove, on the balance of probabilities, that he sustained an injury within section 32, and that the exclusion in section 32(5)(a) does not apply to the injury proved. Whether management action was reasonable is judged objectively. Prizeman v Q-COMP [2005] QIC 53; (2005) 180 QGIG 481 states that the focus is on what the employer actually did, not on how the worker perceived it. The contrary contention in the Amended Form 9A filed 7 April 2026 is withdrawn.

**Check:** ✅ The 9A's paraphrase ("the focus is on what the employer actually did, not on how the worker perceived it") matches "the reality of the employer's conduct and not the employee's perception of it". Citation 180 QGIG 481 confirmed.

## 2. Delaney v Q-COMP [2005] QIC 11; (2005) 178 QGIG 197
**Source:** the judgment is **not** in the repo. Taken from Review Decision 69983 (`documents/Review_Decision_69983_24.10.2024.pdf`), p 26 and footnote 5.

> In the decision of Delaney v Q-COMP5 the Industrial Court held that in determining the issue of whether management action was reasonable and taken in a reasonable way, a global evaluation of management actions must be made. In Delaney, it was held that when management actions are joined by subject matter, time and personality, the actions are entitled to be evaluated on a global basis.

Footnote:

> 5 [2005] QIC 11; 178 QGIG 197

**9A, Part C, paragraph 6:**

> 6. Assessment as a whole, where appropriate. Delaney v Q-COMP [2005] QIC 11; (2005) 178 QGIG 197 supports assessing management action as a whole where its parts are closely enough connected by subject matter, time and the people involved. The Appellant relies on that approach only where the Commission considers the connection close enough. Each stressor is also pleaded on its own, so that the reasonableness question can be answered without bringing in a fact from another stressor.

**Check:** ⚠ Citation and holding consistent with the Respondent's own statement of the case ("joined by subject matter, time and personality" ↔ "subject matter, time and the people involved"). **Verify against the judgment itself**: download *Delaney* from jade.io and save it here.

## 3. Workers' Compensation Regulator v Langerak [2020] ICQ 2 at [86]
**Source:** `Workers_Compensation_Regulator_v_Langerak_2020_ICQ_2.pdf` (Martin J, President, 5 March 2020)

> [86] The ultimate question under s 32(5)(a) is whether the injury, not any one stressor, arose out of, or in the course of, reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way by the employer in connection with the worker’s employment. The determination made in respect of s 32(5)(a) is one made by weighing reasonable management action against other employment factors in light of the whole of the evidence.

## 4. Carr v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2022] QIRC 59 at [59]–[60]
**Source:** `Carr_v_Workers_Compensation_Regulator_2022_QIRC_59.pdf` (1 March 2022)

> However, simply because a large number of stressors are nominated does not mean a consideration of the impact of the stressors on a global basis is justified.58 [60] His Honour Martin J observed in Workers' Compensation Regulator v Langerak (emphasis added): The ultimate question under s 32(5)(a) is whether the injury, not any one stressor, arose out of, or in the course of, reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way by the employer in connection with the worker's employment. The determination made in respect of s 32(5)(a) is one made by weighing reasonable management action against other employment factors in light of the whole of the evidence.

## 5. Simon Blackwood (Workers' Compensation Regulator) v Mahaffey [2016] ICQ 10 at [53], [55], [57]
**Source:** `Simon_Blackwood_WCR_v_Mahaffey_2016_ICQ_10.pdf` (Martin J, President, 7 April 2016)

> [53] In Blackwood v Adams25 I agreed26 with what Hall P had said in Hochen, namely, that an enquiry as to whether or not unreasonable management action was the dominant cause of an injury was an erroneous approach.

> [55] Where the only cause of a personal injury is reasonable management action etc. then s 32(5) will work to exclude it from the definition of “injury” in s 32(1). The difficulty, as has become painfully obvious over the years, is where a psychiatric or psychological disorder can be seen to have arisen from a mixture of actions including reasonable management action. Experience in this jurisdiction shows that it is not uncommon for psychiatric disorders to be the result of a number of factors.

> [57] The difficulties in construing s 32(5) support the conclusion that more than one interpretation of s 32 is available and that, therefore, the beneficial interpretation approach should be applied. In the cases decided in this Court any attempt to provide some type of formula or application of dominant cause has been rejected. Section 32 must be applied in the light of the evidence accepted by the Commission. If, after considering all the relevant evidence and weighing up the factors which were accepted as having given rise to the personal injury, the Commission forms the conclusion that any of the conduct referred to in s 32(5) does not, on balance, displace the evidence in favour of the worker then a finding in the worker’s favour must follow.

**9A, Part C, paragraph 7:**

> 7. The ultimate statutory question concerns the injury as a whole. Workers' Compensation Regulator v Langerak [2020] ICQ 2 at [86], as applied in Carr v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2022] QIRC 59 at [60], states that the ultimate question under section 32(5)(a) is whether the injury, not any one stressor, arose out of or in the course of reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way. That requires the reasonable management action to be weighed against the other employment factors, in light of the whole of the evidence. Simon Blackwood (Workers' Compensation Regulator) v Mahaffey [2016] ICQ 10 at [57] is consistent with the proposition that, where other employment factors also caused the disorder, the presence of reasonable management action among the causes does not by itself answer the statutory question. The review decision's finding that the rostering of 17 and 18 March 2024 was unreasonable management action is relied on as an admitted document, not as a binding decision.

**Check:** ✅ The 9A's sentence on *Langerak* is the first sentence of [86] word for word, and its second sentence paraphrases the second sentence of [86]. *Carr* at [60] quotes [86]. *Mahaffey* at [57] supports the proposition the 9A states (it says only "is consistent with"). *Carr* at [59] is the limit on global assessment, which 9A C.6 already respects.
