1. Ms Chloe Taylor

capacity, from around April 2023 to 1 July 2024 supervising rostering, attendance and shifts. 
This witness will give the following evidence:  
• 
Denies maintaining an erratic physical presence or imposing unassessed unilateral directives without 
consultation, and denies any managerial hostility. (SOFAC para 11) 
• 
That on 6 June 2023 Ms Taylor found an entry in the switchboard team’s shared Communication Book that 
she considered and removed it without knowing at the time who had written it.  (SOFAC para 12(a)) 
• 
Earlier that same day she had sent the whole team, including the Appellant, a reminder email about the 
book’s proper use. When the Appellant arrived for his afternoon shift and learned the entry had been 
removed, he questioned her closely and loudly in front of other switchboard staff.  (SOFAC para 12(c)-
(d)) 
• 
She asked him to stop talking over her and offered to discuss it privately, or with Ms Reese, instead. She

• 
Ms Taylor’s evidence is that he was comparing himself to full-time colleagues, whose shifts that

Although not required to, she offered him an additional weekend shift, so in the result he worked the

ordinary day shifts.  (SOFAC para 19(c)-(d))

Confirms she sent the Appellant an email on 14 November 2023 to discuss compliance with shift and break

Confirms the AVAC correspondence of 3, 21 and 28 May 2024, in which she was involved in emails about

3. Ms Kimberley Wright 
Ms Kimberly Wight was Director, Client Services and Performance, Payroll Transactional Services. She has no 
personal dealings with the Appellant. Her evidence comes from her oversight of the payroll function and 
payroll’s records concerning his employment and pay.

This witness will give the following evidence: 
• 
General position on the Appellant’s pay queries. Payroll’s review of the pay issues the Appellant raised 
across 2023 and 2024 shows they were investigated and, where a discrepancy or error was found, corrected. 
There are no outstanding underpayments owed to him. (SOFAC para 20(a)-(c), (f)) 
• 
The public holiday allowance versus “public holiday not required”.  
• 
Ms Wright explains the distinction that an employee on a straight Monday-to-Friday roster who is not 
required to work a public holiday is simply paid “public holiday not required” within their ordinary 
contracted hours, whereas a continuous shift worker whose roster has been varied around a public 
holiday can instead be paid a discretionary public holiday allowance (an additional 7.6-hour payment, 
outside ordinary hours), which must be approved and submitted by the line manager on an AVAC.  
(SOFAC para 20(d)) 
• 
The Appellant was not separately entitled to a “public holiday not worked” amount on top of whichever 
of these two payments was appropriate to his circumstances. (SOFAC para 20(d)) 
• 
That the Appellant signed an “eight-hour shift agreement” on 17 June 2020, a Metro South-specific 
document, made under clause 15.1 of the Hospital and Health Services General Employee Award State 
2015, reducing his break between shifts from ten hours to eight for more than one break period, with no end 
date, so that it continues to apply indefinitely unless replaced.  (SOFAC para 22(e)) 
• 
She confirms that payroll processes and holds these agreements but does not itself assess an employee’s 
entitlement under them, or under any change in employment status. (SOFAC para 22(e)) 
• 
That  when the system did not apply an additional payment for a shift separated by only a seven-hour break 
17–18 March 2024., Ms Wright’s team escalated the query to their internal Technical Support team to check 
whether this was a payroll error. The advice returned was that the Award’s fatigue provision applies to 
breaks around overtime, not between two ordinary shifts, and that what had occurred was better 
characterised as a rostering practice issue for the line manager rather than an unpaid entitlement. (SOFAC 
para 24(b))

This witness will give the following evidence:

That Ms Earl’s review of the Appellant’s pay for the fortnight including 30 March 2024 shows he was 
originally paid an eleventh, additional shift because that public holiday had been processed as “public 
holiday not required”, a payment that sits inside an employee’s ordinary contracted hours, which pushed him

over his contracted 80 hours for the fortnight and triggered an automatic hours reduction on his payslip.

payable in respect of it. (SOFAC para 20(a))

That from the roster, the Appellant’s break between two shifts 17–18 March 2024 was only seven hours, that
