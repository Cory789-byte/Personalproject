## A. Costs — the Guide is stale; the 2025 Regulation is better than feared

The *Workers’ Compensation and Rehabilitation Regulation 2014* expired 31 August 2025 and was remade as the *Workers’ Compensation and Rehabilitation Regulation 2025* (SL 2025 No. 116), commenced 1 September 2025. There is no regulation 132. The hearing-costs provision is now **s 191**. Expert top-up is **s 192**.

Authorised text (extracted from the official PDF, pages 118–119):

**s 191 Costs of hearing before industrial magistrate or industrial commission**

1. A decision to award costs of a hearing before an industrial magistrate or the industrial commission is **at the discretion** of the magistrate or commission.
2. If costs are awarded:
   - (a) counsel’s or solicitor’s fees = UCPR 1999 sch 2 pt 2 **scale C**;
   - (b) witnesses = *Uniform Civil Procedure (Fees) Regulation 2019* pt 3;
   - (c) bailiffs = UCP (Fees) Regulation 2019 sch 2A.
3. The magistrate or commission **may award up to 1.5×** the amounts under (2)(a), in total or on any item, if satisfied those amounts are inadequate having regard to the work involved, or the importance, difficulty or complexity of the matter.

**s 192 Payment of additional amount for costs**

1. Applies if the Regulator or an insurer is required to pay costs in a proceeding in relation to a witness who is a medical practitioner or gives evidence of a professional nature, **and** the fees/expenses payable by the party who called them exceed the costs awarded.
2. The Regulator or insurer **may**, on the application of the party that called the witness, pay an additional amount it is satisfied is reasonable, having regard to the subject matter of the proceeding.

### What that means for position

- “Costs follow the event” remains the Guide’s description of *practice*. It is not the rule. The rule is a discretion. The Commission may decline to award costs at all.
- Any award of lawyers’ fees is capped at scale C, with a 1.5× ceiling. Not indemnity. Not open-ended. A known and bounded quantity.
- Still real money on a multi-day hearing. *Firat (No 2)* [2024] QIRC 166 (under the old reg 132, same scale architecture) fixed hearing costs at **$10,450.05**. That is the order of magnitude, not six figures.
- If you win, and Krishnaiah’s and Hawes’s fees exceed scale, **s 192 is the direct answer to the conduct-money / expert-fee problem**. Application is to the Regulator (or insurer). Discretionary, but it lands on exactly those two professional witnesses.
- “Settle because the costs tail is unbearable” does not stand. Settlement remains preferable on merits and medical risk, not on fear of costs.

## B. The three unread authorities — now in the bundle

### 1. Turay v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2023] ICQ 13 (Davis J, President, 16 June 2023)

- File: `01_priority_unread/Turay_v_WCR_2023_ICQ_13.pdf`
- Also first-instance: [2023] QIRC 1 (Power IC) and (No 2) [2023] ICQ 19 (strike-out of further applications).
- **Not a s 32(5) reasonableness case.** It is an out-of-time / “reasonable cause” / waiver case, plus the nature of an ICQ appeal under the WCR Act (rehearing, not confined to error of law).
- QIRC dismissed. ICQ dismissed. Later applications struck out in (No 2).
- Read it for: standard of ICQ review; preference of medical evidence on capacity; limits of a further “rehearing” after dismissal.
- It does **not** move a s 32(5) percentage range by itself.

Queensland Judgments: https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/icq/2023/13  
PDF: https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/icq/2023/13/pdf

### 2. Neale v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2023] ICQ 17 (O’Connor VP, 10 August 2023)

- File: `01_priority_unread/Neale_v_WCR_2023_ICQ_17.pdf`
- First instance: [2023] QIRC 9 (Pidgeon IC) — multi-day Townsville hearing; review decision **confirmed**; s 32(5)(a) exclusion applied.
- ICQ: appeal **dismissed**. Appellant in person. No error identified in the Commissioner’s stressor-by-stressor reasoning that management action was reasonable and taken in a reasonable way.
- This is a **loss** for the worker on s 32(5). Useful as a recent ICQ restatement that the Commission’s task is to assess the management action actually taken, not to design the “reasonable” alternative. Cites Turay [2023] ICQ 013 on the appeal standard.
- Does not improve the 55–70 band. If anything it is a cautionary comparator on multi-stressor workplace conflict + “reasonable management action”.

Queensland Judgments: https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/icq/2023/17  
PDF: https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/icq/2023/17/pdf

### 3. Mackenzie v State of Queensland (Queensland Health) [2023] QIRC 121 — **do not cite for s 32**

- File: `01_priority_unread/Mackenzie_v_Qld_Health_2023_QIRC_121_NOT_s32.pdf`
- Full Bench (Davis J, O’Connor VP, Dwyer IC), 5 May 2023.
- **This is not a workers’ compensation case.** It is an unfair-dismissal / COVID vaccine-mandate (HED 12/21) strike-out. Application for reinstatement struck out. Costs later assessed.
- The search-engine summary that this was “investigation not conducted in a reasonable way and s 32(5) did not exclude the injury” is **wrong**. It must not be cited for that proposition.

The case that actually matches that description is:

**State of Queensland (Queensland Police Service) v Workers’ Compensation Regulator & Stretton [2023] ICQ 15** (O’Connor VP, 28 June 2023)

- File: `03_investigation_reasonable_way/QPS_v_WCR_Stretton_2023_ICQ_15.pdf`
- Catchwords on the face of the judgment: Commission found the investigation into the complaint was **not conducted in a reasonable way**; injury **not excluded** by s 32(5); QPS appeal **dismissed**.
- That is the lead. Read Stretton (ICQ) together with **Maher v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2021] QIRC 313** (McLennan IC) — appeal **allowed**; disciplinary process was management action that was reasonable in kind but **not taken in a reasonable way** (suspension meeting without notice/support person; show-cause deficiencies beyond a “mere blemish”).

## D. 2024–2026 s 32 psychiatric / psychological appeals (the comparators that matter)

Keyword sweep of QIRC/ICQ decisions 2024–2026. This is not every WC appeal. It is the published psychiatric/psychological s 32 cohort that could be obtained.

### Allowed, or worker’s accepted claim left standing

1. **Dodd v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2024] QIRC 273** (Pidgeon IC, 25 November 2024) — **ALLOWED**  
   Secondary psychiatric injury to an accepted neck injury. Employment (pain and consequences) a significant contributing factor. **s 32(5)(c)** (insurer/Regulator action) did **not** exclude. Regulator decision set aside; injury within s 32.  
   File: `04_comparators_2024_2026/Dodd_v_WCR_2024_QIRC_273_ALLOWED.pdf`  
   https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/qirc/2024/273

2. **Austin v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2025] QIRC 110** — **ALLOWED**  
   Psychiatric injury arising from accepted work-related back injury / chronic pain. Employment a significant contributing factor. Review decision of 3 March 2023 set aside; application for acceptance. Medical preference: treating/detailed history over a brief Telehealth Axis-I threshold approach.  
   File: `04_comparators_2024_2026/Austin_v_WCR_2025_QIRC_110_ALLOWED.pdf`  
   https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/qirc/2025/110

3. **State of Queensland (DAF) v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2024] QIRC 53** (Merrell DP, 28 February 2024) — **employer appeal dismissed; claim stands**  
   Adjustment disorder with anxiety and depression; workload. s 32(5)(a) and (b) **did not apply**. Review decision confirmed.  
   File: `04_comparators_2024_2026/DAF_v_WCR_2024_QIRC_53_employer_appeal_dismissed_claim_stands.pdf`  
   https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/qirc/2024/53

4. **Maher [2021] QIRC 313** (slightly earlier than the 2024–26 window, but the clean “process not taken in a reasonable way” comparator) — **ALLOWED**.  
   File in folder 03.

5. **QPS v WCR & Stretton [2023] ICQ 15** — ICQ dismissed the employer’s appeal; Commission finding that the investigation was not conducted in a reasonable way **stands**.  
   File in folder 03.

### Dismissed (worker lost)

6. **Firat v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2024] QIRC 137** (O’Connor VP, 29 May 2024) — **DISMISSED**. A secondary blog summary that this appeal was allowed is wrong. Costs of the hearing later fixed at $10,450.05 in *Firat (No 2)* [2024] QIRC 166.  
7. **Granato v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2024] QIRC 162** — **DISMISSED**.  
8. **Richards v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2024] QIRC 241** — **DISMISSED**.  
9. **Kerr v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2025] QIRC 190** (Dwyer IC) — 15 stressors; RMA taken in a reasonable way; **dismissed**. Not in the zip (obtain from https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/qirc/2025/190/pdf).  
10. **Anderson v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2025] QIRC 361** (Hartigan DP, 19 December 2025) — conversation with junior doctor found to be RMA taken in a reasonable way; **dismissed**. https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/qirc/2025/361/pdf  
11. **Canning v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2026] QIRC 51** (Pidgeon IC, 25 February 2026) — failed first limb of s 32; medical witness treated as advocate; **dismissed**. https://www.queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/qirc/2026/51/pdf  
12. **Dones v Workers’ Compensation Regulator [2026] QIRC 25 / 225** (O’Neill IC, 15 July 2026) — employment not major significant contributing factor; **dismissed**.

Pattern: recent *allowed* psychiatric appeals that published are concentrated on **secondary psychiatric injury to an accepted physical injury** (Dodd, Austin) or on **employer appeals failing to establish RMA** (DAF, Stretton). Straight “workplace investigation / performance management / interpersonal conflict” worker appeals in 2024–26 are still more often dismissed (Firat, Granato, Richards, Kerr, Anderson, Canning).

## E. Where that leaves the range

The unread trio is now readable.

- Turay does not move a s 32(5) range.
- Neale is a recent ICQ loss for the worker on RMA.
- Mackenzie is the wrong case. Substitute Stretton [2023] ICQ 15 and Maher [2021] QIRC 313.

The 2024–26 allowed set does not contain a run of primary-psychiatric investigation cases going the worker’s way. The two clean worker wins on psychiatric injury in this sweep (Dodd, Austin) are secondary-to-physical cases. DAF and Stretton help on “RMA not made out / process not taken in a reasonable way”, which is the better doctrinal home if the case is about how an investigation was run.

On that material the previous **55–70%, centred 60–65%** still holds as a working band. Stretton + Maher support the upper half *if* the evidence actually shows process defects beyond blemish (notice, support person, particularisation, delay, misstatement of evidence). Neale + Kerr + Anderson pull the other way if the Commission characterises the same facts as ordinary, imperfect management. The band should not be widened on these authorities alone.

Costs no longer justify a discount below that band. Discount, if any, is merits and medical, not tail-risk.
