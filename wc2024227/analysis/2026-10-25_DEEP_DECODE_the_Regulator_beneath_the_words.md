## Layer 6: the machine underneath

**How a decision gets made:**
1. The officer (Matheson) holds the file and promises process.
2. Counsel advises, in writing, after seeing the conference: you, your doctors' plan, and Dwyer's reaction.
3. A delegate signs any concession.

That is why the realistic concession point is **at or soon after the conference**, not before it (before about
20%, after about 55%).

**The "new information" problem.** An institution concedes only with a story that protects its first decision. The
Guide gives it the words: "Following the conference, the Respondent may decide to review its position or consider
conceding the appeal where **new information** is presented that they may not have yet considered."

**The story is already in the record.** The review (Oct 2024) relied on the employer's account. The appeal produced
documents the review did not have:
- the attachments "were present" (their own 13 May ¶14(e));
- the leave decline "appears to have been made in error";
- the 7-hour break was "human error"; "a rostering practice issue for the line manager";
- the payroll corrections ran 425 days.

**Their concession sentence will read something like this:** "on the material now available, which was not before
the review officer…". Any concession will be framed as the file's doing, as on 24 Sep ("upon further review of the
appeal file"), and never as an admission that the review was wrong (strong).

**Your lever:**
- At the conference, say **"these documents were not before the review"**, never "the review got it wrong".
- That hands them the face-saving path at no cost to you.
