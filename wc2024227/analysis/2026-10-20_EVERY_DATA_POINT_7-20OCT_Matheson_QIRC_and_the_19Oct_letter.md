## 2. The 19 October letter: what Cory did

He did four things in one email.

1. **He gave them what they asked for first:** all three dates, with no preference. Nothing he did could
   delay the listing, and he said so.
2. **He counted the record for them.**
   - 38 of 39 documents are admitted or confirmed, and only Tab 24 is outstanding.
   - Facts 228–231 are matched, row by row, to the spreadsheet they supplied and vouched for on 12 Oct.
3. **He asked six closed questions where "yes" costs them nothing on reasonableness:**
   1. facts 228–231 (their own spreadsheet);
   2. tender without further proof, and the ground for any Tab 24 dispute;
   3. any relevance or admissibility objection, to be named now;
   4. s 32(1), from their own review's "Conclusion" (facts 261–262);
   5. consent to leave, and how much time they want to amend;
   6. notice that ¶27 (fact 303) will be raised at the conference.

   The letter says that agreeing tender "would not require the Respondent to accept the truth of any
   statement", and that reasonableness remains for the Commission. That removes their reason to say no.
4. **He set a date and a consequence.** The date was soft ("where practicable"). The consequence was firm:
   matters not agreed go to the Commission at the conference, "and, if necessary, … directions or … mention".

**Small gaps, all low-cost:**
- s 552B is not addressed. Agree if asked.
- The as-sent version lacks the sentence accepting that the hearing is de novo. 9A Part C ¶9 already
  accepts it.
- "Now admitted" is used for the confirmed documents. Their own 16 Oct email calls a confirmation "the
  admission of the Regulator to a document's existence".

## 3. Why she answered the way she did

The literal answer is "a diligent officer acknowledges mail". The signals say more.

1. **To keep Dwyer from making orders.** On 12 Oct they asked him to leave items 2–7 to the conference "in
   lieu of ordered in directions". If six open questions reached the conference unanswered, with Cory asking
   for directions, Dwyer would have the reason he lacked on 13 Oct. Answering Cory privately is the cheapest
   way to keep the file free of orders. That is also why the Registry email does not mention the letter.
2. **The date suits Counsel.** Counsel is back on 22 Oct, so 30 Oct gives her six business days to settle the
   answer. It lands two business days before the earliest conference date, so the answer frames the
   conference. Cory chose the date, and it happens to suit them, so she took it outright.
3. **Their own record of slipping.** On 7 Oct she noted his 16 Oct date for two asks and answered one. A
   second partial answer, against a letter that promises to take unagreed matters to the Commission, would
   look like a pattern. "Respond by… as requested" is a commitment she can be held to, and she knows it.
4. **Order of consequence.** The Commission came first (9:44), then Cory (9:48), and she pointed him to the
   cc. It was first thing the next business morning: Dwyer said "as soon as possible", and Cory's
   confirmation was the only thing holding up the Registry email.
5. **"Thankfully" repairs 12 Oct.** On 12 Oct she told the Commission Counsel was "not contactable until
   22 October". A week later Counsel was in contact. "Thankfully… while she finishes her last days of leave"
   presents the contradiction as good fortune before anyone asks.
6. **"The parties have confirmed joint availability"** is Dwyer's own phrase ("joint range of
   availability"). It shows a joint, cooperative front to the Commission.
7. **The Guide 5.1 record.** "Following the conference, the Respondent may decide to review its position or
   consider conceding the appeal where new information is presented". Written agreements before the
   conference build the record of a party that engaged and considered new information. Any later change of
   position then reads as principled.

**What it does not show.**
- "Review… and respond" is not "agree".
- Matheson cannot concede; Counsel and the delegate decide.
- An ordinary party in an ordinary defended case would also send these two emails. They are cooperative, not
  concessive.
