## 7. Is the Regulator shaping a concession? (internal, 2 Oct)
- The reservation is on the witness list only (24 Sep 11:50): "reserves its right to amend this list depending
  on the case presented by the appellant at the hearing". It is standard wording and predates the 1 Oct 9A.
  It doesn't extend to an expert, and FDO(3) still governs. Election item 6 already offers 14 days to respond.
- Signs consistent with keeping a concession available and narrow:
  - four lay witnesses and five pages; no expert or medical witness; nobody on causation;
  - the outlines explain how things happened ("human error", "rostering practice issue for the line manager",
    "appears to have been made in error") rather than defending them as reasonable;
  - ¶27 is still unparticularised (fact 303);
  - senior MSH authors (CE, Director, HR) are not called;
  - early filing, and four documents confirmed in the same window.
- Equally consistent with a lean defence that relies on s 32(5) and cross-examining the doctors. To tell
  the two apart, watch: whether ¶27 is particularised, whether leave is sought for an expert, and what
  comes back from MSH.
- If a concession comes, the likely ground is the narrowest one: the admitted break plus the Regulator's
  own reviewer (fact 260), with the new admissions tipping s 32(5). The ground doesn't change the result for
  the Appellant, so don't resist the framing.
