## 1. Where the room was

administrative: how many documents, what the objection covered, what an email filter is,
*"A to E and 10 alone."* Everybody present was operating in the language of volume and burden.

## 3. Why it worked — four reasons

**(a) It gave the room nothing to argue with.** Across the whole hearing the appellant used
**0 evaluative terms and 0 mental-state attributions in 1,378 words.** There is no *unreasonable*,
no *deliberately*, no *failed*. An opponent can dispute a characterisation. Nobody was going to
dispute that a cardiac arrest is a cardiac arrest.

**(b) It refused the category he was being managed in.** The available frame was
*self-represented litigant with a grievance*. Flat clinical narration at 62 dB cannot be filed
under it. The register itself declined the discount.

**(c) The delivery contradicted every instinct.** An advocate lifts on *"cardiac arrest."* It was
delivered at **−0.18 semitones — below his own median pitch.** Loudness carried it instead
(**+4.8 dB**), and rate accelerated through the chain to his 71st and 74th percentiles. Then the
brake: the single word ***"distress"*** at his **8th percentile**. The slowest thing in the passage
is the last noun.

**(d) It stopped one step short.** The final clause — *"we're now not even contacting them by their
numbers"* — is present tense and states a condition, not a conclusion. **The room had to take the
last step itself.** A conclusion a room reaches on its own is not on the record and cannot be
objected to.

## 5. What the sound of nothing actually is

It is not silence. There is no silence in a building.

At −63.5 dBFS what remains is **air handling** — low-frequency, broadband, the register of a
ventilation system — plus the recorder's own electronic floor. Measured spectral centroid 621 Hz,
low-frequency dominant. A hum with a faint hiss on it. That is the whole content of those five
seconds.

### Why you hear it as an arrival, not a departure

Hearing is differential. A steady background gets adapted out: everyone in that room had stopped
hearing the ventilation by about five past nine, because a constant, uninformative signal is
suppressed as irrelevant.

When the foreground stops, that suppression is suddenly wrong. The background becomes the loudest
thing present and **re-enters awareness.** Which is why nobody in such a room reports "people
stopped talking." They report *"you could hear the air conditioning."* The conscious experience is
the hum **arriving**, not the voices leaving.

### Why it is a social measurement, not an acoustic one

This is the part that matters.

**The noise floor of an occupied room is made of involuntary human activity.** Weight shifting in a
chair. A page settling. A pen set down. Breathing. Somebody re-crossing their legs. Nobody decides
to produce any of it; bodies simply do not hold still, and the sum of all of it is what a room
sounds like when it is full of people who are only half-attending.

The measured floor fell 11.6 dB in the first 250 ms and held for **4.88 seconds**.

> ⚠ **Correction, 21 September 2026.** The *speed* of that fall is **ordinary**: with the voice tail
> removed, the room-drop at this gap ranks **99 of 198** gaps in the hearing — the median. Every
> gap drops this fast, because voice decay is fast. **The drop is not evidence of synchronised
> freezing.** What distinguishes this gap is the **hold**: in every other gap that reached this floor,
> something moved within one to two seconds. Here nothing did for 4.88 s. See
> `2026-09-18_FULL_RUN_corrections.md` §"The drop".

You can choose not to speak. **You cannot choose to switch off the ambient noise of your own body —
unless you are holding still.** For the floor to drop that far, that fast, and stay there, several
people had to stop moving at the same instant and remain stopped.

⇒ What is recorded in those five seconds is not an absence of speech. It is **the sound of a room
full of people holding still.** The noise floor of a room is the sound of bodies not paying
attention. When it vanishes, what you are hearing is attention itself.

### And why it felt wrong to everybody in it

Conversational handover runs on a very tight clock. In this hearing the median was **0.54 seconds**.
A gap past about a second already reads as trouble; past three it reads as an event.

At 5.74 seconds the clock had been broken by **an order of magnitude**, and — this is the part that
makes it public — each person could tell that everyone else had noticed too.
