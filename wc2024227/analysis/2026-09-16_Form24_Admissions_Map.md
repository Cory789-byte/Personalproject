## 1. The finding: both ends of the patient-safety chain are already admitted

Paragraphs 6 to 9 of the Notice were pleaded as a single causal chain. The response splits them, and
the split is favourable.

| ¶ | The Appellant's fact | The Regulator's answer (18 Feb 2026) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6** | On 6 June 2023 Ms Taylor removed a page containing the Appellant's handwritten entry from the Communication Book | "**admits that Ms Taylor removed a page** from the workplace communications book but does not admit that the page contained the appellant's handwriting **because the respondent does not have a copy of the page**" | **Removal admitted.** Authorship not admitted, for one stated reason only |
| **7** | The removed entry contained operational information on the necessity of verifying and updating doctors' contact details | "**does not admit** the facts contained therein **because the Respondent does not have a copy of the document referred to and is unaware of its contents**" | **Contents not admitted, for one stated reason only** |
| **8** | Maintaining accurate contact details for medical staff is a critical function of the Switchboard to ensure effective clinical handover and patient safety | "**admits the facts contained therein** and says that there was a procedure in place for this to occur" | **Criticality admitted. Unqualified.** |
| **9** | By removing the entry without consultation, Ms Taylor prevented the communication of safety-critical information to other staff | "**denies as untrue** the fact, because there was already a procedure in place" | Consequence denied on a single stated ground |

**Link 1 is admitted. Link 3 is admitted. Only link 2 — what was written on the page — is not.**

And the reason given for link 2 is not a denial of the fact. It is an assertion of **absence of the
document**: *"does not have a copy," "unaware of its contents."* Twice, in consecutive paragraphs.

### What is already banked, and where

None of the above is new to the file. **Form 20 ¶6 already pleads ¶8 as an Admitted Fact**, and
confirmed-record.md already carries the ¶8 pinpoint block, the ¶6/¶7 position and the warning that
**¶8 does not carry ¶9**. That discipline holds and is not disturbed here: the criticality is
admitted, the *consequence of the removal* is denied, and the two must not be run as one.

### The one addition: the ¶9 denial names no procedure

¶9 is denied *"because there was already a procedure in place."* ¶8 is admitted with the rider
*"there was a procedure in place for this to occur."* The Respondent asserts a procedure twice and
**identifies it nowhere** — no name, no document, no date, no author. It does so while saying, in the
adjacent paragraphs, that it holds no copy of the record and is unaware of its contents.

That is answerable by one narrow written request: **identify the procedure.** It is not an attack on
the denial and it does not seek to make ¶8 carry ¶9. It asks the Respondent to particularise the only
ground it has pleaded. If a procedure is identified, it is a document, and a document is disclosable.
If none can be identified, the denial stands on an unparticularised assertion and the Commission can
weigh it accordingly at hearing.

**Secondary, and for the contradiction matrix only — not for the 64G.** A pleaded "procedure in
place" for verifying and updating doctors' contact details sits awkwardly beside ¶10 (the book is not
a government record) and ¶12 (Ms Taylor identified it as "a professional tool"). Noted, not argued.

## 2. The mention, folded on top

**7 August 2026, before Commissioner Dwyer, at ~36:03**, walking Item 18:

> "And she'll give evidence about that. **There's no documents required for that, is there?** Did she
> physically tear the page out of a book? Yes, she tore it. So maybe the book. So we can see the torn
> out page. **But she might admit she tore it out, in which case we probably don't need the book.**
> But anyway, that's the matter. **It's not a particularly document heavy point, that one.**"

That reasoning is sound on the facts the Commissioner had. It rests on a single premise: **that the
dispute is whether the page was torn out.**

It is not. The tearing out has been admitted since 18 February 2026 (¶6). The dispute is **what was
written on the page**, and on that the Regulator has said in writing, twice, that it **cannot resolve
it without a copy of the document** (¶6, ¶7).

So the position on Item 18 is the opposite of what was put:

| What was assumed at the mention | What the Form 24 response actually says |
|---|---|
| The live issue is whether Ms Taylor tore the page out | That is admitted (¶6). It is not in issue |
| If she admits it, the book is not needed | The book is the **only** thing that can resolve the issue that *is* live (¶7) |
| Not a document-heavy point | Correct — it is a **one-document** point. The narrowest item in the whole application |

**Item 18 is the strongest produce-or-swear item on the list, not the weakest.** It is one document.
Its relevance is admitted by the Respondent's own ¶8. Its necessity is established by the
Respondent's own stated reason for not admitting ¶7. It satisfies r 64B(2) on its face: there is no
other reasonably simple and inexpensive way to prove the contents of a page that only one party ever
held and which that party says it does not have. *Adani v Pennings* — this is spear fishing, not
drag-netting.

**This was not put to the Commissioner, because the Form 24 response was not in front of him.** That
is the gap to close, in writing, when the Form 29 comes back on.
