## The posture in one paragraph

The Regulator is a statutory respondent defending its own review decision. It does not pay the compensation (the
insurer does), so what it stands to lose is institutional:
- the integrity of its decisions;
- the scheme's rules;
- published reasons about its method;
- costs;
- its standing with a Commission it appears before constantly.

Throughout this matter it has behaved as a **verifier, not a partisan for Metro South**. It tested the evidence,
including the employer's own paper, and conceded on paper where the paper failed. Having now verified the facts
largely as pleaded, it is in **managed retreat**: conceding facts, protecting its record and its standing, and
keeping one cheap defence option (medical causation by stressor) until the conference gives it a principled
reason to stop.

## 1. What kind of litigant it is

**A repeat player.** Galanter's distinction (*Why the "Haves" Come Out Ahead*, 1974) applies:
- a repeat player plays for rules and reputation, not just the case;
- it settles cases likely to produce unfavourable precedent and litigates those that can make good law.

Dwyer, 7 Aug (59:48–59:58): the Regulator operates "in the way that I've always experienced them operating both
as a commissioner and as a practitioner, appearing against them on many occasions".

**What a published loss here would put on the record:**
- the review's counting method ("two out of the three causative factors"), which Langerak [86] and Mahaffey [57]
  reject;
- findings on fatigue rostering in a non-clinical 24/7 role;
- the scope of the 2020 eight-hour agreement after a move to full time.

**The possible gain** (a *Carr*-type causation ruling) is weak on these facts.

## 4. The defence it has actually built

**Mistakes are not unreasonable management, and they did not cause the injury.**
- "Human error" is pleaded twice: the leave decline (¶14(f)) and the 7-hour break (¶22(a)). Payroll adds "a
  rostering practice issue for the line manager".
- "Not causative" (¶22(f)).
- The medical card is causation by stressor (*Carr*, ground 2). It is to be played by cross-examining the treating
  doctors, with no expert bought.

**Strength:** management does not have to be perfect, so isolated error inside a reasonable process can be argued
as reasonable management action.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Their own review** found the roster "unreasonable management action".
- **Errors cluster in the same window:**
  - leave declined in error (Feb–Mar 2024);
  - the 7-hour break (17–18 Mar);
  - the 23-day fatigue response (Apr–May);
  - the AVAC delay and "Part Completed" (May).

  A pattern undercuts "isolated" (*Delaney*, anti-atomisation).
- **The question is whole-injury** (Langerak [86]).
- **The treating records** name roster and pay.
- **MSH's own statement:** fatigue assessment after 30 Jun 2024.

## 5. What drives the current posture (ranked)

1. **Precedent and published-reasons risk** (repeat player).
2. **Realism from its own verification.**
3. **The model-litigant duty and Dwyer's stated expectations**, before a member it appears before often.
4. **Decision architecture:** officer, then Counsel, then delegate. A concession needs a principled trigger (Guide
   5.1, "new information") and the right time (after the conference).
5. **Care with a self-represented appellant who has complained about officers:** vetted, pre-prepared and polite
   texts, with no characterisation.
6. **A cheap residual option**, cross-examination, rather than a costly expert.

## 6. What the posture is not

- **Not attrition:** no costs threats, strike-out or delay. They have been early.
- **Not personal:** 0 characterisations of the Appellant in 23 emails.
- **Not protective of MSH:** they tested MSH's paper and conceded.
- **Not yet concession:** no exit words, and the SOFC and witnesses are still in place.

## 7. At the conference (prediction)

**Counsel speaks and Matheson is silent.** Expect them to:

## 8. What would flip it

1. **The Appellant widening** (perception words, motive, accusations). That hands them the s 32(5)(b) frame they
   have kept since 2025.
2. **Thin medical evidence**, which makes the residual card live.
3. **A neutral private view from Dwyer.**
4. **A hearing that could make good law for them** (unlikely on these facts).

## 9. Meeting the posture

- **Give them the principled trigger** as "new information":
  - the admissions;
  - the corrected 9A;
  - the doctors' attendance.
- **Keep the exit clean:** consent orders accepting the claim; the compensation claim only.
- **Answer only the defence they have built** ("error-tolerant reasonableness plus causation") with:
  - their review's finding;
  - the cluster of errors in one window;
  - the 23-day interval;
  - MSH's "after 30 June 2024";
  - the doctors.
- **Never widen.**
