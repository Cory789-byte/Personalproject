## 2. What he says about the things this appeal turns on

**Self-represented parties and belief v proof.** [222] "In my experience, self-represented litigants are invariably
affected by a loss of objectivity. This impairment will often undermine a self-represented litigant's judgment about
the veracity of facts upon which they rely." [247] "Ms Benjamin would not be the first self-represented litigant to
stand confidently at the bar table advocating a case they firmly believe in, only to discover that the strength of
their personal belief as to certain matters does not equate with proof." [198] "a lawyer who's lost objectivity in
the conduct of proceedings is very quickly going to get into perilous situations".

**Efficiency, unnecessary applications, restraint.** [41], [50], [66]–[67]: the "most efficient way" to deal with an
unnecessary application "was to not deal with it"; a contested interlocutory dispute "would take at least 2-4 months
… at cost to the respondents, Ashdale, and the taxpayers"; [74] "unsolicited interlocutory applications … premature or
unnecessary in each case"; [75] enmity "can lead to a combative approach that will invariably add delay and cost …
As the member responsible for the efficient case management of these proceedings I consider it entirely proper for
me to be on guard against such conduct and to warn against it"; [82] "restraint in bringing applications only when
necessary"; [232] "Generally, when submissions come in, they come in at my request, not without my request"; [248]
"Were it not for the delay caused by numerous mentions and now this application, Ms Benjamin's matter would be well
progressed towards a hearing by now."

**Pleadings: plead only what is material; keep PID content out.** [89] "Despite a myriad of facts spread across a
lengthy timeline, Ms Benjamin's complaint is no more complex than those two elements." [91] "a question arises as to
whether there is any reason to plead details of that PID". [111] "why be discreet? A responsible legal practitioner …
would be expected to only include precise detail of the PID allegation where it was materially relevant". Transcript
at [132]: "parties craft their pleadings in a way that doesn't involve contravening relevant confidentiality
surrounding PIDs. That conversation in those pleadings doesn't necessarily appear to be a material fact". [251] the
Commission "would not embark on an examination of its truth or otherwise". [43] in standard AD directions he raises
the SOFC page limit "from 10 pages to 15" as a precaution.

**Case manager, not the hearing member.** [22] "allocated to me for the purposes of case management only. With the
exception of interlocutory matters, I will not play any role in deciding the substantive complaint"; [24] after a
second conference "the case managing member will refer the file back to the Registry for allocation to another
member who will proceed to hear the matter"; [26] interlocutory rulings "can be appealed to the Industrial Court".

**Raise it at the time, on the record, or lose it.** [28]–[30]: she "failed on each occasion to exercise her right to
appeal"; [53] complaint first raised "some 4 months later"; [143] the transcripts she never applied for "plainly
dispelled" most of her complaints; [217] "I have scoured the correspondence and the transcripts … I can find no
express demand for reasons to be provided in writing".

**Disputes are not resolved by email.** [147] "it is not my practice, nor is it proper, to resolve such disputes via
email … the proper manner to address the controversy is to list the matter for mention and hear from the parties".

**Reasonable extensions are consented to.** [151] opposing a two-day extension was "unreasonable"; "mere doubts …
could never be enough to reasonably refuse the request".

**Precision in what is asked for.** [232] "if you can't describe with precision what documents it is that you want,
that creates a real difficulty in terms of what documents can be ordered"; [234] it was proper to "reality test" the
competence of her applications.

**Tentative views are proper and helpful.** [17] judicial officers may form and share preliminary opinions; "litigants
(particularly those who are self-represented) are usually assisted by hearing those opinions". This is what the
7 August 2026 mention was: tentative views offered to be dealt with, not findings.

## 4. The two residual points

1. **Length.** His instinct for a statement of facts and contentions is 10 pages, raised to 15 "as a precaution" [43].
   The 9A is 25. Nothing in the WC directions or the Guide sets a limit (FDO(3) has none; the Guide limits only the
   one-page witness outlines), and the 9A is a different instrument from an AD SOFC. But a case manager who thinks in
   10 to 15 pages will notice 25. The answer is already in the document: the "In summary" callout on page 2 and Part D
   are the 10-page version; the rest is the record for the hearing member. If he raises length at the conference, the
   response is that every paragraph is an admitted fact with its number, and that the Respondent has admitted it.
2. **Belief v proof [247].** Everything in the 9A is proved by admission except the register (Tab 31, facts 228–231),
   the call statistics, and the doctors' oral evidence. Those three are where his sentence bites. Do not add to them.
