"Constructed" marks an answer it would have to build without a pleaded basis or a witness. The weights and probabilities are judgement from the record, not measurements.

**Weight scale (1 to 5).** For each chain:

## Chain 1: roster and fatigue
**The chain:** 3(a) to 3(h) and 2(g)
- 7 Aug 2023: the Manager offers "the required rest period".
- 17 to 18 Mar 2024: the seven-hour break, with the codes on both shifts.
- 19 Mar: recorded against your own sick leave.
- 8 Apr: a single email serves as both the pay enquiry and the fatigue enquiry.
- 16 Apr: a roster line error.
- 1 May: refused on the 2020 agreement, "going forward"; the toolkit is raised.
- 10 May: the Director records your fatigue, a "moderate" rating, "a few rostering errors made by Chloe", and that your roster "will not be considered, however Cory is not yet aware".
- 20 May: the guideline goes to HR.
- 21 May: the Director asks what concerned you.
- No fatigue risk management before 30 June.

**Its best answer (mock):**
1. The break was "human error", "not intentional or repeated" (9C para 22(a); Taylor).
2. The 2020 agreement "continues to apply", and HR confirmed that at the time (Wright, Taylor, Reese).
3. You "could refuse shifts at any time" (9C para 22(b)).
4. Clause 18.10 gives fatigue leave only after overtime, so the refusal applied the Award correctly (9C para 24; Wright).
5. The 10 May decision was part of a roster restructure, later consulted on as "a more equitable roster", and the moderate rating shows the risk was assessed. *(Constructed; needs Reese.)*
6. The toolkit and guideline show the Director took advice. *(Constructed.)*

**Why it does not hold:**
- Seven hours is below eight as well as ten.
- HR's own email limits the agreement to staff-initiated swaps, and no swap is alleged.
- The agreement was never reviewed, and was signed at a time the Respondent does not allege the Manager held the role.
- The Manager knew "the required rest period" in August 2023.
- "Not yet aware" is the Director's own line.
- The review decision already found the rostering unreasonable.
- Its payroll witness calls the break "a rostering practice issue for the line manager".

| Link weight | Answer weight | Net |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1.5 | **Strongly yours** |

## Chain 6: from onset to the Respondent's own finding
**The chain:** B.1
- Last shift 3 Jun; date of injury 18 Jun.

- 1 Jul GP: "withhold pay ... manipulate his roster ... lates then earlies".
- Oct 2024: diagnosis.

**Its best answer (mock):**
1. The referral of 16 May lists "Anxiety" from October 2022, as a pre-existing history (9C para 8).
2. The GP histories are your own report, not proof of cause.
3. The February 2025 report records non-employment matters.
4. The review finding does not bind a de novo hearing (9C para 5).

- The Respondent's own reviewer, on the same records, found employment significant.
- With no expert, its causation case rests entirely on cross-examining your doctors.
