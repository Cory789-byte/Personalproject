## What the request does

**It accepts the Appellant's structure and removes one thing: compulsion.**
- **What it adopts.** Direction 1 of 1 Oct already proposed a second conference "at which items 2–6
  may be dealt with". The Regulator agreed to that conference and that agenda (points 2–7).
- **What it declines.** Only the ordering of points 2–7, and only "in the first instance".
- **What it leaves alone.** Nothing in the 1 Oct letter is contested.

## The 18 reasons

### From the 1 October letter (8)

1. **Timing.**
   - The election was made on 1 Oct, the day after the Respondent's time for witnesses, outlines and
     experts expired (30 Sep).
   - Its case is closed as filed. Gaps cannot be repaired without leave.
2. **The admitted record.**
   - 298 of 303 facts are admitted, and none denied.
   - The issues left are management action, reasonableness and causation.
3. **Its own review decision.** Facts 261–262 are admitted. RD 69983 states "you sustained a
   personal injury of a psychological nature" and "your injury arose out of employment, to the extent
   that it arose out of factors 2, 3 and 4, where employment was a significant contributing factor".
4. **No medical or expert evidence** was served by 30 Sep.
5. **Its own witnesses' words.**
   - The seven-hour break was "the result of human error" (outlines, line 46).
   - Payroll's technical advice was that it was "better characterised as a rostering practice issue
     for the line manager" (line 170).
   - The second SPL decline "appears to have been made in error" (line 215).
   - The outlines give no account of 1(b), 1(f), 1(h) or 1(k).
6. **Para 27.**
   - Fact 303 is admitted: para 27 does not identify the management action.
   - Direction 2 would have required the Respondent to say at the opening whether it maintains para
     27, and to particularise it.
7. **Direction 5: the main reason for "in lieu of ordered".**
   - It requires a written position on 23 causal particulars: 1(b), 1(d), 1(f)–(h), 1(k), 1(l);
     2(a)–(i), 2(l), 2(m); 3(b)–(f).
   - For each one, the Respondent would state whether it was management action and the facts relied
     on for reasonableness.
   - Given reasons 5 and 6, any written answer either gives particulars up or contradicts the
     Respondent's own witnesses.
   - A discussion at a conference commits it to nothing in writing.
8. **Direction 6's clock.**
   - An order would start 14 days to amend the SOFC, witnesses and outlines while Counsel is
     overseas.
   - "In the first instance" avoids a deadline that could not be met.

### From the proposed Second Amended Form 9A (4)

9. **It accepts the onus,** so there is nothing to attack on burden.
10. **It creates no prejudice.**
    - There is no new stressor, the descriptions of conduct are withdrawn, and the witness list and
      outlines of 9 Sep stand.
    - Prejudice is the usual ground for opposing an amendment. There is none, and leave was not
      opposed.
11. **Every particular rests on an admitted fact.**
    - Each particular is a dated event tied to a fact admitted on 8 Sep, cited in square brackets.
    - Contesting a particular means contesting the Respondent's own admission. What remains is
      whether the conduct was management action, and whether it was reasonable.
12. **Each stressor stands on its own facts.** The case cannot be disposed of by attacking one
    stressor; each must be answered.

### From the forum and the Commission (3)

16. **The conference is the vehicle for a change of position** (guide Part 5.1).
    - Its stated purpose is to "review, refine or narrow down the issues" and "whether any elements of
      s 32 of the Act can be agreed".
    - The guide continues: "Following the conference, the Respondent may decide to review its position
      or consider conceding the appeal where new information is presented."
    - The proposed 9A and the admitted record are that new information.
17. **The case manager's posture.**
    - Dwyer IC did not order points 2–7. He instructed the Regulator to obtain Counsel's dates "as
      soon as possible following 22 October 2026", and said a conference "will then be scheduled".
    - At the mention he urged resolution without his intervention and referred to the Regulator's
      responsibilities as a model litigant (internal only).
18. **Costs (the weakest reason).**
    - Costs "generally 'follow the event'" and are "limited to the 'costs of the hearing'" (guide
      p 37; s 558(3)). Calderbank #2 (served 1 Jul, rejected 16 Jul) is on the file.
    - A self-represented party recovers out-of-pocket costs, not time.
    - The larger cost to the Respondent is its own: Counsel and four MSH witnesses through a
      contested hearing.

## Seven signals of concession or negotiated narrowing

1. **The Appellant's structure was adopted.** Only compulsion was removed.
2. **No answer on the merits.**
   - Nothing says para 27 is maintained, and leave is not opposed.
   - There is no challenge to 298/303, the outline quotations or the absence of an expert.
3. **The work came first.** Authenticity was cleared before the reply, including the document behind
   the last contested facts.
4. **No requests of its own.** There is no application for leave to serve a late expert report, no
   request for further particulars, no strike-out and no extension.
5. **Counsel is to attend.** The conference is being treated as a decision point.
6. **Direct dealing.** There is an undertaking to agree dates jointly with the Appellant.
7. **The path the guide links to conceding.** The guide ties a second conference to the Respondent
   reviewing its position or conceding on new information.

## What "negotiated" means here

- **No commercial settlement at the conference.** The guide says "No commercial settlement is possible
  at the conference" (Part 5.1).
- **What is negotiated is the issues:**
  - which s 32 elements are agreed;
  - whether para 27 survives;
  - which particulars are accepted as not management action, or not reasonable.
- **When a concession would come.** After the conference, as the Respondent reviewing its position.
- **What it would look like.**
  - Consent orders setting aside the review decision and substituting a decision (s 558(1)(c)).
  - Under s 558(2) the substituted decision is taken to be the insurer's decision. That is acceptance
    of the claim.

## What it does not show

- **It is not a concession.** Nothing on management action or reasonableness has moved in writing.
- **"In lieu of ordered" keeps every option open.** It suits a party that will defend as well as one
  that will concede.
- **It costs time.** The conference will not be before late October; November is more likely.
- **Two things are open.**
  - Tab 24 is unaddressed.
  - Leave for the 9A (direction 3) is undecided; the 13 Oct email does not mention it.

**The deferral fits the 1 Oct (evening) decision.** Leave will be dealt with at the conference, which
is where the corrected proposed 9A (change 97) is to be handed to the Commission and the Respondent
together, before leave is decided.
