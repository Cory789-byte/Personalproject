# "Not requiring a party to prove a matter which the State knows to be true": what it covers here (22 Oct 2026)

> Internal. Never accuse them of breaching it. Verified text: `documents/instruments/QLD_MODEL_LITIGANT_PRINCIPLES_rev2010_VERIFIED_TEXT.md`.
> RD 69983 and the 13 May 2026 pleading were read from the PDFs today.

## Short answer

- **The duty is real, but its source is not the Act.** It is in the Queensland **Model Litigant Principles**
  (Cabinet directions, revised as at 4 October 2010). Under the "principles of fairness", the State and all
  agencies must conduct themselves as model litigants by "not contesting matters which it accepts as correct,
  in particular by: not requiring a party to prove a matter which the State knows to be true".
- **The same list also requires:**
  - "dealing with claims promptly and not causing unnecessary delay";
  - "keeping the costs of litigation to a minimum";
  - "not seeking to take advantage of an impecunious opponent".
- **Dwyer put it on the record on 7 Aug:** "They have responsibilities as a model litigant… they can't hide stuff
  from you, they can't be sneaky or tricky".
- **Its clearest application here is s 32(1).** Their own Review Unit found all three s 32(1) elements. They have
  served no contrary medical evidence. Their 2026 pleading still puts all three to proof.

## 1. The three s 32(1) elements: found by their own review, put to proof in their pleading

**RD 69983 (24 Oct 2024), verbatim:**

| Element | The review's words |
|---|---|
| Injury | "Having regard to the medical evidence, and in the absence of evidence to the contrary, I am satisfied you sustained a personal injury of a psychological nature." |
| Arising out of employment | "I am satisfied that factors 2, 3 and 4 have been established to an extent. Therefore, to the extent that your psychological injury has arisen out of these factors, I am satisfied that the injury has arisen out of your employment." |
| Employment a significant contributing factor | "Having regard to the medical evidence, I am satisfied your employment was a significant contributing factor to the psychological injury." |

**The review excluded the claim only under s 32(5).**

**The 13 May 2026 pleading, ¶¶5–6:** "the appellant must prove, on the balance of probabilities, that:
(a) the appellant suffered a personal injury that is a psychiatric or psychological disorder; (b) the injury has
arisen out of, or in the course of employment with the employer; (c) employment was a significant contributing
factor to the injury; and (d) …". Their ¶22(f), in the same pleading, speaks of "the appellant's injury".

**What they hold against (a) to (c):**
- no expert;
- no IME;
- no medical witness (24 Sep list: Taylor, Reese, Wright, Earl);
- no medical outline.

Their medical point is causation by stressor, which goes to s 32(5) (did the injury arise out of reasonable
management action?). It does not go to whether there was an injury that employment significantly contributed
to.

**What proof costs you.** Guide p 34: "presenting a medical report on its own cannot be considered without
having the expert witness give evidence orally… and being available for cross-examination". So proving (a) to
(c) means the treating doctors attend, with conduct money and fees, paid by an appellant with no income. This is
exactly where "keeping the costs of litigation to a minimum" and "not seeking to take advantage of an impecunious
opponent" meet "not requiring a party to prove a matter which the State knows to be true".

## 3. What the principle does not require

- **It does not require conceding the contested evaluative questions:**
  - whether management action was reasonable and taken in a reasonable way;
  - whether the injury "arose out of" it (causation by stressor).

  The "principles of firmness" let them "appropriately [test] all claims". The preamble says compensation
  demands "should be carefully scrutinised".
- **"Not intended to be applied rigidly":** the Principles and Guide Part 11 both say so.
- **De novo.** Guide 5.1 lets them decline an element their review accepted. Its words are "may previously have
  accepted… does not mean that this element will be accepted… at the appeal stage". "May not accept" is not
  "should make you prove what they accept".
- **Not enforceable by you.** Reported commentary on *Malone* [2020] FCA 1188 (not read) says they create no
  rights for other parties. Their force comes from the Commission, which can notice conduct in how it manages
  the case, and the Regulator's own institutional standards.

## 4. How it bears on the timing question

"Dealing with claims promptly and not causing unnecessary delay" means the move to 3 Dec is no reason to hold
back on what they already accept.
- The 30 Oct answers are the place to agree s 32(1) and facts 228–231.
- The full concession decision can properly wait for the conference. Agreement of known-true matters should not.

## 5. How to use it without hurting yourself

1. **Do not name the Principles or allege a breach in writing.** Pressure has always hardened them, and Dwyer has
   already said it in their presence. As the 7 Aug prep note put it, it is "for Dwyer to notice, not for Cory to
   say".
2. **Let the questions do the work.** Questions 1 (facts 228–231) and 4 (s 32(1)) of the 19 Oct letter already
   ask for exactly this. The 30 Oct answer is the test.
3. **If s 32(1) is not agreed on 30 Oct, add one neutral agenda line:**
   "s 32(1) (injury; arising out of employment; employment a significant contributing factor): found in RD 69983
   (admitted facts 261–262). The Respondent has served no medical evidence. The Appellant asks the Respondent to
   identify any element it contests and the evidence relied on."
4. **At the conference, the same question, calmly:** "Could the Respondent confirm which elements it actually
   contests, so the Appellant is not put to proof of matters its own review accepted?" If it still declines to
   agree, ask for a direction that any medical evidence the Respondent relies on be served by a fixed date.
   - **Why that direction helps:** with no contrary evidence by that date, a non-admission becomes hard to hold
     before the hearing member.
5. **Realistic expectation.** The likeliest 30 Oct answer on s 32(1) is "de novo; to be discussed at the
   conference" (about 50%). The principle does not compel more in writing. It does make the question very hard to
   dodge at the conference.
