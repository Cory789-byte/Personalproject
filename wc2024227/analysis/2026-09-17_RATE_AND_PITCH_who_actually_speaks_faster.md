When actually producing sound, the two men speak at **the same speed**. The difference is not rate.
It is **continuity**:

| unbroken run (gaps < 0.30 s) | SHEPHERD | DWYER | ratio |

| median | 2.68 s | 5.94 s | 2.2× |
| 90th percentile | 7.66 s | 28.06 s | 3.7× |

Floor time 463 s vs 2,949 s — **6.4 : 1**.

⇒ "Faster" is *more speech per turn, uninterrupted, at higher volume*. Not more syllables per second.

**The shape.**

1. **Open high.** +3.00 st on *"direct calls to this number"* — top decile of his own range. Pitch
   marks the new information.
2. **Flatten to deliver.** Pitch returns to baseline and stays there for the clinical content.
   *"cardiac arrest"* is spoken at **−0.18 st — below his own median.**
3. **Carry it on loudness and speed instead.** +4.8 dB on *"emergency"* and again on *"cardiac
   arrest"*; acceleration through the chain to the 71st and 74th percentiles of his own rate.
4. **Brake on the last word.** *"distress"* alone drops to **2.53 w/voiced-s — his 8th percentile.**
   The single slowest thing in the passage is the final noun.
5. **Stop.** 5.74 s of silence; median Shepherd→Dwyer handover in this hearing is 0.54 s — **10.6×**.

⇒ He raises pitch to *frame*, drops it to *state*, and uses loudness and rate to carry the causal
chain — then brakes to a near-stop on the word that names the harm. No evaluative term is used at
any point. The emphasis is entirely prosodic.

## 5. Limits

Segment-level f0 medians on short segments approximate a single pitch target rather than a contour,
which inflates tail counts for the speaker with shorter segments (Shepherd's median segment is
1.93 s against Dwyer's 2.96 s). Percentile ranks in §4 are against Shepherd's own 208 segments.
