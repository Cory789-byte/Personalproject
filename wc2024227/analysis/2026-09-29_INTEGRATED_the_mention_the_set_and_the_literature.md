## 1. The mention through the literature
- **Form**: managerial judging (Resnik 1982), facilitative not restrictive (Zorza 2004). Dwyer 86% talk share — the pattern Moorhead & Sefton (2005) record where a bench does the missing lawyer's work.
- **56:34 — he built the frame himself.** Double redressive preface ("acting for yourself… in no way at all being critical", ↑+4.2 st) before the face-threatening act (Brown & Levinson). Then, unprompted, the bench *formulates* the case: "a broader grievance… systemically… failings… non-compliance with proper protocol, procedure, legislation" (wide, slow 0.8 w/s, quieter). In institutional talk the speaker's formulation becomes the working version of the case (Heritage & Watson 1979). Hedged ("I may just be speculating" — epistemic downgrade, Heritage & Raymond 2005); conceded ("you may be right about all of that"); self-repaired before the Regulator's officers ("I shouldn't beat up the public sector"); closed with self-deprecation ("easier for someone like me… not get it wrong").
- **His materiality rule, in his words**: non-compliance "happens every single day in every single workplace… it's only a problem if somebody gets sacked or somebody gets killed… it's only then when these things get a light shone on them and they become relevant." This is the admission filter of a coherence-based reasoner (Simon 2004) and matches Benjamin v Sharp (Recusal) [2025] QIRC 54: objectively demonstrable reality; no motive without evidence; consequence over grievance.
- **Neutrality displayed** (Tyler): characterisation "a problem for them, not for you"; "have I placed a burden on you that you're not prepared to accept?" — Matheson: "No."
- **Shift to reading**: 10.4 s and 16 s silences against Jefferson's ~1 s standard maximum — the room stops being about the parties and becomes about the documents.
