## 1. What it does well

1. **Your availability for 3 Dec is on the record.** It notes, factually and without blame, that their Counsel is
   unavailable.
2. **The six matters are on the Commission's file.** Until now Dwyer had seen neither the admissions nor the

3. **It raises the stakes of the 30 Oct answer.** They now know Dwyer will read it. Expect a more careful,

4. **Its themes are Dwyer's own:** efficiency and a path to hearing ("getting this thing to a hearing", 7 Aug).
5. **It is legally sound.** A further conference is discretionary (s 552A), so going to hearing without one is a

## 2. What it risks

1. **Timing and channel.** Matheson asked you to confirm dates to her so she could write to the Commission jointly
   ("I will again email the Commission (cc'ing you in)"). Going to the Registry first breaks the joint front she
   had built ("the parties").
   - "Rather than postponing the conference into 2027" quietly points at them.
   - **Read:** mild, but in the pressure family. That family has always cost goodwill.
2. **The conference is their decision point.** Guide 5.1: "Following the conference, the Respondent may decide to
   review its position or consider conceding". If there is no conference:
   - the principled trigger goes;
   - the default ("defend") runs on to the hearing;
   - a hearing listed by another member may not come any sooner than January plus hearing directions.
   - **The chance of the conference actually being skipped is low** (about 5–10%), so the realised cost is likely
     small.
3. **Directions against their stated preference.** They asked for no orders until the conference. A short report
   direction is modest, and Dwyer may like it. But it is a direction they did not want.
4. **Small slips.**

     was your request. A fair inference, not her words.
   - "(and Schedule, if separate)" reads as a leftover placeholder. The Schedule is inside the 11-page PDF.
   - Neither matters.
