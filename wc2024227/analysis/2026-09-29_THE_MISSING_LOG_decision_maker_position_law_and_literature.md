## 2. Law (verify pinpoints before any filing use)
- Blatch v Archer (1774) 1 Cowp 63 at 65: evidence weighed by what one side could have produced and the other contradicted.
- Jones v Dunkel (1959) 101 CLR 298: unexplained failure to call/produce → inference the evidence would not have assisted; permits more ready acceptance of the opposing account; does not permit inferring content. QIRC not bound by rules of evidence but draws it.
- QPS v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2025] QIRC 203 (O'Neill IC): model-litigant principles applied to a State respondent in a disclosure dispute.
- McCool [2021] QIRC 374 [65]–[67] (Dwyer IC): an evidentiary vacuum does not activate an exclusion; the party relying on it needs material. The Respondent relies on s 32(5)(a); the employer's records would show reasonableness; the employer says they were not kept.
- Form 4 ground 7/9: an un-sworn assertion of non-existence is not a basis on which an objection can be sustained.

## 3. Literature
- Spoliation / missing-evidence effects: decision-makers shift against the party that controlled and failed to produce evidence; larger shift with a thin explanation ("upgraded, not retained" v an admitted spreadsheet for the two days).
- Noticeable absence (Schegloff); Grice: silence where a reply is due is informative — 30:20 "a bit of work to do down there at the bar table"; "MSH is going to need to call evidence"; no reply.
- Verifiable detail as the diagnostic truth cue (Hartwig & Bond 2011; Nahari et al., verifiability approach): a number, a room, two conditions, a consequence; times and bed numbers in the register; v "system upgrade".
- Source credibility (Hovland): first-hand, contemporaneous, against-interest v institutional letter two years later.
- Strategic use of evidence (Hartwig & Granhag), inverted: the operator states the content of the record the institution says it does not hold, in its presence; the institution does not adjust.

## 4. Boundary
- MSH is a non-party: route the inference through the Respondent (relies on the exclusion; chose the witnesses; produced nothing on the emergency-call point) — 9A ¶ 272 and B.6 already do this.
- Tab 31 is the Appellant's own document; keep it pleaded as such.
- Do not plead the inference. Chronology, never motive. ¶ 268 side by side; ¶ 272; C.4. The reader has McCool in his own back catalogue.

**One line:** the employer told the Commission its emergency log did not exist, admitted a spreadsheet did, sat silent while the operator described what the log would show, and the Regulator has put nothing against it. On the law the silence cannot activate the exclusion; on the research it counts against the party that chose it; on his own decisions he has already said so.
