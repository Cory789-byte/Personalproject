## 3. Adversarial passes (written independently)

**Blue (advocate).** A year of admitted conditions: operators locked out of the directory they were responsible for;
emergency calls misrouted, including a MET call; reports unanswered for days; directives "effective from today"; a
manager whose hours were never fixed; a seven-hour break below every standard; a 23-day fatigue response; pay
corrections 25 days after payroll's instruction; leave twice declined, the second "in error". The manager's own role
description made rostering to protocol, emergency-code auditing, OHS and staff communication her duties. The medical
record runs with it: shift-work sleep problems recorded in November 2023, work stress, pay and "lates then earlies"
named at first presentation, no pre-existing factor on the certificates, a psychiatrist's diagnosis. Delaney requires
the whole course; the conditions are not management action; the Respondent has not identified, action by action, what
it says was reasonable. No medical evidence contradicts causation.

**Red (Respondent's counsel).** Causation is the Appellant's burden and he has no report prepared for the proceeding.
His own schedule shows a psychiatric referral renewed on **16 May 2024, the day after the retraction request**, with a
2022 anxiety and ADHD history. Onset 18 June follows the retraction, not the March break. The retraction was a
proportionate response to a team-wide email questioning a manager's attendance, taken on HR advice; the break was a
single human error; pay and leave errors were corrected; he could refuse shifts; the agreement allowed eight hours. The
7:09 pm reply ("misuse of authority") shows his manner. Global s 32(5): the injury arose mainly from reasonable
management action. The Review Unit itself so found.

**Skeptical decision-maker.** The facts are mostly agreed and the Respondent's outlines are thin on 1(a), fatigue and
causation. But I decide causation on medical evidence, and I need a treating psychiatrist who has considered the 2022
history and the timing of May–June 2024 and still attributes the disorder significantly to employment. If Dr
Krishnaiah gives that evidence and survives cross-examination, the appeal is likely made out; s 32(5) then turns on
whether the retraction is the *main* cause, which the whole-of-year record makes hard to sustain. If the doctors do not
attend, I have diagnosis without a tested causation opinion.

**Record auditor.** On the record: the facts, the documents, the diagnosis, the first-presentation notes. Asserted
only: "accusation", "refused", HR advice (no document, no HR witness), the 4:12 pm email, "removed it herself",
"not repeated", "indefinitely". Not yet on the record: a causation opinion tested in evidence; the Appellant's start
date (only "since at least 17 June 2020" and his own "since 2014"); the 17–18 March actual times; page 1 of the
agreement; the process 3270583 date.

**Costs / risk analyst.** Costs of the hearing are discretionary and generally follow the event (WCRA s 558(3); WC
Regulation 2025 s 191: UCPR sch 2 pt 2 scale C, up to 1.5×; witnesses at UCP (Fees) Reg pt 3). Exposure if he loses is
the Respondent's costs of the hearing, not pre-hearing work. His own outlay: medical witnesses' conduct money and
fees, recoverable only if he wins. The Calderbank of 1 July 2026 (rejected 16 July) is relevant to costs if he beats
it. The cheapest path to the same outcome is a concession after a conference that narrows the issues.

## 5. Self-attack (metanoia)

**Too high?** The Review Unit, looking at the same events, applied s 32(5) globally and excluded the claim. The 16 May
referral renewal is a strong timing document for the Respondent and is in the Appellant's own schedule. Treating
doctors can be cross-examined on history they were not told. The 2022 anxiety entry means B2 turns on "a significant"
contribution being shown over a pre-existing vulnerability.

**Too low?** 298 admissions; no Respondent medical evidence; the Respondent's own reviewer accepted injury and
significant contribution; the 16 Nov 2023 shift-work sleep entry places work-related symptoms months before the
retraction; the 16 May referral was a *renewal* "for ongoing care" with no psychotropic medication listed; the
whole-of-year conditions (1(a), fatigue) are outside s 32(5); the Respondent has not identified the action it relies
on; a concession after a narrowing conference is a real path.

**Pre-mortem: "we lost; why?"** (1) Dr Krishnaiah did not give evidence or gave it without addressing the 2022 history
and the May–June timing; (2) the Commission accepted that the injury arose mainly from the retraction episode and that
it was reasonable; (3) the Appellant's manner in the witness box (the 7:09 pm opening) overshadowed the documents;
(4) the case was presented as stressor-by-stressor instead of the whole course, letting the Respondent pick off parts.

**Hidden assumptions:** that the doctors will attend; that no Respondent expert arrives late (the witness list reserves
amendment); that the conference is listed before a hearing; that the Commission applies Delaney as expected.
