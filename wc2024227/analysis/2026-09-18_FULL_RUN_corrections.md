# WC/2024/227 — FULL-RECORDING RUN
## Corrections to the extract-based findings, and the results that survive

The earlier acoustic work was done on two published extracts totalling **9.3 minutes** of the
64.6-minute hearing. The full audio has now been analysed. **Two headline claims are wrong and are
retracted. One is confirmed and is now properly grounded. Two new results are established.**

the busiest stretches (the document exchange at 29–32 min and the closing at 58–64 min).

| the ten quietest seconds of the hearing | |
|---|---|
| −66.3 dBFS | **16:36.25** |
| −66.1 | **33:33.75** |

**None is inside the 5.74 s.** The quietest second of the silence is −65.6 dBFS; the quietest
second elsewhere is **−66.3 dBFS — 0.7 dB quieter.** And **219 of 15,459** windows elsewhere are
quieter than the silence's own loudest second.

## ✅ CONFIRMED, and now properly grounded — no other gap is both long and quiet

This was the §5 framing and it survives on the full data.

**Among the 40 gaps of 2.0 seconds or more, the 5.74 s ranks 1 of 40 by level.**

| the six gaps LONGER than it | length | level |
|---|---|---|
| 25:01.82 | 25.58 s | −54.6 dBFS |
| 25:34.08 | 16.82 s | −50.9 |
| 23:03.04 | 16.02 s | −57.1 |
| 25:58.18 | 15.80 s | −56.5 |
| 22:14.50 | 10.38 s | −55.3 |
| 21:16.48 | 8.38 s | −51.7 |
| **29:39.88** | **5.74 s** | **−63.5** |

Every gap longer than it is **6 to 13 dB noisier** — these are the document-handling pauses around
21–26 minutes, with the Form 29 file being located and passed. Every gap quieter than it is **under
1.4 seconds.** The next quietest gap of 2 s or more is −62.6 dBFS at 56:01.

⇒ The room does reach that floor elsewhere — **but only for half a second at a time.** The
distinguishing property was never depth alone. It is **depth sustained for 5.74 seconds**, and that
is unique in the hearing.

Against the median second of the hearing (−42.2 dBFS) the silence is **23.4 dB down — about 15×
lower sound pressure.**

## ✅ NEW — articulation does not vary with stakes, and this distinguishes the two speakers

A first pass showed an 89% collapse on throwaway turns (p < 0.0001) — which looked like effortful
control switching off. **It is a phonetic confound:** *"Okay"* and *"Yeah"* contain no sibilant, so
there is nothing for the measure to find.

Controlling for it — comparing long turns against short turns **that contain a sibilant**:

| | long turns (≥8 words) | short turns **with** a sibilant | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MR SHEPHERD** | 43.5× (n=68) | **34.7× (n=22)** | **0.36 — no difference** |
| DWYER IC | 74.0× (n=519) | **37.7× (n=27)** | **0.024 — significant drop** |

*(Short turns with no sibilant: Shepherd 4.6×, Dwyer 20.7× — the confound, not a finding.)*

⇒ **The Commissioner relaxes his articulation on throwaway speech. The appellant does not.**

---

## What this says about baseline versus effort

The appellant's articulation is **constant** — statistically flat across 65 minutes (p = 0.41) and
statistically identical between a fifty-word passage and a one-word reply (p = 0.36). The
Commissioner, who has no reason to be managing his diction, is the one who varies.

Constancy is the signature of a **habit**, not of effort: effort is costly, and it characteristically
lapses when the stakes drop or the hour wears on. Neither lapse occurs.

**What the data cannot distinguish** is "never needed to try" from "tried uniformly for 65 minutes
without a single lapse." What it does exclude is the ordinary middle case — concentrating on the
parts that matter and letting the rest go. That pattern is absent.

## ⚠ RETRACTION 3 — "the floor fell 11.6 dB in 250 ms" as evidence of synchronised freezing

**The drop was ordinary.** Measured for all **198** inter-turn gaps of ≥0.60 s:

| measure at 29:39.88 | value | rank of 198 | share of gaps that beat it |
|---|---|---|---|
| total drop, speech → +300–500 ms | 18.7 dB | 56 | 27.8% |
| **room drop, voice tail → +300–500 ms** | **10.2 dB** | **99** | **49.5% — the median** |
| absolute level at +300–500 ms | −63.5 dBFS | 30 | 14.6% |

Every gap drops fast, because voice decay is fast. Once the tail is removed, this gap's onset is
exactly median. **The inference from drop speed to a synchronised orienting freeze is withdrawn.**

### The control: 34:38.94, the gap that dropped harder and did not hold

After the appellant's *"Anyway."* — the deepest instantaneous room-drop in the hearing, **27.3 dB**:

| offset | 34:38.94 | 29:39.88 |
|---|---|---|
| +0.50 s | −63.7 | −64.4 |
| +1.00 s | **−65.7** | −64.9 |
| +1.25 s | −52.1 | −64.6 |
| +1.75 s | **−34.2 — an event at near-speech level** | −65.5 |
| +2.25 s | −64.2 | −65.7 |
| +4.00 s | −64.0 | −64.1 |

Same floor, reached at the same rate. Then at +1.75 s something moved — the Commissioner locating
*"Unilateral destruction of work health and safety"* in the document — and the gap was refilled.

At 29:39.88 nothing moved. **4.88 seconds with no event above the floor.**

### What survives

The distinguishing property of the 5.74 s is **non-resumption**, not speed of onset. In every
other gap in the hearing that reached this floor, the room restarted within one to two seconds —
paper, a chair, the next speaker. This is the only one in which it did not.

The orienting-response reading survives on that basis: the freeze is the *hold*, not the *drop*.
The earlier claim that the 250 ms onset showed everyone stopping in the same instant is not
supported and is withdrawn.

## Retraction 4 (22 Sep 2026): the filled-pause counts

The counts of 2 (Commissioner) and 0 (appellant) filled pauses were artefacts of the default
Whisper pass, which suppresses *um/uh*. A second pass over the full audio (large-v3, word
timestamps, disfluency-preserving prompt, minute-by-minute, window-start insertions excluded)
gives:

| speaker | words | filled pauses | per 1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commissioner | 8,313 | 100 (*um* 69, *uh* 38 raw) | **12.0** |
| appellant | 1,345 | 15 | **11.2** |

Both inside the 6–20 norm and within one per thousand of each other. The "zero filled pauses"
claim in the parataxis, teachability and SRL-literature files is withdrawn. What survives: the
appellant's rate is identical to the bench's, and the bench's rate moves with task.

Commissioner by phase (per 1,000): opening 0–12:38 **18.7**; testing the limbs 12:38–29:17
**12.5**; 29:17–32:02 **4.2** (2 in 471 words); 32:02–53:41 9.5; 53:41–end 11.2. Five-minute
window 25–30: **0 in 393 words**; 30–35: 4.8.
