# How it works — personal notes (29 Sep 2026)
> ⛔ INTERNAL. Personal notes only. Nothing in this file goes into any filed or served document, any letter,
> or anything said at the conference. The pleading never uses these words; they are the Commission's to supply.

## 1. Why the 9A works as a pleading
- The form's order (facts → contentions → questions → orders) is the statute's order (injury → arose out of
  employment, significant contributing factor → s 32(5)(a) → s 32(5)(b)). A Member reads the Act and the pleading
  side by side.
- Every fact cites its admission (298 of 303) or is marked as the Appellant's own evidence or a document. The
  Respondent's answer can only be "admitted" plus an adjective.
- Characterisation is put to the Commission (C.4, C.5, D.3, D.4), not asserted. The intervals are arithmetic on
  admitted dates and carry the argument without arguing it.
- B.5 pleads the negative space: everything the Respondent does not allege, admitted as to the state of its own
  pleading and list. Paragraph 27 identifies no management action → the exclusion has nothing to attach to.
- Each stressor stands alone (own page, own facts incl. shared facts, own sequence, own medical link, own
  statement of the Respondent's answer). The list (Guide 4.8), the summary, the table and the closing medical
  callout make the three one injury from one course of conduct (Langerak via Carr: the injury as a whole).
- Length is the chronology of a documentary case over eleven months; "concise" in the Guide is about argument
  and medical extracts, not admitted facts. One sentence if asked: the injury arose over eleven months in a
  documented workplace, every material event is in writing and admitted, and the pleading sets out the record so
  the Commission can decide on it rather than on characterisation.

## 3. The adjectives a Member could use, with the fact that earns each (and why not a stronger word)
1. **Critical** — the Respondent's own word (¶ 289). The standard everything else is measured against.
2. **Unremedied** — access removed 18 Jul 2023, no restoration alleged before 18 Jun 2024 (¶ 55). States the omission, not a reason.
3. **Unconsulted** — "effective from today" (¶ 51); no consultation document listed (¶ 273); no ballot/cl 6.2 agreement (¶¶ 180–181).
4. **Unattended** — nine misdirected calls; 5 d 18 h 14 min to any communication (¶¶ 56–69). Not "ignored": that asserts a state of mind.
5. **Unsafe** — once only, tied to ¶ 289 and the MET team unable to find the emergency (¶ 58). The employer's own standard applied.
6. **Dilatory** — the Respiratory Service: five days; reply two hours after stated hours; no amendment (¶¶ 89–110).
7. **Undefined** — office hours, 23 Aug 2023 to 17 May 2024 (¶ 73).
8. **Disproportionate** — 1:15 pm request; 6:23 pm retract; hours published two days later (¶¶ 74–84). Anchored in the publication, not motive.
9. **Protracted** — 3 May direction → 13 May unactioned → 21 May awaited → 28 May lodged → "Part Completed"; 33 / 425 / 509 days.
10. **Inaccurate** — "remedied in a timely manner"; "no outstanding underpayments" (¶¶ 207–208) against the July 2025 payslip.
11. **Careless** — two declines on attachments admitted present (¶¶ 116, 118, 134); the employer's own "human error" (¶ 135).
12. **Non-compliant** — 7 h against 10 h (¶ 285); 8 h agreement swaps-only (¶ 225); no swap alleged (¶ 234).
13. **Unreasonable** — the Regulator's own reviewer's word (¶ 260). Adopted, not added to. The word that carries the decision.
14. **Absent** — fatigue assessment, training and records: "do not exist" (¶¶ 263–265, 269–271). Not "inadequate": nothing was there.
15. **Systemic** — the omission is the employer's, across a function, over a period; its own guideline treats every HHS employee as a worker (¶¶ 219, 222–223, 264).
16. **Unresponsive** — 8 Apr enquiry answered 1 May with a refusal; nothing between (¶¶ 249–250).
17. **Unparticularised** — paragraph 27 identifies no management action (¶ 303).

Words a Member would not use, and why: hostile / vindictive / retaliatory / deliberate (assert purpose; the record proves
timing and omission; a purpose finding invites a losing appeal); negligent (different cause of action); toxic (not a finding).

## 4. The verdict the record invites
Injury: MDD with anxious distress, diagnosed 24 Oct 2024, date of injury 18 Jun 2024; arose out of employment;
employment a significant contributing factor (contemporaneous notes; the Regulator's own finding; no contrary expert).
Several circumstances are conditions of the work, not management action. Of the management action: the rostering of
17–18 March not reasonable; pay corrections not reasonably taken; April process changes not reasonably taken for want of
consultation; pandemic-leave decisions not reasonably taken. Some management action reasonable, and said so. Weighing the
whole: s 32(5)(a) does not exclude the injury. s 32(5)(b) has no application: admitted events, not perception. Appeal allowed.

## 5. Why a dismissal would be uncomfortable on this record
Route 1 (no injury / no causation): no evidence to write it from; against the only medical evidence.
Route 2 (RMA covering the whole injury) requires published findings: that 17–18 March was reasonable, against the
Regulator's own reviewer and its own witnesses' "human error"; identifying management action the Respondent never
identified; treating the directory, the misdirected calls and the five days as management action reasonably taken in a
function admitted to be critical to patient safety; that no fatigue system in a 24/7 emergency function was reasonable;
that 425 and 509 days were "timely"; and that all of it covers the whole injury when the employer admits nothing changed.
Every one is a finding against an admission or a document. Appellate exposure: conditions treated as action; s 32(5)(a)
applied without identified action; failure to weigh the whole (Langerak via Carr). Decisions are published (Guide 8.1;
s 580 IR Act suppression aside).

## 6. Where the pressure sits, and where it does not
- Case manager: not on the result (he will not hear it). On efficiency, his stated overriding consideration. Guide 5.1:
  the Respondent "may decide to review its position or consider conceding" at the second conference. His one question:
  what management action does the Regulator rely on.
- Hearing Member: none. A duty to write reasons that are correct on the record and survive the Industrial Court.
- The Commission as an institution: publication and the model-litigant principles press the Regulator, which appears
  before the same Members next month and would be named in a published decision explaining why it defended, to hearing,
  a case its own reviewer had partly decided against it.
- ⛔ Never say or imply any of this in a letter or at the conference. Saying the Commission is under pressure converts a
  record-based case into a perceived one (Benjamin: "loss of objectivity"). The 9A puts the record on the page and asks
  six questions. Let the case manager ask the seventh.

## 7. The strongest stressor, alone and together
Alone: Stressor 3 (~.65) — the reviewer's own finding; every element admitted. Stressor 1 (~.45) — the widest, the case
manager's "one area", the conditions-of-work contention. Stressor 2 (~.33) — the cleanest chronology, the weakest law
(pay is management action; "corrected" is the s 32(5)(a) answer, beaten on the way it was done). Together (~.72–.74 at
hearing): one injury, one course, same manager, same period, same single channel; the Respondent must cover the whole
with one word. Lead with 3, then 1, then 2, and say once that they are one injury from one course of conduct.

## Correction (29 Sep 2026): "floundering" was not impatience

Earlier reading in this session: 56:34 as judicial impatience (Trinder et al. 2014 register). Withdrawn on re-reading the canonical transcript.

What the record shows at 56:34–58:20: double redressive preface ("I understand you're acting for yourself… in no way at all being critical", ↑+4.2 st); then, unprompted, the bench formulates the systemic case itself ("You, I think, have as a broader grievance a view that… systemically, the place in which you worked had a number of failings… non-compliance with proper protocol, procedure, legislation" — wide, slow 0.8 w/s, quieter); hedges it ("I may just be speculating"); concedes it ("you may be right about all of that"); self-repairs before the Regulator's officers ("I shouldn't beat up the public sector"); closes with self-deprecation ("easier for someone like me to understand it and not get it wrong").

Literature fit: face-work / redressive action (Brown & Levinson); formulation by the institutional speaker (Heritage & Watson 1979) — the bench's restatement becomes the working version of the case; epistemic downgrade (Heritage & Raymond 2005). Facilitative, not restrictive (Zorza).

The materiality rule he stated, in his own words: non-compliance "happens every single day in every single workplace… it's only a problem if somebody gets sacked or somebody gets killed… it's only then when these things get a light shone on them and they become relevant." On the file as at 30 Sep 2026: B.3.1(b) records the 8 Oct 2024 termination while certified unfit; Stressor 3 is the seven-hour break in the function that receives and pages MET/Code Blue calls; Stressor 1 has an emergency call misdirected. Both limbs of his own test are pleaded as chronology, never named. Not engineered; a consequence of the stressors-only design he asked for.
