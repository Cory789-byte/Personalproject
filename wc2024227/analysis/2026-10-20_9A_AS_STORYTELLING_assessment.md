## Verdict

**Yes, it is good storytelling of the right kind for this forum.** It tells the story through dates, documents,
intervals and the other side's own words, not adjectives. That is why it is hard to plead against, and why the
outlines did not answer it.

**Its costs:** length, three small places where the story outran the record, and an ending that asks the
Respondent's question.

## Why it works

1. **The spine is on page 1.** Part A "In summary" is the whole case in one paragraph:
   - a 24/7 patient-safety function carried on while database access was removed and not restored;
   - changes "effective the day they were notified";
   - misdirected calls;
   - pay wrong in four fortnights;
   - a 7-hour break;
   - 23 days for a fatigue answer;
   - their own review's "unreasonable management action".

   In short: responsibility kept, means withheld.
2. **It says how it will tell the story:** "each such document is pleaded as the step taken, on the date and in
   the terms recorded, rather than characterised". Chronology, never motive.
3. **The intervals (B.4) are arithmetic as narrative:**
   - 5 days 18 h 14 min to answer the MASPER Registrar;
   - 23 days for the fatigue answer;
   - 425 days to the last pay correction;
   - 13 min 35 s to approve leave on the third try, after ten days and two declines.
4. **The silences are built in (B.5):** "Matters the Respondent does not allege".
5. **The credibility moves:**
   - the onus is accepted, and the April contention withdrawn (C1);
   - "No aggravation is claimed";
   - the medical records are "not relied on as a finding of how the injury was caused";
   - the Nov 2023 prior-health record is put candidly.
6. **Their witnesses become narrators (C5A):** "On the Respondent's own outlines…".
7. **The medical thread is told without overclaiming:** shift-work sleep in Nov 2023; the referral the day after
   the retraction request; first presentations naming roster and pay.

## What it costs (verified 20 Oct)

1. **Length:** 30 pages, against Dwyer's "keep it short and sweet… a simple message is always the best…
   Fewer documents means simpler case" (7 Aug, 10:12 and 61:17). The story is in page 1 and B.4. The rest is
   the evidence base.
2. **Where the story outran the record.** Each slip is small, and each invites "the pleading overstates the
   record":
   - **"When the 17 March shift ended at 23:00 he had been awake and working for about sixteen hours."**
     Awake, yes; working, eight. The pair-of-shifts sentence ("about sixteen hours of work, on two eight-hour
     shifts") is accurate.
   - **"…a MET call at 14:38 on the shift of 18 March".** That shift ended at 14:00. The code list itself is
     correct: "Six of those fell between 06:00 and 14:00".
   - **C7: "Langerak [2020] ICQ 2 at [86], as applied in Carr v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2022] QIRC 59
     at [60]".** That decision was set aside in *WCR v Carr* [2023] ICQ 1 (Merrell DP, 10 Jan 2023;
     queenslandjudgments.com.au/caselaw/icq/2023/1). Cite Langerak [86] and Mahaffey [57] directly.
3. **The ending.** Part D Q6 asks the Commission to decide s 32(5)(b) (perception).
   - The Regulator recites (b) in its legal framework (SOFC ¶6(d)(ii)) but pleads no facts for it, and Dwyer
     named only (a) on 7 Aug.
   - C5B already answers it in one sentence.
   - **Consider dropping Q6 in the completed version and keeping 5B** (interlock: never open the (b) door).
     This is a judgement call to take with Cory.
