## 3. Does the record show he wasn't "the difficult employee"?

**The legal question is different:** did the injury arise out of reasonable management action taken in a
reasonable way? But the record now answers the personal question in the employer's own words.

### In his favour (admitted; their words)

**How he raised things:**
- in writing, to managers, HR and payroll;
- on 31 Aug 2023 he recorded his intention to become union delegate "in the same sentence as his willingness
  to take on additional night shifts".

**How long he waited:**
- 23 days for an answer on fatigue after a 7-hour break;
- 50 days from asking for a pay review (8 Apr 2024) to a correction still recorded as "Part Completed".

**Taylor:**
- 7 Aug 2023: "My sincere apologises about your rostered Monday 7th 0700-1500 shift, I can confirm this was an
  oversight."
- 6 Jun 2023: "I did raise my voice and asked him to please stop talking over the top of me."

**Reese:**
- "I acknowledge there has been a few rostering errors made by Chloe with regards to Cory's line in past
  rosters."
- 10 May 2024: "Cory's roster will not be considered, however Cory is not yet aware of this".
- 21 May 2024: "With regards to your concerns about having more clarity as to what are Chole's business hours
  I will follow up on the issues raised". That came six days after her 15 May email saying his email "did not
  comply with our Code of conduct".

**The review and the pleading:**
- RD 69983: the roster was "unreasonable management action".
- Their pleading never calls the roster reasonable.

### Where the "difficult" picture survives (honestly)

Two episodes in their outlines:
1. **6 Jun 2023, the Communication Book.** Taylor says he questioned her "closely and loudly". Her own email
   that day says she raised her voice.
2. **15 May 2024, his email to the department about Taylor's hours.** Reese calls it an accusation sent without
   a chance to respond.

They are their best remaining points if it is heard. They are also limited:
- they are two moments in fourteen months;
- the first comes with her own admission;
- the second drew Reese's own "I will follow up on the issues raised";
- the treating records name roster and pay, not these exchanges.

The employment track has its own documents (for example the September 2025 attendance letter) that this
record does not answer.

### Verdict

No record proves anyone was never difficult. This one does something more useful. It shows, in their own
documents, that:
- the problems he raised were real;
- they were acknowledged at the time;
- the employer's people now call the key events mistakes.

That replaces "difficult employee" with "an employee who raised real problems in writing and waited a long
time for answers". The Regulator has stopped arguing otherwise.

**Discipline.** Never say "I wasn't difficult" at the conference. Let the documents say it, and never
characterise motive.
