# Most likely thesis: the Regulator's position after 12–13 October 2026

> Internal analysis. Not for filing or correspondence. Mention-transcript references are internal
> only. All probabilities are judgement, not measurement.

## Thesis

The Regulator is deciding whether to keep defending the appeal, and that decision is waiting on
Counsel.

The 1 Oct package took the file beyond what the appeals team can answer on its own:
- a pleading built on the Regulator's own admissions;
- set against its own witnesses calling the key events errors;
- no medical evidence on its side;
- para 27 admitted to identify no management action (fact 303).

For the first time in the case, the Regulator did not restate that it will defend. Instead it:
- cleared the documents it could not contest;
- agreed to the conference;
- asked to keep everything else out of writing until Counsel advises.

The Commissioner read it the same way. He paused the matter rather than programming it, and put the
clock on the Regulator. **Most likely result: a concession, or a narrowing that leads to one, in
Nov–Dec 2026.**

### The real intention

On 7 Aug the Commissioner left the Regulator a choice: "They may wish to contradict that. They may
not want to contradict that" [28:12]. The Regulator is keeping that choice open until Counsel decides
it, while doing everything else he asked, so that the delay reads as model-litigant cooperation and
not avoidance.

They cannot contradict:
- the case rests on their admissions;
- their witnesses call the key events errors;
- they have no medical evidence.

They will not concede on paper before Counsel advises. **So the choice has been moved off paper and
into a room.** The intention is a controlled exit (concession or a narrow remainder) on their terms,
without an order recording that they were made to.

**The dilemma (Cory's point).** For the pay and call-routing items, either label loses:
- "management action" requires reasonableness, against admitted errors and delays;
- "not management action" means s 32(5)(a) cannot reach it.

The fatigue and the pay shortfall are by-products of management decisions (roster, AVAC lodgement).
Linking them to management action only brings in reasonableness, where their own witnesses call the
source events errors. Carr [60], per this repo (pinpoint to be confirmed): the question is whether
*the injury* arose out of reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way.

**Cost of the 9C.**
- 23 causal particulars, plus context, each admitted and then argued.
- The last amended 9C took five weeks (sought in April, filed 13 May). With Counsel back on 22 Oct,
  this one runs into December, then amended outlines, then a conference: the hearing goes to 2027 on
  a matter on foot since late 2024.
- The result would be a signed document that, read honestly, mostly concedes.

**Why the Commissioner let them skip it.** "The efficient administration of justice, which is an
overriding consideration that I've got my eye on" [00:00, 7 Aug]. Ordering the 9C now buys months of
pleading that may be pointless. The conference decides whether a 9C is ever written. The alternative
to writing one is to concede.

**Real intention, sharpened.**
- The request is a request not to write their 9C, because an honest one mostly concedes.
- Direction 5 is a mini-9C (the same three questions per particular). They asked not to answer even
  that in writing.
- At the conference, "what do you contradict, and with what?" is direction 5 asked aloud.

**Action.** Organise the one-page conference table by these four groups. Hold it; produce it only if
asked.

### An honest 9C would mirror the 9A's facts (added 14 Oct, Cory's point)

**The facts layer would be the 9A.** Every causal particular is a dated event tied to an admitted
fact. It cannot be denied without leave (r 49(3)), and restating it changes nothing. The 9C's facts
section would be the 9A with "admits" in front.

**What the 9C could add:**
1. **Labels:** management action; reasonable; human error; not intentional or repeated; operational.
   This is almost all of its original content, and each label needs evidence. The outlines concede
   error on the central events.
2. **A thin layer of its own facts,** only where the four witnesses go beyond the 9A:
   - Taylor on 6 Jun 2023;
   - Reese's eight-month review;
   - the HR advice behind the retraction;
   - "could refuse shifts"; the 2020 agreement "continues to apply";
   - pay "corrected"; cl 18.10.

   Mostly around 1(l) and Stressor 3.
3. **A causation contention:** the Oct 2022 anxiety entry; employment not a significant factor. No
   expert.

**Side by side, the documents show the dispute is labels only.** That is what guide 5.1 has the
conference do ("review, refine or narrow down the issues"; "whether any elements of s 32 … can be
agreed"). The facts are already agreed. What remains is whether their labels survive their own
evidence. On 7 Aug [28:43] the Commissioner said a characterisation "may be contradicted"; here it is
the Regulator's characterisation that must stand up.

**Nuance.** The 9A pleads contentions (injury; arising out of employment; s 32(5) does not exclude),
as any 9A must. The particulars are facts; the contest lives in the contentions, which is where their
9C would push back.

**Why they will not write it.** It would show, in their own hand, that the facts are the Appellant's
and that their defence is labels they cannot evidence on most particulars. Direction 5 asks for
exactly that in miniature.
