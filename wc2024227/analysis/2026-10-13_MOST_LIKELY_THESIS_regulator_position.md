# Most likely thesis: the Regulator's position after 12–13 October 2026

> Internal analysis. Not for filing or correspondence. Mention-transcript references are internal
> only. All probabilities are judgement, not measurement.

## Thesis

The Regulator is deciding whether to keep defending the appeal, and that decision is waiting on
Counsel.

The 1 Oct package took the file beyond what the appeals team can answer on its own:
- a pleading built on the Regulator's own admissions;
- set against its own witnesses calling the key events errors;
- no medical evidence on its side;
- para 27 admitted to identify no management action (fact 303).

For the first time in the case, the Regulator did not restate that it will defend. Instead it:
- cleared the documents it could not contest;
- agreed to the conference;
- asked to keep everything else out of writing until Counsel advises.

The Commissioner read it the same way. He paused the matter rather than programming it, and put the
clock on the Regulator. **Most likely result: a concession, or a narrowing that leads to one, in
Nov–Dec 2026.**

## Evidence chain

1. **The "defend" line has gone.**
   - 18 Feb: "we will be defending the appeal" (Calderbank #1, next day).
   - 16 Jul: "The Respondent's position remains to defend the appeal" (Calderbank #2, after 15 days).
   - 7 Oct: "looking over both emails and their contents".
   - 12 Oct: no defence statement.

   Restating it costs nothing, so its omission is the strongest single signal.
2. **Counsel is back.** Willson appeared on 27 Feb, 13 Mar and 7 Apr (the pleading fight), then
   stood down (no Counsel on 22 May or 7 Aug). The 1 Oct package reopened the brief.
3. **Their own evidence concedes the core** (outlines, 24 Sep):
   - Taylor: the seven-hour break was "the result of human error, and was not intentional or
     repeated";
   - payroll's Technical Support advice: "better characterised as a rostering practice issue for the
     line manager";
   - payroll: the second SPL decline "appears to have been made in error".

   The defence of the break is the atomisation clamp, which *Delaney* answers. They have no medical
   evidence. RD 69983 itself found a psychological injury arising out of employment (admitted as to
   wording; truth reserved, 8 Sep).
4. **The factual contest is over.**
   - 298/303 facts admitted; 38/39 documents confirmed.
   - Facts 228–231 now rest on the Regulator's own disclosed Tab 31.
5. **MSH is in the loop.**
   - Documents prepared at MSH 24–28 Sep (metadata); "following up with MSH" (7 Oct); disclosed
     12 Oct.
   - All four witnesses are MSH staff.
   - Ordinary consultation; recorded neutrally (rule 1).
6. **They asked for nothing:** no expert, no extension, no particulars, no opposition to leave.
7. **The Commissioner paused the matter.**
   - No leave on the papers, no repleading order, no hearing programme, no criticism of the 9A.
   - One step: Counsel's availability "as soon as possible following 22 October 2026", then advise
     the Appellant.

## The other thesis

Time is being bought for Counsel to build the defence around the Appellant's weakest point:
- he carries the onus;
- he has no medico-legal report;
- causation could be contested by cross-examining the treating practitioners.

This is real, and it caps the concession estimate. But a party building that case asks for
something (medical particulars, time for its own expert, a hearing date), and none was asked for.

## Tells

  - no "defend" line in any communication;
  - a prompt approach after 22 Oct that goes beyond dates;
  - Tab 32A admitted;
  - at the conference, para 27 not maintained and s 32 elements agreed;
  - any mention of the form of orders or costs.
- **Toward contest:**

  - leave sought for expert evidence;
  - inquiries into the Appellant's medical evidence;
  - opposition to leave;
  - para 27 particularised.
