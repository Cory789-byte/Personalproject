## 2. What the two clinical terms are (background; not evidence)

**Cardiac arrest** — cessation of effective cardiac output. Consciousness is lost in ~10–20 s. The
brain holds no oxygen reserve; hypoxic neuronal injury begins at roughly 4–6 minutes and compounds
with each further minute. In hospital this is a **Code Blue**, and per Affidavit ¶12 the response is
**approximately ten clinicians paged simultaneously**.

**Respiratory distress** — increased and failing work of breathing (rising rate, accessory muscle
use, falling saturations, rising CO₂). It is the most common physiological precursor to in-hospital
cardiac arrest. The **MET (Medical Emergency Team) call** exists precisely to reach a deteriorating
patient in the window **before** arrest.

⇒ **These are not two items on a list. They are two points on one timeline, and the MET call is the
intervention designed to prevent the first becoming the second.** An arrest reached through hypoxia
carries materially worse outcomes than a primary shockable arrest — both the window and the quality
of outcome are lost.

⇒ **The Switchboard is the hinge.** It is the single point converting the trigger into the response
(Affidavit ¶6; Role Description; **Form 24 ¶8 admitted**). A routing failure there is not a slow
workflow — it is the removal of the intervention the rapid-response system exists to deliver.

## 4. What "bottlenecked emergency workflows" loses

1. **Wrong failure mode.** "Bottleneck" is a throughput metaphor (queueing, slowness). The pleaded
   defect is a **routing** failure — fast, to the wrong destination. Different defect, different
   consequence; the chosen word names the wrong one.
2. **No clock.** Nothing on its face conveys that the unit of delay is minutes and the unit of
   consequence is neurological injury.
3. **No person.** "Workflows" is a process noun. A workflow can be suboptimal with nobody harmed.
4. **No actor.** Agentless — the directive disappears from the sentence.
5. **It invites the answer that was in fact given.** [27:22]: *"Managers make decisions without
   consulting staff all the time. It's totally legitimate."* That answer is complete against
   "bottlenecked emergency workflows." It is **not** available against "the page does not reach the
   arrest." The appellant began the point at [27:28] — *"But I think when it's in a patient safety
   environment that they—"* — was cut off at [27:32], and made it in full at [29:17].

## 5. Evaluation

**What the spoken version supplied.** The missing premise of the s 32(5)(a) evaluation. The question
is not whether a manager may direct without consulting — she may. It is whether **this** action,
taken **this** way, was reasonable, which is answered by what the altered system is for.
"Bottlenecked workflows" renders the alteration ordinary; the dispatch chain renders it a change to
a life-safety configuration made without assessment.

**What it cost the bench.** The disposal route offered at [27:52]–[28:04] — *the existence of the
emails is not in dispute, so the documents are unnecessary* — works on a **characterisation**
dispute. It does not reach a **systems** fact. The fifty words re-founded Stressor 1(a) on ground the
exit did not cover. Hence the 5.7-second pause, the pricing rather than refusal at [29:45]–[32:00],
and the concession at [62:30] naming the unassessed directives as *"the one one area… that there
might be something to look at."*

**What it is NOT.** It is **not** a case requiring proof of patient harm. No patient outcome needs
to be proved and none is pleaded. The criticality of the function is **already admitted** (Form 24
¶8) and stated in the employer's own Role Description — *"strictly adhering to protocols and
timeframes."* The chain is an **explanation of why the management action was unreasonable**, not a
new case in need of evidence.

⚠ **Scope discipline.** If the chain becomes *"the hospital was dangerous,"* it is the broader
grievance warned against at [57:00] and it is ***Delaney*** territory — composite course, the
Respondent's frame. **The case is *Mahaffey*: one mechanism, one admitted instance.** The chain is
threaded to Stressor 1(a) and Stressor 3, and no further.

## 6. Risks

1. **"Wrong side of the room" does not map.** It is capable of meaning the page went to the wrong
   destination, or something about the Switchboard console. **Affidavit ¶29 is the precise form** —
   *"directed to the wrong or an unavailable clinician."* That is the sentence for the witness box;
   the room phrase invites cross-examination for no gain.

2. **Joint 5 cannot be proved from the appellant's own records.** Affidavit ¶9: his monthly
   statistics tally **volume only**. Which clinician was paged, acknowledgement, misdirection and
   timestamps are SPOK system-level data held only by MSH — and MSH's answer to Items 1–2 is that
   *"records predating that upgrade were not retained."*
   ⇒ **The chain described at 29:17 is the chain Item 1 exists to prove, and MSH's answer to Item 1
   is that the record is gone.** This is the produce-or-swear target: Schedule C requires the
   upgrade's date and nature, whether pre-upgrade records were migrated/archived/destroyed, and the
   identity, author, custodian and source system of the *"spreadsheet of recorded MET calls"*.

3. **Two characterisations in the pleading raise the appellant's own bar.** *"Dereliction"*
   (Stressor 1(a)) and the Stressor 1 heading *"reprisal / suppression"* characterise persons.
   *Briginshaw* lifts the standard of persuasion for serious allegations, and characterisation
   language edges toward **s 32(5)(b)** — the perception limb the Regulator has **not** pleaded.
   The dated sequence yields the inference without them: the 18 July 2023 database-access email, the
   9 May 2024 "switched off" email, and the admitted ¶8 criticality.

1. **He read the PID's subject matter aloud himself — "clinical risks."** That is the same subject

2. **He read the ESU determination aloud — "formally determined."** Not alleged. Validated.
