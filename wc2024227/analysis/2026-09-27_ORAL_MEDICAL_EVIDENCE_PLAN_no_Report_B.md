# Oral medical evidence plan: no Report B; Dr Hawes and Dr Krishnaiah by telephone

**INTERNAL. 27 Sep 2026. Not for service.**

**The decision.** No medico-legal report will be prepared. If the matter goes to hearing, the treating GP (Dr Peter
Hawes) and the treating psychiatrist (Dr Ravikumar Bangalore Krishnaiah) give their opinions orally, by telephone or
video, and are cross-examined. Both are already on the witness list filed 9 September 2026 (items 2 and 3, described
as expert witnesses under Guide 4.11), and the Schedule of Medical Documents served the same day says in terms: "No
report has been prepared for the purposes of this proceeding… the Appellant does not rely upon that opinion otherwise
than through the oral evidence of its author."

**Is that a worse position than Report B?** Not much, and in two ways it is better.
- The Guide requires oral evidence anyway: "presenting a medical report on its own cannot be considered without having
  the expert witness give evidence orally… and being available for cross-examination" (Guide 7.6.5). A report without
  the doctor is inert; the doctor without a report is not.
- The Regulator has no expert either. Both sides' medical case is treating records plus oral evidence. On Dwyer's test
  ([14:49], [28:56]) uncontradicted treating opinion is accepted.
- The exposure a report would have created (a fresh document written for litigation, attacked as advocacy) does not
  arise. What is left is the treating record as it stands, which the Regulator obtained itself under its Form 29.

**What it costs.** Everything now turns on two telephone calls that last perhaps 40 minutes each, from doctors who have
not read the admitted facts. The whole of s 32(1) is decided in those calls. The preparation below is not optional.

## 1. Mechanics

| Step | Rule / Guide | When | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notice of the opinion evidence | Guide 4.10 (no ambush); 9 Sep witness list and schedule; new **9A para 1.11** | Done / with the 9A | Para 1.11 states the four topics each doctor will address. That is the notice a report would otherwise give |
| Attendance notices | Rules r 58; Guide 6.2.2, 7.6.4 (Form 32 + 32A/B/C) | After hearing dates are set | Apply promptly. Even a willing doctor should be under notice: it fixes the time and protects him with his practice |
| Telephone / video | Guide 7.6.5: experts may appear by phone or video "if the Commission allows" | Same application as the attendance notices | Ask for a fixed time slot for each (e.g. Dr Hawes 9:30 am, Dr Krishnaiah 2:00 pm). Ask that each may have his records and the bundle in front of him |
| Expenses | Guide 7.6.5 (calling party pays); Guide 10.1; r 62 (per HEARING-PLAN) | Tender before the date | Agree a fee with each practice in writing now. Unpaid expenses give a witness a lawful reason not to attend |
| What the doctors receive beforehand | Disclosed by letter to Matheson at the same time | ≥ 21 days before | See §2. Send the identical pack to both, copy the covering letter to the Regulator. This is the substitute for a letter of instruction, and it is what makes the oral opinion admissible as an informed one |
| Order of witnesses | Guide 7.6 | Hearing plan | Doctors after the Appellant and before the Regulator's witnesses, so their evidence sits on a proved factual base |

## 3. Dr Hawes (GP): examination-in-chief

He proves: first presentation, the contemporaneous history, no prior depressive illness, certification, and the referral. He is a fact witness with a clinical overlay. Keep him there.

Non-leading questions, in order:
1. Your role at Our Medical Ashmore; how long Mr Shepherd was a patient; who else in the practice saw him.
2. Take the record of 16 November 2023 (Dr Nanayakkara). What does it record about sleep, shift work, and mental state? What was prescribed and why?
3. Between January 2023 and June 2024, does the practice record any diagnosis of, or treatment for, depression? Any antidepressant or other psychotropic medication? (M1.)
4. The 16 May 2024 referral (Dr Zhao): what does it record as past history, and what medications were current? What does "26/10/2022 Anxiety" in a history list mean clinically, and what does it not mean?
5. 28 June 2024 (Dr Slawinski): what was recorded as the reason for the visit and the history?
6. 1 July 2024, your consultation: what did he tell you? What did you record? (Let him read "they withhold pay at times, no overtime- not processed, manipulate his roster- so he works lates then earlies".)
7. What did you diagnose on 1 July 2024, and on what basis?
8. What did you certify, and for how long, across 1 July to 6 October 2024? Why did you refer to a psychiatrist?
9. In your certificates you recorded no pre-existing factor or condition. Why?
10. Having now read the admitted facts at tabs [list], does anything in them change the view you held in July 2024? In your opinion, was his employment a significant contributing factor to the condition you diagnosed?
11. Are you aware of any other cause, in or out of work, that would explain the presentation of June–July 2024?

⛔ He must not: give a psychiatric diagnosis beyond his own (anxiety/stress; the MDD is Krishnaiah's); characterise management ("victimising"); speak to patient harm at the hospital. If asked about the certificate wording "ongoing breaking of workplace rules by bosses, victimizing him", his answer is that it records what the patient reported, in the patient's words.

He proves: the diagnosis, its onset relative to the history, the mechanism, and the answer to the non-work factors. He has already said he "will address whatever is put to him".

1. Qualifications; the Mind and Memory Service; when you first saw Mr Shepherd (24 Oct 2024) and on whose referral.
2. What did you find on 24 October 2024? What did you write that day at 11:45 am? What treatment did you start and why? (M3.)

5. Take the admitted facts at tabs 284–285 and 260 (the seven-hour break), 242–250 (the 23-day refusal), 264 and 269–271 (no fatigue system), 40 and 55 (database), 49–53 and 273 (directives without consultation), 74–82 (the office-hours email and the request to retract), 185–203 (pay). Are these the kinds of matters he described to you? Do they fit the clinical picture you found?
6. In your opinion, was his employment a significant contributing factor to the Major Depressive Disorder? What in the history and the presentation leads you to that view?
7. The date of injury certified by the GP is 18 June 2024. The roster event was 17–18 March 2024. How, clinically, does a shift-work and roster stressor in March relate to a depressive presentation in June? (The answer sought: a continuing exposure with a cumulative effect, not a single event; the record of "lates then earlies"; the sleep entry of Nov 2023.)
8. Your report records "relationship breakdown, job loss, and bereavement" as life stressors. When were those recorded, relative to June 2024? Were they causes of the disorder you diagnosed, or matters affecting its course and treatment afterwards? Did you, at any time, form the view that the disorder was caused by something other than work?
9. Your report refers to "unfair dismissal proceedings" and a "former employer". What was your source for that? Does anything about the employment relationship in late 2024 alter your opinion on what caused the condition that presented in June 2024?
10. The GP records list "26/10/2022 Anxiety" and "ADHD" as history items. What is the significance, if any, of those entries for a diagnosis of Major Depressive Disorder in 2024? Is there any record of treatment for either before June 2024?
11. Your description of him as "detail-oriented, perfectionistic… consumed by issues he perceives as unjust". Is that a diagnosis? Does it mean the workplace matters were not a cause of the disorder?
12. Your report says "premature exposure to the workplace is more likely result in significant deterioration". What does that tell the Commission about the relationship between the workplace and the condition?
13. Prognosis and current treatment, briefly.

⛔ He must not: use "moral injury" or "learned helplessness" as labels; adopt "victimised" or "bullied"; say anything about the litigation being the barrier to recovery unless asked, and if asked, keep it to prognosis, not cause. He must be told before the day that the appeal is about **cause in June 2024**, and that everything after 24 October 2024 goes to prognosis only.
