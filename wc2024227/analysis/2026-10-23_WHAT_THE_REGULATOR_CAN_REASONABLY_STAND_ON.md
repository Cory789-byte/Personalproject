## 3. What they can reasonably stand on, strongest first

### A. Medical causation by stressor: their best ground (moderate to strong)

**Why it is legitimate:**
- **The onus is yours** (¶5).
- ***WCR v Carr* [2023] ICQ 1** requires evidence linking a stressor to the injury.
- **Their own review cited *Rowe v Q-COMP*:** "an injured worker is not always best placed to identify the cause
  of a psychological injury and that factors identified by an injured worker may not be found upon investigation
  by health professionals to be real or actual factors contributing to the injury".
- **Guide p 34:** "presenting a medical report on its own cannot be considered without having the expert witness
  give evidence orally… and being available for cross-examination".

**How they would run it:**
- **The gap in time.** The roster event was 17–18 March 2024. The injury came in June 2024, three months later.
  The May 2024 events (factor 2) and the April–May pay problems (factor 3) sit closest to onset.
- **The first certificate** puts the cause in your own terms, how you said managers treated you, rather than in
  specific events. That lets them argue *Rowe*, and hints at perception (s 32(5)(b)).
- **The GP's later attribution** (2 Sep 2024 call: rules, the complaint response, pay, the roster) came after the
  claim was made, and came from your history.
- **No expert report has been served** (the 28 Aug "forthcoming expert report" never followed).

**Your answer:**
- Their own review found factor 4 among the factors "medically causative" and the injury arising out of factors
  2, 3 and 4, "Having regard to the medical evidence".
- The errors cluster in one window (the leave decline, the 7-hour break, the 23-day fatigue response, the AVAC
  delay), and *Delaney* tells against breaking a course of conduct into pieces.
- The treating records name the roster and pay.

**What decides it:** whether the treating doctors attend and link the roster (and the fatigue response) and pay
to the injury under cross-examination. **This is the single swing variable in the appeal.**

### C. Factor 3 (pay) was a reasonable process, even if imperfect (weak to moderate)

**Why it is legitimate:**

- Wright and Earl give evidence.

**Your answer, from the admitted facts:**

**What decides it:** whether the Commission sees a process that was reasonable but slow, or a delay that was not
reasonable. On the record, the second.

### D. The weighing: reasonable management action displaces the rest (moderate; depends on A to C)

**Their argument:** weighed on all the evidence (*Langerak* [86]), the injury arose out of the May 2024 response
and the pay process. The one roster error, three months earlier and "not intentional or repeated", does not
change that. That is weighing, not counting, so it is a legitimate argument.

**Your answer (*Mahaffey* [57]):** unless reasonable management action "on balance, displace[s] the evidence in
favour of the worker… a finding in the worker's favour must follow".
- If pay falls (C) and the roster is linked (A), there is little left to displace with.

**What decides it:** A and C.

## 5. Overall verdict

**The case they can honestly run is narrow and coherent:**
1. "We accept you were injured."
2. "We do not accept the March roster contributed."
3. "The injury came from the May 2024 response and the pay process, both reasonable."
4. "Prove otherwise with your doctors."

**It is legitimate, but it is not strong.** It rests on two things:
- the medical evidence failing to link the roster (and pay) to the injury;
- factor 2 holding up under cross-examination.

**What the medical evidence does to the odds** (judgement):

| Medical evidence at hearing | Your chance if heard |
|---|---|
| Treating doctors attend and link the roster, the fatigue response and pay | about 80% |
| You rely on records alone | about 50–55% |
| Overall now | about 70% |

**What to do with this:**
- **Line up the treating doctors' availability to give evidence.** ⛔ Nothing about content.
- **Keep the evidence to events, dates and their words.** That denies them F, and blunts B.
- **Expect a partial yes on question 4 as their most defensible answer.** It is not a refusal. It narrows the
  appeal to A to D. At the conference, the fair question is what evidence, beyond the three-month gap, they have
  that the roster did not contribute, when their own review found it did.
