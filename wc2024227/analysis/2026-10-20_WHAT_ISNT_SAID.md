## How to read a silence

A silence means something only if three things hold:
1. saying it would have been normal in that document;
2. it would have been cheap to say;
3. a party preparing to fight would have wanted to say it.

If all three hold and it still was not said, the silence is a choice. If not, it is brevity or role.

**Separate silence by role from silence by choice:**
- **Role (no weight):**
  - Matheson cannot concede or argue merits, so her emails will not carry either.
  - Under Guide 5.1 a change of position follows the conference, so no exit words beforehand is the expected
    order.
  - Calderbank rejections without reasons are normal practice.
- **Choice (weight):** what Counsel and the delegate chose not to put in the pleading, the evidence and the
  legal correspondence. Those are the people who decide.

**Why silence carries legal weight here.** Dwyer, 7 Aug (14:52–15:49): "the regulator does have to contradict
things that you say… if you say something and it's not contradicted… there's a likelihood that it'll be
accepted."

## The shape of the negative space

**Unsaid.** Everything a defender must say to win:
- answer the facts;
- bring medical evidence;
- call the roster reasonable;
- name the management action;
- oppose the amendment;
- warn on costs;
- press for a hearing.

**Said.** Everything needed to keep their conduct clean: documents confirmed, dates given, promises kept,
rights reserved.

**Also unsaid:** the exit.

**Reading.** A party that knows it cannot win the case it pleaded, has not yet been authorised to stop, and is
using the conference to get the reason. Under Dwyer's rule, its silence on the facts also counts against it.

## What to listen for

The first time they say any one of these, it shows which way they have decided:
- "reasonable" about the roster;
- a named management action for ¶27;
- costs;
- time to amend;
- an expert;
- or "review its position" / "consent orders".
