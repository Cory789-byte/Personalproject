# If they answer the 19 Oct letter properly, does "the appeal should be allowed" follow? (22 Oct 2026)

> Internal.
>
> **Read from source today:**
> - RD 69983, "Conclusion" (p 26);
> - *Workers' Compensation Regulator v Langerak* [2020] ICQ 2 at [86];
> - *Simon Blackwood (WCR) v Mahaffey* [2016] ICQ 10 at [57] (judgment texts in `documents/caselaw/`).
>
> ⛔ Not *Carr* [2022] QIRC 59: it was set aside by *WCR v Carr* [2023] ICQ 1.

## Short answer

**Mostly yes.** A proper answer to question 4 is one consistent with:
- their own review's conclusion;
- the absence of any contrary medical evidence;
- "not requiring a party to prove a matter which the State knows to be true".

That answer accepts that the injury arose out of employment through factors 2, 3 and 4, and their own review
called factor 4 (the roster) "unreasonable management action". On the authorities, they could then keep the
exclusion only by showing that reasonable management action, on balance, displaces the evidence for the worker.
On this record that is hard.

**It is not automatic.** One genuine question remains: the weighing under s 32(5). Your own letter leaves to the
Commission whether factors 2 and 3 were reasonable, and how they weigh against the roster.

## 1. The chain

1. **RD 69983, Conclusion (p 26):**
   > "you sustained a personal injury of a psychological nature
   > your injury arose out of employment, to the extent that it arose out of factors 2, 3 and 4, where employment
   > was a significant contributing factor
   > factors 2 and 3 amounted to reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way
   > factor 4 amounted to unreasonable management action."
2. **Question 4** asks whether, "to that extent", s 32(1) is not in issue, so the hearing is confined to s 32(5).
   - A proper answer, with no contrary evidence, is yes.
   - That accepts the roster as part of the cause. It contradicts ¶22(f) "not causative", which has no medical
     basis served.
3. **Langerak [86]:**
   > "The ultimate question under s 32(5)(a) is whether the injury, not any one stressor, arose out of, or in the
   > course of, reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way… The determination… is one made by
   > weighing reasonable management action against other employment factors in light of the whole of the
   > evidence."
4. **Mahaffey [57]:**
   > "the beneficial interpretation approach should be applied. In the cases decided in this Court any attempt to
   > provide some type of formula or application of dominant cause has been rejected… If, after considering all
   > the relevant evidence and weighing up the factors which were accepted as having given rise to the personal
   > injury, the Commission forms the conclusion that any of the conduct referred to in s 32(5) does not, on
   > balance, displace the evidence in favour of the worker then a finding in the worker's favour must follow."
5. **So, with an unreasonable roster accepted as part of the cause,** the exclusion survives only if the
   reasonable parts outweigh it enough to displace the evidence for you.
   - Their reasonable parts are factor 2 (the May 2024 response) and factor 3 (pay).
   - Pay is weak for them on the admitted record: the 3 May direction was not actioned by 13 May; the AVAC was
     "Part Completed" on 28 May; the last correction came 425 days on.
   - Their review's "two out of the three causative factors" is the counting the authorities reject.
   - **The logical end point of a proper answer is that the appeal should be allowed.** That is why the
     clean-yes scenario carries about 85% for a concession.

## 2. The gaps (honest)

- **"Properly" does not require them to concede that factors 2 and 3 were unreasonable.** The letter itself
  says reasonableness and weight "remain matters for the Commission". The weighing is the one live issue.
- **A partial answer can be legitimate,** if they genuinely contend the roster did not contribute.
  - Their point would be the gap between the March roster and the June onset.
  - *WCR v Carr* [2023] ICQ 1 (ground 2) requires evidence linking a stressor to the injury.
  - But a proper partial answer needs a basis. They have served no medical evidence, and their own review found
    the link "Having regard to the medical evidence".
- **The onus stays with the Appellant.**
- **An answer does not allow the appeal.** Only consent orders (their decision) or the Commission's decision can.

## 3. What follows

| Their answer to question 4 | What happens next |
|---|---|
| **Proper** | The appeal reduces to one weighing question, on ground that favours you. That is the usual run-up to a concession |
| **Partial or deferred** | The same question goes to the conference. There, the fair ask is on what evidence they dispute their own review's finding (Guide 5.1 "whether any elements of s 32… can be agreed"; the model-litigant duty, left for Dwyer to notice) |
