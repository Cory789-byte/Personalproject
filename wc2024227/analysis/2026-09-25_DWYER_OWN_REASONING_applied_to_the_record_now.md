## 2. His decisions (from the reference file), and one correction

- **McCool [2021] QIRC 374 [68]:** the Act "is intended to confer a benefit and it ought to be applied in that way"
  (s 194(3)). **[65]–[67]:** without evidence, an exclusion is not activated. This is a journey claim under s 36, so
  it's an **analogy only**.
- **Davis [2021] QIRC 107; Andrews [2020] QIRC 104:** he dismisses appeals for non-cooperation with directions. You
  have complied with every direction.
- ⛔ **Correction to the McCool analogy (checked today).** In QIRC psychological-injury appeals the onus stays on
  the appellant for every element, **including that s 32(5) does not exclude the injury**. See Grace v WCR [2021]
  QIRC 285 (Power IC) and Du Preez v WCR [2020] QIRC 109 [6] (O'Connor VP); both read today via page summaries
  only. Dwyer said the same at [14:49] ("you have the onus… and that's true"). So the argument is **not** "they
  have to prove the exclusion". It is his own formulation: **the onus is yours, and it is discharged when your
  evidence is uncontradicted**. The Regulator has not identified the action it relies on (fact 303) and has no
  medical evidence, so there is nothing to weigh against you.
- **How the other Members decide s 32(5)** (a different Member will hear the appeal): Carr v WCR [2022] QIRC 59
  (McLennan IC). At [60]: "The ultimate question under s 32(5)(a) is whether the injury, not any one stressor,
  arose out of… reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way". At [58]: if the s 32(5) conduct "does not,
  on balance, displace the evidence in favour of the worker then a finding in the workers favour must follow". One
  unreasonable stressor out of nine was enough there. ⚠ Confirm the pinpoints from the PDF before citing.
- **Benjamin (21 Feb 2025):** known from news reports only; citation not found. Not relied on.
