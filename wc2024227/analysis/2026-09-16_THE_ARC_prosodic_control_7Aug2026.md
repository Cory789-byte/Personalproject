Interleaved pauses: **16.0 s · 25.6 s · 16.8 s · 15.8 s.**

**Inference.** Short, high, fast, loud interrogatives separated by 15–25-second silences is not
disengagement. It is the profile of a decision-maker **reading the document between questions**.
He went to the Form 29, located item 10, and worked A to E on the record.

### The register change — [26:34]

> *"You see, in a hearing, if you forget all these, right, forget all these documents that you
> want to produce to prove that she sent you these emails."*  `wide quieter`

Range widens, volume drops: examiner → instructor.

### Joint construction — [27:52]–[27:59]

> **DWYER IC:** *"You can give that evidence. You can say and she gave us these directives. All
> the time, never asked us anything, she just kept sending these emails through saying—"* `↑+3.6st`
> **MR SHEPHERD:** *"effective immediately we're going to do this, effective immediately we're
> going to do that."* `↑+2.7st`

He was building an exit, closed at [28:04]: the existence of the emails is not in dispute, so
disclosure of them is unnecessary — *"that's a problem for them, not for you."*

## 4. Why he doubled down — [29:17]

The exit was not taken. Fifty words, `flat`, one rise:

> *"so for me it was direct calls to this number* `↑+3.0st` *but this number is an emergency
> contact and then those doctors the ones that get called for an emergency they're sent to the
> wrong side of the room they're not sent to someone having a cardiac arrest they're not sent to
> someone in respiratory distress we're now not even contacting them by their numbers"*

→ ***(5.7 s pause)***

**Inference — the mechanism.** The disposal route works only if Stressor 1(a) is a
*characterisation* dispute about emails, which the Regulator's non-contradiction can cure. The
fifty words re-found the stressor as a **telephony/database reconfiguration with a clinical
response consequence** — which non-contradiction cannot cure, because it does not turn on whether
emails were sent. The route closed. The 5.7 s is the recalculation.

What emerged was not a better argument. It was **price**.

## 5. How the doubling-down was executed — three moves in 90 seconds

**Move 1 — reassert the frame, name the appellant** [29:45]:
> *"It's going to move on for a moment, okay? Because after my speech about efficiency of conduct
> of proceedings, I'm concerned at the direction this is going in, Mr Shepherd."*

Reaches back to his own 00:00 opening. An authority move, not a merits move.

**Move 2 — the peak** [30:12.98]. Over the top of *"I actually understand what you're getting at"*:

> *"If we can't get to that, I'm going to schedule this disclosure dispute for a hearing."*
> **`↑+5.9st fast 6.4w/s louder`**

**This is the single most marked utterance Dwyer produces in the hearing** — his maximum pitch
excursion, simultaneously fast and loud, on the one sentence that converts a mention into a
contested hearing, delivered over the appellant's voice. Verified as the file maximum for this
speaker.

**Move 3 — the toll** [31:00]–[32:00]: engaged solicitors; *"they'll bring their lawyers along and
I'll allow that"*; *"there'll be cost consequences"*; *"longer than a day"*; closing:

> *"So, yeah, this is going to be a big sideshow, probably bigger than the hearing at the end of
> the day* `↑+2.1st` *and if you want it, you can have it."* `wide`

**He never refuses the application.** Not once in 65 minutes. The door is left open with a price
on it — which is what a decision-maker does when the application cannot be disposed of on the
merits from the bench.

## 6. The pivot onto MSH — inside the same utterance — [30:20]

> *"And the health service is going to need to call evidence to talk to their objections,* `wide`
> *and the regulator may or may not have anything to say, but in the meantime, the party should
> work very hard at formulating a bundle of documents that might satisfy you. I don't know to what
> extent that's happened so far. **I see a very comprehensive objection,*** `wide louder` *but what
> the responsibility of the parties is when you have a disclosure dispute like this is to get
> working on whether they can resolve it without my intervention. That's what the parties need to
> be doing as well. I don't know whether that's happened or not or whether it's just a, this is
> what I want, no, you can't have it. If that's where we're at, well, **you've got a bit of work to
> do down there at the bar table**."* `↓-2.3st flat fast 6.0w/s`

Three observations:

1. **"very comprehensive objection"** `wide louder` is the only volume rise in the hearing about a
   document that is not the appellant's, and it is immediately followed by the responsibility
   point. It is not praise.
2. **"the health service is going to need to call evidence to talk to their objections"** — MSH
   would have to put its objection on oath and be cross-examined on it. A real cost, stated aloud.
3. **"down there at the bar table."** The appellant is not at the bar table. That sentence is
   addressed to Ms Matheson and Ms Ruttan.

The cost warning and the criticism are one continuous utterance with **two different addressees**.
The prosody falls exactly at the pivot — `↓-2.3st flat`, straight down off the `↑+5.9st` peak.
**The criticism of the objection is where the pressure discharges.**

## 8. Where the arc closes — [62:30]–[63:30]

Unprompted, at the end:

> *"If you're going to talk about those things that we talked about before, which was **the
> unassessed directives** or whatever, **you might be on to something there. They might have an
> obligation to provide those.** … they're likely to come out one way or the other… **that
> particular point is the one one area I think in all of what we've been through that there might
> be something to look at.**"*

**The one area identified as live is the exact stressor the appellant was in the minutiae of at
29:17, when the 5.7-second silence occurred.**

Escalated on it → priced it → criticised the objection to it → came down → named it as the only
live one. The arc resolves on the single ground that was not traded away.

## 9. The two pressure peaks, and the two opposite answers

| | Peak 1 — documents | Peak 2 — union delegate |
|---|---|---|
| Span | 29:45 – 32:00 | 45:26 – 49:53 |
| Dwyer's markers | max `↑+5.9st`, `fast 6.4w/s`, `louder` | `louder` ×4; `↑+4.5st`; *"Yes or no? Mr Sheppard"* `↑+4.2st louder` |
| Window `louder` count | 1 (25–30) → escalation | **4** (45–50) — highest of the hearing |
| Appellant's move | **Did not concede** — went further into mechanism [29:17] | **Conceded** — *"I don't know if I can prove it as much"* [46:10, A2] |
| Result | Escalation, then concession of the point at [62:30] | Pressure off within three minutes |

**Inference.** Two loads, two opposite responses, both effective: held where the ground was
documentary and provable; gave where it was not. That discrimination was made in real time, under
load, from 13.6% of the speaking floor.
