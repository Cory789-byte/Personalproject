## Part 1 — What the Respondent does first: its answer to the application

**Documents (item 3).** It will say it has asked MSH for copies of nine documents, and ask for time. Low stakes.

**Particulars of the s 32(5) contention (item 4).** It has three choices, each with a cost:

| Choice | What it would say | What it costs the Respondent |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Oppose** | "The onus is on the Appellant (guide 7.3; 9C para 5). The Respondent is not required to particularise its case." | Weak. It *pleads* the exclusion at 9C para 27 and has admitted that para 27 names no management action (fact 303). A party who pleads a contention can be directed to particularise it. Opposing looks like having no particulars to give, which is the impression a case manager notices. |
| **B. Consent, then answer broadly** | "Each particular that is management action was reasonable for the reasons in the outlines." | Unlikely to satisfy the direction. The Commissioner can direct a proper answer, and a second direction on the same point is where the question "do you wish to proceed" usually arises. |
| **C. Consent and answer particular by particular** | The table in Part 2. | It must say, in writing, that several matters were not management action at all, and on others it has only "human error". That is the table below. |

**Most likely: C, after a short attempt at A at the conference.**
