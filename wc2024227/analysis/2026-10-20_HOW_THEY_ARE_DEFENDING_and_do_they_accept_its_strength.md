## Short answer

- **The facts: yes, they accept they are formidable.** They have conceded them: 298 of 303 admitted, 0 denied,
  the documents vouched for, and errors conceded in their own pleading and outlines.
- **The claim: no, they have not accepted it is lost.** They hold two legal lines:
  1. responses to the worker's conduct were reasonable, and an error is not unreasonable management;
  2. causation by stressor.
- **What they pay for tells you their real view:** Counsel to manage the process, no expert to win the medicine.
  That is how a party behaves when it rates a hearing as more likely lost than won and wants to keep an exit and
  an option open. Estimated internal view of winning a hearing: about 25–35% (inference; consistent with the mock
  counsel brief's 25–30%).

2. **Witnesses:**
   - Reese: the 2023 roster-fairness response, which is their best-documented reasonable episode; the May 2024
     retraction after HR advice;
   - Taylor: the Communication Book exchange ("loudly"); 14 May; human error; the agreement;
   - Wright and Earl: pay and the agreement.
3. **Cross-examining the Appellant on:**
   - the 15 May email;
   - the Communication Book;
   - the right to refuse shifts;
   - the 2022 and Nov 2023 entries;
   - perception.
4. **Cross-examining the doctors on:**
   - attribution;

   - the "not for medico-legal use" footer.
5. **Submissions:** management need not be perfect; isolated error inside a reasonable process; *Carr* (ICQ) on
   evidence linking the stressor to the injury.

**The plan's dependency.** It needs two things: the "difficult employee" frame for May 2024, and a weak medical
link.
