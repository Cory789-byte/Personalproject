## 3. The architecture

**What it is.** Reasonableness is pleaded per stressor, on facts repeated where needed so that no stressor borrows from
another; causation is pleaded once, for the whole injury, on the composite course. Part D.3 adds a classification
question: which circumstances are management action at all.

**Is it coherent?** Yes, and it is the correct shape in law. Section 32(5)(a) asks whether *the injury* arose out of
reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way. The Commission has to (i) classify each circumstance, (ii) assess
the reasonableness of each that is management action, and (iii) weigh the whole. Pleading (i) and (ii) stressor by
stressor and (iii) globally is exactly that sequence. The Review Unit got to its result by skipping (i) and doing (iii)
with a head-count ("two out of three"). Part D forces the steps.

**The one tension, and why it is not a problem.** The stand-alone stressors need no per-stressor medical attribution,
because the draft does not ask for three injuries. It asks for one injury from a course, of which a significant part
(at least the roster and the conditions at the console) is either not management action or not reasonable. Langerak /
Carr: the injury, not any one stressor. That is why the "cumulative medical case" paragraph and the stand-alone
structure sit together. **The dependency this creates:** Report B must say, in terms, that the roster/fatigue exposure
and the conditions in which the work was done were significant contributors, not just "workplace stress". If the
report only says "the course of conduct", the Regulator argues the injury arose "mainly" from the parts it calls
reasonable, which is the Review Unit's reasoning again.

**The classification question (Part D.3).** New, and low-cost. If the Member finds the database removal, the
misdirected calls and the directory were *conditions of work* rather than management action, s 32(5) never reaches
them, and the injury is compensable on s 32(1) alone. If the Member finds they are management action, you are back to
reasonableness, on admitted facts. You lose nothing by asking. Expect the Regulator to say "management action" is broad
and covers operational decisions; it probably does, so do not build the closing on D.3.

**Stressor 2 now holds.** Moving the pandemic leave in (2(f)) was the right call: Review Decision Factor 3 dealt with
it under "Withholding of pay", Ms Earl's outline concedes the second decline "appears to have been made in error", and
the draft already pleads the entitlement/process rather than unpaid wages, which is what Earl's "continued to be paid"
would otherwise answer.

**Stressor 3 is the keystone and reads as one.** Admitted break (284), admitted standard (285), the agreement's scope
(225, 234), the 23 days (249–250), no FRMS before 30 June (264, 269–271), the Review Unit's own finding (260). The
Respondent's own payroll witness will say the break was "a rostering practice issue for the line manager". Nothing in
the four outlines answers 225 or 234.

## 8. How to put it to Dwyer

Not at a conference, and not as a surprise. The route is:

1. **Fix §7.** Re-date it.
2. **Write to Matheson first** (Rule 4 is about not asking before filing; an amendment needs leave, so consent is the filing): one paragraph, neutral. "The Appellant proposes to seek leave to file a Second Amended Form 9A (enclosed). It withdraws the characterisations in the 7 April document, accepts that the onus is the Appellant's, and confines the case to the facts admitted on 8 September 2026. It adds no new fact. Please advise by [date] whether the Respondent consents, and whether it seeks a period to amend its statement of facts and contentions." Offer 14 days.
3. **File the application for leave with the Direction 5 election** (from 30 Sep): elect the second s 552A conference, and ask that leave to amend be dealt with at or before it. The two go together: the conference is where a narrowed pleading does its work, and he chairs it.
4. **One-page cover note to the Registry**, procedural only: what the amendment does (withdraws, confines, accepts onus), that all facts are admitted, that no hearing is listed, that consent has been sought.
5. **At the conference, three sentences:** "The case is now the admitted facts, stressor by stressor. The Respondent has served no medical evidence and its paragraph 27 identifies no action. The question for the Respondent is what in Part B it contradicts, and with what."

**Do not** table it for the first time in the room, hand it up as a "note", or describe it as a response to the outlines. It is your pleading, amended because he told you to narrow.
