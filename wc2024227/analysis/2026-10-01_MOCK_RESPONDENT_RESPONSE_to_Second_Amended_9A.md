# MOCK: the Respondent's likely response to the Second Amended 9A (1 October 2026 structure)

**INTERNAL. Not for service. Nothing here is quoted to the Regulator, MSH or the Commission.**

**What this is.** A mock of how the Respondent would most likely plead back against the Second Amended Form 9A in the structure of 1 October 2026:
- Stressor 1(a) to (n), with 1(a), 1(c), 1(e) and 1(i) pleaded as context;
- Stressor 2(a) to (o);
- Stressor 3(a) to (h), with the 2020 agreement as unlettered background.

**What it is built from.** Every position below comes from the Respondent's own documents:
- its amended statement of facts and contentions (Form 9C) of 13 May 2026 (cited as 9C);
- its outlines of evidence of 24 September 2026 (Taylor, Reese, Wright, Earl);
- its response of 8 September 2026 admitting 298 of 303 facts.

Nothing is invented for it. Where it would have to depart from those documents, that is marked.

The Respondent is bound by its admissions: withdrawal needs leave under r 49(3). So a realistic response does not deny admitted facts. It admits them and fights on four grounds:
1. characterisation;
2. whether the action was management action;
3. reasonableness;
4. causation.

## Part 2 — How your structure shapes that response

### What it cannot do

- **Deny the facts.** Everything in 12 to 36 starts with "admits". Your structure puts a paragraph number against every fact, so each denial would need leave under r 49(3). The pleading contest collapses to characterisation, reasonableness and causation.
- **Answer a theme in one sentence.** The 9C answered each old stressor with one "reasonable in all respects". With 37 dated particulars, it must say why each one was reasonable. At 13, 17, 18, 34 and 35 it has no pleaded answer and no witness today.
  - Nobody from the database project.
  - Nobody on consultation.
  - Nobody on the MASPER or Integrated Respiratory Service reports.
  - Only Reese on the roster decision.
- **Keep its witnesses consistent with its admissions.** The mock exposes four collisions. These are cross-examination material only:
  1. **The agreement.** Taylor says HR confirmed the agreement "continued to apply". HR's own email of 7 July 2026 says it "is only applied where staff initiated shift swaps have occurred", and no swap is alleged (para 234).
  2. **The approval date.** Earl says the pandemic leave was approved on 29 February. The admitted record says the Director approved it at 16:05:08 on 1 March (para 123).
  3. **The characterisation of the break.** Wright's team called the break "a rostering practice issue for the line manager rather than an unpaid entitlement". That is the Respondent's own payroll witness placing it with the Manager's rostering.
  4. **Who signed.** Taylor says "all switchboard staff" signed. The Respondent does not allege she managed the team in June 2020 (para 25).
- **Say the 7-hour break complied with anything.** It was below ten, and below eight. The only answers left are "human error", "could refuse" and "not causative". The first two are reasonableness points, and they concede that the rostering itself fell short. The third is causation.

### Where your structure gives it an opening

| Opening | How it uses it | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1(j) and 1(n) pleaded as causes | Paras 20 and 22 above: ordinary management, and an absence on the injury date itself. Easy wins that make the rest look like grievances. | Move 1(j) to context or merge it into 1(k). Fold 1(n) into 1(d) as a fact. Both were recommended on 1 October. |
| Context particulars in the list | Para 23: strike out as irrelevant. | Low risk. The scope paragraph explains why they are there, and chronology is admissible. Keep them. |
| Length and the Neville history | Para 4: not clear and concise. | Case management only. Answer with Part D. The Neville section is the most exposed passage; it could be cut to two sentences without losing a fact. |
| Your contention paragraph's concession on the HR email | Para 29: the email is July 2026, so it proves nothing about March 2024. | Delete "That email was written in July 2026 and describes the practice then stated". The admitted sequence above it already carries the point. |
| The Easter 2023 comparison and the 14 May 2024 attendance email | Paras 20 and 27: attempts to recast the Appellant as the problem. | Your pleading shows the Respondent alleges no disciplinary or performance process and no warning (paras 300 to 302). Leave that to hearing. |

### Three risks worth taking seriously

1. **Causation.** This is its real ground (para 38(a)). The problem list shows "Anxiety" and "ADHD" from 26 October 2022. It has no expert, so it must run this through cross-examination of your GP and psychiatrist. The answer is Tab M1 itself: no antidepressant, no mental-health medication and no psychiatric diagnosis before the injury. The test is "a significant contributing factor", not the only one.
2. **Atomisation.** It will try to show each particular is small and reasonable. The answers are Langerak, as applied in Carr at [60], Delaney, and Mahaffey. Stressor 3 stands alone on its own review decision's finding.
3. **A late s 32(5)(b) plea.** It could add "perception" (para 38(c)) to the retraction and roster decisions. That needs leave, because the 9C does not plead it. Your 5B contention already answers it. Keep the tone neutral so nothing in the pleading reads as perception.

## Part 3 — The likely result, step by step

| Step | Likely outcome | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Leave to amend | Granted, with 14 days for the Respondent to amend | No hearing date. No new stressor. Every particular is an admitted fact. Your election itself offers the Respondent that time. |
| The Respondent's amended 9C | Something close to Part 1: admissions, then reasonableness and causation | It cannot deny the facts, and the 9C and outlines fix its lines. |
| Second s 552A conference | Real settlement pressure on the Respondent | It is defending a rejection its own review decision partly undercut, with four lay witnesses, no expert, and witness accounts that collide with its admissions. |
| Hearing: Stressor 3 | Strongest. The exclusion is unlikely to cover it. | Below ten and eight; no swap; the review finding; "rostering practice issue for the line manager"; a 23-day answer that relied on an agreement HR says applies only to swaps. |
| Hearing: Stressor 2 | Strong on delay; weaker on whether pay administration is management action | Each error and each date is admitted. If it is not management action, s 32(5) cannot exclude it at all. |
| Hearing: Stressor 1 | Mixed | Database, directory and consultation are strong as working conditions. The retraction and office-hours items carry the most dispute. 1(j) and 1(n) are weak if left as causes. |
| Hearing: causation | The deciding issue | Treating records made at the time, against a pre-injury anxiety entry with no treatment. The Respondent has no expert. |

**Bottom line.** Your structure turns the Respondent's pleading into a list of admissions with "reasonable" attached. Its defence narrows to two things: human error on Stressor 3, and causation. On the record as it stands, you are more likely than not to succeed. The realistic contest is causation, not the facts. The most likely path is settlement pressure at the second conference, not a contested hearing.

**Two changes before filing would improve the result:**
- make 1(j) context, and fold 1(n) into 1(d);
- delete the one conceding sentence about the HR email.
