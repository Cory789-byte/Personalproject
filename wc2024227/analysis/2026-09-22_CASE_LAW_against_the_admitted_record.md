## The authorities, and where the admitted record meets each

| authority | principle | on this record |
|---|---|---|
| *WorkCover Qld v Kehl* (2002) 170 QGIG 93 | "reasonable" means reasonable in all the circumstances of the case | the circumstances are admitted: a patient-safety-critical function (Regulator's 18 Feb 2026 admission; fact 8), altered without consultation, misroutes reported and unanswered for 5 d 18 h |
| *Davis v Blackwood* [2014] ICQ 009 | assess holistically; a blemished course may still be reasonable | the course here is not blemished at points; it lacks every step that would make it reasonable (consultation, assessment, monitoring, response), on the pleadings themselves |
| *Scott v WCR* [2021] QIRC 110 | action without warning or consultation, "blindsiding" the worker, is more than a mere blemish and is not taken in a reasonable way | directives "effective from today" (fact 51), "you are to" (81); Respondent alleges no consultation; Ms Taylor's own later email offers consultation "before implementing" (101) |
| *Carr v WCR* [2022] QIRC 059 | one unreasonable stressor among nine suffices where it contributes to the injury | the Regulator's own review already found the rostering of 17–18 Mar 2024 "unreasonable management action" (fact 260); the notice adds the database/directives sequence and the payroll loop |
| *Richards v WCR* [2024] QIRC 241 | failure to act on known safety risks is not protected management action | nine wrong-team emergency reports 2–8 May 2024, first reply 9 May; respiratory service "we can not help patients" 15 and 20 May, no response pleaded before 20 May; Metro South: no fatigue assessment, no register, assessment began only after 30 Jun 2024 |
| *Read v WCR* [2017] QIRC 72; *Allwood v WCR* [2017] QIRC 88 | management action is action directed at the employment, not everyday operational matters | if the misdirected-call consequences are operational rather than management action, s 32(5) does not reach them at all, and the only question is contribution |
| *Newberry v Suncorp* [2006] QCA 48; *State of Qld v Coyne* [2003] QIC 118 | contribution assessed on the medical evidence; mixed stressors weighed by their causal share | the treating entries (28 Jun, 1 Jul 2024) name pay, roster, complaints not listened to — facts 185–203, 211–221, 266–267, admitted |
| *King v WCR* [2020] QIRC 180 at [178] | weight of each stressor turns on the medical evidence identifying it | the medical schedule maps each treating entry to the admitted paragraphs it describes |
| *Robinson v WCR* [2016] ICQ 16; *Yousif v WCR* [2017] ICQ 004; *Blackwood v Adams* [2015] ICQ 001 | the Commission finds the facts first, then applies the exclusion to the facts found | here the facts arrive found: 298 admitted, chronology uncontested |

## What is different from every case in the table

Each of those decisions was reached after a hearing in which the facts were contested and found
on oral evidence. In *King* the Commission worked through four stressors on the witnesses'
accounts; in *Carr*, nine; in *Scott*, the content of one meeting. The exclusion was then applied
to the facts as found. Here the finding stage is complete before the hearing: the chronology,
the wording of every directive, the dates of every report and reply, the absence of consultation,
restoration, response and assessment, and the Regulator's own prior finding of unreasonableness
are admitted. The contest that remains is the one those cases decided last, characterisation and
contribution, and it is to be decided on facts in the other side's words, with contemporaneous
medical entries that name the same facts. No reported decision in the sources above was reached
on a record in that state.

## The Regulator's pleading

Its amended statement of facts and contentions identifies, at paragraph 27, no management
action said to be reasonable (fact 303, admitted). *Davis* requires the action to be identified
before its reasonableness can be assessed. On the pleadings the exclusion has no subject.

Reading: three procedure changes in five weeks, each announced by the Line Manager in writing, one introduced because of "many ongoing issues" about misrouted calls; the head of the organisation told the Commissioner no consequential change occurred and complaints went nowhere but the Line Manager's inbox. The contradiction is between two hospital documents, both admitted. This is the "character of those emails" the Commissioner flagged at 62:15 as "the one area… that there might be something to look at": an email announcing a change, from an employer whose Chief Executive says no change occurred, is an unassessed unilateral directive on the employer's own account. Ms Taylor's answer in the box is either that it was a change (Item 3(c) is wrong) or that it was not (a hospital emergency function had its call handling altered by something not a change to procedure). No non-party has standing at the hearing to say the Chief Executive did not mean it.
