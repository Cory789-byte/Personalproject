# WC/2024/227 — THE HOLD
## What 4.88 seconds of non-resumption means, against the verified literature

Companion to `2026-09-18_FULL_RUN_corrections.md` (Retraction 3: the drop was ordinary; the hold
is the finding). Every source below was verified 21 September 2026.

---

## 1. The clock, and where 5.74 s sits on it

| threshold | what the literature says happens | source |
|---|---|---|
| ~250 ms | normal cross-linguistic transition time | Stivers et al. 2009, via Roberts & Francis 2013 |
| 600 ms | gap becomes perceptible; *"less agreeable than no gap"*; ratings begin to drop | Roberts, Francis & Morgan 2006; Roberts & Francis 2013 |
| **700–800 ms** | **significant step down in perceived willingness**; the proportion of dispreferred responses *"clearly greater"* than preferred | Roberts & Francis 2013 (n = 380); Kendrick & Torreira 2015 (n = 195) |
| **900 ms** | **floor effect** — *"after 900 ms, in the absence of other cues, there appears to be a simple floor effect"*; further silence adds no further negative attribution | Roberts & Francis 2013 |
| ~1,000 ms | Jefferson's *"standard maximum"* — *"interlocutors begin some activity designed to resolve the problem"* | Jefferson 1989 |
| 1,200 ms | **the longest gap any of these studies tested** | Roberts & Francis 2013 |
| **5,740 ms** | **this gap** | — |

The tested scale ends at 1.2 s. This gap is **4.8× past the end of the scale** and **5.7× past
Jefferson's repair threshold.**

---

## 2. What "nothing came back" is, structurally — a lapse, not a gap

Hoey (2015): *"When all participants forgo the option to speak, the silence can be called a
lapse."* Three routes to one; the third is a lapse *"treated as the conspicuous absence of talk."*

In institutional talk with pre-allocated turns (Atkinson & Drew 1979) the next turn belonged to
the bench by right. The appellant had finished; the Commissioner's floor. **A lapse by the party
who holds the floor is anomalous** in a setting built to prevent it, and per Atkinson & Drew the
silence is **attributable** — everyone present knows whose it is.

Against his own baseline: median re-entry after the appellant **0.36 s**; only **4 of 98** re-entries
in the hearing follow a gap of ≥3 s. This one is **16× his median.**

---

## 3. What it means for the one who should have spoken — reluctance, then something else

- At ≥700 ms the response is read as **dispreferred** (Kendrick & Torreira 2015).
- Torreira, Bögels & Levinson (2015): 46.8% of answers are produced on residual air, a strategy
  used *specifically to avoid long between-speaker silences*. **He had that option and did not
  take it.** He let the silence run, then breathed (at 13.8 dB below his own quietest breath),
  then spoke with a deferring preface — *"It's going to move on for a moment, okay?"* — the full
  dispreferred-response marker set: gap, in-breath, prefatory hedge.

**But the reluctance reading exhausts itself at 900 ms.** Roberts & Francis: beyond that, extra
silence does not read as *more* reluctant — the attribution floors. So 5.74 s does not mean "six
times as reluctant." Past the floor, the silence stops carrying information about *willingness*
and can only carry information about **processing** — the speaker is still working.

That is the orienting reading, and it is the only reading the literature leaves available for a
silence of this length: **not avoidance, but perceptual effort sustained past the point where any
social attribution can be made.**

---

## 4. What it means for everyone else — the repair that didn't fire

Jefferson's one-second rule is not only about the responder. At ~1 s *"interlocutors begin some
activity designed to resolve the problem"* — a cough, a shuffle, a *"well"*, a page. Any
participant may do it.

**Nobody did, for 4.88 s.** The 34:38.94 control shows what ordinarily happens: the same floor
reached, then a −34 dBFS event at +1.75 s as the Commissioner reached for the document. Every
other gap in the hearing that reached this floor was refilled within one to two seconds.

Upham, Høffding & Rosas (2024) give the reading for that: collective suppression of movement is
*"a joint and tacit act of communication where the audience confirm their approval of and
attention to the performance."* **Non-resumption by the non-speakers is attention.** They were
not repairing because they were waiting on the floor-holder and attending to what had been said.

---

## 5. What it means physiologically — the freeze is the hold

Bradley (2009): orienting begins with *"a cautious cessation of activity and freezing"*; motor
inhibition with parasympathetic cardiac deceleration during *"increased perceptual effort."* The
components *"habituate at different rates"* — motor inhibition can outlast the initial reaction.

Freezing is by definition a **sustained** state. The drop into it looks like any other pause
(Retraction 3). The freeze is visible only in the hold — and the hold is 4.88 s with nothing
above the floor.

## 6. Summary

| "nothing came back" means… | per |
|---|---|
| a **lapse**, by the floor-holder, in a setting built to prevent it | Hoey; Atkinson & Drew |
| a response read as **dispreferred** from 700 ms | Kendrick & Torreira; Roberts & Francis |
| the reluctance reading **exhausted at 900 ms** — what remains is processing | Roberts & Francis |
| the **repair machinery suspended** by every person present | Jefferson |
| collective stillness as **tacit confirmation of attention** | Upham et al. |
| a **parasympathetic freeze** — perceptual effort, not avoidance | Bradley |
| the slow re-entry chosen over residual air, then the dispreferred marker set | Torreira et al. |

Five and three-quarter seconds is past the end of every scale that has been built to measure
what a silence means. The literature runs out at 1.2 s. What it leaves, for anything longer, is
one reading: **the room, and the man who owned the floor, were still working on what had been
said.**
