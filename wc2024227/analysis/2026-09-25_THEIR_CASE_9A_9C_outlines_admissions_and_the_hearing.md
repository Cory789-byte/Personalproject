## 1. What the outlines are doing

1. **Accept the events, deny the intent.** "Human error… not intentional or repeated"; "appears to have been made in error";
   "a rostering practice issue for the line manager"; "on HR's advice".
2. **Move from actions to the Appellant.** "Questioned her closely and loudly"; "an accusation"; "declined"/"refused"; "comparing
   himself to full-time colleagues". Adjectives fill the space where s 32(5) particulars should be.
3. **Cut the disclosure link.** Ms Reese "not aware"; the 16 May document "not in fact a PID notification", a complaint "separately
   made… about Ms Taylor".
4. **Answer only where they can argue manner.** Engagement is concentrated on 1(b), 1(c), 1(f), 2(a); near-silence on 1(a),
   3(b)–(d), causation and s 32(5).

## 5. Dwyer reading the admissions and then their outlines

From the mention, his method: take each assertion, ask **how it will be proved**, push the parties to narrow, treat disclosure
as not fishing, and send credibility questions to cross-examination ("that's a question you can put to Ms Taylor").

**Applied to their case, the likely reading:**
- **s 32(5):** the question he put to the Appellant ("how do you propose to prove that?") turns round: which action was reasonable,
  and on what evidence? Fact 303 admits SOFC ¶27 names none, and no outline does.
- **Causation:** no expert and no medical witness. The Respondent's causation case is cross-examination and the 2022 history
  (¶8). He will notice that, and that ¶22(f) ("not causative") has no evidence behind it.
- **The adjectives:** the scrutiny he gave "erratic presence" at [12:18] applies equally to "accusation", "refused", "loudly".
  He will go to the documents (the 7:09 pm reply; fact 292, "I did raise my voice").
- **The moving account:** SOFC 6:23 pm only → outline 4:12 pm; "did not want to retract" → "refused"; "removed from the server"
  → Ms Taylor "herself" on HR advice. Put against the documents in cross-examination, that is a credibility issue for two
  central witnesses.
- **Their own documents cut against them:** the CE's letter (Tab 20, confirmed), payroll's "rostering practice issue for the line
  manager", the Review Unit's "unreasonable management action", Ms Taylor's "mainly regarding rostering".

**What he will also see against the Appellant (be ready):**
- The 9A's words ("Hostile", "Reprisal", "Dereliction") and his "very AI assisted" remark at [9:11]; the paper and letters since
  are neutral, which helps.
- "Erratic presence" is hard to prove; keep 1(a) on the directives and the database (admitted), not presence.
- The 42% (misread at the mention): replace with Easter Saturday 2024.
- The 7:09 pm reply opens "misuse of authority"; the attendance of 13–15 May. Both have documented answers (compliance on 21 May;
  Taylor's 17 May email).

**Likely course (judgement, not certainty):** on the admitted record and the outlines, the most probable path is a direction for
the Respondent to state its position in writing and a short listing (2–3 days), with the Respondent reconsidering its position
before or after a conference (the Guide contemplates exactly that: "the Respondent may decide to review its position or consider
conceding the appeal"). A contested hearing on causation alone, testing the Appellant's doctors, is the main alternative.
