## Short answer

- **The delay mostly moves the timing, not the odds.** Their decision point was always the conference:
  - their own rule is "new information… we may decide to concede";
  - the Guide sets the review "Following the conference";
  - the decision runs from officer, to Counsel, to delegate.

  A concession, if it comes, now most likely comes **after 3 Dec**: a little in mid-December, but mostly
  **January to March 2027**. The overall chance stays **about 55–65%**.
- **Waiting costs them almost nothing, so nothing forces them to decide sooner.** The one exception is the
  model-litigant duty: if Counsel tells them the claim should succeed, they should not sit on that for five weeks.
- **Six weeks of waiting cuts both ways.** It gives time for things that help (Counsel's full review, your
  answers and agenda, the doctors' availability) and for things that hurt (momentum fading, an expert
  application, a widening step).
- **It matters more to you than to them, through money and the common-law clock.**
  - A common-law claim needs a **notice of assessment** (s 237(1)(a)(i)), which can only follow an accepted
    claim.
  - Proceedings must be brought within the limitation period, **about 18 June 2027**, unless a complying notice
    of claim is given before it ends (s 302(2)).
  - **Every month the appeal takes comes off that runway.**

## 5. What it does to you

1. **Money.** If the claim is accepted, entitlements go back to the claim period. Waiting delays the money; it
   does not cut it.
2. **The common-law clock is the real issue.**
   - **s 237(1)(a)(i):** the worker is entitled to seek damages only if they have "received a notice of
     assessment from the insurer for the injury".
   - **The order of steps:** acceptance, then assessment (once the injury is stable), then a notice of
     assessment, then the s 275 notice of claim.
   - **s 302:** proceedings must be brought within the limitation period (three years for personal injury:
     about 18 June 2027, per the earlier note). After that, only if before it ends the claimant has given a
     complying notice of claim, or the insurer has waived compliance, or a court has made a declaration (s 297)
     or given leave (s 298).
   - **What the timing does to that:**
     - a concession in early 2027 leaves roughly three to five months, which is tight;
     - a contested hearing decided after mid-2027 would collide with it.
   - **This is the one place where the delay truly matters.** Get a lawyer's advice now on protecting the June
     2027 date (Hall Payne via Petering, per the funding notes). Never raise it with the Regulator or the
     Commission as pressure.
3. **The employment track** carries on in parallel. Keep it separate.
