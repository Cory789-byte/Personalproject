## The three layers of the termination email

1. **The operative reason is money.** The first paragraph is entirely funds ("Without funds in trust, Saines Legal
   is simply unable…"), and the legal basis cited is **clause 29 of the costs agreement**. That is a commercial
   exit right, not a professional judgement.
2. **The justification is prospects.** "Significant concerns… as noted in our advice dated 12 May 2025". The
   timing undoes it as the trigger: a firm stopping for prospects stops in May, not after billing through June
   and July. What changed on 22–23 Jul was the money, and the arrival of the Regulator's SOFC.
3. **The self-protection** (strong reading; it is in the wording):
   - "We owe fiduciary duties… to ensure they are informed of the risks";
   - an options list that includes **discontinuance**;
   - "Although we did achieve an earlier outcome in negotiating your reinstatement".

   Together these build a file that answers any later complaint: *we warned him, we offered options, we
   delivered on the other matter.* The refusal of "background" help on 30 Jul does the same job, because limited
   help after termination carries liability with no retainer to cover it.

**What is missing** says as much:
- no referral to another firm (you asked on 29 Jul; none was given);
- no offer to seek an extension of 13 Aug for you;
- no written cost estimate for the July–August "bulk of work", although one was promised on 25 Jun (only the
  barrister's day rate, on 22 Jul);
- no engagement with the substance of your summary determination idea.

## What they were trying to do (ranked)

1. **Avoid working on credit** (strong). The sequence runs: overdue invoice, then a refused plan, then a deposit
   demand, then a deadline, then exit.
2. **Leave a matter that no longer paid** (moderate–strong). The next stage needed a barrister at $3,300–$4,400 a
   day, for a client who couldn't fund it. Hearing costs are recoverable only on a small scale (see the spend file).
3. **Leave with a clean file** (strong, from the wording): risk warnings, options, the reinstatement "win", and no
   ad hoc exposure.
4. **Record a genuine prospects concern** (moderate). It was real, but weighed on the 2025 record and used as
   justification.

## Assessment: what it means now

1. **Don't carry their prospects view forward.**
   - It was formed before the payroll production, before your April 2026 amended 9A, and before the admissions.
   - The concern behind it (proof, through witnesses and the doctors) is exactly what the case still turns on.
   - The record that answers it now exists.
2. **What the $9,734 bought:**
   - the 12 May advice;
   - the June 2025 9A, filed in one morning and since replaced by your April 2026 amended 9A;
   - the 2025 correspondence;
   - an exit that left the 13 Aug date to you.
3. **Lessons for engaging counsel for the hearing:**
   - **Money first.** Counsel will want fees in trust before work, as Saines did. Have the about $25,000 (direct
     brief) planned before you elect hearing, and agree a staged schedule in writing.
   - **Get a written costs estimate and a defined scope.** Ideally a fixed or capped fee for the hearing brief. The
     estimate Saines promised in June 2025 never arrived.
   - **Engage early.** Do it before you elect hearing, so their dates are known (s 552B, and the "particular
     counsel's availability" line cuts both ways). Saines' trouble came from leaving the barrister decision until
     three weeks before a deadline.
   - **Brief on today's record:** the admissions schedule, the outlines, your six questions and their 30 Oct
     answers, and the doctors' details. A lawyer's prospects view is only as good as the record they are given.
   - **Expect written risk warnings.** They are how lawyers protect themselves, and not necessarily a verdict.
   - **Don't start a hearing you can't fund to the end.** A barrister who withdraws mid-way is worse than none.
4. **Privilege.** These emails and the 12 May advice stay privileged.
