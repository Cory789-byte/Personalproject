## Short answer

- **The letter itself will almost certainly not end the appeal.**
  - Your six matters ask for positions, not a concession.
  - Matheson cannot concede; the delegate decides on Counsel's advice.
  - Guide 5.1 puts any review of position "Following the conference".
  - A WC appeal cannot be settled for money. It ends only by:
    - consent orders that the appeal is allowed;
    - withdrawal;
    - a decision.
  - Chance the 30 Oct letter itself concedes: **about 5%**.
- **But it can decide what the appeal is about, and question 4 is the hinge.**
  - RD 69983's Conclusion (p 26) says the injury "arose out of employment, to the extent that it arose out of
    factors 2, 3 and 4, where employment was a significant contributing factor". It also says "factor 4 amounted
    to unreasonable management action".
  - Question 4 asks whether, "to that extent", s 32(1) is not in issue, "so that the issue for any hearing is
    confined to section 32(5)".
  - **A clean "yes" accepts that the injury arose partly out of the roster, which their own review called
    unreasonable.** That contradicts their ¶22(f) ("not causative").
  - Their exclusion argument would then rest on the review's "two out of the three" counting, the method
    Langerak [86] and Mahaffey [57] reject.
  - **That is the one answer that could, in effect, settle it.**

## 3. What to watch for in the wording

**Strongest:**
- "to that extent";
- "factors 2, 3 and 4";
- "not in issue";
- "confined to section 32(5)".

**A partial yes:** "the Respondent accepts the appellant sustained a psychological injury" with nothing about
causation.

**A deferral:** "de novo"; "Guide"; "at the conference".

**Exit language, the strongest of all:**
- "review its position";
- "consent orders";
- "without admission".

**The hearing path:**
- "as outlined in our Statement of Facts and Contentions";
- ¶27 particulars;
- objections named;
- "time to amend";
- "expert".
