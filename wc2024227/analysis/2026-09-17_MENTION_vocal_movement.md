## 4. ⚠ CORRECTION to the QPS finding at §7 of `ACOUSTIC_SHAPE_FINDINGS.md`

That note read C1's 15.7:1 up/down asymmetry as showing he "speaks from the floor
of his own range." **The cross-recording data does not support that reading.**

Segments ≥ +2 st above own baseline vs ≤ −2 st below:

| Speaker | above | below | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| QPS C1 | 16.8% | 1.1% | 15.7 : 1 |
| **Mention Dwyer** | 6.7% | **0.7%** | **9.3 : 1** |
| Mention Shepherd | 8.3% | 1.9% | 4.3 : 1 |
| **QPS C0** | 16.0% | **13.9%** | **1.1 : 1** |

Dwyer — assured, in control, holding 86% of the floor — goes **below** his
baseline *less* often than QPS C1 does. **Upward skew in f0 is the normal state**,
because f0 has a physiological floor at the bottom of modal register and is
unbounded above. The ratio is therefore **not diagnostic of vocal posture.**

**The real outlier is QPS C0 at 13.9% below baseline — 7 to 20 times every other
speaker measured.** That is what a **two-speaker cluster** looks like: a
lower-voiced second officer pulls segments below the blended median. It
independently corroborates the failure of the k=3 split to resolve a third
speaker.

## 5. What this says about the mention

Across a hearing in which the Commissioner **got louder and faster** while
controlling pitch and volume independently, and in which he held 86.2% of the
floor, **the appellant's own vocal output did not change on any measured
channel.** Rate, pitch and loudness were all flat from the first minute to the
last, and he claimed the floor no differently than the bench did.
