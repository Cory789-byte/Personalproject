## 1. The problem Stressor 1 now presents to the Respondent

Paragraph 4 of Part C, read with election item 4, forces a choice on each core particular. Both answers cost it something:

| Particular | If it says "management action" | If it says "not management action" |
|---|---|---|
| **1(b) Database access removed, not restored** | It must show that eleven months without restoring access was reasonable, for a function it admits is critical to patient safety (para 289). The only correction path was one person, on Tuesdays and second Mondays, with after-hours requests left to wait (paras 41 to 44). It has **no witness** on the database. | It is a working condition. **Section 32(5)(a) cannot exclude it.** |
| **1(h) Misdirected emergency calls** | It must justify no communication to staff for five days, 18 hours and 14 minutes after nine occasions. It has **no witness**. | Working condition. Not excludable. |
| **1(k) Directory error: "we do not have any doctors"** | It must justify a clinical service's repeated reports going uncorrected over five days. It has **no witness**. | Working condition. Not excludable. |

**Its realistic choice is "not management action" on all three.** That is the only answer it can sustain without witnesses. It concedes the exclusion on Stressor 1's core.

## 3. How its causation answer on Stressor 1 fares

| Its point | Your record |
|---|---|
| The conditions were ordinary and too minor to be significant | Admitted as a function "critical ... to ensure effective clinical handover and patient safety" (para 289); running for eleven months; nine misdirected emergency-call occasions; a clinical service unable to help patients |
| The Appellant had pre-existing anxiety | Tab M1: "Anxiety" and "ADHD" appear as history-list items from 26 October 2022, with no antidepressant, no mental-health medication and no psychiatric diagnosis before the injury |
| The injury was really about the roster | Tab M1, 28 June 2024, ten days after onset: "stress at work", "upset by people not following rules". Stressor 1 is about rules and processes at the console. And the test is "a" significant contributing factor, not the only one |
| (no expert) | The Respondent has named no medical or expert witness, and served no expert report |

**Result.** On Stressor 1's core, the Respondent's only argument is that database and directory failures in a patient-safety function were not significant. It has no evidence to make that argument with.

## 4. The whole defence after the revaluation

| Issue | Where the Respondent is left |
|---|---|
| Stressor 1 core (1(b), 1(h), 1(k)) | Not excludable. Causation only, with no expert. |
| Stressor 1 edges (1(d), 1(l)) | Its best reasonableness contest: the retraction on HR advice. |
| Stressor 2 | Mostly "not management action", so not excludable. Delay unanswered. |
| Stressor 3 | "Human error"; its review found the rostering unreasonable; the agreement HR says applies only to swaps. |
| Causation overall | Its real ground, argued through cross-examination of your treating doctors only. |

**Two foundations, either sufficient on its own:**
- **Stressor 1's core,** which the exclusion cannot reach.
- **Stressor 3,** conduct its own review found unreasonable.

To win, the Respondent must defeat both. It can do that only on causation, and it has no expert.
