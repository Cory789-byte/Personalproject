## 2. THE MECHANISM DWYER HANDED OVER, TWICE, UNPROMPTED

This is the hinge of the whole sequence. At **[14:53]**:

> *"It's for the regulator to contradict that… It's often thrown out there, as an appellant, that you have the onus of proving your case. That's true, **but the regulator does have to contradict things that you say**. It's not the same thing as an onus, but **if you say something and it's not contradicted, then the likelihood is — it's not guaranteed, but it's the likelihood — that it will be accepted.**"*

And again at **[28:56]**:

> *"if you give evidence that you were sent unassessed directives by Miss Taylor, as you call them, and the regulator doesn't contradict it, doesn't cross-examine you, doesn't produce documents for you to comment on… **well, that's a problem for them, not for you.**"*

He described a **likelihood**. Rule 49 converts that likelihood into a **deemed admission** in fourteen days. The Commissioner gave the principle; the notice operated it as a rule.

That is the single most consequential thing said in the room, and it was said by the Commissioner, in the Appellant's favour, without being asked.

## 5. WHAT THE RESPONSE STILL RESERVES — AND WHAT THAT IS WORTH

The covering letter of 8 September reserves that an admission is not a concession that:

- the fact or document is **relevant** to any issue the Commission must determine;
- any document is **admissible**, or that tender will not be objected to;
- **any characterisation, conclusion, inference or submission** drawn from an admitted fact is accepted.

That is orthodox and correct. It is also the entire remaining field. **The primary facts are closed**; what is contestable is what follows from them. That is the argument to be having, and it is the one *Delaney* frames — a composite course of conduct evaluated globally, on facts no longer in dispute.
