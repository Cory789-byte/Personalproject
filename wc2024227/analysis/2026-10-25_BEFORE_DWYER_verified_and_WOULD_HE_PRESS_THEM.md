## Short answer

1. **Can the Commission decide it "on its own merits" without a hearing?** **No.**
   - The appeal is heard de novo.
   - The conference member does not hear it (Guide §5.1).
   - "No commercial settlement is possible at the conference".
   - Summary determination is not available (UCPR ch 9 pt 2 is not imported by s 553; register, 25 Sep).
   - It ends without a hearing only if the Regulator concedes (consent orders under s 558(1)(c)), or you
     discontinue.
2. **Could Dwyer look at everything and ask the Regulator why it still objects?** **Yes, and that is the second
   conference's stated purpose:**
   > "the Member of the Commission will then help the parties to review, refine or narrow down the issues…
   > Specifically, the conference can assist in understanding whether any elements of s 32 of the Act can be
   > agreed" (Guide §5.1).

   Likelihood that he asks them to state what remains in dispute and why: **about 85–90%.**
3. **Could he "warn" them?** **Not formally.** No sanction is available at a conference, and costs come only
   after a hearing, on a small scale. In substance, yes: he can give "general guidance", put questions to them
   in front of their Counsel, and give a frank view in a private session, as he did with you on 13 Mar (you
   withdrew parts of your application within a week).
   - A frank view that a position is hard to sustain: **about 40–55%**, depending on their 30 Oct answers. It is
     higher if they refuse s 32(1) or leave ¶27 unparticularised.
   - A formal warning or a decision by him: **under 5%.**
4. **Objectively, on the admissions, the review decision and the October 9A:**
   - injury is effectively not in dispute;
   - s 32(1) should not be;
   - the Regulator's s 32(5)(a) case is hard on this record, but not empty.

   So a fair-minded member **could well form the view you describe.** The weighing under s 32(5) and the medical
   evidence still decide it.
5. **The Guide's own caution:** "The more likely outcome of a conference is that parties will have a better
   understanding of the issues". That is a base rate across all appeals. This matter is unusual (298 of 303
   facts admitted), which is why the concession estimate stays at **about 55% at or after the conference path,
   and 60–65% overall before a contested hearing.**

## 2. What the Commission can and cannot do

| Can | Cannot |
|---|---|
| Call the conference; the parties must attend (s 552A) | Decide the appeal at the conference; the chairing member "will not be the Member who hears the appeal" |
| "Explore and refine any issues in dispute"; "help the parties to review, refine or narrow down the issues"; ask "whether any elements of s 32… can be agreed" | Broker a deal: "No commercial settlement is possible at the conference" |
| Give "general guidance"; ask the Regulator to state its position (your 1 Oct letter asks for this on ¶27 "at the opening of the conference") | Advise a party "about how a party may conduct their case" |
| Put expectations on a party in the room, as on 7 Aug | Summary determination, which is not available |
| Make hearing directions | Order costs at the conference. Costs are "of the hearing" (s 558(3)), on scale C, with up to 1.5× (Reg 2025 s 191) |

**And the Guide's own trigger:** "Following the conference, the Respondent may decide to review its position or
consider conceding the appeal where new information is presented that they may not have yet considered." **The
conference is built to be the Regulator's decision point.**

## 4. What this changes: the conference is where your question gets answered

What you are asking for happens in the **conference**:
- the Commission looks at everything;
- it asks the Regulator why it still objects;
- in substance, it warns them.

A **hearing** goes to a different member, who rules but does not warn. The conference also costs you nothing,
while a hearing costs you about $25,000 for counsel (`2026-10-23_RESPONDENT_SPEND…`).

**One part of the 23 Oct decision rule (`2026-10-23_HEARING_BEFORE_2027_feasibility.md` §2) is revised:**

| Their 30 Oct answer | Earlier rule | Revised |
|---|---|---|
| s 32(1) agreed and most points resolved | Elect hearing | **Elect hearing only if they also say they will defend s 32(5) at hearing regardless.** Otherwise keep the conference and ask for hearing directions at it |
| s 32(1) partial | Elect hearing | **Keep the conference.** Dwyer can test the "partial" in the room. Ask for hearing directions at it |
| Deferred | Keep 21 Jan; hearing directions at it | Unchanged |
| **A fight letter** | Elect hearing | **Keep the conference.** This is when Dwyer's questions are worth most. Ask now that hearing directions be made at it, so no time is lost |

**The counterweight is time.**
- The conference path costs about 2–3 months against an early hearing election.
- That matters only if the about-June-2027 common-law limitation cannot be protected another way.
- **Get the limitation advice (Hall Payne, via Petering) before electing.** If it says speed is essential, the
  hearing path wins.
