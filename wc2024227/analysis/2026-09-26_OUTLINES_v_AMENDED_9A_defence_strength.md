## Reading
- Where the 9A pleads **facts**, the outlines have little or nothing: admitted, conceded by their own witnesses, or not addressed.
- Where the 9A pleads **characterisation** (hostile, reprisal, suppressed, capriciously, forced, reckless, non-delegable), the outlines engage. Their defence is built against the adjectives.
- Their only substantive answers: 1(c) (a documented response process), 1(g) (support offered), 3(c) (fatigue pay clause is overtime-only).

## Weak points in the 9A itself (they will use these)
1. Part C Contention 2: "The Respondent bears the onus under Prizeman" — wrong (appellant bears the onus; Grace, Du Preez).
2. "Independent Review Office (IRO)" — it is the Regulator's Review Unit.
3. "Admitted Fact: Form 24, Para X" tags — numbering drift after row 26; several are wrong.
4. 1(a) pleads delays to "pathology results"; the admitted proof is misrouted calls and the directory.
5. 3(a) commute/"~4 hours sleep" and 3(c) "statutory paid fatigue leave" — assertions without proof; the latter meets Wright's cl 18.10 point.
6. Characterisation language raises the Briginshaw standard and invites s 32(5)(b); Part 3 post-injury items cannot be causes.

## Recommendation
Run the case on the admitted facts. At the conference and in opening/submissions, say once that the Appellant relies on the admitted facts and that the descriptive words in the pleading are not elements he must prove; correct the onus point himself (appellant bears it; uncontradicted evidence). Do not seek to amend now (delay; last amendment cost five weeks). Characterisation goes nowhere; chronology carries it.
