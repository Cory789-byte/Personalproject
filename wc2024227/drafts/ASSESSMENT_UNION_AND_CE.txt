Dear Ms Cridland,

I write in a different capacity to my letter of 3 August, and about a different subject. I
ask for nothing for myself in this letter.

I have worked as a switchboard operator at Logan Hospital. In June 2024 I sustained a
psychological injury. I have since been away from the workplace, and I have experienced
the financial and personal consequences that follow. I mention this only because it is why
I am writing: I know the work of that room from inside it, and I do not want another
operator to arrive where I have arrived.

Switchboard is the hospital's communications hub. Operators are the first point of contact
for emergency codes and for people in distress, they work nights and weekends without
supervision present, and they absorb the consequences when information held elsewhere in
the organisation is out of date. I have raised concerns over several years about rostering,
fatigue, and how complaints are answered. My own experience is one data point. I do not
believe it is the only one.

I therefore ask that the Health Service commission an independent psychosocial risk
assessment of Switchboard Services, under the Managing the risk of psychosocial hazards at
work Code of Practice 2022 and consistently with ISO 45003, with the operators consulted.

So that the request is practical rather than abstract, I have obtained an indicative
proposal from an independent provider, Psychgroup, led by registered psychologists
(attached). Their Psychosocial Risk Infrastructure Audit reviews the organisation's own
data — reporting systems, claims, rosters, policies — and interviews staff across the
relevant functions, reporting against the Code and the ISO standards. The indicative cost
is between $10,000 and $30,000 depending on scope. I have no association with the provider,
and the choice of assessor is a matter for the Health Service; I obtained the proposal only
so that cost and availability would not be unknowns.

I have also prepared, as a worker's contribution, a hazard mapping, a set of proposed
controls and a proposed methodology. They are offered as an input for consultation and can
be confirmed, corrected or discarded by whoever conducts the assessment. I would provide
them to whoever you nominate.

I understand there are matters between the Health Service and me that are being dealt with
through their own processes, and I have deliberately kept them out of this letter. This
request is not about my employment, my claim, or my dispute. It is about the room and the
people who are still working in it.

Yours sincerely,
Cory Lea Shepherd
AO3, Switchboard Services, Logan Hospital
0417 400 227 · coryshepherd1@hotmail.com

WHY THIS WORKS
- ⭐ THE REQUEST COMES FROM THE UNION (organiser-signed, delegate named) = institutional
  correspondence a CE must answer, not an employee letter HR can absorb; engages cl 3.1.5;
  immune to any leverage characterisation; survives his settlement.
- His account annexed = personal testimony carried inside an institutional ask, which is
  where it is strongest.
- Union first = the request gets an owner who survives his settlement; delegate status is
  notified by the union (third-party fact, not self-assertion); collective complaints and
  the HSR election get a proper home.
- CE letter asks for NOTHING personal — no pay, no return, no dispute — so it cannot be
  characterised as leverage and can be actioned without conceding anything.
- The hardship paragraph is standing, not claim: one short factual passage, then the room.
- The quote kills the cost unknown; the "no association / choice is yours" line kills the
  conflict objection; the offered materials make saying yes cheap.
- ⛔ TIMING: nothing to the CE before the Friday mention. Nothing that competes with the
  Stage 2 referral on Monday. Mid-next week, union copied.
