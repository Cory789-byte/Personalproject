**What the chain shows**

- **13 July:** HR (Michelle Harrison, Injury Management) phoned and then wrote to my QSuper case
  manager advising the Health Service is "unable to facilitate" my return — before the further
  medical information they say they need had been requested from my GP.
- **13 July (me):** I asked four things — what was disclosed to the fund and on what authority;
  that the Request for Medical Information to my GP go out within 3 business days with a copy to
  me, and the decision timeframe once his response arrives; that my rostered shifts while held out
  be paid and not taken from my leave; and a response to my standing offer of interim duties.
- **15 July (their letter):** the request to my GP has **no timeframe** ("unable to commit"); pay is
  **refused** on the basis that I "hold a current QSuper Income Protection Claim" (I don't — it is
  closed, and I've asked the fund for written confirmation); interim duties are **refused "in any
  capacity"** pending the un-timetabled GP request; and my situation "**remains at the discretion
  of the Employer**" because I don't have an accepted workers' compensation claim, with reference
  to employees "being managed through **Health Management processes**."

**Issue 1 — the situation is being mischaracterised as a "return to work."** I have been
continuously employed and working my shifts for the past year, on hours I adjusted within my
certified capacity. Throughout that period I kept the fund side correct myself: while the appeal
was ongoing I asked QSuper to assess my payslips and adjust my benefit to partial payments
reflecting the hours I was actually working, and ultimately my income protection payments stopped
at my own request, to prevent overpayment, because I was working. On 3 July at about 2:32pm I provided the Employee Capability
Checklist my GP completed certifying me **fit to work with identified restrictions** — as a serving
employee updating my medical information while finalising my super matters, not as an application
for permission to work. By about 3:30pm the same day — under an hour — I was held out of the
workplace "pending review," with my rostered shifts "paid out of [my] available sick leave." I have
been out since, thirteen days now, with no timeframe. Accurately described, I'm a working employee
**directed not to attend by my employer while certified fit** — not an absent employee requesting
to return. Their letters (including to my super fund) describe me as "a QSuper Claimant request[ing]
to return to duties," which is wrong on both counts.

**Issue 2 — pay and leave.** I am and have been ready, willing and able to work my rostered shifts;
the decision that I not attend is theirs. No stand-down, suspension or other power has been
identified to me, yet the shifts are being debited against my own **sick leave** — leave that exists
to cover incapacity, which on their own file I don't have, and which I have already substantially
exhausted through my illness. Their stated basis for refusing pay — that the fund is responsible —
rests on a claim that is closed, and in any event an insurance benefit isn't wages.

**Issue 3 — the policies and frameworks I believe are being misapplied or ignored.** Their letter
treats the absence of an accepted workers' compensation claim as leaving everything to "the
discretion of the Employer." As I understand it, that is not complete:
(a) **QH's Workplace Rehabilitation Standard (QH-IMP-401-5)** — rehabilitation, suitable duties and
temporary adjustments may be provided to employees **without** an accepted claim where operationally
reasonable, and managers are responsible for identifying such duties;
(b) **QH's Reasonable Adjustment policy (QH-POL-210)**;
(c) the **Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 (Qld)** (including the positive duty in force since
1 July 2025) and **Human Rights Act 2019 s 58**;
(d) **WHS Act 2011 ss 47–49** — consultation. There has been none: the 3 July decision was made and
communicated within the hour, and I haven't been consulted since, even though their letter now
frames the assessment in psychosocial-risk terms.

**Issue 4 — "Health Management processes."** Their letter says the rehabilitation obligations don't
extend to employees "being managed through Health Management processes" — but no one has told me
whether I am in such a process, which one, under what instrument, or who the decision-maker is. If
this is the *Public Sector Act 2022* ch 3 pt 8 pathway, I don't understand how its preconditions
could be met — I was working until directed not to attend, and I hold a current fitness-for-duty
certification.

**Issue 5 — the request to my GP.** No timeframe to send it, no timeframe to decide once answered,
and their letter describes its subject as "the nature of the matters identified by Dr Ma" — which
reads wider than the restrictions themselves. I want any request to my GP copied to me and confined
to my capacity, restrictions and their operational application, and I've said so.

**Issue 6 — the two-year reversal on "psychosocial risk."** The psychosocial concerns in this
workplace were raised **by me**, beginning more than two years ago: fatigue and rest-break breaches
(including the March 2024 rostering that left me a 7-hour break between shifts), unresolved pay
failures, and switchboard workload and communication-flow issues — including in a written grievance
on 7 August 2023 that was dismissed the same day it was lodged, and later in the disclosure I
mentioned in my first email. To my knowledge, no psychosocial risk assessment of the switchboard
was ever undertaken in response to any of it, and I managed my own exposure over the past year by
adjusting my hours within my certified capacity. The first time the Health Service has applied
psychosocial-risk language to this workplace in writing is the 15 July letter — and its subject is
not the hazards I raised, but **me**: whether the "matters identified by Dr Ma" (that is, my
medical condition) "present any psychosocial hazards or risks." Two years on, the hazards remain
unassessed, and the worker who raised them — and was injured — has become the subject of the
assessment. Given you mentioned switchboard hazards are a recurring theme from members, I'd
particularly value the industrial team's view on that sequence.

I had prepared a full response to HR raising all of this (with the policy citations) — I'm holding
it and won't send anything until you and the industrial team have advised. One further matter for
