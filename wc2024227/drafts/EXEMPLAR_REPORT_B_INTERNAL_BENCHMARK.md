## 11. Opinion — causation, s 32(1) *(~3–4 pages; the heart of the report)*

**The question:** whether employment was a significant contributing factor to the injury
of which onset is pleaded at 18 June 2024.

**My opinion: yes.** My reasons follow.

**11.1 The temporal architecture.** In August 2023 Mr Shepherd sought an increase to
full-time hours — conduct consistent with engagement and functional capacity. In November
2023 his general practitioner recorded no psychological illness. The first documented
disturbance of functioning appears in April–May 2024, contemporaneous with and following
the rostering events of March 2024, in self-authored, employer-received correspondence.
Onset is pleaded at 18 June 2024; first medical presentation followed on 1 July 2024 —
an interval of approximately two weeks. This chronology places the development of the
condition squarely within, and following, the occupational sequence, and before every
non-occupational stressor relied upon as an alternative cause.

**11.2 The mechanism.** The assumed facts describe an occupational environment of sustained
high demand (a 24-hour emergency-coordination function), low control (rostering imposed;
processes changed without consultation; a direction to retract raised concerns), and
diminishing support (written safety concerns unanswered for six weeks and refused;
no fatigue risk management system in existence). Sustained exposure to high-demand,
low-control, low-support conditions is an established contributor to depressive illness.
The March 2024 rostering — a 7-hour break within consecutive shifts in a role requiring
sustained vigilance — and the subsequent unresolved correspondence are, in my opinion,
not merely background: they are the identifiable operative stressors under which the
condition developed. The pattern here — an engaged worker who continues to attend, whose
first complaints are about the *conditions* rather than his own health, and who presents
late — is a recognised presentation in high-responsibility occupations.

**11.3 The prodrome and the trajectory.** The April–May 2024 fatigue reports, read with
the diagnosis subsequently made, are in my opinion consistent with the prodromal phase of
the depressive illness. The trajectory — from seeking additional hours in August 2023 to
difficulty attending rostered shifts by May 2024 — is the recognisable course of a
developing depressive disorder rather than evidence of any longstanding condition. I note
partial insight is a feature of the condition itself; late self-recognition is expected,
and was compounded here by the absence of any workplace system monitoring fatigue or
psychosocial risk.

**11.4 Conclusion on s 32(1).** On the assumed facts and the clinical material, employment
was, in my opinion, a significant contributing factor to the development of the condition
to at least June 2024 — on any view of the other matters addressed below. This opinion does
not depend on the resolution of any disputed allegation about individual conduct; it rests
on the rostering sequence, the correspondence, the absence of fatigue management, and the
clinical course.
