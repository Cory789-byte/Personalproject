Dear Manager / Safety and Wellbeing team,

## 1. What I am asking, and what I am not

I ask that Metro South Health commission a **workplace psychosocial risk assessment of Switchboard Services at Logan Hospital** — that is, of **how the work is designed and managed** — under the *Managing the risk of psychosocial hazards at work Code of Practice 2022* and sections 17, 19 and 47–49 of the *Work Health and Safety Act 2011* (Qld).

I am **not**:

- making a personal complaint about any named individual, or asking for any finding about anyone;  
- asking WHS to run or replace the Request for Medical Information (RFMI) process;  
- authorising release of my clinical records to Corporate Services or to this assessment;  
- abandoning my employment or any entitlement.

I **am** engaging with the RFMI and Injury Management process. That process goes to **my** capacity and adjustments. This letter is **parallel**: it concerns assessment of the **department’s work systems**.

On 3 August 2026 I wrote to the Chief Executive under section 89 of the *Public Sector Act 2022* about conflict management, separation of clinical material from operational decision-making, and the fact that the matters treated as “psychosocial” cannot be answered by a medical practitioner writing about me. This letter does not repeat that disclosure. It asks the unit that looks after **WHS / psychosocial risk** to do the assessment that those workplace matters require.

## 2. Why WHS is the right department

Correspondence from Injury Management and Human Resources (including 7, 13, 15 and 29 July 2026 and the RFMI of 31 July 2026) has framed my hold-out and return by reference to:

- whether matters identified on the Employee Capability Checklist present any **psychosocial hazards or risks**;  
- **safe** return / health and safety of workers and others;  
- **Work Health and Safety Act 2011** sections 17 and 19 (and G3 Reasonable Adjustment).

The RFMI letter confirms MSH will meet **reasonable costs of preparing the medical report** about me. That funds a **medical** enquiry. It does **not** fund or substitute for a Code-compliant **psychosocial risk assessment of the work unit**.

The Code identifies psychosocial hazards by reference to **features of work and its management** (including job demands, remote/isolated work patterns, fatigue, organisational justice and change management). Those are **PCBU / department** matters. They are not cured by further diagnosis of one employee.

The letter of 7 July 2026 records that the Health Service did not have sufficient information to understand “unresolved workplace matters involving line management” or to assess whether they present psychosocial hazards. I take that at face value. That gap is one **only the Health Service can close** — by assessing the **workplace**, not by asking my doctor to investigate the department.

## 3. Workplace matters already put on the record (not medical)

I set these out in my letter to the Chief Executive (3 August 2026). They concern **how work is organised and decided**, not the character of any officer:

(a) how **patient-safety concerns** raised at Switchboard are escalated and answered;  
(b) how changes to the **work, roster and on-call arrangements** are introduced, and whether affected staff are consulted;  
(c) **fatigue and recovery** between shifts, and how rostering is managed against it;  
(d) the ability of staff to have genuine **input into decisions** that affect the work (organisational justice under the Code).

None of those can be answered by a medical practitioner writing about me.

## 4. Work-system indicators drawn from my sworn Form 20 and the operational role

I am an AO3 Switchboard Operator at Logan Hospital. In my Form 20 affidavit in WC/2024/227 I have deposed, among other things, to the following **work-system** features (summarised here for WHS; the full affidavit is available on request — I do not attach clinical material):

**Role and demand**

- Switchboard is the hospital’s **communications hub** and a critical part of **patient-safety** response (code activation and paging via SPOK; concurrent systems; high call volume; concurrent urgencies).  
- Operators absorb **complaints and blame** for failures that often originate in upstream roster/directory/departmental systems, not in operator error.  
- The role includes **roster and leave administration** functions and weekend coverage of work that is management-owned on weekdays.

**Rostering and fatigue**

- I was rostered with a **7-hour break** between consecutive shifts (17–18 March 2024), against the Award / FRMS minimum (10 hours, or 8 only by written agreement) — a matter the Regulator’s own Review Decision treated as **unreasonable management action**.  
- Under that exhaustion I was required to manage **life-safety emergency codes** and doctor paging.  
- When I needed recovery, I was required to use **my own personal leave** rather than paid fatigue leave.  
- I had previously raised **WHS concerns about unsafe rostering and fatigue** (including a grievance on 7 August 2023; a rostering error on night shifts was acknowledged in writing).

**Leave as a way of managing the work**

- Leave is not only personal recovery. In this environment, **staff using leave to manage workload, fatigue and the work environment** is a **system signal** (informal fatigue and demand control), not a reason to treat the hazard as located only in one employee’s medical file.  
- MSH’s own Form 29 response (Chief Executive letter K-LM26/729, 5 June 2026) is already on the Commission file and records, among other things, that **fatigue risk management at Switchboard** was implemented only **after 30 June 2024** in connection with an organisational reporting-line change, and that there had been **no consequential changes to operating procedures** over the earlier period. I refer to that letter as MSH’s own record; I do not re-argue it here.

**Change and consultation**

- Operational changes (including restriction of directory/database access and “effective immediately” directives) have been experienced at the console without the consultation the WHS Act contemplates when work affecting health and safety is redesigned.

These are the kinds of data sources the Code expects a **workplace** assessment to use (rosters, leave patterns, demand, complaints vocabulary, incident history, consultation record) — not a further diagnosis of me.

## 5. Independent psychosocial examination of the department

I have engaged with an **independent psychosocial assessment framework / mapping** for Switchboard against the 2022 Code (work-as-designed, work-as-done, rostering/fatigue, leave–demand signals, organisational justice). That material is for **consultation and scoping**. It is **not** a substitute for the Health Service’s own PCBU assessment, and I do not present it as a concluded external audit of Metro South Health unless and until a third-party assessor is commissioned under Health Service control.

I consider a formal assessment **necessary** because:

1. HR/IM have already invoked **psychosocial hazard** and **WHS** in connection with my return;  
2. the operational record (including rostering, fatigue, leave used to manage work, and patient-safety critical demand at Switchboard) points to **work-system** risk; and  
3. holding one employee out of the workplace while those system matters remain unassessed does not discharge the duty to identify and control psychosocial hazards **so far as is reasonably practicable**.

## 6. Separation from the medical process (important)

| Process | Object | Owner | Funding / information |
|--------|--------|--------|------------------------|
| RFMI / medical report | My capacity, restrictions, adjustments **for me** | Injury Management | MSH has stated it will meet reasonable costs of **that report**; clinical material medical-in-confidence |
| This request | Psychosocial risks in **Switchboard work systems** | **WHS / Safety and Wellbeing** (this letter) | PCBU assessment cost; **no clinical file** required |

I ask that:

(a) this assessment **not** be treated as answered by the RFMI medical report;  
(b) **no medical records** about me be sought or shared for the department assessment (system data only: rosters, leave aggregates, demand, procedures, consultation records, de-identified team patterns);  
(c) Corporate Services receive, if anything, **controls and adjustments to implement**, not clinical reasoning about me — consistently with the separation I proposed to the Chief Executive.

## 7. Consultation

Sections 47–49 of the WHS Act require consultation with workers who are, or are likely to be, directly affected when identifying hazards, deciding controls, and proposing changes affecting health or safety. That consultation has **not** occurred with me in relation to the psychosocial characterisation of my hold-out since late June / early July 2026.

I ask to be included in scoping consultation, and that other Switchboard operators and any health and safety representative be offered participation in the ordinary way. My union is available to assist.
