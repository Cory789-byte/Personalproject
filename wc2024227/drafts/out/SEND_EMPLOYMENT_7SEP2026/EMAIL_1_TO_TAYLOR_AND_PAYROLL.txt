WHY I ASK

I have looked for it and have not been able to find one. I attach the instruments so you do
not have to take my word for any of it; the first page lists what each one carries.

Section 94 of the Industrial Relations Act 2016 applies Part 4 of the Act to long service
leave. Section 134(3) provides that "An employee's continuity of service with an employer
is not broken by an absence, including through illness or injury - (a) on paid leave
approved by the employer; or (b) on unpaid leave approved by the employer." Section 93
defines continuous service, for long service leave, simply as the employee's continuous
service with the same employer.

Where the Act intends a period not to count towards service it says so expressly, as it
does for parental leave at section 90(2). It does not say so for approved unpaid leave.

The award and the agreement add nothing further. The award that covers me is the Hospital
and Health Service General Employees (Queensland Health) Award - State 2015: clause 4.1
applies it to the employees described in Schedules 2 to 6 and to "each hospital and health
service, in their capacity as the employer", and Schedule 3 sets out the Administrative
Officer levels including AO3. Clause 22(a) points back to the same Division of the Act. The
agreement is the Queensland Public Health Sector Certified Agreement (No. 12) 2025,
certified 23 December 2025, and clause 9.10.1 points to HR Policy C38, which contains no
exclusion either.

Each of those instruments also gives access at seven years. Award clause 22(c): "Employees
who have completed 7 years' continuous service are entitled to take long service leave on
full pay or half pay." Directive 10/24 clause 8.1: "employees are entitled to take pro rata
long service leave after 7 years continuous service." HR Policy C38 section 1: employees
"may apply for pro rata long service leave on full pay, or half pay, after completing seven
years continuous service". Seven years fell on 25 March 2026.

I accept that under clause 7.1 of the Directive the taking of long service leave is subject
to entity convenience. The same instruments provide that requests are not to be
unreasonably refused, and clause 7.4 requires the employer to respond "in a timely manner
indicating whether the leave applied for has been approved or not".
