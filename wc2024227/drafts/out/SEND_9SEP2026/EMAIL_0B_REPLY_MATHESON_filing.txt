Dear Ms Matheson,

Thank you for your email of this morning, and for confirming that the Regulator will
respond by close of business on Friday 11 September 2026. I agree that is the date: the
notices were served on 28 August 2026 and rule 49(2) runs the 14 days from receipt.

My understanding is that rule 49(1) of the Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011
provides for a notice to admit to be served on the other party, and that the rule is silent
on filing. Rule 50(4), by contrast, requires an affidavit to be filed before it may be used,
so I have taken it that where the Rules require filing they say so. The Commission's
Workers' Compensation Appeal Guide does not deal with notices to admit, and no direction in
this appeal requires them to be filed.

My understanding of the effect of the notices is this. Under rule 49(2), a fact not
disputed within the 14 days is taken to be admitted for this proceeding, and a document whose
authenticity is not disputed is taken to be authentic for this proceeding. On that footing
the admitted facts do not need to be proved by evidence at the hearing, and the documents in
Annexure A whose authenticity is admitted can be tendered without further proof of what they
are. What remains in issue is whatever the Regulator disputes in its response, and that is
what I will need to prove. If my understanding of that is different from yours, I would be
grateful to know.

On timing, I recognise the notice is long, and I want to explain why it takes the form it
does. After the mention on 7 August I withdrew my application for further disclosure and
decided not to seek any more documents. I have instead confined myself to the material
already in the proceeding, and the notice asks the Regulator to admit what that material
shows. Of the 39 documents in Annexure A, 24 are the Regulator's own disclosure or Metro
South Health's production under the notice of non-party production, and the great majority
of the facts are quotations from them; the balance are my own emails and records, included
so the sequence can be followed. The notice narrows what is in issue rather than adding to
it, and for that reason I would not expect the volume to be a difficulty.

If, however, the Regulator needs further time for any part of the notice, I would agree to a
short extension for the facts it identifies as requiring further consideration, on the basis
that it serves its response to the balance by Friday 11 September. I would rather have an
accurate response than a rushed one.

On filing, I would welcome your thoughts on which of two courses you consider appropriate.
One is to forward the Form 24 and Form 25 to the Industrial Registry now, so they are on the
record ahead of the response. The other is to hold them and tender the notices, the response
and the admitted documents as evidence at the hearing, in the ordinary way. If the
Regulator's view is that filing is required now, could you please let me know the rule,
direction or practice you rely on, and I will act on it.

Either way, once the response is received and the authenticity of the documents is settled,
I intend to print the complete set - both notices, the Regulator's response and the Annexure
A documents whose authenticity is admitted - and deliver it to the Registry in person, given
the number of pages and the 30-page limit on electronic filing under Practice Direction 3 of
2021. I would rather get the sequence right than file something that is not called for, and
I am happy to follow whatever you suggest.
