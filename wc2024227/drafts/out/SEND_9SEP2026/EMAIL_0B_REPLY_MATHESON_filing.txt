Dear Ms Matheson,

Thank you for your email of this morning, and for confirming that the Regulator will
respond by close of business on Friday 11 September 2026. I agree that is the date: the
notices were served on 28 August 2026 and rule 49(2) runs the 14 days from receipt.

My understanding is that rule 49(1) of the Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011
provides for a notice to admit to be served on the other party, and that the rule is silent
on filing. Rule 50(4), by contrast, requires an affidavit to be filed before it may be used,
so I have taken it that where the Rules require filing they say so. The Commission's
Workers' Compensation Appeal Guide does not deal with notices to admit, and no direction in
this appeal requires them to be filed.

My understanding of the effect of the notices is this. Under rule 49(2), a fact not
disputed within the 14 days is taken to be admitted for this proceeding, and a document whose
authenticity is not disputed is taken to be authentic for this proceeding. On that footing
the admitted facts do not need to be proved by evidence at the hearing, and the documents in
Annexure A whose authenticity is admitted can be tendered without further proof of what they
are. What remains in issue is whatever the Regulator disputes in its response, and that is
what I will need to prove. If my understanding of that is different from yours, I would be
grateful to know.

If the Regulator's view is that filing is required, could you please let me know the rule,
direction or practice you rely on, and what you suggest. I am content to file the notices
if there is a requirement to do so, or if the Commission would find it useful to have them
on the record - and if so I would propose to file them together with the Regulator's
response, so that the Commission has the complete set at the one time. I would rather get
that right than file something that is not called for.
