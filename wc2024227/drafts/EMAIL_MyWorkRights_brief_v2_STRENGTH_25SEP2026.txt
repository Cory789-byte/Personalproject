MATTER A — WORKERS' COMPENSATION APPEAL, WC/2024/227 (QIRC)
==================================================================
The issue: my psychological injury (onset 18 June 2024) was accepted as arising out of employment, but excluded under s 32(5)(a) as reasonable management action. Review Decision 69983 is dated 24 October 2024, and the appeal is de novo. It is the only issue.

What is already established:
1. The Regulator's own review officer found the rostering of 17–18 March 2024 (a 7-hour break between shifts) to be "unreasonable management action given that it was in direct contradiction to the award and the 8-hour agreement". The same decision found that I sustained a psychological injury, and that it arose out of employment with employment a significant contributing factor.
2. The claim was rejected only by counting: "two out of the three causative factors amounted to reasonable management action", so the injury "mainly arose out of such management action". The Industrial Court has rejected a dominant-cause approach to s 32(5): Blackwood v Mahaffey [2016] ICQ 10 at [53] and [57], and Q-COMP v Hochen [2010] ICQ 10.
3. My notice to admit: the Regulator admitted 298 of 303 facts and denied none. It admits the 7-hour break.
4. The employer's own documents, admitted:
   - the line manager's Director wrote to HR in May 2024 of "a few rostering errors made by Chloe with regards to Cory's line";
   - the line manager apologised in August 2023 for an earlier rest-period "oversight";
   - Metro South HR wrote in July 2026 that the 8-hour agreement "is only applied where staff initiated shift swaps have occurred". The March shifts were rostered, not swapped, so on the employer's own statement the minimum was 10 hours;
   - Metro South told the Commission that fatigue risk assessment at the Switchboard was implemented only after 30 June 2024.
5. The Regulator's case, served 24 September 2026, is four lay witnesses (two managers, two payroll). Its own manager-witness confirms the 7-hour break and calls it "human error". As at today it has served no medical evidence, although it pleads a pre-existing condition. Its time for expert material ends 30 September 2026.
6. My treating psychiatrist's diagnosis is Major Depressive Disorder with anxious distress, which he records as "workplace stress stemming from issues with management and rostering".

Where it is exposed:
- I carry the onus, and the Regulator will argue a global evaluation of the management action (Delaney).
- The rostering breach was in March and onset in June. That interval must be explained through the medical evidence and the course of events after March.
- I have no medico-legal report. My treating psychiatrist has records and a treating report, and has said he does not have capacity for medico-legal work. A report may need to be arranged.

What it needs, and when:
- On or after 1 October 2026 I will ask the Registry to list a second s 552A conference. The Guide describes this as the point where the Regulator may review its position. Representation at that conference is the most useful single step.
- An expert report, if the Regulator serves one by 30 September or maintains its pre-existing contention.
- On disclosure: at the mention on 7 August 2026, Commissioner Dwyer said that if the Regulator accepts a specific document exists but says it is not relevant, I "can come back" to him, and that "looking at a single document" he can say quickly whether it should be disclosed.
