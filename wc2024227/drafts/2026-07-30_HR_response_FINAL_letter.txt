3. WHY THAT SPECIFICITY IS NEEDED

3.1 Your email states the request is made under the Health Service's obligation to assess the medical information "alongside the inherent requirements of the role". I accept that the Health Service has its own obligations. Five things follow.

(a) The assessment is task-based, so the questions should be. QH-POL-210 directs adjustments to enabling an employee to safely perform the genuine occupational requirements of the role, tailored to their circumstances, and reflects the obligation under the Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 (Qld) to make reasonable adjustments for an employee with an impairment unless doing so would impose unjustifiable hardship. QH-IMP-401-5 makes managers responsible for identifying suitable duties, guided by medical information. Each operates at the level of identified duties. A refusal of all duties, without identifying which duty cannot be performed or adjusted, does not engage that assessment - and a question not tied to a duty cannot produce information that either instrument can use.

(b) Assessing a risk requires identifying the risk. EB12 clause 7.2 and the Managing the risk of psychosocial hazards at work Code of Practice 2022 proceed by identifying a hazard, assessing the risk it presents, and considering controls. I am not presently able to identify what risk is said to arise, or from which duty - and without that I cannot meaningfully participate in the assessment, as consultation under sections 47 to 49 of the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (Qld) contemplates.

(c) The checklist is the Health Service's own instrument. It was required by the Health Service and completed by Dr Ma after he reviewed the AO3 role description. If it is now insufficient, the entry said to be insufficient should be identifiable, together with what the form did not capture.

(d) The arrangement is not new. I worked in the role on reduced hours within my certified capacity for approximately twelve months before 3 July 2026, and the Health Service's correspondence of 7 July 2026 records that my restrictions were already being accommodated. Could the request please also identify what has changed - that is, which duty is now said to be affected in a way it was not during that period.

(e) The framework identifies hazards in the work, not in the worker. The Code of Practice identifies psychosocial hazards by reference to features of work and its management - job demands, job control, support, role clarity, change management, workplace relationships and organisational justice. EB12 clause 7.2 correspondingly provides for risk assessments of workplaces, conducted using the Code and in consultation with workers and health and safety representatives. I therefore ask that any assessment framed in psychosocial terms be directed to the work and the work system, and that questions to Dr Ma be confined to my capacity to perform identified duties.
