## Background for topic 1 (8 Sep 2026) — what the codes, results and delays mean. FOR PREPARATION AND CLOSING, NOT FOR ORAL EVIDENCE BEYOND THE OPERATOR'S KNOWLEDGE
- **Urgent (high-risk / critical) pathology result**: a laboratory value that indicates a life-threatening or imminently dangerous state (e.g. critical potassium or glucose, positive blood culture, troponin, INR). Under the national requirements (NPAAC Tier 3A, 2020; RCPA/AACB consensus statement) the laboratory must communicate it to the requesting doctor or nominated delegate within a clinically appropriate, pre-determined timeframe, and must escalate (substitute clinician, then the patient) if it cannot reach them. The Switchboard is the link between the laboratory/doctor and the on-call clinician; a wrong on-call entry stops that chain at the console.
- **Respiratory distress**: a patient who cannot maintain adequate breathing/oxygenation — high or very low breathing rate, low oxygen saturation, rising oxygen requirement, use of accessory muscles, altered consciousness. Causes include pneumonia, sepsis, fluid on the lungs, clot, asthma/COPD, sedation. It is the commonest pathway to respiratory then cardiac arrest, which is why it is a MET trigger.
- **MET call criteria (Queensland, Q-ADDS)**: respiratory rate ≥36 or ≤8; SpO2 <90% not responding to oxygen; oxygen >15 L/min or ≥60%; systolic BP <80; heart rate ≥160 or <40; responds only to pain; Q-ADDS ≥8. Physiological change is usually visible in the 48 hours before an arrest, ICU admission or death — the MET system exists to reach the patient before the Code Blue.
- **What delay means**: for a ventricular-fibrillation arrest, survival falls ~10% for each minute defibrillation is delayed; the standard is a shock within 2 minutes of recognition; delays to CPR, defibrillation and adrenaline each reduce survival in in-hospital arrest. For a MET call, the margin is the interval between the trigger and the team arriving. The Switchboard's minutes are inside that margin.
- Evidence rule: in the box, describe codes as the hospital's procedures define them and the consequence operationally (the team is not paged; the result is not connected). The survival figures and the standards go into closing submissions with the published sources, not into oral evidence.
Sources: ACSQHC, Requirements for the communication of high risk pathology results (2020); RCPA/AACB consensus statement (PMC4745612); Q-ADDS/MET criteria (QAS Q-ADDS assessment; Queensland Health Q-ADDS review); NEJM 2008 delayed defibrillation after in-hospital arrest; Anesthesiology 2019 delays in CPR/defibrillation/adrenaline; ACEM policy P40.

## Closing-submission point recorded 9 Sep 2026 — conditions of work v management action
The admitted facts support two limbs and the outlines carry the facts for both: (1) the DECISIONS (access removed 18 Jul 2023, facts 39–48; on-call 15 Apr 2024, 49/181/273; the directives; rostering 211–223; pay 182–210; retraction 74–84) are management action — argue not reasonable / not reasonably taken (Delaney globally; Mahaffey singly). (2) the CONDITIONS those decisions produced at the console (misdirected emergency calls 56–68, 89–104; corrections waiting on an absent manager; call volume; complaints arriving at and returning to the console — outline topic 1 in the sworn words) are the work itself, not management action, so s 32(5) does not reach them at all if the Commission finds them significant contributors. Authority to verify before use: Granato v WCR [2024] QIRC 162 (injury from the nature of the material itself, not from decisions about it, held outside s 32(5)); the "by the employer" / "against the worker" contrast (Exide Australia v WorkCover Queensland, Hall P). Expect the Regulator to characterise everything as management action to bring it within s 32(5); the answer is that a registrar's misdirected MET call is not an action taken by management in managing the worker. Pleaded in the Second Amended 9A at Contention 2(i)-(ii) and in 1(a). No change to the served outlines: the facts are already there in the right order.
