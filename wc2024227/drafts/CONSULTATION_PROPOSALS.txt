PART 2 — THE SWITCHBOARD OPERATING MODEL (PROPOSED CONTROLS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are offered as controls for discussion in the assessment and under clause 7.1.5. Each
concerns the design of the work, not any person. Implementation cost is near zero: rostering
and process changes only.

A.  PATIENT SAFETY AS THE DESIGN PRINCIPLE. The Switchboard activates the hospital's
    emergency codes and relays urgent clinical results; whether the right clinician is
    reached, and how quickly, depends on the Switchboard and the accuracy of the on-call
    information it relies on. I propose: (i) clear ownership, with a backup, for the accuracy
    of the on-call roster and the paging system; (ii) directory updates restored to a
    workable pathway with a backup when the responsible officer is unavailable; and (iii) a
    defined escalation path for when a department's support officer or the manager cannot
    be reached.

2.  CONSULTATION BEFORE OPERATIONAL CHANGE. No change to Switchboard operating processes
    takes effect until the operators who work the board have been consulted. The Switchboard
    is a real-time environment operating multiple concurrent systems; the operators hold the
    working knowledge of it. This is the consultation the Work Health and Safety Act already
    requires, applied systematically.

3.  A ROTATIONAL, INTEGRATED MANAGER. The manager of Switchboard Services works a regular
    rostered rotation on the board — on the phones, in the room. Management of a fast-paced,
    direct, real-time environment is soundest when management holds direct current knowledge
    of it; presence also ensures supervision and support are available where the work
    happens. This is the standard model in emergency communications settings.

4.  A COMPLAINT-RESPONSE STANDARD. Complaints and escalations sent to Switchboard management
    are acknowledged within one business day and substantively answered within three; where
    the manager is unavailable, the matter escalates automatically to the established Client
    Liaison pathway rather than returning to the operators' console.

5.  ONE RULEBOOK FOR THE ROOM. The manager working in rotation is subject to the same
    frameworks as the operators — rostering rules, minimum notice, fatigue breaks, and leave
    processes — applied consistently. Consistency of process is itself a psychosocial
    control.

6.  A 24/7 ON-SHIFT SUPERVISOR FUNCTION, ROTATING WITHIN THE TEAM. The design principle is
    that in a real-time environment, escalation is delay: a decision the board needs now
    cannot wait on a chain to an off-site or after-hours contact. Authority therefore
    follows the clock. On every shift, one rostered operator holds the shift-supervisor
    function — the real-time decision-maker for operational matters arising on the board —
    while continuing to work the board with the team. The supervisor is not a second
    manager: the manager holds the frameworks, administration and business-hours functions;
    the supervisor holds the shift, so that after hours, on weekends, and at 3am the room
    is never waiting on an escalation that cannot answer in time. Only matters that can
    properly wait are escalated to the manager in hours. The function rotates among
    operators trained and assessed for it, and carries a classification increment or
    in-charge allowance for the shifts on which it is held — a development pathway and
    recognition for competence within the room, with shared capability across several
    trained operators rather than dependence on any one person. This is the standard model
    in emergency communications centres. The cost is a modest per-shift allowance,
    ordinarily offset by reduced turnover and absence.

7.  EVIDENCE-BASED ROSTERING. Rosters designed from the demand data the Switchboard already
    generates — call volumes by hour and day, and emergency-code activity — so that coverage
    matches the actual peaks; with fatigue rules (minimum breaks) built into the roster
    design rather than checked afterwards; published with proper notice; and with equity
    across operators visible on the face of the roster. The data required is already
    collected; this proposal only asks that the roster be built from it.
