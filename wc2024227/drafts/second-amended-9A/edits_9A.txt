@@ 7 | How this Part is arranged.
Each stressor is pleaded so that it can be decided on its own facts. Where one admitted fact matters to more than one stressor, it is repeated on purpose. The repetition does not allege separate injuries and does not count causation twice. It shows the different part the same event plays in each stressor within the whole course of conduct. The medical material is placed between the facts only to show what was reported at the time, the diagnosis, capacity and treatment history. It is not used to turn a clinical history into proof of an event that is otherwise disputed.

@@ 234 | 6. Assessment as a whole, where appropriate.
Delaney v Q-COMP Review Unit [2005] QIC 11; (2005) 178 QGIG 197 supports assessing management action as a whole where its parts are closely enough connected by subject matter, time and the people involved. The Appellant relies on that approach only where the Commission considers the connection close enough. Each stressor is also pleaded on its own, so that the reasonableness question can be answered without bringing in a fact from another stressor.
@@ 235 | 7. The ultimate statutory question concerns the injury as a whole.
Workers' Compensation Regulator v Langerak [2020] ICQ 2 at [86] states that the ultimate question under section 32(5)(a) is whether the injury, not any one stressor, arose out of or in the course of reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way. That requires the reasonable management action to be weighed against the other employment factors, in light of the whole of the evidence. Simon Blackwood (Workers' Compensation Regulator) v Mahaffey [2016] ICQ 10 is consistent with the proposition that, where other employment factors also caused the disorder, the presence of reasonable management action among the causes does not by itself answer the statutory question. The review decision's finding that the rostering of 17 and 18 March 2024 was unreasonable management action is relied on as an admitted document, not as a binding decision.
