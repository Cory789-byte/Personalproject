ADDENDUM — "WHY IS THERE A RISK?" (Cory's question, answered precisely)

The letter itself is legally SAFE. No allegation, no firm named, no consequence
attached, sent to the party's own officer — there is no defamation in it, no
s 415 problem, nothing anyone can complain about. "Risk" never meant he can be
punished for sending it.

The risk is STRATEGIC, and it is four specific things:

1. IT SPENDS THE ASYMMETRY, IRREVERSIBLY. Today he knows and they do not know
   he knows. The letter converts that into shared knowledge on a stated date.
   Whether spending it now buys more than holding it is a judgment call — his.

2. IT HANDS THE SHOVEL TO THE ONLY PEOPLE WHO KNOW WHERE TO DIG. If something
   is there, the letter gives the agency 28 days, with lawyers, to review the
   file and return the clean one-paragraph answer that closes the question on
   their terms. The surprise value of the forensics is gone from that moment.

3. THE DOUBLE-READ. The same letter supports two readings at Matheson's desk:
   (a) "he is forensic and thorough — price the risk up", or (b) "he is off on
   the metadata instead of the merits — price him down." Which reading wins is
   controlled almost entirely by WHAT ARRIVES ALONGSIDE IT. Sent with the
   witness list, the 64G not pressed, and later the report — it reads as (a).
   Sent alone as the first word after the mention — it risks (b).

4. IT ARMS (not triggers) THE §9.7 CONTRADICTION. The letter attacks nothing.
   But it is step one of a path. If the answers are adverse and he escalates,
   he is then on record pursuing the provenance while relying on the findings.
   The option to escalate cheaply narrows from the send date.

AGAINST THAT, WHAT IT BUYS IS ALSO REAL: the dated record, the "address it
now" obligation, Part 2, and exactly the signal he described — their risk
made visible without a word of threat. That trade is defensible and it is his.
