1.  THE MATERIAL

Item 5 of the Respondent's document request of 4 July 2025 sought, in terms,
"an email or text from Mr Shepherd saying that he was going to be late" for the

I make no criticism of the request of 4 July 2025, which was narrow and proper,
and I make no allegation against any person. The material supplied simply
exceeds what was sought, and the excess concerns an identifiable child.

2.  WHY I RAISE IT

The additional content has no connection to any pleaded issue. Paragraph 9
concerns lateness and rostering; the lateness message is within the thread and
I take no issue with that part of it.

Information of this kind sits at the intersection of two areas in which the
legislature has required particular restraint: information tending to identify
a child connected with a domestic and family violence matter (see the
publication restrictions in the Domestic and Family Violence Protection Act
2012, including section 159), and personal information held by an agency, which
the Information Privacy Act 2009 requires to be used and disclosed only within
the privacy principles (including QPP 6) and to be protected, and destroyed or
de-identified when no longer needed for a permitted purpose (QPP 11). As a
public entity, the Regulator is also required to give proper consideration to
the human rights engaged, which here include privacy and reputation (Human
Rights Act 2019, s 25) and the protection needed by a child (s 26(2)).

I do not suggest the Respondent has done anything with this material. My
concern is prospective: that material about a child, unconnected to any issue
in the appeal, should not remain in circulation in the appeal file or find its
way into any brief, outline, or tender.

WHAT THE JANUARY 2024 POLICE INSTRUMENT LIKELY WAS (and why it can stay vague)

Your recollection — "less than a PPN, a must-behave, not a protection order" —
is consistent with the statutory architecture. Under the DFVP Act 2012 a
domestic violence order or police protection notice could not have been made
against your sister at all: she was 15, and the Act does not permit orders
against a child respondent in a FAMILY relationship (child respondents are
reachable only in intimate-personal or informal-care relationships). So
whatever the police issued was necessarily non-DFVP-Act: most likely a caution
or warning under the Youth Justice Act 1992 (Part 2), or an informal
good-behaviour direction recorded in the QPS occurrence. That is EXACTLY why
the email says "no protection order was made" and goes no further — it is true,
it is verifiable, and it does not require naming the instrument or the child.
(Verify the DFVP Act child-respondent limitation against the current
consolidation before relying on it in anything filed.)
