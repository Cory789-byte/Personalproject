# PART II — THE ARCHITECTURAL MOVE

## The problem with the pleading as it stands
It is a **list**: Stressor 1(a)–(g), Stressor 2(a)–(b), Stressor 3(a)–(d). Thirteen items.

⛔ **A list is the shape the Regulator needs.** It picks items off one at a time — *the grievance
was answered; the roster was equitable; the delegate process was followed; the AVAC was corrected*
— and then runs *Delaney*: **globally, the management action was reasonable.** Twelve reasonable
items outvote one unreasonable one. **The list arms the dilution argument.**

## The move
⭐⭐⭐ **Recast thirteen grievances as ONE MECHANISM with ONE ADMITTED INSTANCE.**

> **The mechanism: responsibility imposed, and the means to discharge it withheld.**

| Element | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The function is **critical to patient safety** | Form 24 **¶8** | ⭐ **ADMITTED** |
| Maintaining the directory is **his responsibility** | AO3 Role Description — *"Collate information and maintain Omnivista database… to ensure information held within Switchboard Services is accurate"* | **MSH's own document, served 31 Jul 2026** |
| His **access to the database was removed** | Stibbard, 18 Jul 2023 — *"removing everyone's access to the database"* | Documentary |
| Changes were **imposed without consultation**, though the Role Description requires him to *"support and participate in change initiatives"* | Role Description; 16 Jun 2023 | **MSH's own document** |
| When he raised it, **the response came back at him** | Form 24 **¶21** — directed to retract, 48 hours later | ⭐ **ADMITTED** |
| When he grieved it, he was **sent back to the same manager** | 7 Aug 2023; Form 24 **¶5** | ⭐ **ADMITTED** (the rostering error) |
| ⭐⭐ **The one measurable breach of the employer's own rule** | Form 24 **¶1** and **¶3** — the 7-hour break | ⭐⭐ **ADMITTED** |

⇒ **Not thirteen complaints. One exposure, and one instance of it that the Respondent has admitted
and its own delegate called unreasonable.**

⭐ **This is also what solves the three-month gap** (17–18 March → 18 June 2024). Causation attaches
to a **continuing exposure of which the March roster is the proven instance** — which keeps the
specificity *Mahaffey* requires while explaining the interval, and does **not** hand the Regulator
a global-course case to dilute.

## The anchor, and the thread
⭐ **ANCHOR: the seven-hour break.** It is admitted (¶1), the standard is admitted (¶3), it is
**arithmetic rather than impression**, and the Respondent's own Review Unit characterised it as
unreasonable. **Every other fact is threaded back to it as context, never advanced as an
independent ground.**

# PART IV — THE OUTLINE ITSELF (skeleton, ~45 paragraphs)

## Section 1 — Framing and relief (¶¶1–4)

**¶1 — the loaded issue. Drafted last, placed first.**
> On 17–18 March 2024 the Respondent's insured rostered the Appellant to finish at 23:00 and
> recommence at 06:00. That is a seven-hour break. The minimum was ten hours, or eight by written
> agreement. **Both facts are admitted.** The Appellant was required, on that break, to operate the
> console through which every emergency code at Logan Hospital is activated — a function the
> Respondent admits is critical to patient safety. The Respondent's own Review Unit has already
> characterised the rostering as unreasonable management action. **The single question on this
> appeal is whether an admitted breach of the employer's own minimum rest standard, in a
> patient-safety-critical role, is management action taken in a reasonable way.**

⭐ **Hoffmann move — what the case is NOT about (¶2):** it is not about whether every management
decision over three years was reasonable; not about the Appellant's perception; and not about
whether the Review Decision binds the Commission. It does not.

**¶3 — relief.** **¶4 — posture:** decision under review, s 550(4) de novo, procedural history.

## Section 2 — Issues for determination (¶5)
1. Did the Appellant sustain a **personal injury** (s 32(1))?
2. Was **employment a significant contributing factor**? ⛔ **"A significant" — never "major."**
3. Does **s 32(5)(a)** exclude the injury?
4. If some management action was reasonable, does that **restore** the exclusion?

## Section 3 — Legal framework (¶¶6–14) — *the meta-tool: draft the judgment for the bench*
- **¶6** s 32(1) — the elements, separately stated.
- **¶7** ⛔ **The test is *"a significant contributing factor."*** The *"major significant"* test for
  psychiatric injury was **repealed in 2019**, before this injury. Competing causes need not be
  outweighed.
- **¶8** ⭐ **Onus, conceded squarely.** The Appellant bears it on the balance of probabilities,
  including that s 32(5) does not apply. *(Correcting the filed pleading, which asserted the
  Respondent bore it "under Prizeman" — a proposition Prizeman does not contain.)*
- **¶9** s 32(5)(a) — the two limbs: **reasonable management action**, taken in a **reasonable way**.
- **¶¶10–13** The authorities, in a **hierarchy**: Industrial Court first, then QIRC.
- **¶14** ⭐ **The dilution point, stated as a rule of law:** the presence of reasonable management
  action does not restore the exclusion where unreasonable management action with a sufficient
  causal connection is established; and it is an error to ask whether it was the *"dominant"* cause
  or to outnumber one causative unreasonable stressor with others said to be reasonable.

## Section 4 — Facts, thematically (¶¶15–30) — *the evidence weave*
Four themes. **Not thirteen items.** Each theme: proposition → admission → corroboration → the
concession the Respondent must make.

- **Theme A (¶¶15–18) — The function and its criticality.** ¶8 admitted · Role Description ·
  15–50 codes/24h · 269–444 calls per shift, average 360.
- **Theme B (¶¶19–22) — Responsibility without the means.** Role Description requires him to
  maintain the directory · access removed · changes imposed without consultation against a Role
  Description that requires participation in change.
- **Theme C (¶¶23–27) — ⭐⭐ THE ANCHOR: the seven-hour break.** *(the bind sits here)*
- **Theme D (¶¶28–30) — Raising it produced a response directed at him.** 7 Aug 2023 grievance,
  returned to the same manager · ¶21 retraction direction · the AVAC delay, ¶¶40–41.

### ⭐⭐⭐ ¶¶24–27 — THE LOGICAL BIND. The centrepiece.
> **¶24.** The Respondent admits the Appellant was rostered to a **seven-hour** break (¶1).
> **¶25.** The Respondent admits the minimum was **ten hours, or eight by written agreement** (¶3).
> **¶26.** The Respondent **positively asserts** that in June 2020 the Appellant **signed an
> agreement permitting an eight-hour break** (¶4 — pleaded as a denial *"as untrue"*, which is an
> affirmative case the Respondent must prove).
> **¶27.** ⭐⭐ **The Respondent's affirmative case does not answer the breach. It only changes its
> size.**
>
> | If ¶4 is made out | If ¶4 is not made out |
> |---|---|
> | Minimum **8 hours**. Break was **7**. **Short by 1 hour** | Minimum **10 hours**. Break was **7**. **Short by 3 hours** |
>
> **On the Respondent's own best case, the break was short. There is no version of the admitted
> facts in which it was not.** The Respondent must either produce a 2020 agreement said to govern a
> permanent full-time employee four years later — or accept the ten-hour minimum. **Either route
> ends at a breach.**

⭐ **This is what replaces the current overstatement.** The pleading presently asserts a ten-hour
minimum as an *"Admitted Fact"* when ¶4 denies it. **Conceding the contest and pleading the bind is
stronger than asserting the contest away** — and it cannot be attacked, because it runs on the
Respondent's own pleading.

⭐ **Understatement discipline for ¶23:** *"The Appellant finished at 23:00 and recommenced at
06:00. The commute is approximately two hours each way. No fatigue assessment was conducted. On
19 March 2024 the application for fatigue leave was refused."* **No characterisation. Let the
bench do the arithmetic.**

## Section 5 — Application (¶¶31–40) — *layered*
- **¶¶31–32 · LAYER 1 (primary).** The seven-hour break is management action, and it was not taken
  in a reasonable way: it breached the employer's own minimum on any view (¶27), in a
  patient-safety-critical role, after the Appellant had raised the identical risk in writing on
  7 August 2023, and with fatigue leave refused the following day.
- **¶¶33–35 · LAYER 2 (alternative).** Even if the seven-hour break stands alone as reasonable, the
  exclusion still fails: the pleaded course, evaluated in all the circumstances, was not reasonable
  management action taken in a reasonable way.
- **¶36 · LAYER 3 (residual).** To the extent any part of the pleaded course is not **management
  action** at all, s 32(5)(a) has nothing upon which to operate.
- **¶¶37–38 · The pre-emptive strikes.** ⭐ **Both raised by us, first:**
  - **Reality, not perception** — *Prizeman*. The case is grounded in **admitted** facts (¶¶1, 3, 5,
    8, 14, 21, 40, 41). A misperception case has nothing to attach to: **the break is arithmetic.**
  - **Competing causes** — the 2024 cessation of employment (**employer conduct, later reversed**)
    and the litigation **both post-date the 18 June 2024 onset** and cannot bear on causation.
    ⭐ **And s 32(1) requires employment to be *a* significant factor, not the only one.**
- **¶¶39–40 · Concessions, made openly.** Much of the management action was reasonable. The Review
  Decision does not bind. The Appellant does not say every decision over three years was
  unreasonable. ⭐ **He does not need to** — that is precisely the point at ¶14.

## Section 6 — Admissions and evidentiary tension (¶¶41–44)
- **¶41** — the admissions table: ¶1, ¶3, ¶5, ¶8, ¶14, ¶20, ¶21, ¶40, ¶41, ¶46.
- **¶42** ⭐ **¶8 was admitted with no exhibit at all** — the patient-safety criticality is
  established without a document.
- **¶43** — the Review Decision: **contents admitted (¶37)**, characterisation for the Commission
  de novo. ⚠ *"tension"*, never *"contradiction"*.
- **¶44** ⭐ The Respondent's own Review Unit reached its conclusions in **October 2024** — before
  the appeal existed, on the Respondent's own material. **Stated once. Not pressed.**

## Section 7 — Relief (¶45)
