1. THE FRAMEWORK THE HEALTH SERVICE HAS INVOKED

The letters cite Human Resources Policy G03 (QH-POL-210) Reasonable adjustment, and sections
17 and 19 of the Work Health and Safety Act 2011. I accept both as the applicable framework.
It is worth recording what that framework provides, because it answers much of what is in
issue.

G03 states its purpose as "to assist employees requiring support to meet the genuine
occupational requirements of their role by applying principles of reasonable adjustment." At
clause 1.1 it provides that temporary reasonable adjustments exist so that employees "continue
work during their recovery." At clause 1.1.1 it confirms that workplace rehabilitation support
is available to employees "who have an injury/illness impacting their current ability to
perform their role, with no claim or entitlement to compensation (health management case)."
At clause 1 it provides that "Managers are responsible for ensuring work environments and
work, do not unreasonably prevent employment for people."

The policy therefore contemplates that an employee in my position remains at work, with
adjustments, while the assessment is undertaken. It does not contemplate exclusion pending
assessment, and I have not been directed to any provision which does.

G03 also provides, at clause 1: "When applying this policy, proper consideration must be given
to the Managing the risk of psychosocial hazards at work Code of Practice 2022, to ensure the
process of providing reasonable adjustments in the workplace, identifies and manages
psychosocial hazards and risks."

That obligation attaches to the process itself, and I ask that it be given effect.

THE TEST I HAVE APPLIED

The letter to Dr Ma states the purpose of the request: "to identify and implement any
appropriate workplace controls or reasonable adjustments to support a safe and sustainable
return to his substantive position."

G03 clause 3 sets the same limit: "Reasonable adjustment may need to be supported by medical
or rehabilitation information, where necessary, to either identify required workplace
adjustments and/or assess the suitability of proposed adjustments."

I have therefore asked of each question: is this information necessary to identify required
adjustments, or to assess the suitability of proposed adjustments? Where it is, I will answer
it. Where it is not, I ask that it be withdrawn or reframed, and I have proposed an
alternative in each case.

I record that section 124(1) of the Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 provides that a person "must
not ask another person, either orally or in writing, to supply information on which unlawful
discrimination might be based," and that under section 124(3) the defence requires the
respondent to prove "that the information was reasonably required for a purpose that did not
involve discrimination." Impairment is a protected attribute. I raise this not as a complaint
but so the Health Service can satisfy itself that each question it presses meets that
standard.

I am not declining to provide information. I am asking that the questions be directed to the

Q1(a)  "When was Mr Shepherd first diagnosed with MDD?"
       → The date of a historical diagnosis cannot identify a required adjustment, nor assess

Q1(c)  Whether the stressors are based "solely on Mr Shepherd's self-report, your own clinical
       assessment, or other medical information/reports available to you."
       → This goes to the reliability of the causal history rather than to any adjustment, and
         does not meet the limit in G03 clause 3. I ask that it be withdrawn. To the extent
         the question seeks to identify other medical reports concerning me held by third
         parties, I do not consent to that inquiry.

Q2     Whether I am "able to follow a reasonable and lawful direction issued by [my]
       supervisor" and can "participate in discussions in relation to [my] workplace

         entitled to be told of it and given particulars, and to procedural fairness — or

         PROPOSED INSTEAD: "Does the employee have capacity to participate in ordinary
         workplace meetings and supervision, and are any supports required?"

Q3     Whether I am "medically fit to return to their substantive role under the existing
       reporting arrangements, including working with and reporting to their current line
       manager."
       → This asks a treating doctor to certify that a workplace arrangement is safe. Under
         section 19 of the Work Health and Safety Act that duty rests with the Health Service,
         and under the Managing the risk of psychosocial hazards at work Code of Practice 2022
         a psychosocial hazard is a feature of the work, to be identified and controlled by
         the person conducting the business or undertaking. G03 requires proper consideration
         of that Code in applying this process. The assessment sought is a workplace risk
         assessment, not a medical opinion.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: "Are any adjustments recommended in relation to supervision
         arrangements, and if so, what are they in functional terms?" — together with a
         workplace risk assessment conducted by the Health Service, as proposed at paragraph
         9(f) of my letter.

Q6     Whether I am "able to fulfill the full inherent requirements of [my] Role ... without
       restrictions or modifications to duties."
       → This applies the wrong test. G03 states its purpose as assisting employees "to meet
         the genuine occupational requirements of their role by applying principles of
         reasonable adjustment," and at clause 3 provides that adjustments "should support the
         employee to undertake the genuine occupational requirements of their role, without
         risk to their own health and safety or that of others in the workplace." Section 34
         of the Anti-Discrimination Act is to the same effect. Neither asks whether an
         employee can perform the role without adjustment.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: "Can the employee perform the genuine occupational requirements of
         the role with the adjustments you have recommended?"

Q9     "If we are not able to accommodate the restrictions you have recommended, is Mr
       Shepherd able to safely return to the workplace?"
       → This presupposes an inability to accommodate before any accommodation has been
         assessed. G03 clause 2 provides that unjustifiable hardship "is tested against the

         onus is on Queensland Health, as the employer, to prove an adjustment is
         unreasonable, not on the person to prove that it is reasonable." The question asks my
         doctor to answer the final step first, on a hypothetical the Health Service has
         constructed and not yet tested, and on which the onus is not mine. I ask that it be
         withdrawn as premature.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: once the Health Service has assessed the recommended adjustments
         against the whole organisation, if it considers any cannot be accommodated, I ask
         that it identify which and its reasons, so that the question can then be put
         properly.
