1.  WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is a request under clause 10.3.2 of the certified agreement for a change in the way I work.

I make it as a separate document, rather than within my response to the Request for Medical
Information of 31 July 2026, so that there is no question about what it is or when it was
received.

Clause 10.3.2 provides that, in accordance with the Industrial Relations Act 2016, an employee
"may ask the employer for a change in the way the employee works, including the employee's
ordinary hours of work". Section 27(1)(c) of that Act provides that the request may be for "a
change to the way the employee works, for example, the use of different equipment as a result of
a disability, illness or injury".

Clause 10.3.3 requires that the request be in writing, state the change in sufficient detail to
allow a decision to be made, and state the reasons. Parts 2 and 3 below do each of those things.

I make the request under two separate limbs of section 27(1), and I ask that they be considered
separately.

  (a)  ORDINARY HOURS — Part 2.1. This is made under section 27(1)(a), which concerns "the
       employee's ordinary hours of work", and under clause 10.3.2, which is in the same terms.
       That limb stands on its own. It does not depend on illness or injury, and neither section
       27, nor section 28, nor clause 10.3 makes medical information a precondition to making
       such a request or to deciding one. My reasons for it are matters of rostering history and
       are set out at Part 3.

  (b)  THE WAY I WORK — Parts 2.2 to 2.5. These are made under section 27(1)(c), which concerns
       "a change to the way the employee works, for example, the use of different equipment as a
       result of a disability, illness or injury".

I separate them because a decision on one is not a decision on the other, and because the
question of my ordinary hours does not require, and should not await, information sought for a
different purpose. If the Health Service is not in a position to decide (b) at present, I ask
that it decide (a), and say so.

2.  THE CHANGE I AM ASKING FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1  MY ORDINARY HOURS

That my rostered pattern continue at the level at which it has in fact operated over the twelve
months preceding the Employee Capabilities Checklist of 3 July 2026.

The Employee Capabilities Checklist describes that level, at Additional Comments 3, as a
"reduced pattern of approximately six shifts per fortnight", and records that it "is the pattern
Mr Shepherd has in fact worked and tolerated over the past twelve months without deterioration,
and maintaining rather than increasing it best supports recovery while minimising risk of
exacerbation". The first page records the arrangement as a "continuation of existing
arrangement".

I am not asking for any reduction from the pattern I have been working. I am asking that the
existing pattern continue. It was set by the Health Service, worked by me, and sustained across
twelve months. No concern was raised with me at any time that it was insufficient, and no request
was made that I work more.

I record for completeness that my substantive appointment is full-time at 76 hours per fortnight,
and that the pattern described above is less than that. It is, however, the pattern the Health
Service in fact rostered and accepted throughout the twelve months in question, and the Employee
Capabilities Checklist describes it as a "continuation of existing arrangement". Clause 10.3.2
expressly contemplates a request concerning "the employee's ordinary hours of work", which is why
I have made the request under that clause rather than assuming the position.

If the Health Service takes the view that the requirements of the role cannot be met on that
pattern, I ask that it identify what changed, and when, given that the pattern operated for twelve
months without any concern being raised with me.

The Health Service holds the roster and payroll records from which the precise figure can be
established. I ask that it be established from those records, and that the figure so established
be the one used.

I also record that a temporary reduction of hours was raised with me by the Health Service
itself, as a form of support, in the course of a discussion recorded in writing on 30 June 2025.
I did not take it up at the time. I mention it only to show that an adjustment of this kind has
been contemplated within Switchboard Services before, and is not a novel proposal.

2.2  THE WAY I WORK — ATTENDANCE CONFIRMATION

(a)  A telephone call one hour before each rostered shift, to confirm that I am attending.

(b)  If that call is not answered, or attendance is not confirmed, a second call.

(c)  If attendance is still not confirmed after the second call, the on-call operator is
     contacted and cover is arranged for the shift.

The measure is contingent, not standing. The on-call is engaged only if the two calls do not
produce a confirmation, so it carries no cost on any shift I attend. On the shifts I do attend,
which is most of them, the arrangement costs one telephone call.

An on-call arrangement already exists in the unit. On 15 April 2024 the team was advised that
after-hours on call had been added, with the days highlighted on the roster and a contact process
for urgent matters. What I am asking for is the application of a mechanism Switchboard Services
already operates, not the creation of a new one.

To be clear about what this is and is not: it is a confirmation of attendance, of the kind any
roster requires, made early enough to be useful. It is not an assessment of my condition, and I
am not asking that colleagues on shift observe, assess or report on my mental state. That would
place a burden on staff who are not qualified to carry it, would require my health information to
be known to co-workers, and would itself be a workplace stressor. No colleague needs to know
anything about my health for (a) to (c) to work. They need only know whether the shift is
confirmed.

2.3  THE WAY I WORK — IN-SHIFT ESCALATION

A defined escalation pathway which I may initiate during a shift if my condition is acutely
exacerbated — including who assumes the board and how the shift is closed out — so that the
response is planned rather than improvised.

2.4  THE WAY I WORK — COMPLAINT HANDLING

Continuation of the existing arrangement recorded in the Health Service's own correspondence:
immediate escalation of complaints received by Switchboard staff to the Client Liaison Officer
and/or the Manager, Switchboard Services. This is the Health Service's established process and it
requires no change to anything.

2.5  THE WAY I WORK — CORRESPONDENCE ROUTING

That injury management correspondence concerning me be directed through the Injury Management
team, Human Resources. This is what the Employee Capabilities Checklist itself recommends at
Additional Comments 4(c): "return-to-work coordination and correspondence through the Injury
Management team, Human Resources."

2.6  REVIEW

That the arrangement be reviewed at six weeks, or earlier if either party considers it necessary.

I am willing to discuss any of the above, and any alternative the Health Service or my treating
practitioners consider more appropriate. If the Health Service would prefer a graduated
commencement, I am willing to return on a graduated basis by agreement.

3.  THE REASONS FOR THE CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1  The pattern was sustained out of my own leave, and that is what ran out. The roster was
designed by the Health Service. Where my condition was exacerbated I managed it myself, by sick
or annual leave; no adjustment was sought and none was provided. That is why, on 3 July 2026, my
paid sick leave was exhausted — in the course of managing, without assistance, a condition
arising in the workplace.

3.2  The measures replace that with a process. They do not change the roster; they replace an
arrangement under which exacerbations are absorbed by my leave balances with one under which they
are managed. Distress rises as a rostered shift approaches, and there have been occasions where I
have called in at short notice; a late call leaves the board short and the preceding shift unable
to leave. The sequence at 2.2 establishes non-attendance an hour ahead instead.

3.3  It is directed at the only matter ever raised with me — attendance and lateness. No concern
about the quality of my work has ever been put to me, and the correspondence of 31 July 2026
records that the Health Service is not aware of any performance or conduct matters raised for
management. Had these measures been in place, they would have addressed a substantial part of the
attendance history in the correspondence of 8 September 2025.

3.4  The applicable policy contemplates exactly this. G03 clause 1.1 provides temporary
adjustments so an employee may "continue work during their recovery", and clause 1 makes managers
responsible for ensuring work environments "do not unreasonably prevent employment for people".
The adjustment is the thing that allows work to continue while recovery occurs.

4.  THE DECISION, AND WHO SHOULD MAKE IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Clause 10.3.4 provides that the Health Service may grant this request, grant it in part or
subject to conditions, or refuse it. Clause 10.3.5 provides that it may grant it in part or
subject to conditions, or refuse it, "only on reasonable grounds".

I ask that the decision be made by an officer outside Corporate Services.

I do not ask that Corporate Services be removed from the management of my employment, and I make
no allegation against any individual. Part of what this request concerns is the reporting
arrangements within Corporate Services, and the officers who would otherwise decide it sit within
those arrangements. Clause 1.2 of the Code of Conduct for the Queensland Public Service provides
that a conflict of interest is not wrongdoing in itself, but that failing to disclose and manage
one is likely to be wrongdoing. Section 89 of the Public Sector Act 2022 is to the same effect.

The separation I propose is the ordinary one: the clinical material held by Injury Management on
a medical-in-confidence basis; the decision made outside Corporate Services; implementation —
rostering, and putting in place whatever is decided — retained within Corporate Services. The
officer steps out of the decision, not out of the workplace. This requires no restructuring and
no finding to be made about anyone.

I ask that the officer who makes the decision be identified to me, together with the source of
the delegation relied on.

5.  THE FRAMEWORK THE DECISION SITS IN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I set this out for one reason only. Clause 10.3.5 permits the Health Service to grant the request
in part or subject to conditions, or refuse it, "only on reasonable grounds". Grounds are
reasonable against the whole of the law that applies, not against the agreement alone. If the
request is refused in whole or in part, I would like to be able to understand the reasons given
under clause 10.3.6 by reference to the same framework the Health Service is applying.

5.1  THE OBLIGATION

The Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 provides at section 15(1) that a person must not discriminate
"in any variation of the terms of work", or "by treating a worker unfavourably in any way in
connection with work". Section 15(2) provides that "dismissing includes ending the particular
work of a person by forced retirement, failure to provide work or otherwise".

Section 11 provides that indirect discrimination happens if a person imposes a term with which a
person with an attribute does not or is not able to comply, and which is not reasonable.
Impairment is a protected attribute under section 7.

5.2  THE EXEMPTIONS, AND WHAT THEY REQUIRE

Sections 35 and 36 provide that it is not unlawful to discriminate on the basis of impairment
where the person would require special services or facilities and supplying them would impose
unjustifiable hardship, or where the circumstances of the impairment would impose unjustifiable
hardship.

Those provisions are exemptions, and each of them turns on unjustifiable hardship. Section 5
provides that whether something would impose unjustifiable hardship depends on all the relevant
circumstances of the case, including the nature of the services or facilities, their cost and the
number of people who would benefit or be disadvantaged, the financial circumstances of the
person, the disruption that supplying them might cause, and the nature of any benefit or
detriment to all people concerned.

HR Policy G03 puts the same test in the Health Service's own words, at clause 2: unjustifiable
hardship is "tested against the whole organisation, not a division or unit within the
organisation", and "the onus is on Queensland Health, as the employer, to prove an adjustment is
unreasonable, not on the person to prove that it is reasonable".

I would add that the Health Service is a public entity for the purposes of the Human Rights Act
2019, and section 58 of that Act requires a public entity to act and make decisions compatibly
with human rights and to give proper consideration to relevant human rights in doing so.

5.3  WHY I RAISE IT, AND WHAT I AM NOT DOING

I have made no complaint under the Anti-Discrimination Act, and this letter is not one. I make no
allegation against anyone and I ask for no finding to be made about anyone.

I raise it because the measures at Part 2 are modest ones, and I would like the reasons for any
refusal to engage with that. On the shifts I attend, the arrangement at 2.2 costs one telephone
call. The roster at 2.1 is the roster the Health Service already sets. The complaint-handling
arrangement at 2.4 is the Health Service's own established process. The correspondence routing at
2.5 is what the Employee Capabilities Checklist itself recommends.

If the Health Service considers that any of the measures would impose unjustifiable hardship, I
ask only that it identify which measure, and what the hardship is. That is the same thing clause
10.3.6 requires, expressed in the language of the Act.

6.  THE TIME FOR THE DECISION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Clause 10.3.6 provides:

     "The employer must give the employee written notice about its decision within 21 days after
     receiving the request. If the employer decides to grant the request in part or subject to
     conditions or to refuse the request, the written notice about the decision must state the
     reasons for the decision, outlining the reasonable grounds for granting the request in part
     or subject to conditions or for the refusal."

Section 28(3) of the Industrial Relations Act 2016 is in the same terms. Section 28(4) provides
that a notice granting the request in part or subject to conditions, or refusing it, must state
the written reasons outlining the reasonable grounds, and must state that the Commission has
jurisdiction to hear and decide a dispute over the request under chapter 6. Section 29 provides
that if the written notice is not given within 21 days after the request is received, the
employer is taken to have decided to refuse the request.

Twenty-one days from the date of this letter is Monday 24 August 2026.

I record these provisions because a date has not been available in this matter at any stage. I
have asked several times to be told when I would know whether I am returning to work, and I have
not been given a date. The certified agreement provides one, and I would much prefer that the
request be dealt with by agreement well before it.
