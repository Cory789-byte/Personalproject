CATEGORY 3 — QUESTIONS I ASK BE WITHDRAWN OR REFRAMED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In each case I have proposed an alternative which serves the stated purpose. I am not
declining to provide information; I am asking that the questions be directed at the decision
the Health Service says it is making.

Q1(a)  "When was Mr Shepherd first diagnosed with MDD?"
       → The date of first diagnosis does not bear on what adjustments are appropriate in
         August 2026. It does bear on matters in issue in WC/2024/227. I ask that it be
         withdrawn.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: "What is the employee's current condition and prognosis, and over
         what period are the recommended adjustments expected to be required?"

Q1(c)  Whether the stressors are based "solely on Mr Shepherd's self-report, your own clinical
       assessment, or other medical information."
       → This goes to the reliability of the causal history and not to any adjustment. It is
         squarely a matter in issue in the appeal. I ask that it be withdrawn.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: nothing further is required — Q1(d) and Q8 obtain everything needed
         to identify controls.

Q2     Whether the employee can "follow a reasonable and lawful direction issued by his
       supervisor" and "participate in discussions in relation to his workplace performance
       and/or conduct."
       → These are not questions about capacity for the role. They are questions about
         readiness for a management process. Either there is a performance or conduct process
         on foot concerning me, in which case I am entitled to be told of it and given
         particulars; or there is not, in which case the questions serve no purpose in a
         reasonable adjustment inquiry. Please confirm which is the case.
         The premise of the question is separately incorrect and is addressed in my letter.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: "Does the employee have capacity to participate in ordinary
         workplace meetings and supervision, and are any supports required?"

Q3     Whether the employee is medically fit to return "under the existing reporting
       arrangements, including working with and reporting to their current line manager."
       → This asks a treating doctor to certify that a workplace arrangement is safe. That is
         a risk assessment, and it is the Health Service's function, not my doctor's. Section
         19 of the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 places that duty on the Health Service, and
         clause 7.2.1 of the Queensland Public Health Sector Certified Agreement No. 12
         provides the mechanism — a risk assessment of the workplace, requested through a
         consultative forum by either the employer or a union party. The Psychosocial Hazards
         Code of Practice 2022 is to the same effect: the hazard is a feature of the work, and
         the duty to assess and control it sits with the person conducting the business.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: "Are any adjustments recommended in relation to supervision
         arrangements, and if so, what are they in functional terms?" — together with a
         workplace risk assessment conducted by the Health Service.

Q6     Whether the employee can "fulfil the full inherent requirements of his Role ... without
       restrictions or modifications to duties."
       → This applies the wrong test. Neither HR Policy G3 nor section 34 of the Anti-
         Discrimination Act 1991 asks whether an employee can perform the role without
         adjustment. They ask whether the employee can perform the genuine occupational
         requirements with reasonable adjustments, and whether those adjustments would impose
         unjustifiable hardship. A question framed as "without restrictions or modifications"
         inverts the policy the Health Service says it is applying.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: "Can the employee perform the genuine occupational requirements of
         the role with the adjustments you have recommended?"

Q9     "If we are not able to accommodate the restrictions you have recommended, is Mr
       Shepherd able to safely return to the workplace?"
       → This presupposes an inability to accommodate before any accommodation has been
         assessed. The sequence under HR Policy G3 is: identify the restrictions, assess what
         adjustments are available, and only then determine whether any cannot be made and
         why. The question asks my doctor to answer the last step first, on a hypothetical the
         Health Service has constructed and not yet tested. I ask that it be withdrawn as
         premature.
         PROPOSED INSTEAD: once the Health Service has assessed the recommended adjustments,
         if it considers any cannot be accommodated, I ask that it identify which, and the
         reasons, so that the question can be put properly at that point.
