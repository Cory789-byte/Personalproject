2.  THE PROPOSAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I have been held out of the workplace since 3 July 2026 without wages, with my accrued leave
debited, and self-funding the medical costs of this process. G03 clause 1.1 contemplates
temporary adjustments which allow an employee to "continue work during their recovery," and
clause 1.1.1 confirms that workplace rehabilitation support is available to employees "who have
an injury/illness impacting their current ability to perform their role, with no claim or
entitlement to compensation (health management case)." Clause 1 provides that "Managers are
responsible for ensuring work environments and work, do not unreasonably prevent employment for
people."

The Health Service's Workplace rehabilitation standard (QH-IMP-401-5, 7 January 2026) is also
engaged. It provides that the line manager or delegate shall "identify and actively seek ...
meaningful suitable duties, and ensure suitable duties are made available wherever reasonably
practicable"; that the line manager "is best placed to be the primary contact for the worker
wherever possible and facilitate the injured workers on-going connection with the workplace";
that "regular, transparent and timely communication" be maintained with the worker "whether at
work or off work"; and that the worker be informed of "the appropriate dispute resolution
process to follow in such cases where the worker does not agree with elements of a proposed
workplace rehabilitation plan or suitable duties program". The standard applies to a worker
requiring rehabilitation "for either a work or non-work-related injury/illness".

I have been offered no suitable duties — interim duties were declined in any capacity. I have
been held away from the workplace rather than connected to it. I have not been informed of any
dispute resolution process. I ask that each of those be addressed.

I also record that I was not consulted before the decision of 3 July 2026, or since. Section 47
of the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 requires consultation with workers who are "directly
affected by a matter relating to work health or safety". Section 48 requires that I be given a
reasonable opportunity "to contribute to the decision-making process relating to the matter".
Section 49 requires consultation when identifying hazards and assessing risks, when deciding
how to eliminate or minimise them, when proposing changes that may affect the health or safety
of workers, and when making decisions about the procedures for monitoring the health of
workers. The present request is a procedure for monitoring my health.

I propose the following. Each item is within the Health Service's existing processes and, so
far as I can see, requires no material expenditure and no restructuring.

  (a) RETURN TO DUTY. That I return to my rostered shifts now, on the restrictions certified in
      the Employee Capabilities Checklist of 3 July 2026, while the balance of this inquiry is
      completed. If the exclusion is maintained, that the instrument relied on be identified,
      that the period be treated as paid special leave, and that the leave debited since 3 July
      2026 be reversed.

  (b) COMPLAINT HANDLING. That the Health Service confirm in writing that complaints received
      by Switchboard staff are escalated in accordance with the process it has itself
      described. This requires no change to anything.

  (c) ROSTER PREDICTABILITY. That my roster be published in accordance with the certified
      agreement and not altered without my agreement other than in an emergency.

  (d) ORDINARY PROCEDURAL FAIRNESS. That any concern about my performance or conduct be raised
      with me in writing, with particulars, in accordance with the applicable directive, and
      that I be afforded a support person.

  (e) A SEPARATE CONTACT POINT. That leave, injury management and human resources matters
      concerning me be handled by an officer outside Switchboard Services rather than by my
      line manager, who wrote to me on 2 July 2026 concerning leave types and my return to
      duty. This does not alter day-to-day supervision and involves no change to reporting
      lines.

  (f) WORKPLACE RISK ASSESSMENT. As set out at Part 4.2.

  (g) REVIEW. That the arrangements be reviewed at six weeks, or earlier if either party
      considers it necessary.

  (h) GRADUATED RETURN. If the Health Service prefers, I am willing to return on a graduated
      basis by agreement. I am not seeking to avoid any part of my role.

If any part of this proposal cannot be accommodated, I ask that the Health Service identify
which part and its reasons, having regard to G03 clause 2.

I record these subject to two qualifications I do not waive: identifying hazards and determining
controls is a function of the Health Service under Part 3.1 of the Work Health and Safety
Regulation 2011, not of the worker; and the controls that are appropriate are ultimately a matter
for clinical opinion, which question 1(d) of the Health Service's own request seeks.

  (a) A telephone call one hour before each rostered shift, to confirm I am attending; if
      unanswered or attendance is not confirmed, a second call; if still not confirmed, the
      on-call operator is contacted and cover arranged. The measure is contingent, not standing:
      on the shifts I attend — most of them — it costs one telephone call, and where I am not
      attending, that is established an hour ahead rather than at start time. An on-call
      arrangement has existed in the unit since 15 April 2024; this applies it, and would have
      addressed a substantial part of the attendance history recorded in the correspondence of
      8 September 2025. It is a confirmation of attendance, not an assessment of my condition —
      no colleague needs to know anything about my health for it to work.

  (b) A defined escalation pathway I may initiate during a shift if my condition is acutely
      exacerbated — who assumes the board, how the shift is closed out — so the response is
      planned rather than improvised.

  (c) Continuation of the existing complaint-handling arrangement recorded in the Health
      Service's own letter: immediate escalation to the Client Liaison Officer and/or Manager.
      This requires no change to anything.

  (d) Continuation of the rostered pattern that has applied over the preceding twelve months.
      The Checklist records it as "the pattern Mr Shepherd has in fact worked and tolerated over
      the past twelve months without deterioration" and a "continuation of existing arrangement".
      The Health Service set that roster; where my condition was exacerbated I managed it myself,

      If it is now said the role requires something different, I ask what changed, and when.

  (e) Injury management correspondence directed through the Injury Management team, consistent
      with the Checklist's own recommendation.

The only conduct matter ever raised with me is attendance and lateness; the measures at (a) and
(b) are directed at precisely that. If any measure cannot be accommodated I ask that the Health
Service identify which and why, noting G03 clause 2: unjustifiable hardship is "tested against
the whole organisation, not a division or unit", and "the onus is on Queensland Health, as the
employer, to prove an adjustment is unreasonable".

4.  THE STATUS OF THIS PROPOSAL — A REQUEST UNDER CLAUSE 10.3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

So that the Health Service is not left to determine for itself what kind of document this is, I
identify the provision the proposal is made under.

Clause 10.3.2 of the certified agreement provides that, in accordance with the Industrial
Relations Act 2016, an employee "may ask the employer for a change in the way the employee
works, including the employee's ordinary hours of work." Section 27(1)(c) of that Act puts the
matter beyond doubt for a case such as this one: the request may be for "a change to the way the
employee works, for example, the use of different equipment as a result of a disability, illness
or injury."

The measures at sections 2 and 3 are a change in the way I work, sought as a result of an
illness. I have made that request formally, by a separate letter of today's date headed "Request
for a change in the way I work", so that there is no question about what it is or when it was
received. That letter sets the change out in the form clause 10.3.3 requires and states the
reasons for it. Sections 2 and 3 of this document are the fuller explanation of the same
proposal.

I ask that the two be read together, and that the request be dealt with under clauses 10.3.4 to
10.3.6. Nothing in this Part is intended to displace a discussion by agreement, which I would
much prefer, and I remain willing to consider any alternative the Health Service or my
practitioners consider more appropriate.

For completeness, the request under clause 10.3 is separate from, and does not answer, the
matters at Parts 4, 8, 9, 10 and 11 of this document, which are not made under that clause.
