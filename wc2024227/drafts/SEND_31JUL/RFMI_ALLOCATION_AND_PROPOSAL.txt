PART 4 — QUESTIONS FOR THE HEALTH SERVICE

These four questions ask a doctor to answer matters which the Health Service's own policy and
the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 place with the Health Service. I ask that the Health

4.1  QUESTION 2 — is there a performance or conduct process?

The question asks whether I can "follow a reasonable and lawful direction issued by [my]
supervisor" and "participate in discussions in relation to [my] workplace performance and/or
conduct."

These are not questions about capacity to perform the genuine occupational requirements of my
role. They concern readiness for a management process. Either such a process is on foot

procedural fairness — or there is not, in which case the questions do not serve the stated
purpose of this inquiry.

  → FOR THE HEALTH SERVICE: please confirm whether any performance or conduct process
    concerning me is on foot or contemplated, and if so provide particulars.

  → THEN, IF APPROPRIATE, FOR MY PRACTITIONER: "Does the employee have capacity to participate
    in ordinary workplace meetings and supervision, and are any supports required?"

The premise of Question 2 is separately incorrect and is addressed at Part 5.

4.2  QUESTION 3 — is the workplace safe?

The question asks whether I am "medically fit to return to their substantive role under the
existing reporting arrangements, including working with and reporting to their current line
manager."

This asks a treating doctor to certify that a workplace arrangement is safe. Under section 19
of the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 that duty rests with the Health Service. Under the
Managing the risk of psychosocial hazards at work Code of Practice 2022, a psychosocial hazard
is a feature of the work, to be identified and controlled by the person conducting the business
or undertaking. G03 clause 1 requires that "proper consideration must be given to the Managing
the risk of psychosocial hazards at work Code of Practice 2022, to ensure the process of
providing reasonable adjustments in the workplace, identifies and manages psychosocial hazards
and risks."

What is sought is a workplace risk assessment, not a medical opinion. A doctor can advise on
me; a doctor cannot assess the Health Service's reporting arrangements.

  → FOR THE HEALTH SERVICE: a risk assessment of the Switchboard work environment addressing
    the psychosocial hazards identified. Clause 7.2.1 of the Queensland Public Health Sector
    Certified Agreement provides the mechanism.

  → THEN FOR MY PRACTITIONER: "Are any adjustments recommended in relation to supervision
    arrangements, and if so, what are they in functional terms?"

**A further matter on Question 3.** My general practitioner's reference to "unresolved workplace
matters involving line management" is his summary of matters I have raised with the Health
Service. Those matters are recorded in the Health Service's own documents. They are described as
unresolved because they were raised and not determined, and no outcome has been advised to me.

That is not a matter requiring medical explanation. It is a status, and the Health Service holds
the record of it. The Managing the risk of psychosocial hazards at work Code of Practice 2022 —
which G03 clause 1 requires the Health Service to apply to this very process — identifies the
relevant hazard directly. It defines poor organisational justice as "a lack of procedural
fairness (fair processes to reach decisions), informational fairness (keeping relevant people
informed), or interpersonal fairness", and lists among the situations which may lead to it
"inequitable or inconsistent application of procedures across workers or over time" and "workers
or managers believing that rules do not apply to them and failing to follow agreed policies,
guidelines and procedures". Under poor workplace relationships it lists "a lack of fairness and
equity in dealing with workplace issues".

The Health Service is better placed than my general practitioner to identify what those matters
are. The Code identifies what kind of hazard they are.

4.3  QUESTION 6 — what are the genuine occupational requirements?

The question asks whether I can "fulfill the full inherent requirements of [my] Role ...
without restrictions or modifications to duties."

This applies the wrong test. G03 states its purpose as assisting employees "to meet the genuine
occupational requirements of their role by applying principles of reasonable adjustment," and
at clause 3 provides that adjustments "should support the employee to undertake the genuine
occupational requirements of their role, without risk to their own health and safety or that of
others in the workplace." Section 34 of the Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 is to the same effect.
Neither asks whether an employee can perform a role without adjustment.

A clinician also cannot determine which duties are genuine occupational requirements. That is
the employer's determination. The Health Service has itself already made part of it: its letter
states that "established process for client complaints received by Switchboard employees is for
immediate escalation and management by the Health Service Client Liaison Officer and/or
Manager, Switchboard Services," and the Role Description at Attachment 2 contains no
complaint-handling responsibility.

  → FOR THE HEALTH SERVICE: identify which duties of the role it says are genuine occupational
    requirements, having regard to the Role Description at Attachment 2.

  → THEN FOR MY PRACTITIONER: "Can the employee perform the genuine occupational requirements
    of the role with the adjustments you have recommended?"

4.4  QUESTION 9 — can the adjustments be accommodated?

The question asks: "If we are not able to accommodate the restrictions you have recommended, is
Mr Shepherd able to safely return to the workplace?"

This presupposes an inability to accommodate before any accommodation has been assessed. G03
clause 2 provides that unjustifiable hardship "is tested against the whole organisation, not a
division or unit within the organisation," and that "the onus is on Queensland Health, as the
employer, to prove an adjustment is unreasonable, not on the person to prove that it is
reasonable."

The question therefore asks my doctor to answer the final step first, on a hypothetical the
Health Service has constructed and not yet tested, and on which the onus is not mine.

  → FOR THE HEALTH SERVICE: assess the recommended adjustments against the whole organisation.
    If it considers any cannot be accommodated, identify which and its reasons.

  → THEN, IF NECESSARY, the question can be put to my practitioner properly.

PART 7 — PROPOSAL FOR RETURN TO WORK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I have been held out of the workplace since 3 July 2026 without wages, with my accrued leave
debited, and self-funding the medical costs of this process. G03 clause 1.1 contemplates
temporary adjustments which allow an employee to "continue work during their recovery," and
clause 1.1.1 confirms that workplace rehabilitation support is available to employees "who have
an injury/illness impacting their current ability to perform their role, with no claim or
entitlement to compensation (health management case)." Clause 1 provides that "Managers are
responsible for ensuring work environments and work, do not unreasonably prevent employment for
people."

The Health Service's Workplace rehabilitation standard (QH-IMP-401-5, 7 January 2026) is also
engaged. It provides that the line manager or delegate shall "identify and actively seek ...
meaningful suitable duties, and ensure suitable duties are made available wherever reasonably
practicable"; that the line manager "is best placed to be the primary contact for the worker
wherever possible and facilitate the injured workers on-going connection with the workplace";
that "regular, transparent and timely communication" be maintained with the worker "whether at
work or off work"; and that the worker be informed of "the appropriate dispute resolution
process to follow in such cases where the worker does not agree with elements of a proposed
workplace rehabilitation plan or suitable duties program". The standard applies to a worker
