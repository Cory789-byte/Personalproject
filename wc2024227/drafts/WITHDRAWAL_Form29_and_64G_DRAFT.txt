⛔ NOTES — NOT PART OF THE LETTER

1. WHAT THIS DOES, precisely. It ends BOTH live disclosure fronts against the
   non-party in one page: the r 64G application (filed 23 June 2026) and every
   item of the sealed Form 29 not already complied with. Withdrawal is total —
   there is no partial carve-out in this draft. MSH's objection K-LM26/729 is
   never ruled on, which means it is never tested — and never vindicated.

2. WHAT IT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT SAY. No reasons. No comment on the objection,
   the mention, or the merits. Restraint is the message (discipline rules 5–6):
   a party who withdraws without explanation after coming that prepared reads
   as a party who has what he needs. Any sentence of explanation would spend
   that.

3. ⚠ THE ONE ITEM TO WEIGH BEFORE SENDING — THE MET-CALL SPREADSHEET.
   MSH's 5 June letter identified the MET-call spreadsheet for 17–18 March
   2024 as available. It is the one document that puts a number on the Monday
   shift, and the Report B package guide lists it as desirable. This
   withdrawal releases it with everything else. Three ways to keep the door
   open, in descending order of cleanliness:
     (a) do nothing now — para 3 preserves the route through the Respondent,
         and the Regulator can obtain it from MSH if it matters;
     (b) before sending, ask Matheson (correspondence, not application) to
         obtain and disclose it;
     (c) carve it out of para 1 — ⛔ NOT recommended: a one-item carve-out
         keeps the whole non-party fight alive for one spreadsheet and reads
         as indecision.
   If (a) is chosen, nothing changes in the letter. His call.

4. WHAT IS NOT LOST. The complaint-records items (3(a), 3(b), 20) were always
   double-tracked: the Regulator's own 11 June disclosure already contains the
   15–16 May 2024 material (affidavit ¶52, CS-4), and the IP Act application
   (Monday's other letter) reaches the 2026 employment file the Form 29 never
   could. The Item 20 contradiction is banked whether or not the Notice lives.

5. SEQUENCING, Monday 10 August. This letter fits the compliance-positive
   package: witness list + this withdrawal + (if proceeding) the preservation
   letter. It is exactly the "what arrives alongside it" neighbour that makes
   the preservation/conflict enquiry read as forensic rather than obsessive
   (PRESERVATION v2, addendum point 3). Send this and the witness list FIRST
   or together; never let the enquiry letter arrive alone.

6. MECHANICS. Email to the registry, business hours, subject line as above,
   cc Matheson and Ruttan so service and courtesy happen in one send. No
   attachment needed beyond the letter itself. Reference K-LM26/729 in the cc
   line, not the body — the body should not engage with the objection at all.

7. FORM. Correspondence, not a Form 27 discontinuance — the appeal itself is
   NOT being discontinued and nothing in this letter may be capable of being
   misread that way. The words "the appeal" appear only in para 3, in the
   context of its continued conduct. If chambers prefers a formal notice of
   withdrawal of the application, they will say so; para 4 invites exactly
   that.
