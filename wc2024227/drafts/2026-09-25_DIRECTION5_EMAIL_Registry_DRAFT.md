## Evaluation: is resolution without a hearing actually possible?

### What supports it

| Point | Source |
|---|---|
| 298 of 303 facts admitted, 0 denied | Response 8 Sep 2026 |
| The Regulator's own Review Unit found the 17–18 March rostering "unreasonable management action", and found "a personal injury of a psychological nature" | Facts 260, 261 (Review Decision 69983) |
| Payroll's outline: the break was "a rostering practice issue for the line manager" | Wright outline, 24 Sep |
| Leave decline: error conceded in their own pleading and repeated in the outline | SOFC ¶14; Earl outline |
| No expert or medical evidence served by the Respondent | Witness list and outlines, 24 Sep |
| The s 32(5) contention is not particularised | SOFC ¶27; fact 303 |
| Taylor and Reese are **the Respondent's** witnesses, so the Appellant cross-examines them | Guide 7.6.2 |

### What stands in the way

1. **Injury and causation are still in issue, and the onus is his.** SOFC ¶5 puts the onus on the Appellant, SOFC ¶8 raises a "past medical history of anxiety", SOFC ¶9 says the diagnosis is a matter "for determination by the Commission at a hearing", and SOFC ¶22(f) says the break was "not causative". No admission touches s 32(1).
2. **Medical evidence is normally given orally.** Under Guide Part 7, "a medical report on its own cannot be considered without having the expert witness give evidence orally". A decision on the papers therefore needs the Respondent to agree that the reports can be received without cross-examining the doctors. Item (c) of the email asks exactly that.
3. **The Respondent has chosen to call four witnesses and is seeking costs.** That is the posture of a party preparing for a hearing, not one preparing to concede.
4. **The second conference is not a settlement forum.** Guide 5.1: "No commercial settlement is possible at the conference." It is where the Respondent "may… consider conceding the appeal where new information is presented". Item (d) is drafted to that exact function.

### Verdict

- **Narrowing the issues at conference: realistic and likely.** The facts, the documents and the witness list can all be settled there.
- **Resolution without any hearing: possible, not the default.** It depends on one of two things. Either the Regulator concedes on the admitted record, or it agrees that the medical evidence can go in without cross-examination so the Commission can decide on the papers. The email asks for both without demanding either.
- **The most likely outcome:** a conference that narrows the case, followed by a short hearing confined to causation and s 32(5), with Taylor and Reese cross-examined against Tabs 6, 20 and 30.

### What was left out of the earlier version, and why

- **"A public hearing might not be in the public interest"** has been removed. Hearings are public by
  default. Coming from a party who holds the Chief Executive's letter, the line can be read as pressure
  on Metro South Health. The same point is made neutrally by "narrowed" and "without a full hearing".
- **"The documents close the matter"** has been removed. Injury and causation are not admitted, so the
  documents narrow the matter rather than closing it. The draft says what is true: the primary facts
  are largely settled.
- **Contradictions between the outlines and the admissions** are not argued in a Registry email. They
  belong at the conference or in cross-examination.
