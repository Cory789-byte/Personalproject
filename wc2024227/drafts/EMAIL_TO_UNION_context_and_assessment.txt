1. WHY I AM WRITING

I am writing about the Switchboard, not about my own dispute.

So far as I am aware Switchboard Services at Logan Hospital has never had a
psychosocial risk assessment of any kind. It was not brought into the Fatigue
Risk Management System on the basis that it is non-clinical, and fatigue risk
management was introduced there in the second half of 2024.

The work itself is not non-clinical in any practical sense. The Switchboard
activates and pages every emergency code and MET call in the hospital, relays
urgent doctor to doctor results, and is the first point of contact for the public
and for staff across the site.

The fatigue is also not only a question of the break between shifts. A ten hour
break can be entirely compliant and the shift itself still be exhausting. On high
volume days the load is continuous: two to three hundred calls, code activation
and paging, urgent clinical relay, and complaints from across the hospital
arriving at the first point of contact. On those days breaks are deferred because
there is no relief rostered to take the console. A break entitlement is not a
break taken.

I have obtained an indicative proposal from an occupational psychology provider
for an independent psychosocial risk assessment of the service. It is attached.
The work would be commissioned and funded by the Health Service. My own
involvement would be limited to being consulted, in the same way as any other
affected worker, and the report should go to the Health Service and the union
rather than to me.


WHAT I HAVE ATTACHED, AND WHY THIS PROVIDER

I have attached two things:

  1. A short submission setting out what an assessment of the Switchboard would
     need to cover and why. Two pages.

  2. The provider's indicative proposal. The range quoted is $10,000 to $30,000
     depending on scope.

I approached more than one provider. I chose this one on a simple test: they
answered the questions in writing before being engaged, on independence, on
method and on scope. The others asked for a call and would not put anything on
paper. I took that as the difference between an assessment and a sales process.

Three things seem to me to matter more than who does the work, and the union may
see it differently:

  * Independence. The assessor should not be engaged by, or report to, the part
    of the service being assessed.

  * Method. Direct observation across high volume and low volume days and across
    the shift pattern, not a room and a questionnaire. A survey only assessment
    of a small team produces a nil finding by design.

  * Terms of reference agreed in writing before it starts, and provided to the
    union.

I am not attached to this provider and I have no arrangement with them. If the
union or the Health Service would rather use someone else, that is entirely
sensible. I obtained the proposal so that the cost and the scope were known
rather than hypothetical, and so that nobody could say the idea was vague.

Three reasons. So the union holds the record as it happens rather than

I am not asking anyone to adopt what I have written. I am asking that the union
have it.

5. MY BACKGROUND, AND WHY I HAVE APPROACHED IT THIS WAY

Before Queensland Health I worked as a safety advisor on Manus Island, contracted
in the Australian Border Force and Immigration environment. I was a third year
Bachelor of Nursing student and ceased those studies, which is how I came into
Queensland Health. I also hold a Diploma in Fitness.

I mention it only because it is the reason I have treated this as a work health
and safety matter rather than as a grievance. I know what a hazard assessment
looks like, I know the difference between a control and a conversation, and I
know that a service which has never been assessed is not a service without
hazards.

I have raised concerns at the Switchboard since 2023, about consultation on
roster and shift changes, about fatigue, and about how complaints and patient
safety matters are escalated and answered. My experience of raising them is part
of why I want this done as a workplace assessment through the union rather than
as an individual complaint. An assessment does not depend on anyone accepting my
account, and it does not put any colleague in the position I have ended up in.


6. KEEPING MY MATTER SEPARATE

I would ask that my dispute with the Health Service and the union's request for
the assessment be kept apart from one another.

My dispute is mine and I am content to keep running it with the union's support.
The assessment is not about me. If it travels as part of my case it becomes an
individual grievance with a union cover sheet, and both its independence and its
usefulness to the people still on the console are lost.

My own matter is in it for one reason. It is evidence of what I do not want my
colleagues to go through. That is the whole of its place in this, and I would
rather it were read that way than as part of a claim.

On where the submission should go, and this is a judgement for the union rather
than for me: my letter of 3 August to Ms Cridland as Chief Executive is already
with her office and receipt was acknowledged the same day at her direction. A
submission from the union would reach her as a separate matter from a different
party, which seems to me the stronger course. The union may also wish to ask that
it be tabled at the appropriate work health and safety or safety and quality
governance forum rather than referred into Human Resources, since the subject is
the work rather than any individual's employment.
