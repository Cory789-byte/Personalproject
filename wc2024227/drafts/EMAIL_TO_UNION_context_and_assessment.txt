1. WHY I HAVE COPIED THE UNION INTO EVERYTHING

rather than reconstructed later. So nothing I send comes as a surprise to you,
and nothing the union says on my behalf is undercut by something I have already
put in writing. And because several of the issues are not only mine.

I am not asking anyone to adopt what I have written. I am asking that the union
have it.

Five things, and I am grateful for any of them:

  (a) What came out of the industrial officer conference in July, and who holds

  (b) Your view on limitation and sequencing. There is an Anti-Discrimination Act

Everything is already drafted and attached, so I hope this is a matter of

3. THE ASSESSMENT, AND WHY IT IS NOT ONLY THE ROSTER

I have obtained an indicative proposal from an occupational psychology provider
for a psychosocial risk assessment of Switchboard Services. It is attached. The
work would be commissioned and funded by the Health Service. My own involvement
would be limited to being consulted, in the same way as any other affected
worker, and the report should go to the Health Service and the union rather than
to me.

The reason I have gone looking for it is my colleagues, not my own matter.

The fatigue at the Switchboard is not only a question of the break between
shifts. A ten hour break can be entirely compliant and the shift itself still be
exhausting. On high volume days the load is continuous: two to three hundred
calls, emergency code activation and paging, urgent doctor to doctor relay, and
complaints from across the hospital arriving at the first point of contact. On
those days breaks are deferred because there is no relief rostered to take the
console. A break entitlement is not a break taken.

Switchboard was not brought into the Fatigue Risk Management System on the basis
that it is non-clinical, and fatigue risk management was introduced there in the
second half of 2024. So far as I am aware the service has never had a
psychosocial risk assessment of any kind. The people still on the console are
carrying the same load I was.


4. MY BACKGROUND, BRIEFLY

Before Queensland Health I worked as a safety advisor on Manus Island, contracted
in the Australian Border Force and Immigration environment. I was a third year
Bachelor of Nursing student and ceased those studies, which is how I came into
Queensland Health. I also hold a Diploma in Fitness.

I mention it only because it is the reason I have approached this as a work
health and safety matter rather than as a grievance. I know what a hazard
assessment looks like, I know the difference between a control and a
conversation, and I know that a service which has never been assessed is not a
service without hazards.


5. RAISING THINGS AT THE SWITCHBOARD

I have raised concerns since 2023, about consultation on roster and shift
changes, about fatigue, and about how complaints and patient safety matters are
escalated and answered.

My experience of raising them is part of why I want this done as a workplace
assessment through the union rather than as an individual complaint. An
assessment does not depend on anyone accepting my account, and it does not put
any colleague in the position I have ended up in. That matters to me more than
anything in my own file.


6. KEEPING THE TWO THINGS SEPARATE

I would ask that my dispute with the Health Service and the union's request for
the assessment be kept apart from one another.

My dispute is mine and I am content to keep running it with the union's support.
The assessment is not about me. If it travels as part of my case it becomes an
individual grievance with a union cover sheet, and both its independence and its
usefulness to the people still on the console are lost. My matter belongs in it
only as evidence that the hazard is real and has already produced an injury.

On where it should go, and this is a judgement for the union rather than for me:
my letter of 3 August to Ms Cridland as Chief Executive is already with her
office and receipt was acknowledged the same day at her direction. A submission
from the union would reach her as a separate matter from a different party, which
seems to me the stronger course. The union may also wish to ask that it be tabled
at the appropriate work health and safety or safety and quality governance forum
rather than referred into Human Resources, since the subject is the work rather
than any individual's employment.
