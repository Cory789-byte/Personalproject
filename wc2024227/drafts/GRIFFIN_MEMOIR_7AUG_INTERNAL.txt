A SHORT MEMOIR — WEARING LAUREN GRIFFIN'S HAT
The week of the mention, written from the other side of the glass

in the same genre as the Matheson's-chair piece. The EXTERIOR facts are documented
(the distribution list, the 30 April service email, the Form 29 and the objection,

Nothing in this file is evidence, nothing in it may be quoted, served, filed, or

Written 9 August 2026.

---

I am the Director of Employment Relations for a health service that is not a party
to WC/2024/227, and I have read every listing in it for months.

That is not a confession. It is a distribution list. Somewhere early on, the
Commission's registry put four of our addresses on the service line for a matter
in which we are, formally, nobody — Tribunal Matters in the central office, the
Executive Director, our Principal Lawyer, and me. Every time the registry sends a
date, it arrives in my inbox between a rostering grievance and a payroll
escalation, with the neat subject line and the neat case number, and I read it the
way you read weather from a district you used to live in. Not my storm. My sky.

In April I pressed send myself. Our correspondence needed to reach the registry,
someone had to serve it, and the someone was me — "by way of service," copy to the
appellant, copy to our lawyer, copy to the Regulator. Ten seconds of work. I have
thought about those ten seconds more than they deserve, because they are the ten
seconds in which the wall between the two files — the one where I am nobody and
the one where I am the Director — was mine to hold, and I held it with one hand
while pressing send with the other.

Then came the notice. Non-party disclosure: the rosters, yes, fine, the payroll,
fine — and then the complaint records. Complaints received about the switchboard.
What management did about them. What HR did when he went to the ethical standards
people. Our objection went out over the Chief Executive's signature and said, among
other things, that complaints of that kind are managed solely via email or
verbally, and records of them are not retained.

I want to write down what it is like to read a sentence like that from inside the
directorate whose job is to be the retained record. It is like watching someone
describe your own filing cabinet as a rumour. The sentence is defensible — that is
what makes it a sentence lawyers allow — and I could not have drafted it, because I
know what an inbox is.

The week before the mention was the loudest quiet week I can remember. On Monday
his package arrived on the employment side — six documents, five emails, nothing
misspelled, nothing shouted, every clause number correct, the union copied like a
man setting a table. On Wednesday the clocks he had started began to tick audibly.
And on Friday, in a hearing room I was not in, a Commissioner picked up the other
file — the one where I am nobody — and read it aloud.

I was not there. I got the account the way directors get accounts: compressed,
slightly defensive, delivered by someone who was. The Commissioner read the whole
of his case back to him, and when he reached the fatigue material — the seven-hour
break, the leave he used on his own recovery, the payment he asked for and did not
get — he gathered it together in a sentence, the way you gather cutlery: all the
same drawer. I understand the appellant went quickly, and that when the questions
came he had the documents before the questions finished. I understand nothing was
decided that ends anything. Mentions never end anything. They only tell you how
the ending is going to feel.

Here is what I actually know, stripped of everybody's adjectives, mine included.
A man who worked our switchboard says the roster did something to him, and the one
piece of arithmetic at the centre of it — seven hours between shifts — nobody
disputes. The rest is a contest about reasonableness, and reasonableness is my
trade, and I know what it looks like when it is going well and when it is going to
be expensive. And in my other inbox, the same man's employment file is running on
its own clocks — a certificate that says fit with adjustments, a request for
medical information, a dispute notice with my directorate's name inside it.

Two files. One distribution list. And me on both, which is not wrongdoing, and is
not nothing either. It is the particular weather of a big institution: knowledge
arrives whether or not you asked for it, and once it has arrived you cannot un-know
it, you can only document what you did next.

What I did next, mostly, was forward things. That is the honest memoir of a
Director of Employment Relations: I received, I read, I forwarded, I held the
wall. On Friday a Commissioner read the file aloud in a room I wasn't in, and the
part of me that has done this job for a long time noted — professionally, the way
you note a barometer — that the man on the other side of both my inboxes no longer
writes like a complainant.

He writes like someone who expects to be read aloud.
