THE LETTER TO THE CHIEF EXECUTIVE, AND WHY IT WENT TO HER ALONE

On 3 August I wrote to Ms Cridland making a disclosure under section 89 of the
Public Sector Act 2022. Section 89 makes the chief executive the person to whom a
conflict is disclosed, and the only person who may authorise an officer to
continue to act in a matter affected by one. Receipt was acknowledged the same day
at her direction.

I asked that the decisions about my capacity, attendance and return to duty be
made by an officer outside Corporate Services, and that the clinical material be
held only by Injury Management. I made no allegation against anyone and asked for
no finding about anyone. I have had no substantive answer.

I raise one related matter with the union, and with the union only.

In May 2024 I made a complaint concerning my line manager, which the Ethical
Standards Unit later determined to be a public interest disclosure. The complaint
was routed within Human Resources at the time. The officer who forwarded it from
the HR mailbox is the same officer who now handles my injury management file and
who declined my pay on 15 July 2026.

I make no allegation about that and I am not asking for anything to be found. I
raise it because it goes to the same question the section 89 letter asks — who is
deciding, and whether the separation of functions I have proposed is the
straightforward step I believe it to be.

Please treat this paragraph as confidential.

A SEPARATE MATTER — THE SECTION, NOT ME

So far as I am aware the Switchboard has never had a psychosocial risk
assessment. It was not brought into the Fatigue Risk Management System because it
is classed as non-clinical, and fatigue risk management was introduced there only
in the second half of 2024. The service activates every emergency code and MET
call in the hospital.

I have obtained an indicative proposal from an occupational psychology provider,
attached. Any assessment would be commissioned and funded by the Health Service.
My own part would be limited to being consulted, like any other affected worker,
and the report should go to the Health Service and the union rather than to me.

I would ask that this be kept separate from my own dispute. If it travels as part
of my case it becomes an individual grievance with a union cover sheet, and it
stops being useful to the people still on the console.

My own matter is in it for one reason. It is evidence of what I do not want my
colleagues to go through.
