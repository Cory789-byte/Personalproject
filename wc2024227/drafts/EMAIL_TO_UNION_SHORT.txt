THE LETTER TO THE CHIEF EXECUTIVE

On 3 August I made a disclosure to Ms Cridland under section 89 of the Public
Sector Act 2022. Section 89 makes the chief executive the person to whom a conflict
is disclosed, and the only person who may authorise an officer to continue to act
in a matter affected by one. Receipt was acknowledged the same day at her
direction. I asked that decisions about my capacity, attendance and return be made
by an officer outside Corporate Services. I made no allegation and sought no
finding. I have had no substantive answer.

One related matter, for the union only. In May 2024 I made a complaint concerning
my line manager, which the Ethical Standards Unit later determined to be a public
interest disclosure. It was routed within Human Resources at the time. The officer
who forwarded it from the HR mailbox is the officer who now handles my injury
management file and who declined my pay on 15 July 2026. I make no allegation
about that. I raise it because it goes to the same question the section 89 letter
asks — who is deciding. Please treat this paragraph as confidential.


THE PROPOSITION

So far as I am aware the Switchboard has never had a psychosocial risk assessment.
It was not brought into the Fatigue Risk Management System because it is classed
as non-clinical, and fatigue risk management was introduced there only in the
second half of 2024. The service activates every emergency code and MET call in
the hospital.

I propose the union put forward an independent psychosocial risk assessment of
Switchboard Services, commissioned and funded by the Health Service. My own part
would be limited to being consulted, like any other affected worker. The report
should go to the Health Service and the union, not to me.

I ask that it be kept separate from my own dispute. If it travels as part of my
case it becomes an individual grievance with a union cover sheet, and it stops
being useful to the people still on the console. My own matter is in it for one
reason. It is evidence of what I do not want my colleagues to go through.
