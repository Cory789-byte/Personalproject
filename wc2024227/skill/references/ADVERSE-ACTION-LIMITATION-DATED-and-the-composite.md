## PART 1 — ⭐⭐ THE DATING. s 310, VERIFIED VERBATIM

> **310 Time for application**
> **(1)** An application **relating to dismissal** must be made within— (a) **21 days after the
> dismissal took effect**; or (b) if the commission allows a further period under subsection (2)—
> the further period.
> **(2)** The commission may allow a further period if the commission is satisfied there are
> **exceptional circumstances**, taking into account— (a) the reason for the delay; and (b) any
> action taken by the person to dispute the dismissal; and (c) prejudice to the employer…; and
> (d) the merits of the application; and (e) fairness as between the person and other persons in a
> similar position.
> **(3)** An application **relating to a contravention of this part (other than dismissal)** must
> be made **within 6 years after the contravention occurs.**

**There are two clocks, not one, and they run at completely different speeds.**

| # | Head | Provision | Runs from | Expires |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The direction not to attend work** | s 310(3) — 6 years | **26 June 2026**, the day the exclusion condition was created | ⭐ **26 June 2032** |
| 2 | **The continued exclusion after certification of fitness** | s 310(3) — 6 years | **3 July 2026**, the ECC certifying fit with restrictions | ⭐ **3 July 2032** |
| 3 | **The non-payment of wages** | s 310(3) — 6 years | **13 July 2026**, first unpaid period | ⭐ **13 July 2032** |
| 4 | **Each further fortnight of exclusion and non-payment** | s 310(3) | each is arguably a fresh contravention | **rolls forward continuously** |
| 5 | The 2024 separation | s 310(1) — 21 days | effective 20 Sep 2024; confirmed 9 Oct 2024 | ⛔ **expired ~30 October 2024.** Revivable only on **exceptional circumstances** under s 310(2), and it was dealt with elsewhere |
| 6 | ⭐⭐⭐ **A FUTURE dismissal** — medical separation, ill-health retirement, abandonment | s 310(1) — **21 days from the day it takes effect** | not yet running | ⛔⛔ **The only sharp clock in the matter, and it has not started** |

### ⇒ THE ANSWER TO THE QUESTION ASKED
**Nothing is running out. The live heads expire in 2032 and refresh every fortnight the exclusion
continues.** The urgency I recorded this morning was invented.

⭐⭐ **The clock that matters is row 6, and it is prospective.** If the exclusion ripens into any
form of separation, **21 days** starts on the day it takes effect and an extension requires
*exceptional circumstances*. **That is the only date in the employment track capable of being
missed** — and it can only be missed by not noticing the separation for three weeks.

⇒ **Standing instruction: the day any separation instrument arrives, the 21-day clock is entered in
the register before anything else is done with the document.**

## PART 2 — THE FOUR STRANDS, TESTED AGAINST THE ELEMENTS

The elements are: **a workplace right (s 284)**, **adverse action (s 282)**, and **the reason
(s 306(2), reversed)**. Each strand is tested against them rather than described.

### STRAND 1 — dismissed, then reinstated

**As a claim: dead.** It was a dismissal, so s 310(1) applied, and 21 days expired in October 2024.

**As context in an application about the 2026 conduct: admissible and useful, with two warnings.**
It establishes that the employer has previously ended the employment while a compensation claim was
on foot, and then reversed it. That is chronology, and chronology is all it should ever be.

- ⛔ **TD/2024/110 stays out of the workers' compensation track entirely, and the deed terms are
  never discussed anywhere.**
- ⛔ **The only permitted formulation: "reinstated to my employment with an effective date of
  20 September 2024, the reinstatement taking effect despite any payroll documentation."** Never
  the phrase about service being preserved.
- ⚠ **Double-edged.** It also hands MSH a completed, resolved matter to point to, and reopening it
  invites the deed into a forum where it does not belong.

### STRAND 2 — the public interest disclosure

**Workplace right: yes, on two limbs.** s 284(1)(c) — a person has a workplace right if able to make
a complaint or inquiry *"(i) to an entity having the capacity under an industrial law to seek
compliance with that law…; or (ii) if the person is an employee—**in relation to the person's
employment**."* Limb (ii) is unqualified and catches it without argument.

⛔⛔ **DISCIPLINE. This strand lives in the general protections and disclosure track ONLY.** It never
appears in WC/2024/227, in any correspondence with the Regulator, or in the Form 9A. The reason is
mechanical, not presentational: reprisal language opens **s 32(5)(b) WCRA**, the exclusion for injury
connected to the worker's *expectation or perception* of reasonable management action. **The
Regulator has not pleaded it. It does not need to if we hand it over.**

### STRAND 3 — locked out of work

**Adverse action: yes, on the face of two limbs of s 282(1).**
> *"Adverse action is taken by an employer against an employee if the employer— … (b) **injures the
> employee in the employee's employment**; or (c) **alters the position of the employee to the
> employee's prejudice**…"*

Zero gross pay, 76.00 hours coded "NP Sick Leave", leave debited, medical costs self-funded. Both
limbs are satisfied without needing to characterise anything.

⛔⛔ **AND HERE IS THE REAL FIGHT — s 282(6), verified verbatim:**
> *"Adverse action **does not include action that is authorised under**— (a) this Act or **any other
> law of the State**; or (b) a law of the Commonwealth."*

**MSH's defence is not the limitation and it is not the reason. It is authorisation.** They will say
the direction was authorised by their work health and safety obligations — and **their own 26 June
2026 email cites WHS s 19**, which reads as having been written with exactly that in mind.
⭐ **The answer to it is not to deny the obligation. It is that the obligation was discharged**: he
was certified fit with restrictions on 3 July 2026, the psychosocial information was supplied on
3 August 2026, and the exclusion continued past both. **An authorisation spent is not an
authorisation.**

### STRAND 4 — ⭐⭐⭐ locked out *during an appeal*. THE STRONGEST STRAND, AND THE ONE BEING UNDERUSED

**s 284(1)(b)** — a person has a workplace right if the person *"is able to start, or participate in,
a **process or proceedings under an industrial law** or industrial instrument"*.

The instinct is to argue that the WCRA is *"another Act regulating the relationships between
employers and employees"* under the dictionary definition of **industrial law**. That argument is
available and probably right, but **it is not needed**, because of s 283:

> **283 Meaning of process or proceedings under an industrial law or industrial instrument**
> *"Each of the following **is** a process or proceedings under an industrial law or industrial
> instrument— **(a) any conference conducted, or hearing held, by the commission or the court**;
> (b) court proceedings under an industrial law or industrial instrument; … (h) dispute settlement
> for which provision is made by, or under, an industrial law or industrial instrument; (i) any
> other process or proceedings under an industrial law or industrial instrument."*

⭐⭐⭐ **s 283(a) is deeming language and it is unqualified. WC/2024/227 is a proceeding in the
Commission. The mention of 7 August 2026 was a hearing held by the commission. The section makes
participating in his own appeal a process under an industrial law by definition** — so the workplace
right is established without reaching the harder question about the WCRA.

## PART 4 — ⭐⭐⭐ THE POINT THE QUESTION KEEPS CIRCLING. HE DOES NOT HAVE TO MAKE IT LOOK ADVERSE

> **306 Reason for action to be presumed unless proved otherwise**
> (1) Subsection (2) applies if— (a) **in an application in relation to a contravention of a
> provision of this part**, it is alleged that a person took action **for a particular reason**…;
> and (b) taking that action for that reason would be a contravention.
> **(2) It is presumed that the action was taken for that reason UNLESS THE PERSON PROVES OTHERWISE.**

**He establishes three objective things: a workplace right, adverse action, and an application under
s 309. The reason is then presumed against MSH, and MSH must prove otherwise.**

⇒ **The composite's value is not persuasive. It is structural.** Adding the strands together does
not make the inference prettier — it makes the **disproof harder**, because MSH must put a
decision-maker in the witness box to swear to the actual reasons at a moment when every reason they
have put in writing has been answered and the exclusion has continued anyway.

⇒ ⭐ **That is why this head is worth more than its dollar band.** The settlement pressure comes from
who has to give evidence, not from the size of the award.

⚠⚠ **FORUM IS EVERYTHING.** s 306 operates *"in an application in relation to a contravention of a
provision of this part"* — a **general protections application under s 309**. It does **not** operate
in the workers' compensation appeal, and it does **not** operate in a certified-agreement dispute,
which is about the application of the agreement rather than reasons. **The reverse onus is a reason
to consider filing. The internal dispute process will never deliver it.**

**The orders available on such an application — s 314, verbatim:** reinstatement; payment of
compensation; *"an order for payment of an amount to the person for remuneration lost"*; *"an order
to maintain the continuity of the person's employment"*; *"an order to maintain the period of the
person's continuous service with the employer"*; and injunctive relief to *"prevent, stop, or remedy
the effects of"* a contravention.
⚠ Note the trap in the drafting: s 314(1)(d) and (e) are **statutory remedies available in a future
application**. They are not a description of the 2024 reinstatement, and the phrase must never be
recycled into that context.
