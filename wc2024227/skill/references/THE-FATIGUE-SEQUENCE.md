# PART 1 — ⭐⭐⭐ WHY THE GROUPING IS THE MOST IMPORTANT THING TO COME OUT OF THE MENTION

The `STRESSOR-STRENGTH-AUDIT` priced 3(a), 3(b) and 3(c) as **three limbs** — one Tier 1, one Tier
3, one Tier 4. **Read as three limbs that ranking is right. Read as Dwyer read them, it is wrong.**

**They are not three limbs. They are one transaction with five dated steps**, all inside a six-week
window, all about one roster.

⭐⭐ **And that is the answer to the *Delaney* problem.** The standing warning is that thirteen limbs
presented together read as a **composite course**, which is the Respondent's authority. **But this
group is not a composite of unrelated grievances — it is a single stressor with internal steps.**
*Mahaffey* is a single unreasonable stressor with sufficient causal connection. **A single stressor
is allowed to have a beginning, a middle and an end.**

⭐ **The Commission grouped it that way without being asked to.** The framing is not an argument
that has to be sold — **it is how the material reads to a member hearing it for the first time.**

# PART 3 — ⭐⭐⭐ THE SENTENCE NOBODY HAS USED

**Review Decision 69983, p 22, recording Ms Taylor's email of 1 May 2024:**

> *"…after a consultation with payroll and Human Resources, your request for fatigue payment for
> 18 March 2024 would not be processed due to the existing 8-hour agreement signed by you on
> 17 June 2020. **However, she noted you are able to terminate this agreement going forward.**"*

## What that clause concedes

1. ⭐ **The agreement was terminable at will, by him, at any time.**
2. ⇒ **On the employer's own account the 8-hour break was a concession he had granted and could
   withdraw** — not a fixed term of his employment.
3. ⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **The employer therefore knew he could restore the 10-hour minimum by a single act — and
   did not tell him so until after he had been rostered below the minimum and refused relief.**

## ⭐⭐ This reframes the whole limb

The question stops being *"was he entitled to fatigue leave?"* — which is the question **cl 18.10**
answers, and answers against him if SOFC ¶24(b) is right.

**The question becomes the manner question:**

> **Was it reasonable to apply a June 2020 agreement, signed when he was casual, to a 2024
> full-time roster — never having reviewed it, never having raised it, and disclosing that it was
> terminable only after the shortfall had occurred and relief had been refused?**

⭐ **cl 18.10 has nothing to say about that.** It is an entitlement provision. **The
unreasonableness is in the application of a superseded instrument, not in the denial of a
benefit.**

## Three facts that complete it, all already established

- ⭐ **The Award's default is 10 hours.** Review Decision 69983 records: *"the Award stipulated in
  part 5 section 15 that unless there was a mutual agreement of regular rosters, employees were
  entitled to a minimum 10-hour break."* ⇒ **10 hours is the rule; the agreement is the exception.**
- **He was casual in June 2020**, later permanent, then on a different full-time contract. **Nobody
  revisited the agreement.**
- **Ms Taylor was not the manager with whom it was made.** It was applied by a manager who
  inherited it.

# PART 4 — ⭐⭐ THE ANSWER TO *"THE LEAVE WAS PAID"* (SOFC ¶22(c))

**Of course it was paid. Personal leave is paid leave.**

⭐ **The complaint has never been that he went unpaid. It is that his own accrued leave balance
funded the recovery from a shortfall the employer created.** ¶22(c) answers a case he is not
running.

**One sentence, and it is complete:**
> *The employer rostered below the minimum, declined to fund the recovery, and the recovery was
> funded from my own leave.*

# PART 5 — ⛔ TWO THINGS TO PIN DOWN BEFORE THIS IS RELIED ON

## 5.1 ⛔ THE 19 MARCH / 1 MAY GAP — a Form 20 audit item

**Form 20 ¶40** says Ms Taylor *"relied on the 8-hour-break agreement I had signed on 17 June 2020
to deny paid fatigue leave, **requiring me to exhaust my own personal leave on 19 March 2024**."*

⚠ **But the recorded refusal is 1 May 2024 — six weeks after the leave was taken.**

Either there was an **earlier informal refusal on or about 19 March** (which would make the
sequence very tight), **or** the leave was taken first and the *fatigue payment* claim was refused
later — in which case **"requiring me to exhaust" is loose, and it is exactly the kind of loose
phrase a cross-examiner opens with.**

⛔ **Establish which it was before this goes anywhere.** ⭐ Both versions still work — the second is
simply *he absorbed the cost, then asked to be made good, and was refused* — but **the affidavit
must match whichever is true.**

## 5.2 ⭐ THE 9A'S "10-HOUR MINIMUM" — better than "fix it to 8"

Previously recorded as an overstatement to be corrected to 8 hours. **The Review Decision shows the
accurate formulation is stronger than either:**

> **The Award minimum is 10 hours unless displaced by mutual agreement. The Respondent asserts
> displacement by the June 2020 agreement. On the Respondent's own case the minimum was 8 hours,
> and the break was 7.**

⭐ **Plead the rule and the exception. He loses nothing on either footing, and the pleading stops
being vulnerable.** The Review Officer expressly declined to decide the 8-hour question for exactly
this reason.

⚠ **And note: Form 20 ¶40 already concedes he signed the June 2020 agreement.** Existence is not in
issue and should not be argued. **The case is supersession, non-review, and terminability.**

**Same manager. Same instrument. Same answer — use your own leave.** And the 8 Sep 2025 letter

⛔ **But this is post-injury.** SOFC ¶25 pleads post-injury matters are not relevant, and the
standing discipline keeps severity out of the pleaded stressors. ⇒ **Evidence-in-chief, the medical
brief and closing. Not a pleaded limb.**

# PART 7 — WHAT CHANGES

## 7.1 The stressor ranking is amended

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| 3(a) Tier 1 · 3(b) Tier 3 · 3(c) Tier 4 — three separate limbs | ⭐⭐⭐ **ONE STRESSOR, FIVE STEPS — the lead case.** 3(c) is not weak; it is the closing step of the strongest sequence in the matter |

⛔ **The earlier Tier 4 rating of 3(c) was right for a standalone entitlement claim and wrong for
the fifth step of a transaction.** Read alone it dies on cl 18.10. Read in sequence, **cl 18.10
never gets asked.**

## 7.2 ⭐⭐⭐ THIS IS WHAT GOES TO THE PSYCHIATRIST ON WEDNESDAY

**A five-step sequence with dates is something a psychiatrist can form an opinion about. Thirteen
limbs across fourteen months is not.**

The letter of instruction should put **this sequence**, in these words, and ask the s 32(1)
question against it:

> *Rostered consecutive shifts · a 7-hour break, admitted · a request for fatigue relief · refusal
> on a four-year-old agreement he was then told he could have ended at any time · and his own leave
> balance absorbing the cost.*

⭐ **That is a complete causal chain with a beginning, a mechanism and an end** — and it is the one
part of this matter where **every link is admitted, documented, or his own first-hand evidence.**

## 7.3 And it is the opening of the outline of argument

⭐ Together with the answer to *"that's just management action"*, this sequence **is** the case.
Everything else in the Amended 9A is context threaded back to it.
