| 13 | *the email prompted a meeting on 10 August; a grievance option was offered on 29 August; a follow-up on 8 September* |

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **NOT ONE PARAGRAPH ENGAGES WITH CONSEQUENCE.** ⛔ **The MASPER reports of 3 and 8 May, the
Integrated Respiratory Service of 15 and 20 May — *"we can not help patients or other clinical
staff"* — and the fatigue escalation to HR of 8–20 May appear NOWHERE in their answer.**

⭐⭐⭐ **So the two cases are answering different questions:**
| | |
|---|---|
| ⛔ **Theirs** | *"Was the PROCESS reasonable?"* — stressor by stressor, in isolation |
| ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **His** | *"What did it DO?"* — cumulatively, on admitted third-party documents |

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ ***Delaney* requires a GLOBAL evaluation, not a process audit.** ⭐⭐ ***Stretton* [84]: ONE
significant unreasonable aspect suffices — but it must itself be a significant contributor.**
⇒ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The case is won on the ACCUMULATION AND THE SEQUENCE, not on defeating any single
paragraph — which is fortunate, because ¶14(f)'s "human error under high work demands" is a
respectable answer to that stressor taken alone.**
