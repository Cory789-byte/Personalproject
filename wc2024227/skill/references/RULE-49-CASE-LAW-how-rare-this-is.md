# How often has anyone run a notice to admit at this scale in the QIRC?
> Researched 12 September 2026. ⚠ **Read the limits in Part 3 before relying on this.**

## 1. What the search actually found

Searches run across AustLII, Queensland Judgments (Supreme Court Library), JADE and the QIRC's
own site, on r 49, "notice to admit facts", Form 24 and Form 25, surfaced **exactly one QIRC
decision that turns on a notice to admit**:

⭐ **Tuesley v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2020] QIRC 027** (Hartigan IC, 20 February 2020,
WC/2018/50) — copy saved at `documents/authorities/Tuesley_v_WCR_2020_QIRC_027.pdf`.

Nothing surfaced in which an **appellant** served a notice to admit on the Regulator at all, let
alone at scale. No decision was found referring to a notice of more than about **34 paragraphs**.

## 2. Tuesley — the facts, and why it matters here

| | |
|---|---|
| Who served the notice | ⭐ **the Regulator**, on the worker |
| When | 31 January 2019 |
| Size | at least **34 paragraphs** (¶¶ 1–33 plus ¶ 34(a)–(h)) |
| How admitted | **deemed** under r 49(2) — the worker did not dispute within 14 days — **and then formally admitted** by counsel on the first day of hearing |
| Application to withdraw | filed **8 October 2019** — about 8 months after service, **after the hearing had commenced** |
| Outcome | ⭐ **dismissed, with costs against the appellant** |

### The test for withdrawing an admission — [6]–[8]

> **[6]** A party may, with leave of the Commission, withdraw a deemed admission. The exercise of
> such a discretion has been described as being **"broad and unfettered"**.
>
> **[7]** Relevant principles which may be considered in the exercise of the discretion include:
> **(a)** how and why the admission came to be made; **(b)** what is the evidence surrounding the
> issues the subject of the admission and whether there was likely to be **a real genuine dispute
> about the evidence**; **(c)** has there been **delay** in making the application for leave to
> withdraw the admission; and **(d)** what **prejudice**, if any, will the respondent suffer.
>
> **[8]** **An explanation on oath should be given by the applicant** with respect to [(a)] and
> [(b)].

Authorities relied on: *Hanson Construction Material P/L v Davey* [2010] QCA 246; *Ridolfi v
Rigato Farms Pty Ltd* [2001] 2 Qd R 455; *Tyler v Custom Credit Corp Ltd* [2000] QCA 178.

On prejudice, [36] adopts *Tyler*: the prejudice from delay is **"insidious as it is subtle in
that the parties cannot demonstrate what it is that they have forgotten"**.

### ⭐⭐ [39] — the Commission's own statement of what an admission does and does not do

> "The admissions to paragraph 34 are admissions to the facts that during a specified period,
> Ms Tuesley had 'significant non-work related stressors' which are then listed. **It will still
> be a matter for the Commission to determine what weight should be placed on those stressors**
> and whether the employment is a significant contributing factor to the injury."

And at [40], leave was refused partly because she **"will not be prevented or limited in how she
conducts her case"** on the s 32 question.

⇒ **This Commission has already drawn the exact line the parties have now drawn in
correspondence**: the admission fixes the fact; weight and significance remain for the
Commission. That is Matheson's letter of 8 September 2026, and it is paragraph 1 of the
Appellant's letter of 9 September 2026. The framing is orthodox.

## 3. ⚠ The limits of this research — do not overstate it

1. **AustLII's full-text search is Cloudflare-blocked** from this environment, and Queensland
   Judgments' search is JavaScript-driven and could not be queried directly. What is recorded
   above comes from indexed search, not an exhaustive full-text sweep.
2. **Absence from reported decisions is not absence in practice.** A notice to admit is
   interlocutory. It reaches published reasons only when something goes wrong — as in *Tuesley*,
   where a party was caught by one. A notice that works produces no judgment at all.
3. ⛔ **Never submit that this is "the largest notice to admit ever served in the QIRC."** It
   cannot be verified and it invites an easy correction. The defensible statement is narrower and
   better: *the only QIRC decision on r 49 that could be located concerns a notice of about 34
   paragraphs, served by the Regulator.*

## 4. Why Tuesley protects the 298 admissions in this matter

If the Regulator ever sought leave to withdraw, *Tuesley* supplies the test, and every limb is
against it:

| [7] principle | Position here |
|---|---|
| (a) how and why the admission came to be made | **Express, deliberate admissions** by a Senior Appeals Officer after 11 days' consideration, accompanied by a covering letter that quotes r 49 and analyses its effect. Not oversight, not error |
| (b) real genuine dispute | They would have to show a genuine dispute about facts they have admitted in writing, most of them drawn from **their own disclosure** |
| (c) delay | Any application now comes weeks after, with directions 3 and 4 due 30 September 2026 |
| (d) prejudice | The Appellant has served a witness list, outlines and a schedule of medical documents on the footing of the admissions, and has written to the Respondent setting out that footing |
| [8] explanation on oath | Would have to be given by the Regulator |

⭐ Note also the structural point: r 49(3) empowers withdrawal of **"an admission taken to have
been made under subrule (2)"** — a *deemed* admission. In *Tuesley* the admissions were deemed
**and** then formally made. Here the 298 are **express** admissions served inside the 14 days, so
no deemed admission ever arose. The rule's withdrawal mechanism does not, on its face, reach them
at all.

⚠ And the warning in the other direction: *Tuesley* is a worker who lost, with costs, because she
was caught by the Regulator's notice. It is a reminder that the instrument cuts both ways and that
this Commission enforces it.
