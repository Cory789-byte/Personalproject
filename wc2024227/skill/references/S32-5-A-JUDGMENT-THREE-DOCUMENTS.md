# WEIGHING THE THREE DOCUMENTS UNDER s 32(5)(a) — a judgment
> 15 August 2026. The AO3 role description · the Form 24 Notice and the Respondent's Response ·
> the Chief Executive's letter of 5 June 2026. Weighed against the exclusion in s 32(5)(a) WCRA.
> ⚠ **Onus:** the appellant bears the s 32(1) elements. **How the burden on s 32(5)(a) is allocated
> must be settled against authority before it is relied on** — the repo carries both formulations.

---

## PART 1 — ⭐⭐⭐ THE CONTROLLING INSIGHT
**s 32(5)(a) protects *"reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way"*. It protects
ACTION. It does not protect the ABSENCE of management.**

⇒ Wherever the employer's answer is *"no system applied, no assessment existed, nothing changed"*,
**that is not a defence under s 32(5)(a) at all.** It is a concession that there was no management
action there to be reasonable about. The exclusion has nothing to attach to.

⭐⭐ **And the *"non-clinical"* answer makes it worse, not better.** It explains **why** there was no
system. **It does not convert an absence into an action.**

## PART 2 — THE FOUR STRANDS, WEIGHED

### 2.1 ⭐⭐⭐ THE ROSTERING OF 17–18 MARCH 2024 — THE KEYSTONE
| Element | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum break **10 hours** (or 8 by written agreement) | Form 24 ¶3 | **Admitted** |
| The break given was **7 hours** | Form 24 ¶1 | **Admitted** |
| It was *"a rostering error that was accidentally made"* / **"human error"** | Form 24 ¶5; SOFC ¶22(a) | **Admitted** |
| **MET calls recorded on those shifts** | CE letter, Items 1–2 | **Certified by the employer** |
| **Leave taken the next day, 19 March** | CE letter Item 15; SOFC ¶¶22(c), 24(a) | **Admitted** |
| ⭐⭐ **No fatigue risk assessment existed** | CE letter, Items 4, 7 | **Certified by the CE** |
| ⭐⭐ **Fatigue risk management implemented only AFTER 30 June 2024** | CE letter, Item 5 | **Certified by the CE** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **NO consequential changes to operating procedures** | CE letter, Item 3(c) | **Certified by the CE** |

**THE RESPONDENT'S BEST ANSWER:** *an isolated error, and management action need not be perfect —
blemishes do not remove the exclusion.* ⭐ **That is a real answer and it usually works.**

⭐⭐⭐ **WHY IT FAILS HERE, AND IT IS THE CHIEF EXECUTIVE WHO DEFEATS IT:**
1. **It was not remedied.** *"No consequential changes to operating procedures."* An error corrected
   is a blemish. **An error followed by no change is a continuing state.**
2. **There was no system that could have prevented it.** No fatigue assessment applied to the role,
   ever, before 30 June 2024.
3. ⇒ **The employer cannot say "an isolated lapse in an otherwise managed system" when its own
   Chief Executive has certified there was no system.**

⭐ **JUDGMENT ON THIS STRAND: the exclusion has very little to work with.** Not because one error
defeats it, but because **the employer's own evidence converts the error into an unmanaged
condition — and an unmanaged condition is the absence of management action, not an instance of it.**

### 2.2 THE ROLE DESCRIPTION — NOT ACTION, BUT IT SETS THE STANDARD
The role description is not itself management action. **It establishes what the employer knew it
was requiring**: continuous 24/7 shift work · emergency response *"strictly adhering to protocols
and timeframes"* · *"call queues to minimum at all times"* · multitasking under high-volume pressure
· judgement *"where precedence have not been set and procedures not defined"* · limited supervision.

⭐⭐⭐ **Reasonableness is measured against what a reasonable employer in that position would do.**
An employer that defines a role in those terms, and then applies **no fatigue framework to it at
all** because of a training classification, is not managing that role reasonably. **The role
description is the yardstick the CE letter is measured by — and both are theirs.**

### 2.3 THE PAY AND THE AVAC — THE STRAND WHERE THE MANNER FAILS
| | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Payroll instructed the manager on **3 May 2024** to submit an AVAC | Form 24 ¶40 | **Admitted** |
| Not submitted until **28 May 2024 — 25 days** | Form 24 ¶41 | **Admitted** |
| On **21 May** she stated she was *"waiting payroll confirmation"* | Form 24 ¶46 | **Admitted** |
| She had held the written instruction **since 3 May** | Form 24 ¶47 | **Admitted** |

**Respondent's answer:** *she needed confirmation from payroll and to be satisfied the AVAC was
accurate.* ⛔ **That is contradicted by ¶47's own admission.**
⭐ **Delay is management action — but a 25-day delay against an express written instruction, with a
statement on day 18 that she awaited the very instruction she already held, is difficult to
characterise as taken in a reasonable way.** This strand fails on **manner**, not on substance.

### 2.4 THE DIRECTORY, AND THE COMPLAINTS SYSTEM
- **Directory (20 May 2024):** the role description makes maintaining accurate Switchboard
  information a **key responsibility**; the Regulator pleads he *"was not required or given
  authority to do any updating work"* (9C ¶20(b)). ⚠ **Do not overstate** — the plea may mean a
  specific system change rather than the duty. ⭐ **At its lowest it is an unexplained inconsistency
  between the role and the instruction, never explained. Unexplained is not reasonable.**
  ⚠ **Verify the plea survives into the 13 May 2026 SOFC before use.**
- **Complaints (CE letter Item 3(a)):** complaints about operational errors go **to the line
  manager** and are *"managed solely via email or verbally"*, nothing retained. ⭐⭐ **A complaints
  process with no record is not a process** — and the recipient is the manager whose conduct is in
  issue. **Again an absence, not an action.**

## PART 3 — ⭐⭐ APPLYING THE GATES
| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| **1. Is it management action at all?** | Rostering ✅ · pay handling ✅ · the directory instruction ✅. ⛔ **The absence of a fatigue framework, of a complaints record, and of any procedural change are NOT management action.** Gate 1 is not passed for those, and the exclusion cannot reach them |
| **2. Reasonable, AND taken in a reasonable way?** | Rostering: an admitted **error**, unremedied, in an unassessed exposure ⇒ **fails**. AVAC: 25 days against a written instruction, explanation self-contradicted ⇒ **fails on manner**. Directory: unexplained ⇒ **not established as reasonable** |
| **3. Every significant contributor must be excluded** | ⭐ **One surviving significant contributor that is not reasonable management action reasonably taken defeats the exclusion** |

## PART 4 — ⛔⛔ THE DISCIPLINE THAT DECIDES HOW THIS IS RUN
**Gate 3 tempts a composite case. ⛔ Do not take it.** *Delaney* — the Respondent's authority —
requires a **global** evaluation, and a global evaluation of a largely responsive employer may
survive. **Run *Mahaffey*: one mechanism, one proven instance.**

⭐⭐⭐ **RUN THE FATIGUE STRAND ALONE.** It is the only strand where every element is either admitted
by the Regulator or certified by the employer's Chief Executive:
> the rule · the breach · the characterisation as error · the absence of any control · the absence
> of any subsequent change · and the demand profile, in the employer's own role description.

**On that strand the exclusion has nothing to attach to, because the employer's own answer is that
there was no system.**

## PART 5 — ⚠⚠ THE HONEST WEAKNESSES
1. ⚠⚠⚠ **None of this establishes causation.** s 32(5)(a) only arises **after** s 32(1) is made out.
   **Without the report, this analysis decides nothing.** It is the second half of the case, not the
   first.
2. ⚠⚠ **March breach → June onset.** Answered by the continuing-exposure framing, but it is the
   attack and it must be put to the clinician directly.
3. ⚠ **Award cl 18.10** — fatigue leave requires overtime (SOFC ¶24(b)). Real, but it is an
   **entitlement** point, not a causation point. **Never merge them.**
4. ⚠ **"The FRMS never applied, so its absence is compliance, not failure."** ⭐ Answered by the
   hinge: the **10-hour minimum is Award- and agreement-based and exists independently of the FRMS**.
   The breach stands even if MSH is entirely right about the FRMS.
5. ⚠⚠ **A global evaluation may still favour them** if the tribunal looks at the whole course:
   meetings held, replies sent, pay errors ultimately corrected, the 2020 agreement relied on in
   good faith. **That is why the strand must stay narrow.**

---

## PART 6 — ⭐⭐⭐ THE JUDGMENT, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
**On the fatigue strand, and on these three documents alone, the s 32(5)(a) exclusion is unlikely to
succeed — because the employer's own Chief Executive has certified that no fatigue risk assessment
applied to the role, that fatigue risk management was introduced only after the injury, and that no
procedure changed after an error the Regulator admits was made; while the employer's own role
description establishes that the position required continuous 24-hour shift work and participation
in emergency response under strict timeframes. An employer cannot rely on an exclusion that protects
reasonable management action where its answer, in its own words, is that no management of that risk
existed.** ⚠ **That conclusion is worth nothing until s 32(1) is proved. The report is still the
whole case.**
