# SHOULD THE ACCEPTED PID GO TO THE DOCTOR? — the decision, and why
> 15 August 2026, on a reasoned proposal to include it under strict limits.
> **Conclusion: NO for the PID as such. YES for the underlying clinical fact, reworded.**
> ⭐ The dismissal and the overturned decision stay out — that part of the proposal is right.

---

## PART 1 — WHAT THE PROPOSAL GETS RIGHT
1. ⭐ **Foreseeable risk (3.7) is a genuine hook.** A formal acceptance touching danger to public
   health or safety is on its face relevant to a foreseeability question. That argument is real.
2. ⭐ **The completeness point has force.** The letter asks him to address the whole of the material
   and to say if anything qualifies his opinion.
3. ⭐⭐ **Excluding the dismissal and the overturned decision is correct** and for exactly the stated
   reason: they are legal outcomes, and putting them to a clinician invites the adoption of other
   decision-makers' findings that para 4 forbids.

## PART 2 — ⛔ WHERE THE ANALYSIS GOES WRONG
### 2.1 The PID is not a clinical fact. It is a determination about the CHARACTER of a complaint.
What the ESU determined is that **a complaint constituted a Public Interest Disclosure** under the
Act. ⭐⭐ **It did not determine that the danger existed.** Those are different findings, and the
proposal's *"a proper authority has accepted it"* elides them.

### 2.2 ⭐⭐⭐ The underlying seriousness is ALREADY in the pack — from the other side
| The safety pathway | Source | |
|---|---|---|
| Switchboard contact-detail accuracy is **critical to clinical handover and patient safety** | **Form 24 ¶8** | **admitted by the Regulator** |
| **MET calls recorded** on the shifts of 17–18 March 2024 | **CE letter, Items 1–2** | certified by the employer |
| Emergency response *"as per emergency code procedures, strictly adhering to protocols and timeframes"* | **Role description** | the employer's own |

⇒ **Question 2.1 already puts all three to him.** ⭐⭐⭐ **The PID adds no clinical fact that is not
already there. It adds only a legal characterisation.**

### 2.3 ⛔⛔ THE LIMITING INSTRUCTION WILL NOT HOLD
The proposed wording tells a clinician that an authority has **accepted a disclosure concerning
substantial and specific danger to public health or safety**, and invites him to weigh it.
⇒ A clinician told that will write something close to: *"he was working in an environment where a
formal disclosure about danger to patient safety had been accepted."*

**That sentence is reprisal-adjacent, is a legal characterisation adopted from another
decision-maker, and is precisely what para 4 forbids.**
⭐⭐⭐ **You cannot hand a doctor an accepted finding of danger to public health and safety and
expect him not to reason from it. The instruction asks him to hold a distinction the material itself
will not let him hold.**

### 2.4 ⛔⛔⛔ AND THE DECISIVE POINT, WHICH THE PROPOSAL DOES NOT REACH
The proposal is framed as an appeal question. **It is not.** Under the one-report decision **the
report goes to the employer.**

⇒ **Putting an accepted PID about Metro South Health into a psychiatric report, provided to Metro
South Health, funded by Metro South Health, while a capacity process is running, is a materially
different act from disclosing it in the appeal.**

⭐ The PID is admitted at Form 24 ¶20 and SOFC ¶15 — **in the appeal**. It is the reprisal track's
first element: banked, and deliberately not deployed. **Discipline Rule 8 sequences the parallel
tracks behind the WC settlement.** ⇒ **This deploys it, in the weakest available forum, before the
WC matter resolves.**

### 2.5 ⚠ AND "INCOMPLETENESS" CUTS THE OTHER WAY
A report is not incomplete for failing to address something **outside its instructions**. It is
incomplete for failing to address **what it was given**. ⭐ And the closing question — *whether
anything in the records qualifies the opinions* — already catches the real risk: if he thinks
something is missing, he says so.

---

## PART 3 — ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT TO DO INSTEAD: THE SAME VALUE, NONE OF THE RISK
**What the proposal actually wants is for the clinician to understand the seriousness of the safety
pathway he was operating inside.** That is a legitimate and important clinical input — and it is
obtainable without the statutory language.

> **Add as a bare fact:** *that in May 2024 he raised concerns about clinical risks arising from
> Switchboard information systems, and that those concerns were not investigated.*

⛔ **No "Public Interest Disclosure". No "accepted". No "substantial and specific danger". No
statutory framing. No dates connecting it to anything that followed.**

⭐⭐ **What that gives the clinician** is the psychosocial fact and nothing else: **an employee
responsible for information the employer admits is critical to patient safety, who raised concerns
about clinical risk, and whose concerns were not investigated.**
⇒ That is *responsibility imposed, response withheld* — **the mechanism of the whole case** — and it
carries no legal characterisation at all.

⚠ **VERIFY the "not investigated" element against source before it is used.** It is recorded in the
working notes as the PID being *closed uninvestigated*; confirm it.

---

## PART 4 — THE DECISION
| | |
|---|---|
| The **PID as such**, with statutory language | ⛔ **OUT** |
| The **dismissal** | ⛔ **OUT** — agreed |
| The **overturned Review Decision** | ⛔ **OUT** — agreed, and already the subject of a critical defect finding |
| ⭐ **That he raised concerns about clinical risks in May 2024 which were not investigated** | ✅ **IN, as bare fact, in Part B2** |

⭐ **The proposal's instinct is right and its target is right. Only the vehicle is wrong** — and the
substitute reaches the same clinical destination without putting a protected disclosure into the
employer's hands in a medical document.
