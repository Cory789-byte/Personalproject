## 1. OUTCOME

The Appellant indicated he would reconsider the Form 29. The Commissioner stood the application
down four weeks pending word from him. **No order was made against the Health Service. No evidence
was required. The objection was not tested.** Total exposure: one morning, three officers.

⭐ **This is a better outcome than I advised was likely.** I had prepared on the footing that the
Commissioner would set the objection down for hearing.

## 2. THE RISK WE AVOIDED, AND IT WAS THE REAL ONE

At [30:16] the Commissioner said that if the application were pressed, *"the health service is
going to need to call evidence to talk to their objections."*

**That is the outcome I was most concerned about and it did not eventuate.** Justifying the
objection on burden would have required a witness from our side to depose to what our systems
hold, what searches are feasible and at what cost. That witness would have been cross-examined —
by an appellant who, on the day, demonstrated a close command of the documents — **and the
cross-examination would not have stopped at burden.** It would have gone to what records exist
about rostering, fatigue and the switchboard, and to the assertions in our letter of 5 June.

⚠ **Record for the file: the Commissioner also observed that ours was *"a very comprehensive
objection"* and that the parties had *"a bit of work to do down there at the bar table"*.** That
was not a compliment. Had the matter proceeded I would expect some criticism of the objection-only
posture.

## 3. ASSESSMENT OF THE APPELLANT

I attended expecting a litigant in person with a large and undisciplined application. That is
half right, and the half that is wrong matters more.

### 3.1 He is not unprepared, and he is not a crank
Within thirty seconds of the Commissioner complaining about the volume of his material he produced
a **two-page summary he had brought with him**. He then took the Commissioner through it — the
column headings, the status entries, the sub-items A to E, and the concept of a keyword filter —
after the Commissioner twice said he could not follow it. **He was, for several minutes, teaching
the bench.** The Commissioner retained the concept for the rest of the hearing.

⇒ **He wrote his own material and he understands it.** The Commissioner's suggestion that it might
be AI-assisted was not pursued and, on the day, was answered by conduct. **We should not plan on
the basis that he does not know his file.**

### 3.2 He does not overstate, and that is a problem for us
Three times he conceded against his own interest without being pressed:
- that he had not asked the Regulator for the specific documents;
- that he had no knowledge of whether Ms Taylor was treated differently;
- that he could not prove the appointment was *"actively"* suppressed.

⭐ **A witness who concedes readily is difficult to damage.** He will not be broken on exaggeration,
because he does not exaggerate. **Cross-examination built on confrontation will fail with him.**

⇒ **Recommended approach if he is ever cross-examined: patient, incremental, document-anchored.
He concedes when pressed carefully. He does not concede when accused.**

### 3.3 His hedging is a habit, not evasion
He hedges constantly — *"probably"*, *"I guess"*, *"I think"*, *"not that I can recall"*. The
Commissioner corrected him once (*"Well, probably or is. Disclosure's about what you know."*) and
required a yes-or-no answer twice.

⚠ **Two consequences, and they cut opposite ways.** In the box the hedging will read as honesty
rather than evasion, and a tribunal will like it. But **it also means he can be walked backwards
in small steps**, and on the day it cost him his application (§3.4).

### 3.4 ⭐⭐ He gave away his own application through the disclosure
Twice he was asked whether he had documents and said he did not:
- asked whether the disclosure contained examples of the directives he complains of — **"Not that
  I can recall, no"**;
- asked whether he had Ms Reese's email directing him to retract — **"No, I do not."**

⚠⚠ **On my understanding of what has been produced in this proceeding, both answers were wrong.**
He appears not to have worked through the material already in his possession. The Commissioner
took the answers at face value and built the disposition on them.

⇒ **This is the single most useful thing observed on the day, and it will not last.** He will find
those documents. **Plan on the footing that he will.**

### 3.5 Where he is genuinely dangerous — and it is not where the Regulator's exposure is
Twice he moved to the consequence of the directives, and twice the Commissioner moved him off it:
> *"those doctors, the ones that get called for an emergency, they're sent to the wrong side of
> the room. They're not sent to someone having a cardiac arrest. They're not sent to someone in
> respiratory distress."*

The Commissioner's response was *"I'm just going to move on for a moment"*, and earlier *"let's not
get into the case."*

⛔⛔ **This is the passage to bring to the Executive's attention.** The Commissioner's remark that
managers make decisions without consulting staff *"all the time"* and that it is *"totally
legitimate"* is comfort **for the Regulator on the compensation question**. It is not comfort for
us. **The Appellant's framing is not an employment grievance. It is a clinical-governance
proposition about the Logan Hospital switchboard** — that changes were made to emergency contact
and call-routing controls without consulting the operators, and that misdirected emergency calls
followed.

⚠ Note the Commissioner did not reject that framing. He deferred it — *"If you think it's in a
patient safety environment, you can raise that."* **It remains available at the substantive
hearing, where it is a matter for the Regulator to meet but a matter for us to live with.**

### 3.6 What he does poorly
- **He argued the merits in a procedural listing.** He did not appear to understand what the
  hearing was for, and his strongest material was consequently delivered at the wrong moment and
  received as an efficiency problem.
- **He reaches for the systemic.** *"For me, it all works together as one big system."* The
  Commissioner's diagnosis — that he holds a broad grievance about the organisation and does not
  need to prove it — is accurate and is the most useful observation made against him.
- **He compresses badly under pressure**, and answers the question behind the question.

## 4. ASSESSMENT OF THE COMMISSIONER

Efficiency-driven; hostile to interlocutory disputes; **broad on relevance**; and prepared to spend
an hour educating a self-represented party rather than decide against him. He was scrupulous about
fairness — consent on the record, repeated non-criticism, the door expressly left open.

⚠ **He was not partisan in our favour.** He put the resolution burden on the Regulator and on us,
and he flagged that we would have to prove our objection. **We should not read the outcome as
approval of our position.**

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. ⭐ **Do nothing. Do not correspond. Do not volunteer.** Our position is best served by being
   forgotten. Every step we take gives him a reason to bring us back.
2. ⛔ **Do not offer the Regulator witnesses.** We are not a party. If the Regulator seeks our
   managers, that is a request to be considered on its merits at the time, with advice. **It is not
   an obligation.**
3. ⚠ **Anticipate renewed disclosure in narrower form.** The Commissioner expressly invited a
   single-document approach and said he would decide such disputes quickly. **A narrow, well-aimed
   request is far more likely to succeed than the Form 29 was, and is far harder to resist.**
4. **Review the assertions in the letter of 5 June** against what we can actually establish, before
   anyone is asked to stand behind them.
5. **The employment matters concerning this employee should be handled on their own merits and
   without reference to this proceeding.** Any adverse step taken now will be read against the
   background of what occurred on 7 August.

## 6. ⚠ THE CAUTION I WOULD ADD

We won this morning, but **he lost the weakest thing he had and it cost him almost nothing.** The
Form 29 was always going to be difficult for him. What he received in exchange was an hour of the
Commissioner explaining what he must prove, what is relevant, how uncontradicted evidence operates,
and what documents he already holds.

⇒ ⭐⭐ **He did not fight. He listened and adjusted.** In my experience an opponent who adjusts is
more troublesome than one who does not. **I would not record this as a durable win.**
