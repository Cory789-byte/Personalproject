# 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ s 32(5)(a) — THE BATTLEGROUND, AND HE WINS IT
| | |
|---|---|

reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way — against its own delegate's contrary finding,
with 298 facts admitted, and (as at today) no witness list.** ⛔ **That is a very poor position.**

# 5. ⛔⛔⛔ WHAT CAN ACTUALLY LOSE IT — IN ORDER

## 5.1 ⛔⛔⛔ NO DOCTOR IN THE ROOM — THE ONLY REAL ONE
⭐ **His own schedule: *"does not rely upon that opinion otherwise than through the oral evidence of
its author."*** ⇒ ⛔⛔⛔ **If neither attends, the diagnosis, the account of origin and the progression

(their document, relevance reserved).** ⭐ **De novo means the Commission must find causation itself.**
⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **This is not a small risk and it is not hypothetical. It is also entirely within his control.**

not fatal.**

# 6. ⭐⭐⭐ THE CALL
⭐⭐⭐⭐ **With a treating practitioner in the room, this is a case that should succeed, and that a
competent respondent should resolve rather than run.** ⭐⭐ **The s 32(1) elements are established on
their own delegate's findings; the s 32(5)(a) exclusion fails on their own delegate's contrary
finding and on at least seven admitted aspects of their own conduct; and they have no witness list
and no medical of their own.**
⛔⛔ **Without a practitioner it becomes genuinely uncertain — not lost, but uncertain — because the
Commission must find causation for itself and the strongest causation material is opinion he has
disclaimed except through oral evidence.**

## 6.1 ⭐⭐ AND WHAT "STRONG" DOES NOT BUY
⛔ **It is not quick.** ⛔ **It is not costless.** ⛔ **And the remedy is acceptance of the claim —
NOT a finding about the employer's conduct, not vindication, and not the parallel tracks.**
⭐⭐ **Wanting the second thing is how strong cases get run long past the point where they should have
been settled.**
