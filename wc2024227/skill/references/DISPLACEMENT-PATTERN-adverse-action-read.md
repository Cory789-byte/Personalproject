## ⚠️⚠️ 3.1 REDEPLOYMENT IS NOT A FAVOUR — IT IS THE STATUTORY OUTCOME OF THE PROCESS THEY ARE RUNNING
**PS Act s 107** — after a div 5 medical report, the chief executive may:
> *"(a) **transfer or redeploy** the employee; or (b) if it is not reasonably practicable to transfer
> or redeploy the employee— … **retire** the employee."*

⇒ ⛔ **If the RFMI is the front end of div 5, transfer/redeploy is where it ends.** That is the risk
in the current process, and it is why the RFMI response asks at Part 8 **what decision is
contemplated and under what instrument**.
⭐ **s 131(1)(g)** — a **transfer decision is appealable** to the IRC. ⚠️ But **s 132(4)(a)** bars a
*fair treatment* appeal against div 5 decisions, so the appeal must be framed as a **transfer
decision**, not a fair treatment one. **Get this right or lose the route.**

# PART 4 — WHY IT READS AS ADVERSE ACTION, IN THE STATUTORY WORDS
**IR Act s 282(1)** — adverse action is taken if the employer:
> *"(b) **injures the employee in the employee's employment**; or (c) **alters the position of the
> employee to the employee's prejudice**"*

⇒ ⭐⭐ **Neither requires a dismissal.** Removal of duties, unrostered supernumerary status, removal
from the department, exclusion, leave debiting and non-payment all sit comfortably within (b) and (c).
⇒ **s 284** — the workplace rights are engaged (Agreement benefit; ability to start a process; ability
to make a complaint or inquiry about employment).
⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **s 306 — he does not prove the reason. They disprove it.**
⇒ **s 571** — accessorial liability reaches individuals. **s 307** — advising, encouraging, inciting.
⇒ **PID Act s 40(5)** — enough that the disclosure is **a substantial ground**, *"even if there is
another ground"*.
