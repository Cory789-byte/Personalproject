## THE ERRORS, DOCUMENT BY DOCUMENT

### ⭐ 7. TAYLOR — 2 July 2026 (the origin document)
Shortest document; proportionally the most errors; **two of them material.**

1. **WRONG MONTH** — *"as per text message sent on Monday 29th **July**"*, in an email dated
   **Thursday 2 July**. 29 July had not occurred. The Monday before 2 July 2026 was **29 June**.
2. **WRONG DOCUMENT NAME** — *"a completed **Employee Capacity Certificate** (ECC)"*. The document
   is an Employee Capabilit**y**/Capabilit**ies** **Checklist**. "Capacity Certificate" is a third
   name for it and is wrong.
3. Grammatical — *"please wait for my advice **to** when your next returning shift will be"*
   (should be "as to when").

⚠️ **Note the naming problem across MSH generally:** the form itself says "Employee **Capability**
Checklist"; the 31 July letters say "Employee **Capabilities** Checklist"; Taylor says "Employee
**Capacity Certificate**". **Three names for one document.**

### ⭐ 6. HUGHES — 31 July 2026 (the RFMI)
Signed at Director level, most formal in appearance, **most errors of any document in the set.**

**Typographical / grammatical**
1. *"More information about the support TELUS Health can provide **cab be found out their**
   website"* — two errors in five words (employee letter)
2. *"Your report states **Mr Shepherds** 'working memory…'"* — missing apostrophe (Q7)
3. *"is Mr Shepherd able to **fulfill**…"* — US spelling in a Queensland Government letter (Q6)
4. *"**The purposes** of this request **is** to better understand…"* — subject–verb disagreement
5. *"Could you please clarify **the below question**"* — singular, followed by four sub-questions
   (a)–(d)

**Template drift — the decisive marker**
6. **Q3:** *"whether Mr Shepherd is medically fit to return to **their** substantive role… reporting
   to **their** current line manager"*
   **Q4:** *"If Mr Shepherd is not able to return to **his** substantive role…"*
   **Q7(a):** *"how this may affect **their** capacity to perform **their** substantive duties"*
   ⇒ *He* becomes *they* and back between adjacent questions. A gender-neutral template partially
   find-and-replaced.

**Substantive**
7. **Wrong legal test** — Q6 asks about performing the role *"without restrictions or
   modifications"*, when G03 (which the same letter cites) asks about **genuine occupational
   requirements with reasonable adjustment**
8. **False premise** — *"the Health Service is not aware of any concerns being raised"*, eight weeks
   after its Chief Executive signed an objection engaging **PID 24-ESU-1130 by reference number**

**Authorship seam** — written in Harrison's first person (*"I am seeking"*, *"I require"*, *"I will
make a decision"*), signed by Hughes.

## WHAT THE COMPARISON SHOWS

### 1. Quality does not track seniority
The **least** developed documents are from the **line manager** (2 July) and the **Director**
(31 July). The **most** developed are the lawyer-drafted objection and — notably — Harrison's
29 July reply.

### 2. Hughes's own output is inconsistent
His **8 September 2025** letter is competently built: 34 itemised entries, proper structure,
acknowledgment of circumstances. His **31 July 2026** letters carry more errors than any other
document in the set. Same signatory, eleven months apart, markedly different care.
⇒ Consistent with the September letter having been prepared over time and the July one assembled
from a template at speed.

### 3. The 29 July inflection
Harrison's register changes markedly between 15 and 29 July — from conversational to formal, from
"I" to "We", and to correct statutory citation. **It follows Cory's 28 July letter by one day.**

### 4. The 31 July letters are formal in appearance and poor in construction
Director's signature, formal letterhead, statutory citations, structured questions — and pronoun
drift between adjacent questions, four typographical errors, the wrong legal test under the policy
they cite, and a false premise about a disclosure their Chief Executive addressed by reference
number.

⇒ **This is the central finding.** The document carrying the most authority is the one with the
least care in it.

## USE

  Pointing out typos reads as petty and forfeits the register that is currently working.
- **Two errors are substantive and are already deployed** in the response: the wrong test at Q6, and
  the false premise at Q2. Those are argued on the instruments, not as mistakes.

  quietly: if MSH ever asserts a failure to provide "the required document", three different names
