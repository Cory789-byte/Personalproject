# 18 June 2024, 8:58 am — the email on the onset date (Cory, 14 Sep)
> The coincidence is real and is not yet drawn anywhere: the injury date on every certificate is
> 18 June 2024, and at 8:58 am that morning the Manager wrote to the department: "I am taking
> today off, and hopefully will be back tomorrow." "I am sorry I haven't been there for you all
> over the past week." "Ellen will not be in today, however Tammy is in the office if anything
> urgent." (¶¶85–87, Tab 30A — authenticity disputed, contents admitted.) The pleaded onset and
> the admitted email carry the same date, and the email is the morning's first document.

## What it is, and what it must never become
⛔ **The email is the crystallisation, never the cause.** Contention 3 pleads the injury as
"arising from the course of conduct pleaded at Part B.2 and not from an event on a single day" —
that wording is correct law for an accumulation injury and it is protective: it forecloses
"an apology email caused your injury?" The email's role is the **marker**: the morning the
accumulated condition declared itself, and the last document he read before it did. On its face
the email is innocuous; its weight is that it is the ten months in three lines — the manager
absent, the coordinator absent, the support layer gone "for the past week", again — said by the
manager herself, timestamped on the date every certificate carries.

## Why it is evidence gold once Tab 30A is in
- The onset-day link between employment and injury is **documentary and admitted**: no memory,
  no characterisation, the employer's own words on the pleaded date.
- The apology certifies the absence: "I am sorry I haven't been there for you all" is the
  manager's own acknowledgement, on the onset date, that the support had been absent the
  preceding week — the condition pleaded at Stressor 1, confirmed from her hand at the moment
  the injury dates from.
- Nothing about it can be crossed: sent, said, dated — admitted. Only his reaction is his
  evidence, and it is corroborated ten days later ("stress at work", 28 June) and on 1 July
  ("work stress… manipulate his roster").

## The evidence-in-chief paragraph (premise first — his account to supply the missing facts)
> "By June 2024 I had not been able to work since 3 June. On the morning of 18 June 2024 I read
> Ms Taylor's email of 8:58 am. It said she was taking the day off, that she was sorry she had
> not been there for us all over the past week, and that Ellen would not be in either. [WHERE HE
> WAS, WHAT HAPPENED IN HIM — his words, to be supplied: the realisation that it was not going
> to change.] That is the day my injury dates from, and it is the date on every certificate."
⚠ Only he can supply the middle sentence. Prepare it; do not script it.

## The cross, and the prepared answer
*"An email saying your manager was taking a day off caused a major depressive disorder?"* —
"No. Ten months of the conditions the Commission has seen caused it. That email was the morning
I understood they were not going to change. The date is not mine; it is on the certificates,
and the email is theirs."

## The way in from the events — the moral-load stressor (Cory, 14 Sep)
The route that captures the traumatic dimension without the PTSD presumption already exists in
the case, and it is not "trauma from the codes". It is the **position**: a written duty for the
emergency-response function {{8}}, the means of keeping it safe held by others {{39–45}},
harm to patients foreseen and reported — "we can not help patients or other clinical staff",
"switchboard could not tell them where VHUB was" — and nothing done: no communication to the
staff for six days {{69}}, no response to the IRS alleged {{90,93}}, no safety assessment,
investigation or consequential change following any complaint {{266,267,272}}. Sustained
foreseeable-harm burden plus futility. The clinical literature calls it moral distress; the
pleading never needs the label, because **the treating notes already carry it in their own
words, contemporaneously**: 28 Jun 2024, "stress at work… upset by people not following rules";
1 Jul 2024, "they don't listen to his complaints". The doctors wrote the mechanism down within
a fortnight of onset, unprompted. That is the causation bridge for THIS stressor, already built.
**Where it lives:** S1(d) (patient safety), B.2.0 (the admitted environment, ¶289), outline
topics 1 and 7, and the evidence-in-chief moment: what it was like to dispatch a code knowing
the directory might be wrong — the premise-first account (see the 29:17 correction).
**The s 32(5) bonus:** this stressor is a *condition + its non-remedy*, not action taken against
the worker — first-limb territory; and even the complaint-handling limb is management action
whose reasonableness they must prove with the witnesses they cannot afford.
⛔ **The PID discipline holds unchanged:** the WC track pleads the fact of the complaint of
13 May 2024, its determination, and the absence of any response to the functions raised — the
content of the disclosure is never set out, and "patient safety" as the descriptor of the PID
itself stays off WC paper; the patient-safety substance enters through the admitted clinical
emails and MSH's certified absences instead, which is stronger anyway.
⛔ And nothing is ever suggested to the doctors. They found the mechanism themselves in 2024;
their independence is its value.

## Adverse outcomes — the line that is never crossed (Cory, 14 Sep)
The case is built so that **no patient outcome is needed, asserted or implied**, and that is a
strength, not a gap:
1. **His injury is from carrying the risk, not from a result.** The stressor is the duty (¶8),
   the foreseen and reported misdirections (¶68 occurred; "we can not help patients"), and the
   futility (¶¶266, 272). Whether any patient was ultimately harmed changes his injury not at
   all — the burden was the foreseeing. "No one died" does not diminish the case by one line.
2. ⛔ **Never allege, hint or invite an inference of patient harm.** Unproven, it is the fastest
   route back to the "broad grievance" Dwyer warned against, it would read as leveraging patient
   tragedy for compensation, and any real adverse outcome belongs to other jurisdictions
   (clinical incident / RCA processes carry their own statutory privileges; the coroner has his
   own). If such a thing exists, it is not his to run here — and it would only deepen their
   reasons never to let this reach a room.
3. **"No adverse outcome" is a trap for THEM, not him.** If their counsel runs "the codes were
   routine, nothing came of the misdirects": (a) it concedes the events; (b) it trivialises
   their own duty statement — the RD makes response time-critical precisely because outcomes
   turn on minutes; the submission argues against their own document; (c) it asserts a negative
   only their clinical-incident records could prove — records they refused to search as too
   burdensome. The answer, if it is ever put to him: "My duty was to prevent that question from
   ever having an answer. That is why the reports distressed me." Premise first, then stop.
4. **If an adverse outcome ever surfaces independently**, his contemporaneous warnings become
   prescient — vindication that needs no help — and this appeal still does not change: it was
   never pleaded on outcomes and never needs to be.
