# 18 June 2024, 8:58 am — the email on the onset date (Cory, 14 Sep)
> The coincidence is real and is not yet drawn anywhere: the injury date on every certificate is
> 18 June 2024, and at 8:58 am that morning the Manager wrote to the department: "I am taking
> today off, and hopefully will be back tomorrow." "I am sorry I haven't been there for you all
> over the past week." "Ellen will not be in today, however Tammy is in the office if anything
> urgent." (¶¶85–87, Tab 30A — authenticity disputed, contents admitted.) The pleaded onset and
> the admitted email carry the same date, and the email is the morning's first document.

## What it is, and what it must never become
⛔ **The email is the crystallisation, never the cause.** Contention 3 pleads the injury as
"arising from the course of conduct pleaded at Part B.2 and not from an event on a single day" —
that wording is correct law for an accumulation injury and it is protective: it forecloses
"an apology email caused your injury?" The email's role is the **marker**: the morning the
accumulated condition declared itself, and the last document he read before it did. On its face
the email is innocuous; its weight is that it is the ten months in three lines — the manager
absent, the coordinator absent, the support layer gone "for the past week", again — said by the
manager herself, timestamped on the date every certificate carries.

## Why it is evidence gold once Tab 30A is in
- The onset-day link between employment and injury is **documentary and admitted**: no memory,
  no characterisation, the employer's own words on the pleaded date.
- The apology certifies the absence: "I am sorry I haven't been there for you all" is the
  manager's own acknowledgement, on the onset date, that the support had been absent the
  preceding week — the condition pleaded at Stressor 1, confirmed from her hand at the moment
  the injury dates from.
- Nothing about it can be crossed: sent, said, dated — admitted. Only his reaction is his
  evidence, and it is corroborated ten days later ("stress at work", 28 June) and on 1 July
  ("work stress… manipulate his roster").

## The evidence-in-chief paragraph (premise first — his account to supply the missing facts)
> "By June 2024 I had not been able to work since 3 June. On the morning of 18 June 2024 I read
> Ms Taylor's email of 8:58 am. It said she was taking the day off, that she was sorry she had
> not been there for us all over the past week, and that Ellen would not be in either. [WHERE HE
> WAS, WHAT HAPPENED IN HIM — his words, to be supplied: the realisation that it was not going
> to change.] That is the day my injury dates from, and it is the date on every certificate."
⚠ Only he can supply the middle sentence. Prepare it; do not script it.

## The cross, and the prepared answer
*"An email saying your manager was taking a day off caused a major depressive disorder?"* —
"No. Ten months of the conditions the Commission has seen caused it. That email was the morning
I understood they were not going to change. The date is not mine; it is on the certificates,
and the email is theirs."
