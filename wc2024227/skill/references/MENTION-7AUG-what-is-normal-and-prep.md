# PART 1 — WHAT A MENTION IS, AND WHAT IS ACTUALLY REQUIRED A WEEK OUT

**A mention is procedural.** It is not the hearing of the 64G application. Its ordinary business:
confirm where the matter is up to, deal with appearances, and set a timetable (submissions on the
objection, or a hearing date, or orders by consent on anything agreed).

**What the rules actually require of MSH before it: nothing further.**
- The 22 May direction required objections by **4:00pm Friday 5 June**. MSH served its objection
  (K-LM26/729) at **15:02 on 5 June**. ⇒ **That obligation is discharged.**
- There is **no obligation** to file submissions, confirm attendance in writing to him, or
  correspond before a mention, unless directed. No direction requires it.

⇒ ⛔ **So their silence this week is not a breach of anything, and must not be characterised as
one.** A week of quiet before a procedural mention is common.

## SO, HIS THREE QUESTIONS, ANSWERED PLAINLY
1. **Should they have confirmed attendance?** Customary if represented; **not required**. Absence
   of confirmation tells you nothing yet.
2. **Should they have emailed something?** Good practice would be a letter narrowing the objection
   or proposing consent orders. **Nothing compels it.** Their pattern (Part 1 of the obligations

3. **Should they have filed something coming down from the full objection?** ⭐ **No rule requires
   it — and it is BETTER FOR HIM that they have not.** A full-width objection against a restrained,
   partly-conceded application is the least attractive posture an objector can bring to a
   Commissioner. **Every week they do not narrow it, the contrast his application was built on
   gets sharper.** Discipline rule 6 did its work in the drafting; their silence is completing it.
