# 4. ⛔⛔ THE LINE — AND WHY IT IS NOT NEGOTIABLE

> ⭐⭐⭐ **PATIENT SAFETY APPEARS AS THE MEASURE OF THE RESPONSIBILITY. NEVER AS THE SUBJECT OF A
> DISCLOSURE.**

**The permitted proposition** (Mahaffey — responsibility imposed, means withheld):
*"The role required adherence to emergency code protocols and timeframes (fact 8). The Respondent
admits the contact-detail function is critical to clinical handover and patient safety (fact 289).
Clinicians recorded the function failing (facts 56, 58, 89, 91). The Appellant raised it on 20 May
(fact 94). The Respondent does not allege the document was amended, or the staff notified (facts
109, 110)."*
⇒ ⭐ **That is a workload-and-means proposition. It never says he was a discloser, and it needs
nothing from the PID or the CCC.**

**⛔ THE FORBIDDEN PROPOSITION:** that he raised patient safety and was treated adversely for it.

## 4.1 THREE REASONS, EACH SUFFICIENT ON ITS OWN
1. ⛔⛔ **It opens s 32(5)(b).** A disclosure-and-consequence narrative is a motive narrative, and
   motive is the door the file has been built to keep shut (*punishment*, *hostile*, *capricious*,
   *reprisal*). **The chronology, never the motive.**
2. ⛔⛔ **It converts the case from *Mahaffey* to *Delaney*.** The WC case is one mechanism with one
   admitted instance. Reframed as whistleblowing, the issue becomes **how a complaint was handled** —
   and management action on a complaint is far easier ground for the Respondent. ⚠ **The Review
   Decision already went there: it found contacting him on leave reasonable.** Do not hand them the
   same ground again.
3. ⛔⛔ **It collides with rules 8 and 9.** The parallel tracks sit **behind** the WC settlement, and
   the settlement releases **the compensation claim only**. ⭐ **If the WC record carries a disclosure
   narrative, an argument becomes available that a release drafted against this proceeding swept in
   the disclosure conduct. Keeping the tracks apart is what protects the deed scope.**
