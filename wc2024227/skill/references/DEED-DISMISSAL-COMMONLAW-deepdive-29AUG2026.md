## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ THE GATEWAY — s 237 WCRA. THIS IS THE SECTION THAT DECIDES EVERYTHING

> **s 237(1)** *"The following are **the only persons entitled to seek damages** for an injury
> sustained by a worker— (a) the worker, if the worker— (i) **has received a notice of assessment**
> from the insurer for the injury; or (ii) has not received a notice of assessment for the injury
> but…"*
> **s 237(5)** *"To remove any doubt, it is declared that subsection (1) **abolishes any entitlement
> of a person not mentioned in the subsection** to seek damages for an injury sustained by a
> worker."*
> *(ATT26, PDF pp 257–259, Act pp 255–257. "Notice of assessment" = one issued under s 185, Sch 6.)*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **CONFIRMED AGAINST SOURCE: he holds no notice of assessment, has no terminal condition, and
is alive. He is not within s 237(1). He cannot presently seek damages, and s 237(5) does not merely
withhold the right — it ABOLISHES it.**

⇒ ⭐⭐ **The proposition this file has been relying on — that the appeal is the gateway — is CORRECT,
verified from the section itself.**

### ⚠ AND ONE THING THE DEED CANNOT DO
**cl 8 preserves the claim but cannot create one.** s 235(1)–(2) makes Chapter 5 substantive law
prevailing over any inconsistent rule. ⭐ **The deed's own solicitors said so in writing on
13 February 2025 — they could not advise whether such a claim existed.**

### ⭐ AND ONE THING PART 8 DOES NOT DO
⛔ A first-pass claim that Chapter 5 Part 8 is a *"statutory negligence code"* replacing the common
law was **REFUTED**. **s 10(1) defines "damages" as damages for injury in circumstances creating,
"INDEPENDENTLY OF THIS ACT", a legal liability in the employer.** ⇒ **The cause of action remains the
common law action in negligence. Part 8 MODIFIES discrete elements — it does not codify.**
⭐ **s 305B** does import a three-limb breach test *(confirmed verbatim, printed p 291)*.

## 5. ⭐⭐⭐ LIMITATION — THE BIGGEST CORRECTION IN THE FILE

### 5.1 WHAT WAS WRONG
This repository has been operating on a hard deadline of **~18 June 2027** with a panic that the
appeal must beat it. ⭐ **That framing was wrong.**

### 5.2 THE PROVISION NOBODY HAD FOUND — WCRA SCHEDULE 5 s 2
Reached through **s 302(1)(b)**; Schedule 5's own headnote points back to it.

> **Sch 5 s 2(2)** *(Act p 580 = PDF p 582, verbatim):* *"A proceeding for damages for the injury may
> be brought— (a) **within 6 months after the claimant's application is accepted**; or (b) [if the
> claimant asks the insurer for a DPI assessment] **within 6 months after the notice of
> assessment**…"*

**All three s 2(1) conditions are satisfied on this chronology:** application lodged before the end
of the general period; the application has been the subject of a Chapter 13 review (24 Oct 2024) and
appeal (WC/2024/227, filed 26 Nov 2024); and it has not been accepted.

### 5.3 ⛔ BUT TWO FIRST-PASS CONCLUSIONS WERE REFUTED — THE CORRECTED VERSION GOVERNS
| First pass said | ⭐ Corrected |
|---|---|
| Sch 5 **replaces** the general period | ⛔ **s 302(1) is DISJUNCTIVE.** Schedule 5 supplies an **ALTERNATIVE** period, not a substitute. **18 June 2027 still exists** |
| Sch 5 installs a **sharper trap** — a self-executing 6 months extendable ONLY by a DPI request | ⛔ **Schedule 5 s 2 is an EXTENSION provision, not a guillotine** |
| The **notice of claim** must be given before expiry, not the proceeding | ⚠ True as far as it goes (**s 302(2)**), **but** ⛔ **s 275(8)(d) makes a complying notice of claim IMPOSSIBLE without a notice of assessment** |

### 5.4 ⭐⭐ WHAT THAT LEAVES — AND THE ONE ACTION AVAILABLE NOW
The bind is real: to preserve under the general period you must give a notice of claim; you cannot
give a complying one without a notice of assessment; you cannot get one without acceptance.
**Schedule 5 is the answer to that bind** — it keys the period to acceptance instead.

⭐⭐⭐ **AND THE ONE THING THAT CAN BE DONE NOW:** the verifier found that a **request to the insurer
to have the injury assessed for DPI** may be made at any time before the end of the six months —
*"it may be asked now — Sch 5 s 2(2)(b) fixes only an end-point, and s 179(1)'s only precondition, a
s 132 application, is already met."*
⚠ **VERIFY THIS BEFORE ACTING.** If right, it is a one-line letter that materially improves his
position at no cost. ⛔ **Do NOT lodge a notice of claim now — s 303 makes a premature one
affirmatively harmful.**

### 5.5 ⭐ COSTS
**s 316 caps adverse costs in the damages proceeding to post-final-offer costs only.** ⇒ The
downside risk is far smaller than an ordinary civil claim, which matters for no-win-no-fee appetite.
