# PART 2 — ⭐⭐⭐ WHY HE DOES NOT NEED TO PROVE IT
**Every defence MSH has is a *reasonableness* defence. They do not have one that isn't.**

| Their defence | The word it turns on | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The exclusion was management action | *"**reasonable** management action taken in a **reasonable** way"* | **WCRA s 32(5)(a)** — the appeal |
| It was not a reprisal | *"**reasonable** management action taken **reasonably**"* | **PID Act**, restated in the ESU letter |
| We can refuse the adjustment | *"only on **reasonable** grounds"* | **EB12 cl 10.3.5** |
| We need not restore the status quo | *"a **genuine** safety issue"* | **EB12 cl 1.11.4** |
| We have not delayed | *"**unreasonably** or intentionally delay"* | **EB12 cl 1.11.5** |
| The adjustment is too hard | *"**unjustifiable** hardship"* — and the term must be *"not **reasonable**"* | **AD Act ss 5, 11, 35, 36** |
| The information was needed | *"**reasonably** required"* | **AD Act s 124(3)** |

⇒ ⭐ **He never has to prove bad faith. He only has to make reasonableness untenable — and the
documents in Part 1 do that on their own, without a single characterisation.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE ONUS IS ALREADY WHERE HE WANTS IT
**IR Act s 306 — verified, ATT22, current 1 January 2026:**
> *"**It is presumed that the action was, or is being, taken for that reason or with that intent,
> unless the person proves otherwise.**"*

⇒ In a general protections application, **he alleges the reason; MSH must disprove it.**
⇒ **G03 cl 2** does the same for adjustments: *"the onus is on Queensland Health, as the employer,
to prove an adjustment is unreasonable."*
⇒ **PID Act s 40(5)**: it is enough that the unlawful ground is **a substantial ground**, *"even if
there is another ground"*.

**Three separate instruments put the burden on them. He does not carry it. He never has to say the
words.**

# PART 3 — ⛔⛔ WHY "OBVIOUSLY" IS THE TRAP
**The conclusion is right. Saying it is what loses it.**

1. **Bad faith is never obvious to a decision-maker.** It is the last thing a tribunal concludes, if
   it concludes it at all, and only from documents. An appellant who announces it up front asks to
   be believed before anything has been proved.
2. ⚠️ **It moves the question.** The moment he characterises their conduct, the tribunal stops
   assessing **their** conduct and starts assessing **his characterisation**. **He hands them a
   defence they do not currently have: that he is a man who reads motives into administration.**
   ⇒ ⛔ That is the exact narrative MSH has been unable to build, and it cannot be built without him.
3. **It is a live risk, not a theoretical one.** The Monte Carlo puts accusation framings at
   **−16 to −26** points of settlement position.
4. **Discipline rules 1 and 2.** Conspiracy and collusion framing never reaches paper; the
   asymmetry is held in reserve.
5. ⭐ **He loses nothing by silence.** s 306 presumes the reason. G03 cl 2 puts the onus on them.
   PID s 40(5) needs only a substantial ground. **The law says it for him, and says it better.**

## ⭐ THE SUBSTITUTION THAT WORKS
| ⛔ Never write | ✅ Write instead |
|---|---|
| *"This was not in good faith"* | *"On 7 July the Health Service recorded that it did not have sufficient information to assess whether there were psychosocial hazards involving line management. I have asked what assessment has since been undertaken."* |
| *"They deliberately withheld it"* | *"I have asked which instrument is relied on and have not received an answer."* |
| *"Given the history between us"* | ⛔ **nothing. Say nothing.** The history is in the file and the file goes to the union. |
| *"They are targeting me"* | *"I ask that the decision be made by an officer outside Corporate Services."* |

⇒ **Each right-hand version is unanswerable, and each one leaves the inference intact for someone
else to draw.** ⭐ **That is the whole method: the asymmetry does the work; he never announces it.**
