# The public forum — who actually carries the exposure, and why it must never be voiced
> 13 September 2026. Verified: Appeal Guide 8.1; IR Act s 580; the served admissions;
> MSH objection K-LM26/729 of 5 June 2026.
> ⛔ **This note is analysis. Nothing in it may be written, said, implied or relied upon in any
> correspondence, application, offer or submission. Read Part 5 before using any of it.**

## 1 · The fact

**Appeal Guide 8.1:** *"Unless a suppression order has been issued, all decisions are published on
the Supreme Court Library website and are publicly accessible… see s 580 of the Industrial
Relations Act 2016."*

Publication is the **default**. Hearings are open. This was verified this session by downloading
*Tuesley v Workers' Compensation Regulator* [2020] QIRC 027 from that database — including the
appellant's diagnosis and her listed non-work stressors.

## 2 · What a published decision would contain, on admitted facts alone

- emergency response notifications reaching the wrong medical teams on **nine occasions**, reported
  by a MASPER Registrar (¶¶ 56–61);
- a **MET team telephoning the Switchboard to be told where an emergency was, and not being told**
  (¶ 58);
- a clinical service writing twice, marked high importance, that it *"can not help patients or
  other clinical staff"* (¶¶ 89, 91);
- the Switchboard Manager's own words — *"many ongoing issues raised by the MASPER and the medical
  department about calls being transferred to the wrong medical teams"* (¶ 67);
- the Respondent's admission that accurate contact details are *"a critical function of the
  Switchboard to ensure effective clinical handover and **patient safety**"* (¶ 289);
- database access removed **eleven months** on a stated temporary basis (¶¶ 39, 40, 55);
- a seven-hour break already found by the Regulator's own review officer to be **unreasonable
  management action**.

That is a published decision about **patient safety at a Queensland public hospital switchboard**.

## 3 · ⭐⭐ The structural point: the exposure and the control sit in different places

| | Regulator | Metro South Health |
|---|---|---|
| Party to the appeal | **yes** | **no** |
| Controls the conduct of the defence | **yes** | no |
| Defends what, exactly | its own **decision** | nothing — it has no standing |
| Carries the reputational consequence | little | ⭐ **all of it** |
| Whose employees are cross-examined | none of its own | **Taylor, Reese, Stibbard** |

**The party that can settle does not carry the exposure. The body that carries the exposure cannot
settle.** That misalignment is the single most interesting structural feature of the matter.

MSH's posture is already on the record: it **objected** to the Form 29 (K-LM26/729, 5 June 2026),
signed by its Chief Executive, enquiries to its Principal Lawyer. An organisation that resists
producing documents is not one that wants its Switchboard Manager cross-examined on them.

## 4 · What it is actually worth

1. **It is a concession pressure that builds as a hearing date approaches** (there is no settlement in a WC appeal — Guide 5.1) — not now. It bites at
   the point witnesses must genuinely be prepared and a date is listed.
2. **It bears on whether the Regulator calls Taylor and Reese.** If MSH is reluctant to make them
   available, the Regulator may prefer not to call them — which is exactly why the Registry letter
   of 9 September foreshadowing attendance notices matters. It removes that exit.
3. **It explains incentives, which helps prediction.** That is its legitimate use and its only one.

## 5 · ⛔⛔ THE DISCIPLINE — why this must never be voiced

1. **Any suggestion that settlement should follow from the publicity of a hearing, or from what
   MSH's employees would have to say in public, edges toward improper pressure.** In correspondence
   it would read as a threat. In a Calderbank it could taint the offer. Before the Commission it
   would be fatal to credit.
2. It breaches the standing rules of this matter directly: **no motive, no characterisation, ask
   never allege.**
3. ⚠ **It cuts both ways.** A published decision publishes **his** medical evidence — major
   depressive disorder with anxious distress, the treating psychiatrist's report, the general
   practice records. *Tuesley* names the appellant's condition and her non-work stressors on a
   public website six years later. **A win is published too.**
4. ⚠ **He has already sought confidentiality over part of this himself** — draft order 6 seeks
   s 580 non-publication of the PID and the ESU determination. He cannot rely on publicity as
   pressure while seeking suppression of the part that suits him.

⇒ **The correct posture is exactly the one already adopted: plead the conditions, never the
consequences of airing them. Let the other side do its own arithmetic.** It does not need help,
and offering help would be the one thing capable of converting a strong position into a
sanctionable one.

**The same week, in public.** Hearings are open; the decision is published (s 580) and indexed

Cory is not yet aware". Day five: the doctors, then closings built from admissions. Every
sentence quotable, in the employer's own words, in a published decision that names Logan
Hospital's emergency-call failures with MSH's certificate of absence beside them — citable in
every later FRMS, WHS, AD Act or PID forum, and readable by every switchboard operator, union
official and journalist in the state, forever. For the Appellant, the same week is vindication;
he carries nothing into it that is not already admitted. **The publicity is a one-way valve:
everything public hurts one side only — which is why the side it hurts is not the one that
will choose the room.**
