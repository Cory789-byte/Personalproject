## 3. Why the "what it means" contest has no vehicle
1. **Pleading.** SOFC para 27 identifies no management action (admitted fact 303). The Commission decides on the pleaded case: Langerak [2020] ICQ 2 at [72]–[75] (as applied in the Carr appeal, [2023] ICQ 1; cite Langerak, not Carr). Expect an amendment after 22 Oct; hold it to particulars that fit inside the 298 admissions.
2. **Evidence.** Four lay witnesses, no expert. Taylor and Reese wrote the documents. Their outlines concede "human error", "a rostering practice issue for the line manager", and a decline "made in error".
3. **Dwyer, 7 Aug (segs 799–805, about 50:16–50:31), verbatim:** "You make the assertion here. Once you've made the assertion, the regulator's got to respond to it. They've got to address it. They'll address it in cross-examination. They may produce documents that they seek to rely on to contradict it. They've got to disclose those documents to you." And (segs 416–419): "that's a problem for them, not for you."
4. **Commission not bound by rules of evidence:** IR Act 2016 s 531(2)(a), (b) (ATT22, current 1 Jan 2026). A general admissibility reservation is weak. Weight is the real contest.

## 4. The trilemma, for any "that is not what it means"
- **Adopt:** the author says in evidence that the document means what it says. It is proved.
- **Disavow:** the author contradicts her own contemporaneous words. She is impeached by her own email, and the Regulator's case loses credibility.
- **Silence:** nobody contradicts Cory's account. "A problem for them."

## 5. Levers, in order
1. **At the conference (counsel present):** four closed questions.
   - Does the Regulator dispute any of the 298 admitted facts? Which, and on what evidence?
   - Which of the documents the Regulator itself disclosed in July 2025 will it object to at tender?
   - What management action does para 27 rely on?
   - Does it maintain its reservation over the review decision's finding on 17–18 March (fact 260), given its own outlines call the break "human error"? (optional)
2. **Ask for a direction under IR(T) Rules r 41(2)(j):** the parties confer, identify points in issue, and report back. A joint list in which each admitted fact is "not disputed" or "disputed on this ground, with this evidence". Dwyer wants efficiency (7 Aug: "interlocutory disputes… delay and complicate").
3. **If the Regulator seeks to withdraw an admission:** it needs leave and a reason. That is delay, the opposite of what Dwyer instructed on 13 Oct.
4. **Keep the question out of the 17 Oct letter.** If put in writing before counsel returns, it invites a distinction to be built.

## 6. Do not
- Claim the admissions prove the events. They prove words, dates, intervals and what was done in response. Cory's own evidence supplies the events.
- Run an estoppel. There is none. This is forensic and credibility pressure.
- Spend credibility on the post-June 2024 documents (Tabs 17–19, 21–24). Relevance to s 32 is thin and the Regulator can fairly object. Concentrate on the pre-June 2024 core.
- Use "column reflex", "sneaky" or any motive language outside this file.
