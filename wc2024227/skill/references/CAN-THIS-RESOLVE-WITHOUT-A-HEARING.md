# Can this resolve without a contested hearing?

**WC/2024/227 · 17 September 2026 · ⛔ INTERNAL ONLY. Nothing sent; quiet period holds.**

## ⭐ The proposition is right. The reason given for it is not.

---

## 1. The route exists, and it is named in the Guide

**Appeal Guide v2.10, § 5.1, verbatim:**
> *"Following the conference, **the Respondent may decide to review its position or consider
> conceding the appeal where new information is presented that they may not have yet considered.**"*
> *"…the conference can assist in understanding **whether any elements of s 32 of the Act can be
> agreed**."*

**§ 7.5:** *"You can settle and discontinue your matter at any time by agreement"* (Form 27).

⇒ ⭐⭐ **§ 5.1 is an exit that requires no admission of error.** It is framed as *new information not
previously considered* — and **298 admissions and an enlarged documentary record are exactly that,
measured against what was before the delegate in October 2024.**

---

## 2. ⛔ But the reason offered is wrong, and it matters

**"Otherwise they'll be inventing and coming up with things."**

⛔ **They do not have to invent anything.** The s 32(5)(a) case can be run **entirely on the admitted
facts**, recharacterised:

> *these acts occurred — they are admitted — they were management action, and they were reasonable.*

**The admissions give them the same platform they give him.** An admitted fact is neutral as to
characterisation; that is precisely why the notice was built to contain none.

⚠ **And particularising paragraph 27 is ordinary procedure, not invention.** If they amend or
supply particulars in their outline, that is a party fixing a pleading — it does not demonstrate
they have no case. It demonstrates the paragraph was thin **when it was written**.

⇒ ⛔ **So the pressure on them is not that they must fabricate. It is that they must now run a
contested hearing in which every fact is agreed and none of them helps.**

---

## 3. ⛔ And one thing the Guide says in terms, which cuts against the optimism

**§ 5.1, same paragraph:**
> *"**even though the Regulator may have previously accepted an element of s 32 of the Act, it does
> not mean that this element will be accepted by the Respondent at the appeal stage.**"*

⇒ **That is directed at exactly facts 261 and 262.** The delegate's findings on injury and on
employment as a significant contributing factor **do not carry forward**, and the Guide warns of it
expressly. ⇒ **Causation remains his to prove**, on treating evidence, with **no expert report
served by either side** — and that is an element no volume of admissions can close.

⚠ **So a hearing may be necessary whatever the factual position**, because the live elements are
evaluative and medical, not factual.

---

## 4. ⭐⭐ What actually makes early resolution realistic — the arithmetic, not the invention

| Their side of it | |
|---|---|
| To win, they must run a contested hearing | on facts **wholly admitted**, where **no factual proposition assists them** |
| They must call **Taylor and Reese** | the only witnesses who can speak to any of it — ⚠ **and they have not named them** |
| They must particularise an exclusion | which they have **admitted identifies no management action** (303) |
| They have **no expert report** | and the deadline to name one is **30 September** |
| What winning gets them | defending a decision **their own delegate reached on the exclusion alone**, having found injury and causation |
| What losing costs | a **published decision**, and **WCRA s 558(3)** — costs in the appeal body's discretion |

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **That is the case for resolution: not that they cannot construct an argument, but that
constructing it is expensive, visible, and buys them very little.** § 5.1 hands them a route out
that costs them no face.
