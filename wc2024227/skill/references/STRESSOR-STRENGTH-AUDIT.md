# PART 2 — HOW EACH LIMB IS PRICED

Seven questions, in order of weight:

1. **Is the underlying fact admitted or conceded?** (removes the proof burden entirely)
2. **Can he prove it from his own evidence?** (decisive after 7 August)
3. **Is there a document he already holds?**
4. **Is it management *action* at all** — or an omission? (s 32(5)(a) defends action; an omission
   has nothing to exclude)
5. **If it is action, is unreasonableness in the *manner* available?**
6. ⛔ **Does the label open s 32(5)(b)** (perception — not pleaded against him) **or raise his own
   *Briginshaw* bar?**
7. **Does the medical evidence reach it?**

# PART 3 — THE THIRTEEN, LIMB BY LIMB

## STRESSOR 1 — seven limbs

### 1(a) Dereliction of clinical governance — erratic presence + unassessed unilateral directives
**STRONG on consultation · MODERATE on presence · the LABEL is the weakness**

- ⛔ **Not admitted** — *"no particulars"* non-admission, hostility/UMA denied. **But a
  non-admission asserts no positive contrary case, and the "no particulars" ground is now spent**
  (ledger §1).
- ⭐⭐⭐ **Consultation:** documented **twice pre-injury** (16 Jun 2023; 15 May 2024) and corroborated
  from the employer's own side — **Reese asked him on 21 May 2024 to identify the directives.**
- ⭐⭐⭐ **CS-1 — her own 9 May 2024 email** locates the cause in the roster and the doctor's
  availability, and she changed the *Switchboard's* process instead. **Her document against her
  decision.**
- ⚠ **Presence** is bounded by his own shifts; now a cross-examination point, not a documentary one.
- ⭐⭐ **This is the limb the bench tested — *"that's just management action"* — and the answer
  worked on the *"taken in a reasonable way"* limb. It is now effectively the lead limb of
  Stressor 1.**
- ⛔ *"Dereliction"* and *"hostile course"* are characterisation → *Briginshaw* uplift and an
  s 32(5)(b) door.

### 1(b) Destruction of WHS records — the 6 June 2023 comm-book page
**FACT STRONG · LABEL DANGEROUS**

- ⭐⭐ **Removal admitted** (¶6/¶14) — **including the admission *"I took it out last week."***
- ⚠ **Contents and handwriting not admitted** (¶7 — MSH holds no copy). Contents are within his own
  knowledge: **Tier 2, stands if uncontradicted.**
- ⭐ **Arguably not management action at all** — removing a page from a communication book is not
  obviously s 32(5)(a) territory. **If it is not management action, there is nothing to exclude.**
- ⛔ ***"Destruction of WHS records"* is a serious allegation** — maximum *Briginshaw*, and it edges
  toward misconduct. **The fact is clean; the noun is the liability.**
- **Reframe:** *a page on which he had written was removed, and the manager said she took it out.*

### 1(c) Refusal to investigate WHS/fatigue — the 7 August 2023 grievance
**WEAK AS PLEADED · MODERATE IF REFRAMED**

- ⭐ Good anchor: **Reese's admitted acknowledgment of *"a rostering error that was accidentally
  made by Chloe"*** (¶5).
- ⛔⛔ **But SOFC ¶13 pleads four documented grievance responses** — 10 Aug meeting, 29 Aug email,
  8 Sep email, 15 Nov meeting. ***"Refusal to investigate" is answered by four dated events.***
- Grievance handling is classic management action — **their strongest ground.**
- ⭐ **The reframe is much better and it is available:** *the grievance was not upheld; he was
  directed to continue reporting to the same manager; a rostering error was acknowledged; and
  nothing changed* — the last being MSH's own **Item 3(c) *"no consequential changes."***

### 1(d) COVID leave obstruction — four submissions, declined twice, approved on identical evidence
**MODERATE — well documented, low severity, pattern value only**

- ⭐ **The myHR audit trail is Item 11 and MSH PRODUCED it** (5 June 2026). One of the few limbs
  with produced documentary support.
- **Manner point:** declined twice, then approved **on the same evidence** — the decision was wrong
  twice and reversed without new material.
- ⚠ **It resolved. He got the leave.** Low severity standing alone.
- ⚠ Do not confuse `Item_11_AVAC_PRN_15480560_History.pdf` (**pandemic leave**) with the March-shift
  AVAC (**Item 16**).

### 1(e) PID — 13 May 2024 complaint, determined a PID by ESU
**FACT ADMITTED · USE DANGEROUS — this should be chronology, not a stressor**

- ⭐⭐ **Admitted** (¶20). The determination is a fact.
- ⚠ It proves **the disclosure was made** — not that anything followed from it.
- ⛔⛔ **The most dangerous limb in the pleading.** The PID alleged **timesheet fraud against a named
  manager**. Running it as a *stressor* invites the collateral-purpose fight, engages *Briginshaw*
  at its highest, and hands them **s 32(5)(b)** (perception of reprisal).
- ⚠ And **SOFC ¶16(a): *"Ms Reese was unaware that the appellant had lodged a PID."*** Pleaded, and
  hard to displace.
- ⇒ **Keep it as the dated sequence. Give it no weight of its own.**

### 1(f) Retraction directed within 48 hours; a comparable manager email attracted none
**MODERATE-TO-STRONG ON FACTS · WEAK AS LABELLED**

- ⭐ **The direction is admitted** (¶21) — **but ¶21 also asserts *"no correlation between the two
  events"***, and **¶22 denies the comparison.** ⭐ **They have pleaded a positive case, so they
  carry it.**
- ⭐⭐ **SOFC ¶16(b)(v) is a gift: *"Ms Reese having sought advice from Human Resources, emailed the
  Appellant at 6.23pm."*** It establishes the direction was **advised, not spontaneous** — which
  makes *"no correlation"* harder — and **it identifies a document they hold and he does not.**
  ⇒ On the disclosure list.
- ⛔ ***"Reprisal"* is the PID Act's word.** It imports motive, and **motive is not an element of
  s 32(5)(a).**
- **Reframe:** *he was directed to retract a routine email within 48 hours; a substantively similar
  email by his manager attracted no direction.* **Sequence, not motive.**

### 1(g) Suppression of representation — union delegate, ~13 months
**MODERATE — and weakened by giving up Item 19**

- ⭐ **QH-POL-248's *"positive, supportive role"* is admitted** (¶17); **the text was sent** (¶18) —
  though characterised as *"not a formal notification."*
- ⚠ **Denied** that management failed to facilitate (¶19). Contested.
- ⭐ **Best feature: it is an omission** — a failure to facilitate. **s 32(5)(a) defends management
  *action*; a course of omissions measured against a positive policy duty is a different animal.**
- ⚠ **Item 19 (union-delegate correspondence) is being given up** with the 64G. Proof of the
  13-month gap now rests on **CS-2, CS-3** and his own evidence.
- ⛔ *"Suppression"* is characterisation.

## STRESSOR 3 — FATIGUE — four limbs (the keystone)

### 3(a) 17–18 March 2024 consecutive shifts, 7-hour break, ~4 hours sleep
⭐⭐⭐ **THE STRONGEST LIMB IN THE MATTER. THIS IS THE CASE.**

- ⭐⭐⭐ **Admitted** (¶1) — and **SOFC ¶22(a) admits *"a 7-hour break (rather than an 8-hour
  break)."*** **The shortfall is uncontested by anybody.**
- The *"~4 hours sleep"* is his own evidence — Tier 2, uncontradicted.
- ⭐ **The only limb in fourteen months where the employer's own rule produces an answer by
  subtraction, and where his own conduct is not in issue at any point.**

### 3(b) *"The Award and the FRMS mandate a minimum 10-hour rest break"*
⛔⛔ **THE POINT IS RIGHT. THE PLEADING IS A LIABILITY. FIX IT.**

- **¶3 admits 10h *OR* 8h by agreement. ¶4 DENIES the no-agreement point and asserts a signed June
  2020 8-hour agreement. The live floor is 8 hours.**
- ⭐⭐ **It does not matter — 7 < 8 either way**, and the Review Officer **expressly declined to
  decide the 8-hour question for that reason.**
- ⛔ **So the overstatement buys nothing and costs credibility.** A pleading asserting 10 where the
  answer is 8 is exactly what a cross-examiner opens with.
- ⭐ **And ¶4 is their affirmative case — they must produce the agreement.** His answers are ready:
  it was a **casual** agreement; he later moved to permanent and then a different full-time
  contract; and **Taylor was not the manager who signed it.**

### 3(c) Misapplied a "superseded 2020 casual agreement" to deny paid fatigue leave; personal leave 19 March
⚠ **UNRESOLVED — POTENTIALLY WEAK. VERIFY BEFORE RELYING.**

- **Contested on two fronts:** SOFC **¶22(c)** says the 19 March leave **was paid**; **¶24(b)** says
  **cl 18.10 of the Award** denies fatigue leave where **no overtime was performed.**
- ⛔ **Award cl 18.10 is still unverified in this repo.** If ¶24(b) is right, **the limb fails on the
  instrument's own words.**
- ⇒ **Verify cl 18.10. If it holds, delete this limb — do not soften it.**

### 3(d) The Review Officer found the 7-hour break was unreasonable management action
⭐⭐ **STRONG AS A CONTRADICTION · WEAK AS A FINDING · CURRENTLY MIS-DESCRIBED**

- **Admitted as to CONTENTS only** (¶37), relevance reserved. ⛔⛔ **This is where the ¶37 error
  lives** — the pleading and four other documents, **including Form 20 ¶41 on oath**, describe it
  as an admission of *"both findings."* It is not.
- ⚠ **De novo: the Review Decision binds nobody.** It is a document, admitted, persuasive.
- ⭐⭐ **Its real power is different and larger: the Regulator's own delegate determined factor 4 was
  unreasonable, while the Regulator's pleading (SOFC ¶27) contends all management action was
  reasonable.** **That internal contradiction is the single best settlement argument in the file** —
  and it is an argument, not evidence.

# PART 5 — ⭐⭐ THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST

**Across all thirteen limbs, the labels are consistently weaker than the facts.**

*Dereliction · destruction · refusal · reprisal · suppression · hostile course.* **Every one of
them raises his own *Briginshaw* bar, and several hold the s 32(5)(b) door open** — a limb the
Regulator has **not** pleaded against him.

⭐⭐⭐ **The single highest-value edit available to this pleading is to delete every noun of
characterisation and leave the dated events standing.** The facts survive untouched; the burden on
him drops; and **the sequence produces the inference on its own, which is the one form of it that
cannot be answered.**

⚠ **And the structural warning:** thirteen limbs presented together read as a **composite course**
— which is ***Delaney*, the Respondent's authority.** ⭐ **One mechanism with one admitted instance
is *Mahaffey*.** The ranking above is the order in which they should carry weight, not thirteen
arguments of equal size.
