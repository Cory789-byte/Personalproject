# 1. ⭐⭐⭐ THE RIGHT IS BROADER THAN AN ERROR-OF-LAW APPEAL
> **Guide Part 9, verbatim:** *"If you disagree with the decision of the Commission in your matter,
> you may appeal the decision to the **Industrial Court of Queensland**… within **21 days from the
> date that the decision of the Commission was released**… You must use **Form 5 – Application to
> Appeal**…*
> ⭐⭐⭐ *"**Section 561(3) of the Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003 outlines that an
> appeal is by way of REHEARING ON THE EVIDENCE AND PROCEEDINGS BEFORE THE COMMISSION**, unless the
> Industrial Court orders that additional evidence may be heard. **Further, the Court's decision on
> the appeal is final.**"*
> Footnoted to ***Turay v Workers' Compensation Regulator* [2023] ICQ 013** and ***Neale v Workers'
> Compensation Regulator* [2023] ICQ 017**.

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **This is not confined to error of law or excess of jurisdiction. It is a rehearing on the
record — the Court can reconsider the evidence and substitute its own decision.**
⛔ **The two ICQ cases define the scope and have NOT been read. Do not characterise the rehearing's
limits until they are.** ⚠ Appellate restraint on findings that turn on credit will still apply.

---

# 2. ⭐⭐ AND THE COSTS RISK ON APPEAL IS LOW
> **Guide Part 10:** *"If the matter proceeds to an appeal to the Industrial Court, the Court may
> order a party to pay costs incurred by another party **only if satisfied the party made the appeal
> vexatiously or without reasonable cause**."*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **A meritorious appeal carries almost no adverse costs risk** — a completely different regime
from the hearing, where costs follow the event.

---

# 3. ⛔⛔ BUT IT IS NOT A SAFETY NET, FOR TWO REASONS
1. ⛔⛔ **It does not undo the hearing costs order.** Lose at first instance and be ordered to pay
   costs, and **that order stands unless the appeal succeeds.** ⇒ **The first-instance costs tail is
   not neutralised by the existence of an appeal right.**
2. ⛔⛔ **It is a rehearing ON THE RECORD.** Additional evidence only *"unless the Industrial Court
   orders"* — discretionary. ⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Evidence not led, witnesses not called and arguments not run at
   the hearing cannot reliably be fixed later.**
3. ⚠ **The Court's decision is FINAL.** One shot.

---

# 4. ⭐⭐⭐ WOULD HE HAVE GROUNDS? — IT DEPENDS ENTIRELY ON HOW HE LOSES
| How he loses | Prospect on a rehearing |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ **Misapplication of the exclusion** — the Commission counts stressors, applies a *"mainly"* / dominant-cause test, or applies ***Delaney*** global evaluation **without finding the threshold** (subject matter, time, personality) | ⭐⭐⭐ **The strongest ground, and it is already mapped** — it is the exact defect identified in the review decision. **A rehearing is well suited to it.** |
| ⭐ **Weight given to the medical evidence** — employment not a significant contributing factor | ⭐ **Reviewable on a rehearing**, but evaluative and harder |
| ⚠ **Credit findings** — his account not accepted, or Ms Taylor preferred | ⛔ **Hardest.** Appellate restraint is greatest where the primary decision-maker saw the witnesses |
| ⚠ **Procedural unfairness** | Real but rare; depends entirely on what happens |

⇒ ⭐⭐ **Honest verdict: a real appeal right exists, on the merits, at low costs risk — but the most
likely ways he loses (findings of fact, and the medical link) are the least reviewable.** ⭐ **The
most reviewable ground is the one the reviewer already got wrong, which is a genuine reason for
confidence on that limb specifically.**
