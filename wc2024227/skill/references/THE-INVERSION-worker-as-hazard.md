## 1. THE PROOF

### 1.1 Isolation is a control applied to hazards
> **Reg 36(3):** "The duty holder must minimise risks, so far as is reasonably practicable, by doing
> one or more of the following— (a) **substituting** (wholly or partly) **the hazard** giving rise to
> the risk…; (b) ⭐ **isolating the hazard from any person exposed to it**; (c) implementing
> **engineering controls**."
> **(4)** "If a risk then remains… by implementing **administrative controls**."
> **(5)** "If a risk then remains… **personal protective equipment**."

**Five levels. Every one operates on the hazard, the work, or the exposed person's protection.
Removing the exposed person is not among them at any level.**

### 1.2 What MSH actually did
> **Taylor, 26 Jun 2026:** "**we are unable to facilitate your return to work**… we have a legal
> obligation under **section 19** of the Work Health and Safety Act 2011…"

**The control applied was: remove Cory from the workplace, and keep him removed, expressly in
discharge of the s 19 safety duty.**

### 1.3 ⭐⭐ THE BIND — there is no third reading
| If MSH says | Then |
|---|---|
| *"We applied a control under reg 36"* | **The only control that removes a person from a workplace is isolation — and isolation is applied to the hazard.** They classified him as the hazard. |
| *"We did not treat him as the hazard"* | **Then no recognised control was applied at all**, and the exclusion has no basis in the risk-management framework they invoked. |

**They cannot have both.** They invoked s 19 to justify the act. **s 19 is discharged through
part 3.1, and part 3.1's hierarchy has no step called "exclude the worker."**

## 2. AND THE IDENTIFIED HAZARD WAS MANAGEMENT

> **ECC, 3 July 2026, stressor (4):** "**unresolved workplace matters involving line management**"

> **Reg 55A:** "A psychosocial hazard is a hazard that— (a) arises from, or relates to— (i) **the
> design or management of work**;… or (iv) **workplace interactions or behaviours**; and (b) may
> cause psychological harm."

⇒ **On the face of the regulation, the hazard identified in MSH's own document is the management of
work.**
⇒ **The person removed was the worker exposed to it.**

**That is the inversion, stated without adjectives: the hazard was identified as management; the
control applied was to remove the person exposed.**

> **Reg 35(a):** "**eliminate** risks to health and safety **so far as is reasonably practicable**"

Elimination is the **first** obligation, and it operates on the risk — not on the person carrying it.

## 3. ⭐⭐ THE SELF-ASSESSMENT PROBLEM — THE SHARPEST POINT IN THE FILE

**Switchboard Services reports to Corporate Services.** *(LBH_HR, 21 Feb 2025: "Corporate Services
(where Switchboard is now reporting)".)*

**The identified hazard is the management of work — within Corporate Services.**

⇒ **If the psychosocial risk assessment for Switchboard Services is conducted by, or under the
authority of, Corporate Services, then the assessor is determining whether the assessor is the
hazard.**

⚠️ **That is not merely a conflict of interest requiring management under Code of Conduct cl 1.2.
It is a process structurally incapable of producing a valid assessment** — because the honest
finding may be that the assessing officer is the source of the risk.

⭐ **And it may explain the central absence in this matter:** no psychosocial risk assessment has ever
been produced, in five weeks, despite s 55C making it mandatory and despite MSH's stated position
being that it *cannot assess the risk*. **A process that cannot honestly reach its own finding tends
not to be run.**

⇒ **Which is why the alternative decision-maker (§ Part 6.1 of the response) is not a courtesy. It is
the precondition of a lawful assessment.**

## 5. ⚠️ ON "BULLYING" — THE HONEST ADVICE

**The remedy exists.** IR Act 2016 ch 7:
> **s 272(1)** an employee is bullied if, **while the employee is at work**, an individual or group
> **repeatedly behaves unreasonably** towards them **and** that behaviour **creates a risk to the
> health and safety** of the employee.
> **s 272(2)** "does **not** apply to **reasonable management action carried out in a reasonable
> manner**."
> **s 273** an employee who **reasonably believes** they have been bullied **may apply** to the
> commission.
> ⭐ **s 274** "The commission **must start to deal with** an application… **within 14 days**."
> **s 275(2)** the commission may make **any order it considers appropriate** (other than payment of
> money) **to prevent** the employee from being bullied.

### ⛔ BUT DO NOT FILE IT NOW, AND DO NOT USE THE WORD IN CORRESPONDENCE
1. ⚠️ **"While the employee is at work."** He has been excluded since **3 July 2026**. The
   jurisdictional foothold is weakest at exactly the moment he is out of the workplace.
2. ⚠️⚠️ **s 275(1)(b)(ii) — the commission must be satisfied there is a risk he will *continue* to
   be bullied.** **If he is not in the workplace, MSH will say there is no continuing risk and no
   order can be made.** This is the standard answer to stop-bullying applications by absent workers.
   ⭐ *Though note the trap it creates for them: to defeat it they must assert he is not returning —
   which concedes the exclusion. **Hold that; do not spring it yet.***
3. ⚠️ **s 272(2) is their whole defence** — "reasonable management action carried out in a reasonable
   manner" — the same fight as **WCRA s 32(5)**. Running it twice, in two forums, before the first is
   resolved, is not sound.
4. ⛔ **The word "bullying" in correspondence forfeits the register.** It converts a regulatory
   argument he is winning — *hazard, risk, control, hierarchy* — into an allegation with a defined
   threshold **he** must meet, and invites the reasonable-management-action answer by return.

⇒ ⭐ **Use the regulation's language, not the accusation.** "The identified hazard is the management
of work; the control applied was the removal of the worker; the hierarchy at reg 36 does not provide
for that." **That is unanswerable and it never once says the word.**
⇒ **Keep ch 7 in reserve.** It becomes available and much stronger **once he is back in the
workplace** — at which point s 272(1)(a) and s 275(1)(b)(ii) are both satisfied.

## 6. THE PROPOSITION, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Metro South Health invoked **s 19 of the Work Health and Safety Act** to justify keeping Mr Shepherd
out of his workplace. Section 19 is discharged through the risk-management process in **part 3.1 of
the WHS Regulation**. That process requires hazards to be identified, risks eliminated so far as
reasonably practicable, and, if not, minimised through a prescribed hierarchy in which the second
step is **isolating the hazard from the persons exposed to it**. **Removing an exposed worker is not
a control measure the regulation recognises at any level.** The hazard the Health Service's own
document identifies is *"unresolved workplace matters involving line management"* — a hazard arising
from the management of work and from workplace interactions, within the very directorate that
excluded him. **The Health Service therefore either treated the worker as the hazard, or applied no
recognised control at all.** And the assessment that would have determined the question was never
performed — by a directorate that would have had to assess itself.
