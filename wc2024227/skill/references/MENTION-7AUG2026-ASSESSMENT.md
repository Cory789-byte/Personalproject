## 1. WHAT THE MENTION ACTUALLY WAS

It was not a mention. It was **a 65-minute directed pleading review**, announced in advance as a
departure, in which the Commissioner walked the Appellant through **every pleaded stressor in the
amended statement of facts and contentions** and asked, of each: *how would you prove this, and do
you already hold the documents?*

Dwyer was doing three things at once, and they need to be separated because they pull in different
directions:

**(a) Case management.** He wanted the Form 29 gone. His stated reason was efficiency —
*"a tendency to proliferate interlocutory disputes"* [01:02], *"you won't be getting to hearing
until 2028"* [07:52], and the cost consequences of a contested disclosure hearing likely
*"bigger than the hearing at the end of the day"* [31:58].

**(b) Genuine assistance to a self-represented party.** This is real and should not be read
cynically. He explained the s 32 test [02:55], explained that the framework of the case is the
SOFC [03:28], explained the difference between getting documents and getting them into evidence
[08:04], and — most valuably — explained the practical operation of contradiction (§3 below).

**(c) A running critique of the Appellant's presentation.** This is the part the earlier recount
under-recorded, and it matters more than anything else in the hour.

> I'm going to miss the point, like any other human being."*

⭐⭐⭐ **This is the single most important thing said in the hour.** The decision-maker has stated,
on the record, that volume and register cause him to miss the point and *fall into error*. That is
not a stylistic preference. It is a direct instruction about how to communicate with this
Commissioner, and it governs every document served from here.

### 2.2 The answer given to the AI question

The transcription is garbled and the first clause is unrecoverable, but the operative words —

AI-assisted."*

⚠ **This needs to be resolved from the official transcript before it matters.** Two reasons:
(i) if the answer was a flat denial, the register of everything served afterwards either supports
or undermines it, and (ii) candour to the Commission is not a topic to be wrong about by accident.
No action is proposed here beyond ordering the certified transcript and knowing what was said.

### 2.3 The "just management action" exchange — the real words are worse than the recount

The recount renders it: *"Dwyer: 'That's just management action.' Cory: 'It lacked consultation and
adversely affected patients.'"*

The actual exchange [27:20]:
> **Dwyer:** *"What do you mean by unassessed? What does unassessed mean?"*
> **Cory:** *"It would be that she wasn't even in the room… and makes a decision without any input
> of the staff."*
> **Dwyer:** *"**Like a manager?**"*
> **Cory:** *"I mean, usually a manager would need input."*
> **Dwyer:** *"**Like a manager. Managers make decisions without consulting staff all the time.
> It's totally legitimate.**"*
> **Cory:** *"I think when it's in a patient safety environment that they—"*
> **Dwyer:** *"**If you think it's in a patient safety environment, you can raise that.**"*

⭐⭐ This is a preview of the s 32(5)(a) defence delivered by the Commissioner himself. Absence of
consultation, standing alone, is **not** unreasonable management action — it is ordinary
management. The whole of Stressor 1(a) as presently pleaded runs straight into it.

But the last line is an invitation, and it is the way through: **the patient-safety consequence is
what takes the conduct out of "ordinary management" and into the "reasonable *way*" limb.** Not the
absence of consultation — the *effect* of deciding without the information the operators held.

## 5. THE OVERALL PATTERN IN THE ANSWERS

**Counted:** ~49 substantive questions. Roughly **20 answered cleanly**, **17 answered adequately
but loosely**, **9 answered poorly or evasively-sounding**, and **3 answered wrongly on fact**
(Q10, Q32, and the pay-disparity framing at Q42).

Four patterns, all fixable:

1. **Answering the question behind the question instead of the question.** Asked *"is it in an
   email?"* he explained why he no longer had it. Dwyer had to say *"answer my question first…
   Yes or no?"* twice. **In the box, this reads as evasion even when it is not.**

2. **Under-claiming his own document holdings.** Twice he said he did not have or could not recall
   documents that were **already in the Regulator's disclosure**. This is the single most damaging
   pattern of the day: it made the Form 29 look like a fishing expedition for material he had.
   The cause is understandable — no notice, no chance to check — but the cost was the whole
   application.

3. **Reaching for the systemic when asked for the specific.** *"For me, it all works together as
   one big system"* [13:46] is precisely the disposition Dwyer diagnosed at [57:15]: *"you have as
   a broader grievance a view that systemically the place in which you worked had a number of
   failings… **You don't need to prove all of that.**"*

4. **Burying the good answer.** The emergency-call explanation [29:18], the restricted-mailbox
   point [36:38], and the *"I only want the count"* point [24:06] were all correct, all
   under-delivered, and all abandoned.

**Against that — the three answers that worked all had the same shape: short, plain, responsive.**
*"Exactly."* *"Correct."* *"I have everything for this."* That is the entire lesson of the hour,
and it is the same lesson as §2.1.

### ⚠ Two serious risks, and they are the same risk

**RISK 1 — VOLUME. This is the big one.**

Dwyer said, twice, that page-after-page causes him to switch off and *fall into error*. The
response is a **303-fact notice with a 133-page annexure, plus a second notice, plus a summary.**

The mitigation is real: it is addressed to **Matheson, not Dwyer**, it is a mechanical instrument
where length is the point, and Part B exists precisely to give a short way in. But if any part of
it comes before Dwyer — a dispute about the responses, an application about deemed admissions —
it lands in front of the person who told you what volume does to him.

**Practical consequence:** if this ever reaches him, it must arrive as **Part B plus a one-page
cover**, with the itemised notice as the backing document, never the other way round.

**RISK 2 — REGISTER.** *"terms and language and complexities of language that go above and beyond
what you'd anticipate from a human being"* [09:31]. A notice with computed intervals to the minute,
a 39-tab machine-built annexure and formulaic negatives is going to read exactly that way to him.

**This is not a reason to change the Form 24** — it is a rule-49 instrument served on a
professional appeals officer, and precision is its whole function. It **is** a reason that
everything Dwyer personally reads from here — outlines, submissions, any application — must be
short, plain, and visibly human.

### One substantive gap

**The "like a manager" problem is not yet answered.** Dwyer ruled, in effect, that directives
without consultation are ordinary management. The Form 24 proves the directives were sent and were
not consulted on — which is the fact he called *"totally legitimate."* What converts it is the
