# 3. ⭐⭐⭐ §2 — THIS IS WHERE THEY SEE THE PROBLEM

> *"Where the document is **itself the step taken** – a roster as published, a directive as issued by
> email, a payroll instruction or claim as recorded, a request as made and the reply as given, each
> on the date recorded – the Appellant relies on the admission as establishing that the step was
> taken on that date in those terms."*

**They will recognise it immediately, because it is orthodox.** Their heading 3 was drafted for
documents tendered as evidence of a fact *recorded in* them — the category that needs the maker.
⭐⭐⭐ **§2 identifies the other category: the operative document, where the act is constituted by the
writing. An email issuing a directive is not evidence that a directive issued; sending it is the
issuing.**

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Their internal conclusion: the limitation was correctly stated and is largely ineffective
against the ranges he named — and it cannot be narrowed further without saying something that is not
true.** They would have to contend that admitting the existence and wording of an email does not
establish that the email was sent.
