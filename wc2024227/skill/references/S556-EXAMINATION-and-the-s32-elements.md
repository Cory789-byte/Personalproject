# WCRA s 556 examinations, and what "going through s 32 element by element" means
> 13 September 2026. Read from WCRA s 556 (ATT26) and QIRC Workers' Compensation Appeal Guide
> Parts 5.1, 7.1, 7.3, 7.3.2.
> ⚠ Do not confuse this with `ATT18_IME_Guideline_Qld_public_sector.pdf` — that is the **employment**
> track's medical-information framework (the Hughes RFMI), not this.

# PART 1 — s 556, and how to prepare

## 1 · What it actually is

> **556 Additional medical evidence**
> (1) This section applies if… **(b)** the **cause, nature or extent of the injury** … is relevant to
> the appeal.
> (2) **The appeal body may**, at any time before or after the start of the hearing, **order the
> claimant or worker to submit to a personal examination by 1 or more specified registered
> persons.**
> (3) The appeal body may also … make an order about — (a) the way, time and place of the
> examination; and **(b) costs of the application for the order and of the examination.**
> (4) ⭐⭐ **An opinion formed on the examination must be given to the respondent and the respondent
> must make the opinion available to the appellant.**
> (5)–(6) If the worker **fails without reasonable excuse to attend**, refuses to be examined, or
> **obstructs** it — ⛔ **any entitlement to compensation is suspended until he undergoes it.**

⭐ **It is a Commission order, not a Regulator right.** The Regulator must **apply**. The Commission
specifies **who**, **when**, **where** and **who pays**. And the opinion **must** come to the
Appellant.

⇒ It is not an ambush mechanism. It is a controlled one — and the controls are the preparation.

## 2 · ⛔ Do not resist it

Resistance reads as concealment, and s 556(6) suspends entitlement. **Consent promptly and in
writing, and spend the effort on the four things that actually matter.**

## 3 · The four things to be heard on

### (a) ⭐⭐ What the examiner is given — this is the whole game
An examiner's opinion is only as good as the brief. One told *"the worker alleges management
action"* forms a different view from one told:

> the Respondent has **admitted** that emergency response notifications reached the wrong medical
> teams on nine occasions reported by a clinician; that a MET team rang the console to be told where
> an emergency was and could not be told; that accurate contact details are *"critical… to patient
> safety"*; and that the means of correcting the directory was removed for **eleven months**.

⇒ **Ask that the examiner be provided with the admitted facts** — the notice of 28 August 2026 and
the response of 8 September 2026, or the Second Amended Form 9A — **together with the treating
records at Tabs M1 to M4.** ⭐ This is the single most valuable thing to seek, and it is a
reasonable, neutral request: the examiner should have the agreed factual substratum.

### (b) Who
s 556(2) — *"1 or more **specified** registered persons"*. **A psychiatrist**, given the diagnosis is
Major Depressive Disorder with anxious distress. ⚠ Be careful about attacking an examiner's
independence; it rarely lands and it costs credit. Confine it to specialty and to whether the
examiner has previously seen him.

### (c) Costs
s 556(3)(b). **The party seeking the order ordinarily pays.** Ask for that expressly — it belongs
with the conduct-money and costs exposure at `OUTLINE-vs-9A-what-each-does.md` §5.

### (d) Timing
*"before or after the start of the hearing"*. ⭐ **Early is better for him** — it gives his treating
doctors time to consider the opinion before they give evidence. ⛔ **Do not invite it.** But be ready
to consent the day it is raised, and to ask that it be done before the hearing rather than during.

## 4 · At the examination itself

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| answer what is asked, accurately | argue the case, or persuade |
| describe symptoms as they are, on the worst and the better days | minimise, or overstate |
| ⭐ answer honestly about **non-work stressors** | conceal them — s 32(1) needs employment to be **a** significant contributor, not the only one |
| say "I don't recall" when you don't | fill gaps |
| treat it as an examination | treat it as treatment — it is not, and nothing said is confidential |

## 5 · After — s 556(4) is an advantage

The opinion **must** be given to him. ⇒ **His treating doctors can then be asked to comment on it.**
A psychiatrist who has treated him since 24 October 2024 answering a one-off examiner is a strong
position, and it is only available because s 556(4) guarantees disclosure.

# PART 2 — "going through s 32 element by element"

## 6 · The elements he must prove (Guide 7.3.2)

Standard: **balance of probabilities**, applying **Briginshaw** — *"satisfied… to a comfortable
degree, based on clear and cogent evidence."* **The onus is on the Appellant** (Guide 7.3).

| | Element | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | a **worker** within s 11 | not in dispute |
| **(b)** | sustained a **personal injury** | ⭐ Review Decision 69983 found *"a personal injury of a psychological nature"* (fact 261, admitted) |
| **(c)** | arose **out of, or in the course of**, the employment | ⭐ the decision so found (fact 262) |
| **(d)** | employment was **a significant contributing factor** | ⭐ the decision so found (fact 262) |

**And then, for a psychological injury, Guide 7.3.2 adds:**

> *"you must **also show that any psychological or psychiatric injury was not connected to management
> action that was reasonable in the circumstances and/or conducted in a reasonable way**."*

⇒ **s 32(5)(a). That is the whole appeal.**

⛔ And the Guide's next line is the door that must stay shut:
> *"Psychological/psychiatric injuries that are connected to the worker's **expectation or
> perception** of reasonable management action being taken against them are also not able to be
> accepted."*
**s 32(5)(b). The Respondent has not pleaded it. Never open it.**

## 7 · What a Member does with that at a conference

Guide 5.1: *"the conference can assist in understanding **whether any elements of s 32 of the Act can
be agreed**."*

⇒ The Member takes (a), (b), (c), (d) in turn and asks each party whether it is in issue.
**Anything agreed drops out of the hearing.**

⭐⭐ **The prize: if (a) to (d) are agreed, the hearing is confined to s 32(5)(a).** That is where the
298 admissions, the instruments and contention 4 live — and it would shorten the hearing
substantially, which bears on costs and on the efficiency concern the Commissioner voiced at the
mention.

⚠ **But the Guide warns in the same passage:** *"even though the Regulator may have previously
accepted an element of s 32 of the Act, it does not mean that this element will be accepted by the
Respondent at the appeal stage."* ⛔ **Nothing in Review Decision 69983 is banked. Elements (b), (c)
and (d) must be prepared as if contested, and agreed only if they actually agree them.**

## 8 · How to walk into that conference

Have a **one-page element grid** ready: for each of (a) to (d), one line on what proves it and where.

| Element | What proves it |
|---|---|
| (a) worker | not in issue |
| (b) personal injury | Dr Krishnaiah; Dr Hawes; certificates (Tab M2); ⭐ fact 261 |
| (c) arose out of / in the course of | the admitted course of conduct; treating records Tabs M1–M4; ⭐ fact 262 |
| (d) **a** significant contributing factor | ⭐ fact 262; the report of 13 Feb 2025; the baseline at Part B.1; **Dr Krishnaiah and Dr Hawes orally** |
| **s 32(5)(a)** | ⭐ **the live issue** — Part B.2, the instruments, contention 4 (not management action) and contention 5 (not taken in a reasonable way) |

⭐ Then say, in one sentence, what the Appellant proposes is in issue: **s 32(5)(a) only.** Inviting
agreement on (a) to (d) is exactly what the conference is for, and it is a question the Respondent
must answer in front of the Member.
