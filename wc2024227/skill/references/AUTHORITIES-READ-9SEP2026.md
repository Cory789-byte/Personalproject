# 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE HEADLINE — *DELANEY* DECIDES THE OPPOSITE OF WHAT THE REVIEW DECISION USED IT FOR

**Hall P, quoted at length in *Blackwood v Mahaffey* [2016] ICQ 010, verbatim:**
> *"…the submission is inconsistent with ***Delaney v Q-COMP* (2005) 178 QGIG 197**. That case
> involved an appeal against a decision by an Acting Industrial Magistrate which teased out the
> transactions and events and concluded that Mr Delaney's adjustment disorder with mixed anxiety and
> depressed mood was attributable to **a number of interactions with his superiors each of which
> constituted reasonable management action reasonably taken**. **There was a successful appeal to
> this Court.** However… **Mr Delaney did not in that appeal challenge every finding** that an
> interaction contributing (in some way) to Mr Delaney's decompensation was reasonable management
> action reasonably taken. ⭐ **One finding was allowed to remain in place on the appeal.** In
> consequence, Mr Delaney was bound by the finding… **If the submission attributed to Q-COMP at first
> instance was correct, Mr Delaney's appeal should have failed. THE APPEAL SUCCEEDED. The remaining
> finding of reasonable management action reasonably taken was treated by both parties as
> INCONSEQUENTIAL SO LONG AS IT STOOD ALONE.**"*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **A worker whose injury was attributed to several interactions, one of which stood as a
finding of reasonable management action reasonably taken, STILL SUCCEEDED.**

**Now set that against the Review Decision, admitted at Tab 25:**
> *"In the decision of **Delaney v Q-COMP** the Industrial Court held that… a global evaluation of
> management actions must be made… **since two out of the three causative factors amounted to
> reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way, this means your psychological injury mainly
> arose out of such management action**… Therefore, I find overall your psychological injury arose
> out of reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way and section 32(5) is consequently
> enlivened."*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐⭐ **The reviewer used *Delaney* to hold that a majority of reasonable factors defeats the
claim. *Delaney* is authority that it does not.** ⭐ **This is no longer an argument that the reviewer
failed to make a threshold finding. It is that the authority relied on decides the point the other
way.**

## 2.1 ⭐⭐⭐ AND HALL P ANSWERS THE COUNTING APPROACH IN TERMS
Adopting Watson, *Industrial Laws of Queensland*, in the same passage:
> *"To read the subsection in that way would be to read into the subsection the words **'or partly
> arising out of or in the course of'**. Implying words into legislation is not something that courts
> undertake lightly… ⭐⭐⭐ **If there are eight stressors which gave rise to the injury and they all
> arise out of or in the course of employment, only seven are significant contributors whereas the
> eighth is exceedingly minor but arises from reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way,
> WHERE IS THE JUSTIFICATION FOR DENYING A CLAIMANT COMPENSATION IN THOSE CIRCUMSTANCES?**"*
> *"…**s 32(5) should be permitted to speak for itself. Figurative language and undue conceptualism
> are to be deprecated.**"*

## 2.2 ⭐⭐ AND *ROWE* IS A THIRD AUTHORITY AGAINST IT
*Mahaffey* [50], quoting Hall P in ***Q-COMP v Rowe***:
> *"I cannot recollect an occasion on which (in this Court), Q-COMP has submitted that '…**even if one
> stressor is caught by reasonable management action that will be sufficient to remove the
> psychiatric/psychological disorder from the definition of injury** in s 32(1) of the Act.'
> **Neither have I any recollection of accepting such a proposition.**"*

# 3. ⭐⭐⭐ *Q-COMP v HOCHEN* [2010] ICQ 10 — THE CORRECT QUESTION, STATED
> **[9]** *"**Section 32(5)(a) of the Act will operate only where there is a sufficient causal nexus
> between the management action and the injury** to satisfy one or either of the expressions 'arising
> out of or in the course of'."*
> ⭐⭐⭐ **[10]** *"Section 32(5)(a) of the Act operates to exclude… psychological injuries which would
> otherwise be within the definition. **Section 32(5)(a) of the Act does not operate to prohibit a
> worker from pointing to particular stressful events to satisfy the causal requirements of
> s 32(1)**, compare *Prizeman v Q-COMP*."*
> ⭐⭐⭐ **[12]** *"If, indeed, the Acting Industrial Magistrate did enquire whether unreasonable
> management action was the dominant cause of Mr Hochen's disorder, **I consider that His Honour
> erred. The correct inquiry is whether the psychological injury arose out of or in the course of
> reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way.**"*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **[10] is fatal to the reviewer's method.** He found factors 2 and 3 reasonable and thereby
removed them from the causal pool, then counted what was left. **Hochen says s 32(5)(a) does not
prohibit a worker pointing to particular stressful events to satisfy s 32(1).**
⇒ ⭐⭐ **And [12] states the correct inquiry, which is not the one the reviewer asked.** The reviewer
asked whether the injury *"mainly arose out of"* reasonable management action.

# 4. ⭐⭐ *BLACKWOOD v ADAMS* [2015] ICQ 001 — VERIFIED, AND IT CUTS BOTH WAYS
✅ **[23] confirmed exactly as previously recorded:** *"I was referred to *Q-Comp v Hochen*. In that
case Hall P said that **an enquiry as to whether or not unreasonable management action was the
dominant cause of an injury was an erroneous approach. I accept that that is correct.**"*

⚠⚠ **But note two passages that cut AGAINST him, and they are important:**
> ⛔ **[20]** *"Ms Adams had specifically identified the episodes and types of conduct which she said
> led to her injury. Her account was rejected. **It is not open to a Tribunal to then proceed to make
> findings that other matters did give rise to the injury.**"*
⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **The pleaded stressors are the boundary of the case.** ⛔ **Nothing outside the Form 9A can
be relied on, and if a stressor's account is rejected the Commission cannot substitute another.**

> ⚠ **[21]** *"**Professor Whiteford's report was based upon Ms Adams' assertions and recollections
> which… were rejected by the Commission.**"*
⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **That is exactly how an expert's report is destroyed — the history underneath it fails.**
⭐⭐⭐ **AND IT IS THE ONE VULNERABILITY THIS CASE DOES NOT HAVE.** Dr Hawes's note of 1 July 2024
records the pay and the roster; **facts 185–203 and 211–221 are ADMITTED.** ⇒ **The history the
doctors took cannot be rejected, because the Respondent has admitted the events.** ⭐⭐ **That is a
new and substantial point, and it is the strongest answer yet to "the medical case is thin."**

# 5. ⚠⚠ *TURAY* [2023] ICQ 13 — THE APPEAL RIGHT IS NOT AS SETTLED AS I SAID
> **[33]** s 567(1) IR Act and **s 561(3) WCRA** describe the appeal as *"an appeal by way of
> rehearing"*… *"the scope of the appeal will depend upon the proper construction of the statute."*
> **[34]** *"The term 'appeal by way of rehearing' usually requires the appellate court to **review
> the evidence itself and draw its own factual conclusions**, recognising though the advantage of the
> primary court in seeing and hearing the witnesses… **is not one that is limited to errors of law or
> jurisdiction.**"*
> ⚠⚠ **[35]–[36]** *"**Section 557 of the 2016 IR Act specifically limits the grounds which can be
> raised as of right to errors of law or jurisdiction.** Such grounds are narrower than are available
> on an appeal by way of rehearing… **There is arguably a tension between s 561(3) of the WCR Act…
> and s 557(1) of the 2016 IR Act**… There is also an internal tension in the 2016 IR Act between
> s 557(1) and s 567."*

⇒ ⛔ **CORRECTION to what I told him earlier.** ⭐ **The rehearing language is there, and Davis J
describes what it usually means — but the Court expressly identifies an unresolved tension with
s 557(1), which limits appeals AS OF RIGHT to error of law or jurisdiction.**
⇒ ⭐⭐ **Safe position: an error-of-law appeal is available as of right. A full factual rehearing is
arguable and supported by the WCRA's words, but is not settled.** ⛔ **Do not assert a general right
to re-argue the facts.**
⭐⭐⭐ **Which makes the *Delaney*/*Hochen* point more valuable, not less: a misapplication of s 32(5)
is an error of LAW, and therefore appellable as of right on any view of the tension.**

## 6.1 The exclusion argument is now built on read authority, and it is strong
| Step | Authority |
|---|---|
| The correct inquiry is whether the injury arose out of reasonable management action reasonably taken — **not whether it "mainly" did** | ⭐ *Hochen* [12]; *Adams* [23] |
| **s 32(5)(a) does not stop a worker pointing to particular stressful events to satisfy s 32(1)** | ⭐⭐⭐ *Hochen* [10] |
