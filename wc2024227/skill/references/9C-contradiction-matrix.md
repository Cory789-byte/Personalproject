## TIER 1 — contradictions on the face of the record (no disclosure needed)

**T1-A — "Not causative" + "reasonable in all respects" vs the Regulator's OWN admitted Review Decision.**
9C ¶22(f): the 7-hour-break issue "was NOT CAUSATIVE of the appellant's injury"; ¶22 "reasonable in
all respects." BUT the Review Decision (24 Oct 2024) found the 7-hour break "amounted to unreasonable
management action" AND that "employment was a significant contributing factor" — and the Regulator
ADMITS the contents of that decision, both findings (Form 24 Para 37). Worse: the 9C's Decision
Sought ¶2 asks the Commission to CONFIRM the 24 Oct 2024 decision. The Regulator is pleading two
positions (not causative; reasonable) that contradict findings inside the very decision it admits
and asks to confirm. THE central contradiction: their own review body already found for Cory on
UMA and significant-contribution.

**T1-B — the 7-hour breach is CONCEDED on their own pleading.**
9C ¶22(a): the shifts were "separated by only a 7-hour break (RATHER THAN AN 8-HOUR BREAK)"; ¶22(e):
"in June 2020 the Appellant SIGNED AN AGREEMENT allowing an 8-HOUR break." Put together, the
Regulator's own pleading concedes the operative minimum was 8 hours and the actual break was 7 —
i.e. a breach of the applicable minimum is admitted. (This also disposes of the 9A's "10-hour"
overstatement: even on the Regulator's own 8-hour figure, 7 < 8. Argue the CONCEDED 8-hour minimum,
never the 10.)

**T1-C — "human error" is the antithesis of "reasonable management action taken reasonably."**
9C ¶22(a): the 7-hour break was "a result of HUMAN ERROR." ¶14 (COVID): the decline was "human error
by Ms Taylor." The s 32(5)(a) exclusion protects considered management ACTION taken reasonably (Read;
Allwood). An error is the absence of a considered decision. By pleading "human error," the Regulator
concedes these were not reasoned management actions — so there is either nothing for the exclusion to
protect, or the "reasonable in all respects" characterisation of the same events is internally
inconsistent. They cannot be both errors and reasonable management actions.

**T1-D — the COVID decline reason is conceded to be factually wrong.**
9C ¶14: Taylor declined the COVID leave because (on her assessment) the stat dec "was not attached";
"a review indicates the attachments WERE present." Self-defeating: the stated ground for the
twice-decline is conceded to be mistaken. And the excuse offered — "a background of high work
demands" — corroborates the appellant's stressor of an overloaded/under-resourced environment.

**T1-E — "remedied in a timely manner" vs the admitted 25-day AVAC delay.**
9C ¶20: pay "discrepancies/errors remedied in a timely manner"; "reasonable and taken in a reasonable
way." BUT Form 24 Para 40-41 ADMITS payroll directed the correction "IMMEDIATELY" on 3 May 2024 and
the AVAC was not submitted until 28 May — a 25-day delay. "Immediately" ignored for 25 days is not
"timely." Admission contradicts contention.

**T1-F — cherry-picked medical records (baseline vs diagnosis).**
9C ¶8: does not admit the clean baseline "because medical records identify a PAST MEDICAL HISTORY OF
ANXIETY" — relying on the medical records as authoritative. ¶9: does not admit the MDD/Krishnaiah
diagnosis — "matters for determination." They treat the same body of records as proof when it helps
(prior anxiety) and as unproven when it hurts (the diagnosis). And the 16 Nov 2023 record ("no
psychological illness such as depression/psychosis") is ADMITTED as listed (Form 24 Para 34) — a
clean contemporaneous baseline they've admitted, against the "past history of anxiety" they assert.

## TIER 2 — contradiction the MSH disclosure exposes (provable now)

**T2-G — "reasonable management action" on fatigue vs NO fatigue system existing.**
9C pleads the fatigue handling "reasonable in all respects." MSH's 5 June letter confirms: NO FRMS
applied to the Switchboard (Item 4); FRMS implemented only "after 30 June 2024" (Item 5) — after the
injury; "no consequential changes to operating procedures" (Item 3(c)). One cannot take "reasonable
management action" under a fatigue-risk framework that did not exist. The absence of any FRMS is the
concrete refutation of "reasonable in all respects" on the keystone stressor. (Frame as: the
Regulator's contention is contradicted by the non-party's own factual confirmation — NOT as collusion.)

## TIER 3 — contradictions the 64G disclosure will TEST (working — pending production)

**T3-H — "Ms Reese was unaware of the PID" (¶16) — the reprisal linchpin.**
The retraction direction (15 May) fell within 48 hrs of the PID (13 May). The 9C defends by asserting
Reese was UNAWARE of the PID. The 11 June disclosure (CS-4) is the 15-16 May correspondence
concerning "the complaint and the operational and HR response to it." What Reese knew, and when, is
exactly what the Item 20 material (13-31 May window) and the custodian correspondence would show. IF
production shows Reese engaged with "the complaint" in that window, the "unaware" defence is in
jeopardy. WORKING — the disclosure tests it; do not assert.

**T3-I — "rostered in an equitable way... would not have earned 42% less" (¶19).**
Denies the pay-disparity stressor by asserting equitable rostering. The security-access/attendance
records (Items 8-9) and the roster records go directly to whether the rostering was in fact
equitable. Disclosure tests the assertion. WORKING.

**T3-J — the grievance narrative proves the problem persisted (¶13).**
The 9C denies the 7 Aug grievance was "dismissed" by narrating an ongoing process: 10 Aug meeting,
29 Aug + 8 Sept emails, "Taylor emailed 14 Nov re shift/break compliance," 15 Nov meeting. That
timeline is double-edged FOR CORY: an unresolved shift/break-compliance issue running Aug-Nov 2023
corroborates that the rostering/fatigue concern was real, live, and unresolved for months — and the
"14 Nov re shift/break compliance" email concedes a compliance issue existed. The disclosure of that
correspondence supports the appellant. WORKING/mixed.

## THE UNIFYING READ (for submissions / settlement, not the 64G)
The 9C defends by (i) contradicting the Regulator's own admitted Review findings (T1-A), (ii)
conceding the operative breach while relabelling it "human error" (T1-B/C), (iii) asserting
"reasonable in all respects" over conduct its own admissions and MSH's disclosure show was error,
delay, or ungoverned (T1-D/E, T2-G), and (iv) resting key denials on factual assertions the
compelled records will test (T3-H/I/J). A defence redrafted to v4.4 that still contradicts its
author's own review decision is not a settled position — it is the reason the produce-or-swear
application matters and the reason the costs exposure on the Calderbank is real.
