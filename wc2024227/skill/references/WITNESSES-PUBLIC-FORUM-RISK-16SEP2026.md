## 3. 🔴 Ms Conaghan — by a distance the most exposed person in the matter

**She is a serving employee of the organisation whose conduct is in issue, in the same department,
under the same management, and she will say so in an open hearing under her own name.**

**What she carries:**

1. ⛔⛔ **¶¶ 10–11 are her own industrial grievance, given in someone else's case.** She will swear
   that *she* was not consulted and *not balloted*. That is not evidence about the Appellant at all.
   ⚠ **And it binds her**: if she ever wanted to raise consultation herself, she has now given sworn
   evidence about it in a proceeding where she has no standing, no representation and no control.
2. ⚠ **¶¶ 5 and 7 are hearsay carrying clinical risk** — *"what she was told at the console"* about
   results not reaching the treating team and calls reaching the wrong medical team. ⇒ Cross-
   examination writes itself: *you don't actually know that, do you?* **A public credibility hit,
   taken for a fact already admitted.**
3. ⚠ **¶ 12 asks her to describe a colleague's psychological deterioration** in public. It is
   properly bounded (*"She offers no opinion as to diagnosis or cause"*), but it is still a serving
   officer being asked, on the record, about a co-worker's mental state — and she goes back to that
   department afterwards.
4. **Publication.** Her name, her employer, her department, her evidence against her employer — in a
   decision that stays searchable for the rest of her career.

**What protects her — verified verbatim, IR Act 2016 (Qld), current as at 1 January 2026:**

| Provision | Text | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **s 284(1)(c)(ii)** | a person has a workplace right if able to *"make a complaint or inquiry… if the person is an employee—in relation to the person's employment"* | her consultation evidence is about her own employment |
| **s 284(1)(a)–(b)** | a right to the benefit of, or ability to participate in a process under, an **industrial instrument** | the consultation and ballot obligations arise under the certified agreement and Award |
| **s 285(1)** | *"A person must not take adverse action against another person because the other person… has a workplace right… [or] has… exercised a workplace right"* — **civil penalty provision** | the direct protection |
| ⭐ **s 285(2)** | adverse action against a second person *"because a third person has exercised… a workplace right for the second person's benefit or for the benefit of a class of persons to which the second person belongs"* | ⭐ **fits her exactly** — she is in the class |
| ⭐⭐ **s 306(2)** | *"It is presumed that the action was… taken for that reason or with that intent, **unless the person proves otherwise**."* | **reverse onus.** She would not have to prove why |

⚠ **Two honest limits on that protection.**
- ⛔ **Whether a WCRA appeal is itself a proceeding "under an industrial law" is arguable.**
  "Industrial law" is defined as *"(a) this Act; or (b) another Act regulating the relationships
  between employers and employees."* The WCRA regulates workers' compensation. ⇒ **Her protection is
  strongest on the consultation/instrument limb and on s 284(1)(c)(ii), and should not be asserted
  to her on the appeal limb alone.**
- ⛔ **It protects against *adverse action*. It does not protect against the ordinary social cost in
  a small department**, and enforcing it would require **her** to start a second proceeding she has
  no appetite for. The protection is real; it is also a remedy, not a shield.

### ⭐⭐ The single most useful thing available to her: an attendance notice

The covering letter already reserves the right to apply under Part 6 of the Appeal Guide for
attendance notices for items 2 to 5 — **which includes her**.

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Compelling a willing witness is a protective act, not an aggressive one.** With a notice
she attends because she is **required to**, and she can say so to her employer and her colleagues.
Without one she is a serving officer who **volunteered** against her own employer. **The evidence is
identical; her position is not.** ⇒ **If she gives evidence at all, she should be under a notice.**

## 4. 🟠 Mr Harrison-Jones — lower exposure, but a new employer nobody has weighed

He has left Metro South, so the direct employer risk is gone. ⚠ **But the witness list records that
he is now an officer of the Queensland Ambulance Service** — and QAS sits within the Queensland
Health portfolio. ⇒ **He is still a Queensland public-sector health employee giving evidence about a
Queensland Health hospital's emergency communications.** Not disqualifying, and not the same as
Conaghan's exposure, but it has not been assessed anywhere and it should be.

**What he carries:**

1. ⭐ **¶ 6 is the paragraph the bench singled out.** At 34:43 Dwyer said *"you're going to make that
   assertion in your evidence and I would have thought the regulator will call Ms Taylor to give her
   side of the story and you'll get to cross-examine Ms Taylor."* ⇒ **His ¶ 6 is that event** — what
   Ms Taylor said, the sequence, and ⚠ **"the manner and volume in which it was said."**
   ⇒ **He is the one witness who will be cross-examined about a named individual's conduct toward a
   colleague, with that individual very possibly in the room.** That is the most personally
   confronting position of the five, and it is the most valuable evidence he gives.
2. ⚠ **Cross-examination drift is his real professional risk.** As an ambulance officer he may be
   invited into opinion about emergency-response adequacy — *"and in your experience, what happens
   when a MET call goes to the wrong team?"* ⛔ **That is outside his outline, he cannot give expert
   opinion, and an answer would expose him professionally.** His outline is correctly confined to
   what he saw at the Switchboard in 2023; ⇒ **he should be told, in terms, that the boundary of his
   evidence is what he personally observed and that "I don't know" is a complete answer.**
3. **Publication.** His name attached to evidence about a named manager's manner, permanently.

⭐ Otherwise his outline is the cleanest of the three: seven paragraphs, all first-hand, no
departmental proposition anywhere in it.

**What he carries:**

  to take with open eyes, and the conversation with him should happen before any notice issues, not
  after it lands.**

|---|---|---|

| Conaghan ¶¶ 1, 6, 8 — extra shifts, updates left undone, no self-correction | ✅ **admitted / documentary** | ✗ |

| ⚠ Conaghan ¶¶ 10–11 — not consulted, not balloted | partly documentary | ⚠ **hers, and about her** |
| ⭐ **H-J ¶ 6 — the incident, manner and volume** | ⛔ **no** | ⭐⭐ **yes — the one the bench asked for** |
| ⭐ **Conaghan ¶¶ 2–4 — abuse, threats, complaints landing on the operator** | partly | ⭐ **yes — lived conditions** |
| ⭐ **Conaghan ¶ 12 — the change she observed** | ⛔ no | ⭐ **yes** |
| ⭐ **H-J ¶ 7, Conaghan ¶¶ 9, 13 — never hostile** | ⛔ no | ⭐⭐ **yes — the credit answer** |

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Two civilians are carrying permanent, public exposure largely to prove facts the Regulator
has already admitted.** Roughly half of each lay outline is doing no work that the admissions and the
documents are not already doing.

⇒ **The remedy is narrowing, not dropping.** Keep what is uniquely theirs — the incident, the lived
conditions at the console, the observed change, the credit evidence. Everything else is a public cost
with no evidentiary return.

## 9. What I would do, in order

1. ⭐⭐ **Fix the service date from the sent items** (§0). It is the date direction 2 was answered.
2. ⭐⭐ **Decide on the lay witnesses only after 30 September.** The Respondent's list and outlines are
   due **4 pm, 30 September**. ⇒ **If they admit the conditions and call nobody, much of Conaghan's
   and Harrison-Jones's evidence is unnecessary and their exposure can simply be avoided.**
   ⛔ **Do not narrow anything before then** — narrowing early gives away ground for nothing.
3. ⭐⭐⭐ **If either lay witness gives evidence, do it under an attendance notice.** It converts a
   volunteer into a person required to attend. **Same evidence, materially better position for them
   — and Conaghan is a serving officer of the employer.**
4. ⭐ **Tell both lay witnesses the boundary in terms**: what you personally saw and heard, nothing
   else, and *"I don't know"* is a complete answer. ⛔ Harrison-Jones in particular should not be
   drawn into emergency-response opinion.
5. ⭐ **Speak to Dr Krishnaiah before any notice issues**, not after.
6. ⭐ **Expect *"manipulate his roster"* to be read aloud** and have the one-sentence answer ready.
7. ⚠ **Consider whether Conaghan ¶¶ 10–11 are worth her carrying at all.** They prove a consultation
   failure affecting the workforce, which is not the injury, and they are the only paragraphs in the
   matter that put a witness's own industrial position on the public record in someone else's case.

## 10. ⚠ Limits

- ⛔ **Nothing here is said to any witness**, and nothing here is a communication to anyone.

  2026. ⚠ **The "industrial law" limb is flagged as arguable and should not be relied on without
  advice.**

  answered here**.
