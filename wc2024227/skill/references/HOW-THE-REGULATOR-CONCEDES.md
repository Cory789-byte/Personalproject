# How the Regulator concedes — the mechanism, the trigger, the timing
> 13 September 2026. Read from the QIRC Workers' Compensation Appeal Guide (Parts 5 and 10),
> WCRA ss 552A, 553, 554, 558, and IR(T) Rules r 62.

## 1 · ⛔⛔ There is no "settlement" in a workers' compensation appeal

**Appeal Guide 5.1, in terms:**

> **"No commercial settlement is possible at the conference. Conferences for workers' compensation
> matters are not intended to facilitate conciliations."**

⇒ **Correct the scenario model accordingly.** Its "settles" branch was mislabelled. There is nothing
to negotiate and no sum to split. The outcomes are binary:

| | |
|---|---|
| **the Respondent concedes the appeal** | the decision is set aside, the claim is accepted (WCRA s 558(1)(b)–(c)) |
| **the Appellant discontinues** | Form 27 – Request to discontinue proceeding |
| the matter is heard | one side wins |

## 2 · ⭐⭐⭐ The concession mechanism, in the Commission's own words

> **"Following the conference, the Respondent may decide to review its position or consider
> conceding the appeal where new information is presented that they may not have yet considered."**

Three things follow, and each is operational:

| | |
|---|---|
| **When** | ⭐ **following the conference** — not before it, and not at it |
| **Trigger** | ⭐ **"new information … that they may not have yet considered"** |
| **Who** | the Respondent, on a review of its own position |

⇒ **The conference is the concession event.** Everything aimed at concession should be aimed at
getting to a conference and at what is put there.

## 3 · What the conference is for, and one warning

> "the conference can assist in understanding **whether any elements of s 32 of the Act can be
> agreed**"

⭐ So the conference is the forum in which the s 32 elements are narrowed. Injury and *"a significant
contributing factor"* were both found by the Regulator's own review officer; the live issue is
s 32(5)(a) alone. That narrowing is exactly what a conference is designed to do.

⚠ **But the Guide warns in the same breath:** *"even though the Regulator may have previously
accepted an element of s 32 of the Act, it does not mean that this element will be accepted by the
Respondent at the appeal stage."* ⛔ **Nothing in Review Decision 69983 is banked.**

⚠ **And:** *"The Member of the Commission who chairs such a conference **will not be the Member who
hears the appeal**."* Commissioner Dwyer has case-managed this matter, held the mention, and knows
the file. **That accumulated understanding sits with the conference, not with the decision-maker.**
It is an argument for making the conference count, not for saving material for a hearing.

## 4 · ⚠⚠ Costs follow the event — the risk that runs both ways

**Appeal Guide, Part 10:**

> "In workers' compensation appeals costs generally **'follow the event'**. This means that the
> **unsuccessful party will usually be required to pay the costs of the successful party**. Costs
> include the cost of obtaining legal advice and legal representation, as well as any expenses in
> calling witnesses (flights etc.) and any outlays for expert reports and opinions. Costs are
> limited to the **'costs of the hearing'**."

⛔ **This is the most serious downside in the matter and it has not been priced anywhere in the
repo.** Self-representation keeps *his* costs low; it does **not** protect him from an order to pay
the **Respondent's** costs of the hearing if the appeal fails. Counsel, solicitors and expert
outlays are all within scope.

⇒ It is also the mechanism behind *"wasting the Commission's time"*: the Commission does not
sanction that directly; **costs do**, and they fall on whoever loses.

⚠ **And a cost he may not have priced: witness expenses.** Guide 10.1 and **IR(T) r 62** — a party
who calls a witness pays their reasonable travel expenses and conduct money, *before* the day of
attendance, and **"if you do not pay… the witness will have a lawful reason for not attending."**
⇒ If Taylor and Reese are **not** named on 30 September and attendance notices are sought, **the
Appellant calls them and the Appellant pays.**

## 5 · Timing — what the sequence actually permits

| | |
|---|---|
| to 30 Sep 2026 | directions 3 and 4. **No concession mechanism is available** |
| **1 Oct 2026** | ⭐ direction 5 activates — *the Appellant* contacts the Registry for a second s 552A conference or a hearing |
| conference listed | the Commission may also call one itself; **s 552A(2): "The parties must attend"** |
| **following the conference** | ⭐ **the concession window opens** |

⇒ **Revised timing in the scenario model:** concession before a conference ≈ **3%** (was 8%);
**following the second conference ≈ 22%**; after listing before hearing ≈ 8%; at or during hearing
≈ 5%. Similar total, **completely different shape** — and the shape is what dictates what to do.

⭐⭐ **That is the concession case. It belongs at the conference, put once and put whole — not in
correspondence, where it cannot trigger the mechanism the Guide describes.**

## 7 · Two more provisions found in the same pass

- **WCRA s 554** — each party must give the other any document it wants to adduce **at least 10
  business days before the hearing**, and *"a party can not rely on a document that was not given…
  unless the appeal body agrees."* ⇒ the exhibit list is a hard deadline, not housekeeping.
- **WCRA s 553** — applies **UCPR ch 7 pt 2** (disclosure generally, including ⭐ **r 215**, original
  documents for inspection, and interrogatories) and **UCPR ch 9 pt 4** (mediation and case
  appraisal) to this appeal. See the correction at the head of
  `RULES-admissions-documents-and-sequence.md`.

## 6 · The second conference — mechanics, from Guide 5.1, 5.2 and 7.1 (added 14 Sep)
- **Who chairs:** *"Usually the same Member… will also chair the second conference"* — the first
  s 552A conference was 13 March 2026 before Commissioner Dwyer; expect him. **And (5.1, 7.1):
  the conference Member will not hear the appeal; the file is allocated to a different Member for
  hearing.** So Dwyer's accumulated understanding sits with the conference — the concession event —
  and not with the decision-maker. That settles the earlier "may or may not hear it" uncertainty.
- **Trigger:** direction 5 — the Appellant writes to the Registry by 1 Oct requesting a second
  s 552A conference (or a hearing). Chambers lists it. s 552A(2): the parties must attend.
- **Room (5.2):** a conference room around a table — parties, the Member, the Associate. Recorded
  and transcribed (parties may order the transcript). The Member may **go off the record into
  private conference** and speak to each side separately — not recorded. ⭐ That is where a Member
  tells a respondent, privately, what he makes of its prospects.
- **Not adversarial (5.2):** "not about winning… or stating a case." Purpose: review, refine,
  narrow — *"whether any elements of s 32 can be agreed"*; the nature of the evidence; number of
  hearing days; number of witnesses; whether the FDO needs amending for further material or an
  amended SOFC (**this is where leave for the Second Amended 9A is raised, and where any amended
  SOFC of theirs surfaces**).
- **Lawyers (s 552B):** only with the other party's agreement or the Commission's leave. Decide
  before the listing whether to agree to counsel attending for the Regulator. Refusing reads as
  obstruction; agreeing puts the person who advises on concession in the room to hear it.
- **After (5.1):** *"Following the conference, the Respondent may decide to review its position or
  consider conceding the appeal where new information is presented…"* Then, by letter (the 16 Jul
  undertaking: "should our position change… we will advise you"), and consent orders / decision
  set aside under s 558(1). Or (7.1) the Member establishes the parties want a hearing, allocates
  to a different Member, and hearing directions follow (s 554 exchange; exhibit list).
- **What to bring:** the element grid (one page); the admitted record; their 30 Sep list and
  outlines read against it; the seven unrecorded events; ¶260; ¶¶263–268; the register question;
  the s 556 question; a hearing estimate (days) and witness count. Put once, whole, in the
  operational register — premise first.

**⚠ Two things from the sealed FDO(3) itself (read 14 Sep):**
- Directions 2 and 4: outlines are **served on the other party "but not file[d] in the Industrial
  Registry."** Only the witness lists (directions 1 and 3) are filed. So the conference Member does
  **not** have either side's outlines on the file. He has the pleadings, the filed lists, and
  whatever was delivered to the Registry under footing letter ¶5. **Bring both sides' outlines,
  both lists and the element grid to the conference and hand them up** — the Guide's "review,
  refine, narrow" happens on what is on the table, not on the file.
- Direction 5, second sentence: *"Should no action be taken by the Appellant to progress the
  matter, the matter may be placed into abeyance and subject to lapse pursuant to rule 230."*
  ⛔ The 1 October request is not optional. Write to the Registry on 1 October whatever the 25th
  and 30th bring.
