## 2. How the Appellant now presents his case

- **Admission-anchored.** Every material fact carries its admitted paragraph number. The pleading asks the
  Commission to decide reasonableness and causation, not what happened.
- **Three self-contained stressors:**
  1. the conditions of the work: database access, process changes, misdirected calls, the directory, office hours;
  2. pay and leave;
  3. the roster and fatigue.

  Each stressor names its own causal, context and record particulars.
- **The legal frame has been narrowed and conceded.**
  - The onus is accepted (C 1) and the de novo point is accepted (C 9).
  - Langerak [86] and Mahaffey [57] are used for whole-of-injury weighing (C 7), with Delaney only where the
    connection is close (C 6).
  - Some stressors are pleaded as conditions that are not management action (C 4).
- **Our own case is turned against us.**
  - C 5A quotes our outlines' concessions and lists what no outline answers.
  - C 10 uses fact 303 on ¶27.
  - B.5 lists what we do not allege.
- **Medical case.** It rests on the treating records and the oral evidence of Dr Hawes and Dr Krishnaiah (1.9). There
  is no medico-legal report.
- **Presentation.** It is disciplined: written, dated and courteous, with each step anchored to an admission. The
  19 Oct letter shows he will ask the Commission for directions on anything we do not agree.

## 3. Our best points (in order of strength)

1. **Causation by stressor.**
   - There is no medico-legal report.
   - The psychiatrist's report carries a "not for medico-legal use" footer.
   - The 9A itself says the report "is not relied on to say which of the individual events … caused the injury" (1.7).
   - *WCR v Carr* [2023] ICQ 1 set aside a first-instance win:
     - Ground 2: no evidence linked the stressor to the injury.
     - Ground 3: the reasons did not show the weighing of reasonable against unreasonable action.
   - If the treating doctors will not attribute the injury to particular unreasonable or non-management factors, the
     Appellant may fail to discharge the onus he accepts.
2. **The May 2024 sequence as reasonable management action.**
   - SOFC ¶16(b): absences on 13–15 May not notified by the on-call process; Taylor raised it on 14 May.
   - His email of 15 May went to many staff about the manager's attendance. Reese replied after HR advice.
   - ¶16(a): Reese was unaware of the PID.
   - The 9A itself says the referral of 16 May "was made the day after the request to retract" (1.3).
   - The argument: the injury crystallised from management's reasonable response to his conduct, as the review found
     for factor 2.
3. **The seven-hour break is small and isolated.**
   - It was one hour short of the 8-hour agreement he signed (¶22(e)). It happened once, as "human error" (¶22(a)).
   - He could refuse shifts (¶22(b)). The leave next day was paid (¶22(c)). No fatigue leave arises under
     cl 18.10 (¶24(b)).
   - He worked on until 3 June. The SOFC says it was "not causative" (¶22(f)).
4. **Prior history.**
   - SOFC ¶8 relies on the 2022 anxiety entry in the referral history (9A 1.3).
   - The 16 Nov 2023 GP record of poor sleep with shift work pre-dates the March roster.
   - He sought full-time hours and nights.
   - Dr Hawes's certificates say "no pre-existing factor or condition". Test that in cross.
5. **Perception (s 32(5)(b)).**
   - Dr Hawes recorded the mechanism as "ongoing breaking of workplace rules by bosses, victimizing him" (SOFC ¶10).
   - The SOFC lists perception as an element (¶6(d)(ii)), and the 9A puts it up for decision (Part D Q6).
   - There are no pleaded facts on our side. This is a cross-examination theme, not a case.
6. **Reliability of the 9A.**
   - Part C ¶7 cites *Carr* [2022] QIRC 59 "as applied" without noting that it was set aside.
   - The 14:38 "MET call" falls after a shift that ended at 14:00.
   - Fact 272 is widened to "at any time before 30 June 2024".
   - "Awake and working for about sixteen hours" overstates an 8-hour shift.
   - These are small, but each one invites "the pleading overstates the record".
7. **Post-injury material:** of limited relevance (SOFC ¶25). This covers the 2024–2026 documents at Tabs 17–24 and
   the 2026 checklist.

## 4. Our weaknesses (what we must meet)

- **The facts are gone.** 298 of 303 are admitted and none are denied. The documents are confirmed, the contents
  admitted.
- **Our own review decision:**
  - it accepted injury, and employment as a significant contributing factor ("work events were the sole cause",
    Dr Hawes to WorkCover, 2 Sep 2024);
  - it found the roster unreasonable, "in direct contradiction to the award and the 8-hour agreement";
  - it then excluded by counting ("two out of the three causative factors … mainly arose").
  - That counting is the method Langerak [86] and Mahaffey [57] reject, and it is Carr ground 3 in reverse.
- **Our outlines concede:**
  - "human error";
  - "a rostering practice issue for the line manager";
  - a leave decline that "appears to have been made in error".

  They are silent on database access, the 15 April change, the misdirected calls, the directory error, the 23-day
  fatigue interval, the 10 May HR email and fatigue risk management.
- **Our pleading is thin.** ¶27 identifies no management action (fact 303). ¶20 says pay was handled "in a timely
  manner", against admitted facts:
  - a direction of 3 May not actioned on 13 May;
  - "Part Completed" on 30 May;
  - a correction in July 2025.
- **Metro South's own letter to the Commissioner:**
  - fatigue records "do not exist";
  - assessment "after 30 June 2024";
  - complaints "managed solely via email or verbally".
- **Our witnesses against their own emails:**
  - Taylor: fact 292 ("I did raise my voice"), the 18 June apology, ¶220;
  - Reese: facts 218–221, including "Cory's roster will not be considered, however Cory is not yet aware", and the
    code-of-conduct reply followed by "I will follow up on the issues raised".

  Harrison-Jones and Conaghan go unanswered unless Taylor is called.
- **The treating records attribute the injury to rostering and pay**, not to the May exchange:
  - 1 July 2024: "withhold pay … manipulate his roster";
  - February 2025: "issues with management and rostering", and pay "withheld or delayed".
- **No expert, and no medical witness.** An independent examination now needs leave, after the directions expired,
  before a Commissioner who said on 7 Aug that the Regulator "does have to contradict".
