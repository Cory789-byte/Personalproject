| The letter asked | Consequence if a decision is made without an answer |

| ⭐⭐⭐ **(b)** whether any **s 89(1)(b) authorisation** has been given | **s 89(1)(b): an officer *"must not take action or further action concerning a matter that is, or may be, affected by the conflict unless authorised by the employee's chief executive."*** ⇒ **If no authorisation exists, the decision is taken against a statutory prohibition — one placed personally before the Chief Executive and acknowledged at her direction** |

⭐⭐ **The letter is scrupulously framed to engage the section without accusing anyone:** *"I say only
that this is a set of circumstances in which a conflict may exist."* **s 89(1) covers an interest
that conflicts *or may conflict*.** That wording is exact, and it is what makes the questions
unanswerable-badly.

## PART 2 — THE FIVE DEFENCES THE LETTER CLOSED IN ADVANCE
1. ⛔ **"We were not aware of psychosocial hazards."** Part 2A names four — escalation of patient
   safety concerns; consultation on changes to work, roster and on-call; fatigue and recovery; and
   genuine input into decisions — and records that **WHS Act ss 47–49 consultation has not occurred
   at any point since 26 June 2026.**
2. ⛔ **"We could not accommodate."** Part 2B quotes **their own** instruments: **G03 cl 1.1**
   (temporary adjustments so an employee may *"continue work during their recovery"*; cl 1.1.1
   extends it to a health-management case with no compensation claim) and **QH-IMP-401-5** (suitable
   duties *"actively sought"* and *"made available wherever reasonably practicable"*).
3. ⛔ **"The medical process had to finish first."** Part 2B: *"a medical process clarifies functional
   limits; it does not displace the duty to consider adjustments in the meantime."*
4. ⛔ **"The process was fair."** Part 2C records the asymmetry: **seven days on 7 July, seven days on
   31 July, each with a consequence attached** — against *"unable to commit to a timeframe"*
   (15 July) and *"unable to guarantee"* a return (30 July).
5. ⭐⭐⭐ **"We may use the clinical material."** Part 5(c): *"that it not be used in connection with
   any other proceeding. **I do not consent to that.**"* ⇒ **Any use of the RFMI material in
   WC/2024/227 is a use expressly refused, in writing, to the Chief Executive.**

## PART 4 — WHAT WOULD FOLLOW
1. ⭐ **A fresh dismissal creates fresh rights**: reinstatement under the **IR Act 2016** in the QIRC;
   **AD Act** impairment discrimination (continuing conduct, **s 15(2)**, window into **2027**); and
   the general protections / reprisal file.
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **The s 89 letter becomes the centrepiece of all three** — because it establishes, on a
   date, with an acknowledgment, that the accountable officer was told and did not answer.
3. ⚠ **And it would sit against a live appeal** in which the same conduct is not in issue but the
   same organisation is the employer. **Keep the tracks separate; the connection needs no assertion.**
