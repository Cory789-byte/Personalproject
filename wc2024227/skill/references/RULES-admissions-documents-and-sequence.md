## 1. ⚠ SUPERSEDED HEADLINE — the UCPR does not apply *via the IR(T) Rules*

Every reference to the *Uniform Civil Procedure Rules 1999* in the IR(T) Rules is confined to:

- **r 106** — UCPR ch 9 pt 4 (ADR) applies **to a claim** (Part 3, Industrial Magistrates Court), not
  to a commission proceeding; and **r 123ZB** disapplies even that for relevant claims;
- **r 70(2)–(3)** — costs **scales** only (UCPR sch 1, sch 2, ch 17A);
- **r 111(2)** — magistrate's costs for a claim.

There is **no general adoption of the UCPR for QIRC proceedings.** UCPR r 189 is a comparator,
not a source of power here. ⛔ Do not cite UCPR r 189 as if it governed this appeal.

## 2. The rule that governs — IR(T) Rules r 49

> **49 Notice to admit facts or documents**
> (1) A party to a proceeding (the *first party*) may, by notice in the approved form served on
> another party, ask the other party to admit, **for the proceeding only**, the facts or documents
> stated in the notice.
> (2) If the other party does not, within **14 days** after receiving a notice under subrule (1),
> serve a notice on the first party disputing the facts or the authenticity of the documents, the
> other party is taken to admit, for the proceeding only, **the stated facts or the authenticity of
> the stated documents.**
> (3) The other party may, **with the leave** of the court, commission or registrar, **withdraw** an
> admission taken to have been made under subrule (2).

**r 108** is the identical provision for the Industrial Magistrates Court.

### ⭐⭐ The distinction the whole Form 24 turns on

| You ask them to admit | What r 49(2) gives you |
|---|---|
| a **document** | **authenticity only** — that it is what it purports to be |
| a **fact** | **the fact** |

Admitting a document does **not** admit what it says, when it was sent, who sent it, or that
anything in it happened. Admitting a fact does.

**This is why the notice served 28 August 2026 was framed as 303 facts *about* documents**
("That email states: '…'"; "On 3 May 2024 at 3:06 pm X sent an email to Y") and never as a
request to admit documents. Each of the 298 admissions is therefore a **fact** admission.

## 3. Admitting the sequence on the emails

A sequence is a set of facts, and each element is separately admissible under r 49(1):

| Element | Example from the served notice |
|---|---|
| the send, sender, recipients | ¶ 56 — "On 3 May 2024 at 3:06 pm Vivian Kwok, MASPER Registrar, sent an email to Ms Taylor, copied to Dr Pan Jane Wong…" |
| the words | ¶ 58 — "That email records… 'MET call team called x5290 asking where MET call was located…'" |
| the date and time | ¶¶ 89, 91, 94, 100 |
| **the interval** | ¶ 97 — "sent three hours and two minutes after…"; ¶ 125 — "ten days, four hours, forty minutes and twenty seconds" |
| **the absence** | ¶ 69 — "does not allege that any communication was sent… before 10:15 am on 9 May 2024" |

⭐ **Intervals are the strongest form.** Arithmetic computed from admitted times does not depend
on the truth of anything stated in the document, so the Respondent's standing qualification
(admits the contents, not the truth of the contents) cannot reach it. That is the design of
Part B.4 of the Second Amended Form 9A.

### What the admission does **not** give

Where the pleading needs the email's **assertion** to be true — that a MET team actually could
not be told where VHUB was — the admission does not supply it. The pleading therefore pleads
**the record, not the event**: "the email records: …". Keep that discipline.

## 4. If they do not admit — how the sequence is still proved

1. ⭐ **IR Act 2016 s 531** — in proceedings the commission **"(a) is not bound by rules of
   evidence; and (b) may inform itself in the way it considers appropriate"** (s 531(2)), and is
   "guided in its decisions by equity, good conscience and the substantial merits of the case"
   (s 531(3)). **s 531(1) excludes only** proceedings for recovery of amounts (other than a
   relevant amount) and offences against the Act — **a s 550 WCRA appeal is neither**, so s 531(2)
   and (3) apply. WCRA contains no competing evidence provision for the appeal body: s 570(1)(b)
   ("not bound by the rules of evidence") is for an **employer/self-insurer appeal to a court**
   under s 567, not this appeal.
2. **Evidence Act 1977 s 92(1)(b)** — business-records route: a statement in a document forming
   part of a record relating to an undertaking, made from information supplied by a person with
   personal knowledge, **where that person is called**.
3. **s 95** — statements in documents produced by a device or process (email systems); the court
   may presume the process produced it.
4. ⭐ **s 102 — weight.** Regard is had to whether the statement was made **contemporaneously**
   with the facts, and whether the maker had **"any incentive to conceal or misrepresent the
   facts"**. The MASPER and Integrated Respiratory Service emails score on both: contemporaneous,
   and written by clinicians with no stake in this appeal.
5. **s 103** — ss 92–95 and 101 are alternative to and in aid of one another.

## 5. Admission ≠ in evidence. It must still be tendered

Appeal Guide 7.6.1: *"you should tender to the Commission (meaning to make an Exhibit) any
documents relevant to the claim… documents not tendered at the hearing will not be considered as
evidence and the Member… will not take them into consideration."*

An r 49 admission removes the need to **prove** the fact. It does not put the document **before**
the Commission. Tender is still required.

## 6. ⛔ There is no costs-of-proof sanction in r 49

**UCPR r 189(4)** makes a party who disputes a fact or authenticity, and is afterwards proved
wrong, **pay the costs of proof unless the court orders otherwise.**

**IR(T) r 49 contains no equivalent.** So the Respondent's refusal to admit ¶¶ 154 and 228–231
carries **no automatic costs consequence**. Costs in a WC appeal are in the appeal body's
discretion under **WCRA s 558(3)** ("Costs of the hearing are in the appeal body's discretion,
except to the extent provided under a regulation"), and Appeal Guide Part 10 governs. A costs
argument runs through s 558(3), not r 49.

## 7. Disclosure is order-dependent, and admissions can switch it off

**r 46** — the duty to disclose directly relevant documents arises **only if a directions order
requiring disclosure is made** (r 41(o)). It continues "until the proceeding is concluded **or the
matter in issue is admitted**, withdrawn, struck out or otherwise disposed of" (r 46(2)), and does
not reach privileged documents (r 46(3)).

⚠ **r 46(2) cuts both ways.** Admitting a matter can end the other side's disclosure obligation on
that issue. Consider before making or inviting further admissions on anything still being chased.

## 8. Withdrawal — the live risk on 298 admissions

**r 49(3)**: an admission taken to have been made may be withdrawn **with leave**. Leave must be
sought and justified. There is no equivalent express power to withdraw an admission that was
*actually* made rather than deemed — but the commission's general powers and s 531(3) would be
invoked. Treat 298 admissions as strong but not unassailable; do not build a proposition so that
it fails entirely if one admission is withdrawn.

## 9. Practice points for the further notice to admit

- ⭐ Frame **every** item as a **fact**, never "admit the following documents". Authenticity alone is
  nearly worthless here.
- Plead **the record, not the event**, wherever the event is contested.
- Convert everything possible into **intervals** and **negatives** — neither depends on the truth
  of contents.
- Anchor each negative to a **defined universe** ("as presently constituted"; "in the Respondent's
  disclosed material presently before the Commission").
- Remember the **14-day** clock in r 49(2) and that a dispute notice must dispute "the facts or the
  authenticity of the documents" — a qualified answer that does neither may not avoid the deemed
  admission.
