# Could the Regulator concede narrowly (roster/break only, no PID, no safety)? — 25 Sep 2026

Read at source today: Review Decision 69983 pp 25–27 (conclusion); Blackwood v Mahaffey [2016] ICQ 10 [28]–[57]; their outlines 24 Sep; his served outline 9 Sep; live Form 9A 8 Apr 2026.

## The review decision already made the narrow finding and then applied a counting test
- It found factor 4 (the roster, 17–18 Mar) causative: "your injury arose out of employment, to the extent that it arose out of factors 2, 3 and 4". It also found factor 4 "unreasonable management action given that it was in direct contradiction to the award and the 8-hour agreement".
- It still rejected, because: "since two out of the three causative factors amounted to reasonable management action … your psychological injury **mainly** arose out of such management action. Further, factor 4 was … only … one incident".
- Mahaffey [53]: an inquiry into whether unreasonable management action was "the dominant cause" is "an erroneous approach" (Hochen; Adams). Mahaffey [57]: "any attempt to provide some type of formula or application of dominant cause has been rejected"; if the s 32(5) conduct "does not, on balance, displace the evidence in favour of the worker then a finding in the worker's favour must follow". Hochen [15] (quoted at [51]): "The presence of unreasonable management action with a sufficient causal connection to the injury may require that a negative answer be given" to s 32(5)(a).
- "Only one incident": Reese 10 May 2024 "a few rostering errors made by Chloe with regards to Cory's line" (fact 220); Taylor 7 Aug 2023 rest-period "oversight" (facts 156–157); Reese 7 Aug 2023 (fact 287).

## ⇒ The narrow route exists, and it is the Regulator's cleanest exit
A concession resting on factor 4 alone needs no finding on the May 2024 events, the retraction, the PID, Reese or Taylor's conduct generally, or anything about the emergency console. The QIRC gives effect to a concession by consent order: the decision is set aside and the claim accepted. No reasons, no findings.

## What stands in the way
1. **The medical causation / pre-existing contention** (amended SOFC ¶8, since 13 May 2026). A concession means dropping it or accepting that employment remains a significant contributing factor. They served no expert (24 Sep). The reviewer found work the sole cause with no pre-existing condition; they reserve that under de novo.
2. **Their outlines are built for global dilution**: Delaney, Bowers "not perfect", "human error, not repeated".
3. ⚠⚠ **His own live pleading pushes them away from a narrow exit.** The Form 9A of 8 Apr 2026, Stressor 1: "A Hostile Course of Management Conduct, Reprisal, and Suppression of Rights"; (e) "Public Interest Disclosure"; (f) "Immediate Reprisal"; "PID retaliation form a composite, hostile course". His served outline also records "my complaint of 13 May 2024, later determined to be a public interest disclosure (content not set out)". While those stand, a concession looks to MSH like conceding on a reprisal pleading. The Second Amended 9A (unserved) removes the characterisation. **Leave for it is the single most useful step toward a narrow concession.**

## At the conference (non-adversarial, Guide 5.2), put once, neutrally
- the s 32 elements that can be agreed: personal injury (the review decision found it), arising out of employment, and a significant contributing factor;
- that the review decision's own finding is that factor 4 was causative and unreasonable, and the only question left is s 32(5)(a) on the approach in Mahaffey [53]–[57]. Identify the question; do not argue it;
- leave for the Second Amended 9A.
Never: PID, reprisal, patient safety or harm, motive, the metadata.

## What a narrow concession gives him
- An accepted claim: statutory compensation from onset, and the WCRA s 237 gateway for any common law claim.
- No findings either way on PID or reprisal. The PID and adverse-action tracks are neither advanced nor harmed. Their clocks: s 310(3), six years.
- A WC appeal cannot be commercially settled (Guide 5.1). If any document offered with a concession carries release terms, stop and read it (discipline 9).

## Limits
- Whether they concede cannot be known. Model estimate (E34): concession before hearing ≈ 38%, most of it following the second conference.
- A consent order has no reasons; nothing is "found" in his favour beyond the result.
