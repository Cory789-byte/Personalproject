## 1. ⛔ THE WORD "INARTICULATE" WAS WRONG — WITHDRAWN

It was used in the red-team note as shorthand for *unperformed*, and it is inaccurate. **On the
tape he is not inarticulate.** When given a closed question he is exact and fluent, with zero

The accurate description is **front-loaded, and self-repairing under parallel load.** Those are two

## 3. SUBORDINATION — THE ANSWER IS SPLIT, AND THE SPLIT IS THE WHOLE STORY

### 3.1 In speech, he attempts subordination and abandons it; he succeeds with coordination

Watch the filters explanation fail and then succeed, in eight seconds [23:40]:

> ⛔ **Attempt 1 — subordinate:** *"**When** you go into, let's say, eHealth, for instance, **and**
> you can…"* — a *when*-clause opened, an example inserted inside it, the main clause never
> arrives. **Abandoned.**
> ✅ **Attempt 2 — coordinate:** *"Let's say you outlook, **and** you put a word in outlook, **and**
> it filters all the words."* — three flat clauses, joined by *and*. **Lands immediately.**

⇒ **Under load he is markedly better with coordination than subordination.** Every fluent utterance
on the tape is simple or coordinate. Every abandoned one begins with a subordinator (*when*, *if*,
*because*, *so that*).

⚠ A related tell: **connective slips, not logic slips.** At [51:57] *"my manager decided to roster
me on different specific days **or** it ends up being 42% difference"* — the relation is
consequence; the word chosen is alternative. **The reasoning is right; the joining word is wrong.**
That is a load symptom, not a comprehension one.

### 3.2 ⭐⭐⭐ In writing, he subordinates heavily — and that gap is what drew the AI question

His written material is densely subordinated, qualified and nested. His speech, under pressure, is
flat and coordinate.

> Dwyer, [09:25]: *"when I read stuff and I start to see **terms and language and complexities of
> language that go above and beyond what you'd anticipate from a human being**… I immediately get
> suspicious."*

⇒ **He was not reacting to the writing alone. He was reacting to the distance between the writing
and the speaker in front of him.** A large written-to-spoken register gap reads, to a listener, as
*someone else wrote this*.

⭐ **This is the real origin of the AI question, and it also explains why the answer worked.** He
closed the gap by *explaining his own document out loud* at [23:24]–[26:31]. **The suspicion was
resolved by demonstrating that the spoken and written material come from the same head.**

## 4. WORKING MEMORY — WHAT THE TAPE ACTUALLY SHOWS

⚠ *Descriptive only. This is about utterance structure, not about him.*

Look at [13:27]–[14:09]. In one breath-group he has in play:

1. building-access records
2. computer login records
3. an annexed attachment showing a complaint went unanswered
4. the proposition to be proved (*was she there or not*)
5. the abstraction that unifies them (*"it all works together as one big system"*)

**Five items — three evidentiary, one propositional, one organising — held simultaneously.** And
this is what the syntax does with them:

> *"access to the hospital itself would tend to prove **or log into the computer**, if she was there
> or not"* — ⚠ two sources compressed into one clause without repair
> *"The data of her coming in and going, **and then** the data of her logging into the computer,
> as such, would show that people would…"* — ✅ **restarted and properly coordinated**, then broken
> off
> *"I think I attached one attachment and it showed that she didn't reply to a complaint"* — jumps
> to the third item

⇒ ⭐⭐ **Nothing is lost. Everything he was holding is emitted.** The failure is at **serialisation,
not retrieval** — a parallel structure (several items → one conclusion) being forced through a
linear channel. He even **repairs in flight without stopping** (*"it wasn't so much I didn't really
matter what's inside"*), which is a high-cost operation.

**The honest description: he carries a large parallel load and the bottleneck is output, not
storage.** That is consistent with everything else on the tape — he could define his own table's
column headings on demand forty minutes after handing it up, and he retained the whole disclosure
schedule well enough to route the Commissioner through it.

⚠ **And it is consistent with the failures too.** *"Not that I can recall, no"* is a **retrieval-
across-a-large-corpus** question, asked cold, with no file. That is a different operation from
holding a structure he built himself, and it is the one that failed.

## 5. ⭐ THE PRACTICAL FIX — THREE RULES FOR THE BOX

The whole problem is **ordering**, and ordering is trainable.

### Rule 1 — INVERT. Operative clause first, frame last (or not at all).
| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| *"For me, it wasn't so much what's inside those complaints, it's that they happened to me"* | **"The complaints matter because they happened. Their contents don't matter."** |
| *"So I guess over public holidays and things, my manager decided to roster me on different days or it ends up being 42%…"* | **"I was rostered off the public holidays. My colleagues were rostered on. I earned 42% less that fortnight."** |
| *"I was restricted in my—"* (cut off) | **"No. My work email had been restricted. I couldn't check."** |

### Rule 2 — COORDINATE, DON'T SUBORDINATE. Short flat sentences joined by full stops.
Never begin an answer with *when*, *if*, *because* or *so that*. **Your successful utterances are
all flat.** *"You put a word in Outlook. It filters the emails. I only wanted the count."*

### Rule 3 — ONE ITEM PER SENTENCE. Number them aloud if there are several.
When you are holding three sources, **say there are three, then give them one at a time.**
> *"Two records prove it. One: building access. Two: her computer logins."*

That converts the parallel structure into a serial one **before** you speak it, instead of during.

⛔ **And the habit to break, which is the same one from the biggest tell: answer, full stop, then
the reason as a separate sentence.** Never insert the reason before the answer arrives.

# PART TWO — WHAT THE SHAPE SHOWS ABOUT HOW HE REASONS

⚠ *Inference from utterance structure in a single machine transcript. Not a claim about anyone's

## 7. ⭐⭐ THE FIVE-PART SHAPE IS NOT A SPEECH HABIT. IT IS A REASONING PROCEDURE SPOKEN ALOUD

Read the five parts again as *operations* rather than as grammar:

| Part | What it is doing |
|---|---|
| 1 · *"For me,"* | **Orient** — locate himself relative to the proposition before asserting it |
| 2 · *"it wasn't so much X"* | ⭐ **Eliminate the wrong reading** — model the listener's likely misconstruction and kill it first |
| 3 · *"It's that they happened to me"* | **Assert** |
| 4 · *"so that was my point"* | **Verify** — audit whether the output matched the intention, and label it |
| 5 · *"but I wouldn't even need to read them"* | **Concede the surplus** — test the boundary of his own claim and give back what he does not need |

⇒ **orient → eliminate → assert → verify → concede.** That is a stress-test of a proposition, run in
real time, out loud, under pressure. **It is rigorous.** It is simply being performed in a forum
that only listens to step 3.

⭐ **Step 2 is the expensive one.** To eliminate the wrong reading first, he has to hold *his own
meaning* and *the listener's probable misreading* simultaneously, and answer the second before
stating the first. That is why the operative clause ends up in the middle: **it is queued behind an
objection nobody has yet made.**

## 8. HE THINKS IN STRUCTURES, NOT ITEMS

*"For me, it all works together as one big system"* is the most literal description of his own
method on the tape. In the [13:27] passage he holds three evidentiary sources, one proposition and
one unifying abstraction — and the abstraction comes out *as a sentence*, unprompted.

⭐ **The proof is the table.** Forty minutes after handing it up he could define its column
headings, its status entries and its sub-items on demand. **You can only do that if you are holding
the schema, not the entries.**

⇒ **He stores structures with facts hanging off them, not facts.**

## 9. ⭐⭐⭐ HE REASONS BACKWARD FROM CONSEQUENCE — AND THE PLEADING RUNS THE OTHER WAY

Watch the direction of travel at [29:18]. He does **not** begin at *"she did not consult us."* He
begins at the mechanism and runs to the outcome:

> *"this number is an **emergency contact**"* → *"those doctors, the ones that get called for an
> emergency"* → *"they're sent to the **wrong side of the room**"* → *"not sent to someone having a
> **cardiac arrest**"* → *"not sent to someone in **respiratory distress**"*

**Control removed → directory wrong → call misrouted → wrong clinician → patient.** That is a
causal chain traced from cause to effect, and it is delivered in a single unbroken run — the most
fluent thirty seconds of the hour.

⛔⛔ **And this is why Stressor 1(a) keeps failing him.** It is pleaded as *"erratic presence and
unassessed unilateral directives without consultation"* — **the first link in the chain.** His
reasoning lives in the **last** link. He has been made to argue from a premise that is not where he
understands the case to be, against a Commissioner who then said the first link was *"totally
legitimate."*

⇒ ⭐⭐ **The fix is not better advocacy. It is pleading and leading the chain in the direction he
actually reasons — consequence first, mechanism second, consultation last or not at all.** That is
the same conclusion Part Five reached from the law. **It is now reached independently from the way
he thinks, which is a good sign it is right.**

## 10. HE TRIMS HIS OWN CLAIM AUTOMATICALLY

*"I basically just want the count. I don't even… For me, it doesn't matter."* · *"I don't know if I
can prove it as much."* · *"I don't have knowledge of that."* · *"Not those specific things, no."*

⇒ **Most litigants inflate. He deflates, continuously and without being asked.** Step 5 of the
procedure is doing this: every assertion is immediately tested at its boundary and the surplus
handed back.

⭐ **This is why he cannot be broken on exaggeration** — there is none to find — and why a
cross-examiner's only route is patient accumulation of small concessions. It is a strength that
creates one specific vulnerability, and both come from the same operation.

## 11. ⭐⭐⭐ HIS MEMORY IS STRUCTURAL, NOT INDEXICAL — AND THIS IS THE OPERATIVE FINDING

Set the two facts side by side:

| ✅ He could | ⛔ He could not |
|---|---|
| define his own table's headings, status column and sub-items on demand, 40 minutes later | say whether the Regulator's disclosure contained the directive emails |
| route the Commissioner through a 20-item schedule by number | say whether he held Ms Reese's retraction email |

**Both concern the same underlying documents.** The difference is that **he built one and only
received the other.**

⇒ **He can recall anything he has organised. He cannot recall anything he has merely been given.**

⭐⭐ **The practical rule follows immediately, and it is the most useful sentence in this document:**

> **Nothing counts as "known" until it has passed through his own organising step.** Reading the
> disclosure is not enough. **Indexing it is.**

**And this is exactly what the Form 24 did.** Three hundred and eight facts, each dated, quoted and
tab-numbered, built by him — which is why he now knows the disclosure cold and could not answer a
question about it three weeks ago. **The instrument that repaired the failure was also the cure for
its cause.**

## 12. THE WRITTEN/SPOKEN GAP IS ONE MIND WITH AND WITHOUT A BUFFER

Written: subordinated, nested, qualified. Spoken: flat, coordinate, restarted.

⇒ **Writing gives him a serialisation buffer.** He can hold the parallel structure and lay it out
across time, revising the order. Speech denies him that and forces the parallel structure through a
linear channel in one pass — which is where the abandoned subordinate clauses and the connective
slips come from.

⭐ **So the gap Dwyer heard was not two registers and not two authors.** It was the same reasoning
with and without time to serialise. **The writing looks "above what you'd anticipate" because it is
his thinking with the bottleneck removed.**

## 16. ⭐⭐⭐ DWYER'S METHOD: HE STATES HIS UNDERSTANDING AND INVITES CORRECTION

> *"Do you mean by that, **and tell me if I've got this wrong**…"* [16:44]
> *"**Am I right about that? Or have I read that wrong?**"* [46:05]

⇒ **He does not assume. He formulates and offers it back.** And the [51:19]–[52:26] sequence proves

method is to restate and invite correction (§16) — *"Is that what you're saying?"* He is reconstructing
the frame the speech did not supply.

the instance, because it is the half that carries the content.
⇒ And it is precisely what Dwyer complained of: *"my brain doesn't process it… I miss the point."*
**His objection was density, not volume.**

words, 38% shorter than the bench's.** Read as "say more, shorter things", Rule 2 makes the problem worse.

already passed the point at which he is building the structure for you.** Stop. Let him ask.
