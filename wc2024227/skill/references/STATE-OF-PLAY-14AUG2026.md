## PART 2 — ⭐⭐ WHAT THE WITHDRAWAL ACTUALLY DID
Paragraph 3 of the withdrawal letter is the operative sentence:
> *"Should further documents prove necessary for the fair conduct of the appeal, the Appellant will
> seek them **from the Respondent, through its Appeals Officer**, by way of the ordinary processes
> of disclosure between the parties."*

**He closed the compulsory third-party route and redirected the whole pipe through Matheson — by
his own election, in writing, to the Registry.**

| ⭐ What that bought | ⛔ What it cost |
|---|---|
| Removed a non-party fight with a health service — delay, cost, an unpredictable record — which is worth **more to the Regulator than to him** | **There is now no compulsory route to MSH documents.** The Form 29 is gone |
| Concentrated everything on **one officer**, so pressure becomes legible and cumulative | A fresh notice would have to be built from scratch **and explained to Dwyer**, having withdrawn the last one |
| Reads to the Commission as proportionate and cooperative — restraint as posture | The concession was **unilateral and unpriced**; nothing was sought in return |

⚠ **This is the real cost of the week and it must not be glossed.** The upside is procedural
goodwill and a single channel. The downside is that the channel is now voluntary at the other end.
