## How the reader is judging it

- **The bracket is the trust signal.** By 1(c) the reader has learned that [¶ n] means admitted.
  He stops checking truth and reads for meaning.
- **The heat is theirs:** "burn book", "I did raise my voice", "did not demonstrate our iCARE2
  value", "Cory is not yet aware of this", "can not help patients", "I am sorry I haven't been
  there". Where he appears heated, it is mostly someone else describing him. In his own words it
  happens once, in (k): "I have had enough of it". The next line is the manager apologising and
  confirming a rostering error.
- **The disclaimers lower resistance.** People resist being pushed (reactance; Brehm 1966). Every
  "does not allege" removes the push, so the reader draws the inference himself, and
  self-generated conclusions are held more firmly.
- **Two transport points:** the console and the night of 17–18 March. Elsewhere the reader is
  checking; in those two places he is inside the story (story model; Pennington & Hastie).
- **Each stressor closes on the other side's words:** the CE's letter, their reviewer's "blemish",
  and their reviewer's "unreasonable management action".

## ⭐ C 5(vi): the clause point, verified against ATT03 (HHS General Employees Award 2015)

- **Cl 15.2(c)** (PDF p 31): "A shift worker shall not perform more than two consecutive shifts (e.g.
  day shift/afternoon shift; afternoon shift/night shift)." The examples are back-to-back doubles.
  A late shift ending 23:00 and an early shift starting 06:00, seven hours apart, are two shifts,
  not "more than two", and the pair is not of the kind the examples give.
- **Cl 15.2(d):** changes *within* a roster. The 17–18 March shifts are not pleaded as a change
  within the roster.
- **Cl 15.1(d)** (PDF p 30): "Employees must be allowed a break of not less than 10 hours between the
  termination of one shift and the commencement of another shift, except that 8 hours applies
  instead of the 10 hours" (i) on rotation between shifts, or (ii) by agreement. **Eight hours is
  the lowest floor the Award allows in any circumstance. Seven is below it on every reading.**
- This is admitted at ¶ 285. The reviewer found the rostering "in direct contradiction to the award
  and the 8-hour agreement" (¶ 260).
- C 5(vi) incorporates "the standards … pleaded at Stressor 3(f)" generally, but its dash names only
  15.2(c) and (d).
- **This is the Mahaffey instance**, the one unreasonable management action their own reviewer
  found. It should be anchored to the rule that fits on its own words: cl 15.1(d), ¶ 285 and ¶ 260.
  Under the interlock rule, a limb that fails on the rule's own words is deleted, not softened.
- ⚠ Cl 15.1(d)(i) is also the Regulator's likely route to "8 hours applied without any agreement".
  It does not help them, because 7 is less than 8.
