# The 9A, walked as a reader — 18 October 2026

Source walked: the repo build `drafts/out/FORM9A_SECOND_AMENDED_FOR_FILING.pdf` (14 pp). The 30-page
copy sent on 1 October is not in the container, so its pages 1–2 may differ from this build.
The structure and the rule departures are deliberate (Cory, 18 Oct). This file records how the
document reads. It does not propose restructuring it.

## The walk: what the reader meets, and what the reader thinks

1. **Part A.** It says "Not pleaded as arising from an event occurring on a single day" and that the
   date of injury "is the practitioner's". *Careful; not overclaiming.* The temperature is set low.
2. **B.1, 1.2–1.4.** The weak spots come first: shift-work sleep and temazepam (Nov 2023), and the
   2022 ADHD/anxiety history items. 1.3 is "pleaded as to date only; no connection … is alleged".
   Then the car park, and GP notes naming pay and roster before any claim. *He has shown me what
   the other side would show me.* Trust starts here.
3. **1.10.** Their reviewer found injury and significant contribution, and rejected the claim under
   s 32(5) only. *So the fight is narrow.* The scope is set.
4. **2.0 / 2.0.1.** The stakes come from their own admission (¶ 289, "critical function … patient
   safety"). Then the valve: no patient harm is alleged. *Safety-critical, but not a crusade.*
5. **Stressor 1(a)–(d).** The duty is kept. The means are removed "while I am fixing up the
   database", and that temporary arrangement lasts 336 days. The repair path is office hours only.
   Their "procedure in place" defence becomes "the procedure pleaded at (c) … its operation …
   pleaded at (g) and (h)". *Their defence has just become his evidence.*
6. **(f).** The consultation clauses. Later the employer ran a ballot (17 of 17), and on 20 May it
   wrote "put the updated procedures out to the team for consultation before implementing". The
   walk becomes a climb here (instrument text), but it ends on their words.
7. **(g)–(h), "What arrived at the console".** VHUB. Nine occasions. The first reply came 5 days
   18 hours later and asked for her business hours. The respiratory service "can not help
   patients". He finds the cause at 2:05 pm. The reply says "this task was being actioned", and no
   record of that exists. **Transport point 1:** the reader is in his chair. Facts placed apart
   for the reader to join: ¶ 83 (not in until about 11 am on 20 May) against ¶¶ 100/104 ("this
   morning"). *Limit:* the discussion could have been by phone or after 11 am.
8. **(i).** He asked for her hours and was told to retract. The hours were published two days
   later. On 18 June 2024, the certified date of injury, she wrote to the team: "I am sorry I
   haven't been there for you all over the past week". The main causative run (a)–(i) ends there.
   Nobody comments; the reader does.
9. **(j)–(q).** The reader takes in the relationship. In (n), "The sequence, stated neutrally"
   leads the reader to supply the comment himself. (p) is the CE's letter ("do not exist"; no
   consequential changes). (q) then sets (j), (k), (n) and (o) aside as context. That reads as
   restraint, and it comes after the picture has formed.
10. **Stressor 2.** "Not a claim that money is outstanding." The same mechanism appears again, and
    the reader recognises it, so it feels true. The line a decision-maker can lift: "A process is
    not shown to have been reasonable by the fact that it eventually produced the right result."
11. **Stressor 3(b)–(c).** "Cory's roster will not be considered, however Cory is not yet aware of
    this". Eleven days later she asks him which changes concerned him. This is the sharpest
    juxtaposition in the document. Then "the roster was equitable" against their own November 2024
    paper, "intended to introduce a more equitable roster".
12. **3(e)–(m).** About 16 hours awake and about 4 hours' sleep. Then the register: Code Grey 8:48,
    MET calls, neonatal MET calls. Sick leave the next day. 23 days without an answer. Their
    reviewer's "unreasonable management action". **Transport point 2.** This is the only passage
    resting on his own evidence, and it is tied to his "less than 5 hours" of 9 Aug 2024.
13. **B.4–B.5.** Arithmetic and absences, one page each, both built so that the reservation in the
    8 Sep letter cannot reach them. The case is in two pages, and that is where a reader comes back.
14. **Part C.** Concession first, point second. The onus is accepted and the earlier error
    withdrawn. C 4: "not the decision but the condition it left in place for eleven months". C 5:
    no improper purpose. C 9: de novo applies to the whole decision. C 10: para 27 identifies
    nothing. **Part D, Q3** hands the decision-maker the split between management action and
    conditions.

## How the reader is judging it

- **The bracket is the trust signal.** By 1(c) the reader has learned that [¶ n] means admitted.
  He stops checking truth and reads for meaning.
- **The heat is theirs:** "burn book", "I did raise my voice", "did not demonstrate our iCARE2
  value", "Cory is not yet aware of this", "can not help patients", "I am sorry I haven't been
  there". Where he appears heated, it is mostly someone else describing him. In his own words it
  happens once, in (k): "I have had enough of it". The next line is the manager apologising and
  confirming a rostering error.
- **The disclaimers lower resistance.** People resist being pushed (reactance; Brehm 1966). Every
  "does not allege" removes the push, so the reader draws the inference himself, and
  self-generated conclusions are held more firmly.
- **Two transport points:** the console and the night of 17–18 March. Elsewhere the reader is
  checking; in those two places he is inside the story (story model; Pennington & Hastie).
- **Each stressor closes on the other side's words:** the CE's letter, their reviewer's "blemish",
  and their reviewer's "unreasonable management action".

## Objective view

**Verdict.** A neutral reader finishes believing three things:
- the facts are not in dispute;
- the core is a condition left in place for eleven months, not a decision;
- the Regulator has not said what management action it relies on.

The question left open is not "did this happen?" but "what is their answer?". That is the question
Dwyer put to them on 13 October.

**Strengths, in order:**
1. One mechanism, carried into law at C 4.
2. The case is built on their words.
3. Every concession costs nothing.
4. C 4 does not need unreasonableness proved.
5. B.4/B.5 compress the case so it can be found.

**Where a careful reader slows down:**
1. ⭐ **C 5(vi) names the wrong clauses for its best fact.** This is the one substantive point (see
   below).
2. **Blanks.** The repo build has 45 "[¶ ___]". Each one reverses the trust signal, and they cluster
   exactly where the material is not admitted: 1(f), 1(n) and 3(e)–(h). If the filed copy carries
   them, replace each with its true source: the clause, the tab, or "Appellant's evidence".
3. **The seam between (q) and C 5(vii).** (q) says (n) is not relied on as an *independent* cause;
   C 5(vii) argues the complaint handling was not reasonable. It holds, because "independent" is
   the hinge and C 5(vii) answers their reviewer's own finding that the factor contributed.
   Counsel will still probe it. Answer in one line: *not relied on independently; your reviewer
   found it contributory; if it was, it was not reasonable, so it cannot carry the exclusion.*
4. **The medical sequence.**
   - In 1.6, "on the referral" reads back to 16 May, which produced no appointment (1.3); the
     referral meant is the one of 8 Sep (1.5).
   - 1.6 says fluoxetine was "increased", while 1.5 says the medication box was unticked. The start
     date is not in the repo.
   - 1.6 quotes the diagnosis as a fragment. The rest of the sentence, "obsessive preoccupation
     about work cover claim and perceived injustice", is at Tab M3, which is the Respondent's own
     item 9. The repo's medical schedule fences it ("the email records its state four months
     later"); the 9A text does not. That is the one place in B.1 where the weak spot is not shown
     first.
5. **Deliberate costs, named once.**
   - (q) comes after (j)–(o).
   - The instrument text at 1(f) and 3(f) slows the walk.
   - In (o), the 5.03 pm text has no date and the endorsement is from Nov 2025, so (o) is the one
     item a reader cannot place in the period.

## ⭐ C 5(vi): the clause point, verified against ATT03 (HHS General Employees Award 2015)

- **Cl 15.2(c)** (PDF p 31): "A shift worker shall not perform more than two consecutive shifts (e.g.
  day shift/afternoon shift; afternoon shift/night shift)." The examples are back-to-back doubles.
  A late shift ending 23:00 and an early shift starting 06:00, seven hours apart, are two shifts,
  not "more than two", and the pair is not of the kind the examples give.
- **Cl 15.2(d):** changes *within* a roster. The 17–18 March shifts are not pleaded as a change
  within the roster.
- **Cl 15.1(d)** (PDF p 30): "Employees must be allowed a break of not less than 10 hours between the
  termination of one shift and the commencement of another shift, except that 8 hours applies
  instead of the 10 hours" (i) on rotation between shifts, or (ii) by agreement. **Eight hours is
  the lowest floor the Award allows in any circumstance. Seven is below it on every reading.**
- This is admitted at ¶ 285. The reviewer found the rostering "in direct contradiction to the award
  and the 8-hour agreement" (¶ 260).
- C 5(vi) incorporates "the standards … pleaded at Stressor 3(f)" generally, but its dash names only
  15.2(c) and (d).
- **This is the Mahaffey instance**, the one unreasonable management action their own reviewer
  found. It should be anchored to the rule that fits on its own words: cl 15.1(d), ¶ 285 and ¶ 260.
  Under the interlock rule, a limb that fails on the rule's own words is deleted, not softened.
- ⚠ Cl 15.1(d)(i) is also the Regulator's likely route to "8 hours applied without any agreement".
  It does not help them, because 7 is less than 8.
