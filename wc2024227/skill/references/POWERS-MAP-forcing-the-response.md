# PART 1 — WHY THE ASK CHANGED SHAPE

**Until now every request to MSH was answerable by silence.** *"Please confirm I'm right to
attend"* — four weeks, nothing. *"Identify the delegate and instrument"* — nothing. *"Formal
request for review"* — ten months, nothing. The measured pattern is in
`RESPONSE-OBLIGATIONS-REGISTER.md` Part 1: **15 minutes on a leave question, four weeks on
attendance, ten months on a complaint about a Director.**

⇒ **The change is not the content of the ask. It is the vehicle.** The same proposal, made under
cl 10.3.2 instead of in a covering paragraph, converts silence from a wall into a deemed decision.
That is the whole point of the restructure of 1 August 2026.

| | Asked in correspondence | Asked under a power |
|---|---|---|
| Reply | discretionary | **cl 10.3.6 — "must", 21 days** |
| Reasons | none required | **cl 10.3.5–.6 — "only on reasonable grounds", reasons in writing** |
| Silence | costs MSH nothing | ⭐ **IR Act s 29 — deemed refusal** |
| Route | none | **QIRC ch 6 — s 261 notice, s 262 conciliation then arbitration** |
| Onus | on Cory, to keep asking | **on MSH** — cl 10.3.5, and G03 cl 2 |

## 3.3 The conflict of interest — front and centre in all three documents
It is no longer a paragraph in one letter. It now appears as a **live procedural question in each
document, phrased as a request, never as an allegation**:

| Document | Where | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| RFMI response | **Part 6, 6.1** | who the delegate is; the instrument; whether a conflict was identified and managed; the three-way separation |
| cl 10.3 request | **§4** | that the **decision** be made outside Corporate Services; the officer and delegation identified |
| Stage 1 notice | **§5** | that MSH **nominate** the officer to conduct Stage 1 — while confirming he will attend with his supervisor if it prefers |

⭐ **Why §5 of the Stage 1 notice is drafted that way.** cl 1.11.2(a) requires the immediate
supervisor to be informed. He informs her — so Stage 1 cannot later be said to have never started —
**and** puts the conflict on the record in the same document. MSH then either nominates someone
else (conflict conceded and managed) or insists on the supervisor (the insistence is now
documented). **There is no answer that costs him anything.**

⛔ **Never write "conflicted", "biased", or "unlawfully".** Code cl 1.2 does the work: a conflict is
not wrongdoing; failing to manage one is likely to be. Quote the policy, ask the question, stop.

### ⭐⭐ AND THE FIND SITTING NEXT TO IT — Award cl 7.2(d)
EB12 cl 1.12.1 applies **cl 7.2 of the HHS General Employees (Queensland Health) Award – State

     "The employer may appoint another person to investigate the grievance… **The appointed person
     shall be other than the employee's supervisor or manager.**"

⇒ **A mandatory exclusion, in the employer's own award, in the grievance track.** It does not apply
to a cl 1.11 dispute — but if this moves to a grievance under cl 1.12, **Taylor and Hughes are

Also in cl 7.2, and worth holding: **7.2(f)** — Stage 1 24 h / 7 days, Stage 2 7 days, **Stage 3

⇒ **The status quo clause appears twice, in two instruments.** It is not a drafting stray.

## 3.5 ⭐⭐ "WHAT IS THIS PROCESS CALLED?" — Stage 1 notice §2.6 and §4(a)
**Cory's question, added 1 August 2026. It is the shortest thing in the package and probably the
most effective.**

> *"Since 26 June 2026 I have been asked to provide medical information about myself, held out of my
> rostered shifts, had my accrued leave debited and then been recorded as absent without pay, and
> told that a decision will be made regarding my ability to perform my role.*
> ***I do not know what this process is called.***"

**The technical name for it is a request for particulars**, and the entitlement behind it is the
hearing rule of procedural fairness: **a person cannot answer a process they have not been told the
name of.** But it is deliberately not drafted in those words. It is drafted as a man asking what is
happening to him.

### Why it is stronger than any argument in the file
| | Arguing it | Asking it |
|---|---|---|
| What MSH must do | rebut a legal proposition | **name the process** |
| What they can say | "we disagree" | ⛔ nothing that helps them |
| Risk of tipping them off | ⚠️ high — invites them to regularise under PS Act ss 103–109 | none — **it asks only for the name they should already have given** |
| How it reads to a Commissioner | adversarial | **five weeks without pay, and he still hasn't been told what this is** |

### ⭐ It is the same fork as Part 8 of the RFMI response, from the other side
- Name **PS Act s 107** → s 103's entry test (disclaimed by their own 31 July letter) and s 104's
  chief-executive requirement bite, and the unpaid exclusion has no home in the division.
- Name **WHS Act s 19** → a duty has been cited as a power, and reg 36 has no removal step.
  See `WHS-s19-a-duty-not-a-power.md`.
- Name **a policy** → produce it, and it will have steps they have not followed.
- Name **nothing** → ⭐ *the process has no name*, said by them, in writing, at Stage 1.

⛔ **Do not add anything to §2.6.** Its force is that it is short, factual and unanswerable. Every

# PART 4 — ⭐⭐ THE ARCHITECTURE OF THE LEAVE POINT
> *"Without the leave they have nothing — it will show they could not help me in the workplace and
> sought adverse action while I was struggling medically."*
> ⛔ **This is the strategy. It never appears in a letter to MSH in these words.** It is what the
> documents *build*, silently, and what a decision-maker is left holding.

**The six steps, each anchored:**

1. **He was working.** Six shifts a fortnight, twelve months, a pattern **MSH set**. The ECC records
   it as *"the pattern Mr Shepherd has in fact worked and tolerated over the past twelve months
   without deterioration"* and a *"continuation of existing arrangement"*. **No concern was ever
   raised that it was insufficient.**
2. **He absorbed the illness himself.** Every exacerbation was paid for out of his own sick and
   annual leave. **No adjustment was sought and none was offered.** ⇒ *For twelve months MSH's
   psychosocial control measure was his leave balance.*
3. **Then it stopped him working.** 26 June / 2 July. **It did not adjust. It excluded.** Reg 36
   sets the hierarchy of control in order and **contains no step for removing the worker.**
4. **It funded the exclusion out of his entitlements.** Accrued leave debited from 3 July; from
   13 July, unpaid.
5. ⭐ **When the leave ran out, the mechanism became visible.** While there was leave to debit, the
   exclusion looked administrative. With no leave left, what remains is an employee with a known
   illness, **willing and available in writing**, held out of the workplace without pay and without
   an identified instrument, for five weeks and counting.
6. ⭐⭐ **That is the shape of the discrimination case, and it is built entirely from their own
   documents.** **AD Act s 11** — a term with which a person with the impairment **is less able to
   comply** (produce medical clearance to their satisfaction, on their timetable, before working),
   **which is not reasonable**; **s 124(3)** puts the onus of reasonableness on **them**.
   **G03 cl 1.1** says the adjustment is what allows work to continue **during** recovery — so a
   position that adjustments follow recovery inverts the policy.

⚠️ **Two disciplines on this:**
- ⛔ **It is never argued to MSH.** Every document asks for the adjustment and asks them to identify
  their grounds. **The refusal is the evidence.** Arguing it now invites them to manufacture better
  grounds while there is still time.
- ⏳ **AD Act s 138 — one-year limitation.** Runs from the act complained of. **Raise the date with
  Petering before ~8 September 2026.** ⚠️ Do not let the cl 10.3 and cl 1.11 clocks consume it.
