# ⭐ DELEGATE STATUS — what it changes (verified 4 Aug 2026)
> Cory confirmed 4 Aug that he is an OFFICIALLY appointed union delegate (Together Qld).
> This was not previously in the file. It changes the protective picture, the consultation
> standing, and the way the whole matter looks. Verified against EB12 and the IR Act 2016.

## 1. THE AGREEMENT EXPRESSLY RECOGNISES THE ROLE — EB12 cl 3.1.5 (verbatim)
"The employer acknowledges the constructive role democratically elected union delegates
undertake in the workplace in relation to union activities that support and assist members.
**That role is to be formally recognised, accepted and supported to participate effectively
in consultative structures, perform a representative role, and further the effective
operation of grievance and dispute settlement procedures.**"
Also: cl 3.1.6 (employees given full access to delegates during working hours to discuss any
employment or industrial matter); cl 3.1.8 (convenient access to facilities — telephone,
computer, email, meeting rooms, notice boards — and "management will respect the privacy of
delegates during the use of such facilities"); cl 3.1.4 → HR Policy F4 Union Encouragement
(Schedule 2); cl 3.6.4 (delegate may request resignation reports from local HR to monitor
timeframes — a data right relevant to the turnover limb).

## 2. IR ACT PROTECTION — verified ss 290, 291
s 290 "engages in industrial activity" includes: being an **officer or member** of an
industrial organisation; **organising or promoting a lawful activity for or on behalf of**
the organisation; **participating in** such activity; **representing or advancing the views,
claims or interests** of the organisation; and **giving or distributing information, or
organising or encouraging discussion, about wages, employment conditions and WORKPLACE
RIGHTS** for or on behalf of the organisation.
s 291 "Protection": "A person must not take adverse action against another person because
the other person — (a) is or is not... an officer or member of an industrial organisation;
(b) engages, or has at any time engaged or proposed to engage, in industrial activity..."
**Note in the Act: this is a civil penalty provision.** Reverse onus applies (s 306).

## 3. WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGES
1. **A fifth protected attribute.** On top of workplace rights (IR), WHS s 106(h)/(j),
   PID s 40, and AD Act impairment — he now also holds **industrial activity/delegate**
   protection. Any adverse act must survive all five.
2. **His consultation standing rises.** He is not merely an affected worker asking to be
   consulted under ss 47-49 — the agreement says the delegate role is to be "formally
   recognised, accepted and supported to participate effectively in **consultative
   structures**" and to "further the effective operation of **grievance and dispute
   settlement procedures**." That is precisely what he has been doing. Excluding, silencing
   or ignoring a delegate in a consultation and dispute process runs against cl 3.1.5 itself.
3. **The picture changes.** "Employee with a complaint" becomes "the elected delegate for
   the room, excluded from the workplace and unpaid, while raising WHS and industrial
   matters on behalf of members." That is the archetypal general-protections shape.
4. **Union interest rises.** Unions run delegate matters they would not run for a member;
   delegate adverse-action is an organisational issue, not an individual one. ⭐ ACTION:
   tell Petering/Moran expressly that the exclusion has occurred while he holds delegate
   office, and ask whether the union wishes to take carriage of that limb.
5. **Facilities/access limb.** cl 3.1.6/3.1.8 — exclusion from the workplace also cuts
   members off from their delegate and cuts the delegate off from the facilities the
   agreement guarantees. Not a headline claim; a real one, and it belongs to the MEMBERS
   as much as to him.

## 4. DISCIPLINE
- Delegate status is a SHIELD and a standing, never a licence. It confers no power to
  inspect, compel or direct (that is the HSR role — see DELEGATE-VS-HSR-powers.md).
- Do not deploy it as a threat or as "you can't touch me". State it once, factually, where
  it is relevant: in the Stage 2 referral and in any union correspondence.
- ⚠ VERIFY before it enters any filing: date of appointment/election, the work area covered,
  and whether MSH has been notified of the appointment in writing (knowledge matters for
  any s 291 claim). If MSH was never told, obtain the union's confirmation of the date and
  provide it prospectively — knowledge from that point forward is then undeniable.
