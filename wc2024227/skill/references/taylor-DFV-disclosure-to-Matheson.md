## 3. ⭐⭐ WHY THIS IS THE MOST VALUABLE DOCUMENT IN THE 2025 DISCLOSURE

### 3.1 It destroys the "perpetrator" characterisation — from MSH's own hand
Hughes, **28 March 2025**: *"you are the respondent in relation to an order against you from your
ex-partner… **under our policies, you may be deemed as the perpetrator**."*

The January 2024 record — held by MSH the whole time, and **volunteered to the Regulator by the very
manager who received it** — shows Cory:
- **attending a police station "directed by police"**
- **offering the QPS report number** in support of the leave request
- **naming the correct leave type unprompted**: "10 days Family and domestic violence leave (special
  leave)"

**That is the conduct of a person seeking protection, not a respondent avoiding one.** It is
**fourteen months earlier** than the perpetrator line, it is contemporaneous, and **MSH cannot
disown it** — it is in the Regulator's file because MSH's manager put it there.

### 3.2 It establishes TAYLOR'S KNOWLEDGE from 14 January 2024
She was told in terms, she replied *"I'm sorry to hear about your **family situation**"*, and she
reported it up to **Tammy Reese** the same morning. **Five months before the injury date of 18 June
2024**, and throughout the entire stressor period.

⇒ This is the knowledge element for **IR Act 2016 s 296** (adverse action because of domestic
violence) — see `personal-liability-hughes-taylor.md` §1.8. **s 296(2)** expressly removes any
requirement that he be named as the aggrieved under an order or application.

## 4. ⚠️ STRESS TEST — read this before deploying

**Taylor's conduct here is largely defensible. Do not overreach.**

1. **Matheson's request was legitimate.** The 14 Jan 2024 event is **paragraph 9 of Cory's own
   SOFAC** — he put it in issue. She was entitled to ask about it.
2. **Supplying the whole thread is defensible.** The text saying he would be late is *in* those
   messages. Redacting around it would have been selective. **"She leaked his DV" is not a
   sustainable characterisation and must not be advanced.**
3. **Taylor's texts read as measured, not callous.** "I hope everything is ok"; "I'm sorry to hear
   about your family situation"; she explains the two-on-the-board requirement and the night-shift
   impact. A Commissioner will read that as a manager handling a roster problem reasonably.
   **The value of this document is knowledge and the leave substitution — not her tone.**
4. **The privacy point is weak — do not run it.** QPP 6.1 governs disclosure for a secondary
   purpose, but the content is probably **not "sensitive information"** under IP Act sch 5 (which
   captures *criminal record* and *health information*, neither of which this plainly is), and
   **QPP 6.2(b)** — "required or authorised under an Australian law" — is available given the
   Regulator's role in the appeal. **Flagged and rejected.**
5. **The A/L-or-S/L offer was framed helpfully**, not as a refusal. The point is that the DFV
   entitlement was never put on the table — not that she said no to it.

⇒ **Deploy this as evidence, not as a complaint about Taylor.** Its power is that it is
contemporaneous, unchallengeable, and directly contradicts Hughes's March 2025 characterisation.
