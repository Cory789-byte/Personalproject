## Findings, most serious first

### High — legal risk or a standing discipline

**H1. Carr was set aside on appeal.** Part C ¶7 relies on "Langerak … as applied in *Carr v WCR* [2022] QIRC 59 at [60]".
- *Workers' Compensation Regulator v Carr* [2023] ICQ 1 (Merrell DP, 10 January 2023) set Carr aside, restored the review decision, and ordered Ms Carr to pay the Regulator's costs below. The grounds:
  1. the Commission decided on a contention Ms Carr never pleaded (Langerak [72]–[75]);
  2. no evidence linked the workload stressor to the injury;
  3. the reasons did not show how unreasonable management action was weighed against the reasonable (Mahaffey [57] quoted at [85]).
- **Fix:** cite Langerak [2020] ICQ 2 at [86] directly. Drop Carr, or add "(set aside on other grounds: [2023] ICQ 1)".
- The My Work Rights draft also opens with Carr. The firm did act in it, but the result was reversed.

**H2. 1(i) sets out PID content and misdescribes the admission.**
- 1(i) reads "a corrupt conduct complaint regarding clinical risks … The Ethical Standards Unit formally determined this constituted a Public Interest Disclosure (Admitted Fact: Form 24, para 20 …)".
- This breaches three disciplines:
  - "corrupt" is in the never-in-WC-track list;
  - PID content is never set out;
  - "clinical risks" hints at patient harm.
- It also contradicts itself: the next sentence says "The content is not set out".
- The February notice para 20 that was admitted describes the complaint as "regarding Ms. Taylor and Ms. Reese", not clinical risks.
- **Fix:** "13 May 2024: the Appellant made a complaint to the Ethical Standards Unit (Annexure A, Tabs 26–27, paragraph 20, admitted 18 February 2026). Its content is not set out; chronology only."
- SOFC ¶16(a) and Reese's outline raise the PID themselves, so a neutral chronological line is responsive.

**H3. B.3.1(b) names TD/2024/110.** This breaches "TD/2024/110 stays out of WC filings".
- **Fix:** "On 8 October 2024 the employer ended his employment on the basis of abandonment of employment, nominating 20 September 2024 as the separation date; he was reinstated to his employment with an effective date of 20 September 2024, the reinstatement taking effect despite any payroll documentation."
- The abandonment letter is signed 8/10/2024, so the 9A's date is right. Elsewhere "9 October" is the date of receipt.

**H4. Part D question 6 puts s 32(5)(b) up for determination.**
- Correction to the repo's assumption: the SOFC does mention (b), at ¶6(d)(ii), in its list of the elements the appellant must prove. Its only contention, ¶27, is general.
- Part C 5B's short defensive answer is responsive. A separate question for determination elevates it.
- **Fix:** delete Part D Q6.

**H5. "Patient safety" is stretched beyond the admission.**
- Fact 289 admits: "Maintaining accurate contact details for medical staff is a critical function of the Switchboard to ensure effective clinical handover and patient safety."
- The 9A extends it four times:
  - "a continuous 24/7 patient-safety function" (summary);
  - "The function is admitted to be critical to patient safety" (Stressor 2);
  - "a function the Respondent admits is critical to patient safety" (3(b));
  - "a function admitted to be critical to patient safety" (Part C ¶4).
- It also traces individual patients: "a Code Blue to the same patient", the 19 March "same patient", and Ward 3DR bed 11 across two days.
- **Fix:**
  - keep the verbatim admission once, in Stressor 1;
  - delete the four paraphrases;
  - reduce the code paragraphs to counts and times;
  - delete the 19 March codes paragraph (he was on sick leave that day);
  - delete the Code Blue, MET and AS 4083 definitions;
  - drop the Coroner's findings from Schedule Part 2 (listed but never used).
