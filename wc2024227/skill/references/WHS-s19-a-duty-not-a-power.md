# PART 2 — ⭐⭐ WHAT s 19 ACTUALLY SAYS

## 2.1 Subsection (1) — the duty runs *while he is at work*
> **s 19(1)** *"A person conducting a business or undertaking must ensure, so far as is reasonably
> practicable, the health and safety of— (a) workers engaged, or caused to be engaged by the person;
> and (b) workers whose activities in carrying out work are influenced or directed by the person;*
> ***while the workers are at work in the business or undertaking.***"

⇒ **The duty is owed TO the worker, and it operates WHILE HE IS AT WORK.** Removing him from the
workplace does not discharge the duty. **It removes the occasion for it.**

## 2.2 Subsection (3) — every single limb is about the employer's own arrangements
> **s 19(3)** *"…a person conducting a business or undertaking must ensure, so far as is reasonably
> practicable—*
> *(a) **the provision and maintenance of a work environment** without risks to health and safety;*
> *(b) **the provision and maintenance of safe plant and structures**;*
> *(c) **the provision and maintenance of safe systems of work**;*
> *(d) the safe use, handling and storage of plant, structures and substances;*
> *(e) **the provision of adequate facilities for the welfare at work of workers**…;*
> *(f) **the provision of any information, training, instruction or supervision** that is necessary…;*
> *(g) that **the health of workers and the conditions at the workplace are monitored** for the
> purpose of preventing illness or injury of workers **arising from the conduct of the business or
> undertaking**."*

⭐ **Seven limbs. Every one begins with what the employer must PROVIDE, MAINTAIN or MONITOR.**
**Work environment. Plant. Systems of work. Facilities. Information, training, instruction,
supervision. Conditions at the workplace.**

⇒ **Not one of them is a thing the worker must produce, prove, or satisfy.** Section 19 contains
**no power to exclude a worker, no power to suspend, and no requirement that a worker demonstrate
fitness.**

## 2.3 ⭐⭐⭐ THE INVERSION, STATED PLAINLY
**Section 19 is a provision that protects him.** MSH has cited it as the reason to hold him out of
the workplace and stop his pay.

⇒ **A duty owed to the worker has been used as authority against the worker.** That is not a
characterisation — it is what the words of the section do and do not say.

⇒ And it sits directly alongside the reg 36 point already in the file: **the hierarchy of control
at reg 36 sets out, in order, how a duty under s 19 is discharged — and it contains no step for
removing the worker.** The duty points at the workplace; the method points at the workplace.
**Only MSH's application points at him.**

# PART 3 — ⚠️ THE HONEST LIMIT, AND WHY IT MAKES THE POINT STRONGER

⚠️ **Do not say "MSH had no power to direct him not to attend."** That overstates it, and a
Commissioner would correct it.

An employer **can** give lawful and reasonable directions, and in some circumstances a direction not
to attend pending medical clearance will be lawful and reasonable. **But that power does not come
from s 19.** It comes from the contract of employment and the common law right to give lawful and
reasonable directions — and, in the Queensland public sector, from a specific statutory scheme.

⭐ **The correct proposition is narrower and much harder to answer:**

> **Section 19 imposes a duty; it does not confer a power. If a power to hold him out of the
> workplace exists, it lies somewhere else — and in five weeks the Health Service has not identified
> it, although he has asked.**

⇒ **That is exactly the question already in the documents.** It does not need to be argued. It needs
to be asked, and then answered by them.

# PART 4 — ⭐⭐⭐ AND THE INSTRUMENT THAT *DOES* FIT DOESN'T HELP THEM
**Public Sector Act 2022, ch 3 pt 8 div 5 — "Mental or physical incapacity", ss 103–109.**
This is the statutory scheme in Queensland for exactly what MSH says it is doing. Read against it,
the RFMI is not a s 19 measure at all — it is a s 104 measure taken outside s 104.

| Provision | What it requires | Where MSH stands |
|---|---|---|
| **s 103** — division applies only if **(a)** the employee is absent from duty **or** the CE is reasonably satisfied duties are not being performed satisfactorily; **and (b)** the CE **reasonably suspects** that is caused by illness or disability | a stated entry test | ⛔ **Their own letter of 31 July says the Health Service is not aware of any performance or conduct concerns raised for appropriate management.** And "absent from duty" is circular — **he is absent because they excluded him** |
| **s 104** — **the employee's chief executive** may appoint a doctor to examine the employee and require the employee to submit | **the CE**, an **appointed** doctor, an **examination** | ⛔ A Director, Corporate Services, sent **nine questions to the employee's own GP**. No appointment, no examination, no CE |
| **s 105** — sick leave withheld **only** where the employee **fails to comply with a s 104(b) requirement** | the sole statutory basis for withholding leave in this division | ⛔ **He complied with everything asked.** ECC provided 3 July, 2:32pm — 28 minutes before the 2:00pm-Friday deadline had even fully run its course in their own terms |
| **s 106(1)–(2)** — the report must state whether there is an illness that **may adversely affect performance**, its likely effect, and its likely duration | defined scope | ⚠️ The RFMI's nine questions go well beyond it — including causation, which is in issue in the appeal |
| **s 106(3)** — the CE **must give the employee a copy** of the report | disclosure to him, as of right | ⏳ nothing said about disclosure |
| **s 107** — action following the report: **transfer or redeploy**; if not reasonably practicable, **retire** | the exhaustive list of outcomes | ⛔⛔ **There is no step in s 107 for "hold the employee out of the workplace, unpaid, indefinitely."** It does not exist in the scheme |
| **s 109** — the Commissioner's directive on this division **must** be complied with | mandatory | ⏳ no directive identified |

## ⭐⭐ THE FINDING
**The Act contemplates: a stated entry test, the chief executive as decision-maker, an appointed
examiner, a scoped report, disclosure to the employee, and two outcomes — transfer/redeploy, or
retire.**

**MSH has produced: no entry test (disclaimed in its own letter), a Director as decision-maker, the
employee's own GP as examiner, nine unscoped questions, no disclosure position, and an outcome that
appears nowhere in the division — five weeks of unpaid exclusion.**

⇒ **This is why "no instrument has been identified in five weeks" is not an oversight.** ⭐ **The
instrument that fits the conduct is the instrument that most clearly does not authorise it.** Citing
s 19 instead is citing a duty in place of a power.

⚠️ ⛔ **AND THIS IS WHY IT IS NOT ARGUED IN THE LETTERS.** Put ss 103–109 to them now and they can
regularise: refer it to the Chief Executive, appoint an examiner, and cure the defect prospectively.
**Ask the question instead and let them answer it.** RFMI response **Part 8** already asks precisely:
*"what decision is meant by 'a decision regarding your ability to perform your role', and the
instrument under which it would be made"* — and **Part 6** asks for the delegate and the source of
the delegation.

⭐ **That question is a fork with no good exit:**
- Answer **"PS Act s 107"** → s 103's entry test and s 104's chief-executive requirement bite, and
  the exclusion still has no home in the division.
- Answer **"WHS Act s 19"** → a duty has been cited as a power, and reg 36 has no removal step.
- Answer **nothing** → five weeks becomes six, against cl 1.11.5 and the measured response pattern.

# PART 5 — ⭐ THE HEALTH-MONITORING PINCER
MSH's best available answer on s 19 is **s 19(3)(g)** — *"that the health of workers and the
conditions at the workplace are monitored"*. It is the one limb that mentions monitoring health, and
it is the natural home for a Request for Medical Information.

**Both roads out of it close:**

**Road 1 — "the RFMI is health monitoring under s 19(3)(g)."**
Then **s 49(e)(iii)** applies: consultation *"is required"* *"when making decisions about the
procedures for— … **(iii) monitoring the health of workers**"*. ⛔ **There was none.** And s 48
requires that he be given a reasonable opportunity to contribute to the decision-making process and
that he be advised of the outcome.
⭐ **This is already in the RFMI response, at Part 7**, in exactly these terms: *"Section 49 requires
consultation … when making decisions about the procedures for monitoring the health of workers. The
present request is a procedure for monitoring my health."* **The trap is already set and already
served.**

**Road 2 — "the RFMI is not health monitoring."**
Then **s 19 does not support the request at all**, and the authority question at Part 8 is
unanswered.

⚠️ **A third feature of (g) worth noticing but never pressing:** the limb is directed at monitoring
*"for the purpose of preventing illness or injury of workers **arising from the conduct of the
business or undertaking**."* ⛔ **Do not go near this in the employment track.** It brushes against
causation, which is in issue in the appeal, and the discipline is that the two tracks stay apart.
**Noted here only so it is not walked into by accident.**

# PART 7 — THE ONE-LINE VERSION
**Section 19 tells the Health Service what it must do to its workplace, its plant, its systems of
work, its facilities, its information and its supervision. It says nothing about what a worker must
prove before he is allowed through the door. It is a duty owed to him, and it has been cited as
authority against him.**
