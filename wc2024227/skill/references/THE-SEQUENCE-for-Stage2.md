## PART B — THE FOUR PROPOSITIONS THE TABLE PROVES

**1. The exclusion originates in a duty used as a power.**
WHS s 19 is owed **to** the worker and operates **"while the workers are at work"**. Every
limb of s 19(3) concerns the employer's own arrangements. Removing the worker does not
discharge the duty; it removes the occasion for it. Cited **26 June, before anything was
challenged** — MSH's own first, unpressured characterisation of its authority.

**2. The authority degrades as the detriment escalates.**
Stated once (26 Jun) · dropped when the consequence became exclusion (2 Jul) · unnamed
legislation (7 Jul) · **nothing at all when the consequence became no wages** (15 Jul) ·
nothing (30 Jul) · returning only after the framework was challenged (29, 31 Jul).
A lawful direction behaves the opposite way.

**3. MSH's own condition was satisfied and the bar did not lift.**
26 June is expressly conditional — *"until we receive… a completed ECC"*. The ECC arrived
3 July certifying fitness with restrictions. A new condition appeared 7 July. That was
satisfied 3 August. The exclusion continued. **A genuine reason is self-terminating;
each of these was replaced rather than resolved.**

**4. No instrument has ever been identified.**
Not PS Act s 101 (suspension). Not Division 5 ss 103–109 (incapacity). No show-cause, no
discipline. Six weeks, four written requests, a formal dispute — and the question *under
what instrument am I excluded* has never been answered.
