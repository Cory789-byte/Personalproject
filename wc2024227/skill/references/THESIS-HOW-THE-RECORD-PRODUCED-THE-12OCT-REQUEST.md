# Thesis: how the record produced the Regulator's 12 October request to the Commission (18 Oct 2026)

Internal working theory. ⛔ Never in correspondence. State the chronology, never the motive, on paper. The
established items below are verified against the sources in `documents/`. The inferences are graded. Read with
`REGULATOR-12OCT2026-THE-STANDSTILL-READ.md`, `WHAT-THE-SERVED-OUTLINES-FORCE.md` and
`THESIS-WHY-THE-RESERVATION-18OCT2026.md`.

## The thesis

Every document served after 8 September closed one way out for the Regulator. By 12 October only one exit was left,
and that is the one it asked Commissioner Dwyer for:
- a conference **without orders**;
- on **counsel's timetable**;
- with a **private channel to the Appellant**.

That is the shape of a party arranging to concede on its own terms, not one preparing to defend.

## What the Regulator wrote (R29, 12 Oct 09:21, to the Registry, cc Cory)

- Agrees "with the Appellant's request for a second s552A conference".
- Items 2 to 7 "would be better placed to be discussed at the conference in lieu of ordered in directions, in the
  first instance".
- Counsel "on leave overseas and not contactable until 22 October 2026".
- Asks the Commission's "consent for its undertaking to speak with the Appellant … and jointly agree on a range of
  dates … before communicating with the registry".

**Sequence.** It was preceded by R28 at 08:30, which confirmed "the veracity" of the MSH copies of Tabs 1, 5,
17–19, 21, 22, 23 and 31. Dwyer replied on 13 Oct: he "notes" the email, gives one instruction (counsel's
availability "as soon as possible following 22 October 2026", and advise the Appellant), and makes no orders on
items 2–7.

## Which exit each document closed

### 1. Cory's outlines (9 Sep, direction 2): the credibility-contest exit
- **His own outline** opens: "The events at topics 2 to 7 are established by the facts admitted on 8 September
  2026; my evidence on them is what they were from the console and their effect on me." That leaves nothing to
  contradict about the events, only their effect on him.
- **Harrison-Jones** is an eyewitness to the 6 June 2023 Communication Book incident that the Regulator itself
  pleads. He will give "the sequence in which each of them spoke, and the manner and volume in which it was said".
- **Conaghan** is a serving MSH employee. She will say:
  - she was not consulted on the 15 April change and not balloted on the roster;
  - operators could not correct directory entries;
  - callers reported misdirected calls between 2 and 8 May.
- **All three** say he never spoke of anyone in hostile terms.
- **Effect:** only Taylor and Reese can answer them, so the Regulator's witness list was close to fixed before it was
  written.

### 2. The request (9 Sep): the authenticity exit
- It offered a choice: produce the MSH copy, or admit under r 49, by 18 Sep.
- It set a consequence: non-party disclosure to MSH, with "this correspondence" kept for costs.
- It invoked Dwyer's 7 Aug starting point (ask the Regulator first).
- **What followed:**
  - Matheson took an extension to 25 Sep;
  - on 24 Sep she confirmed four documents "upon further review of the appeal file", and for the rest she "requested
    copies … from MSH";
  - MSH's own files then matched his: Tab 1 has the same DocumentID; Tab 5 is the attachment to MSH's 6 Sep 2024
    WorkCover response; the register rows are identical.
- **Effect:** no ground was left. Veracity was confirmed on 12 Oct, 51 minutes before R29.

### 3. Their outlines (24 Sep, direction 4) and the expiry (30 Sep): the contest-the-events exit, closed from their own side
- **The SOFC's footprint.** Each bullet is tagged to the May SOFC ("SOFAC para …"). The outlines answer the April
  pleading, not the 298 facts admitted on 8 Sep.
- **Concessions in their own outlines:**
  - Taylor: "human error, and was not intentional or repeated". That sits against ¶220, "a few rostering errors made
    by Chloe with regards to Cory's line".
  - Wright: "a rostering practice issue for the line manager". This is not in the SOFC; it is new content that
    concedes the domain is management action.
  - Earl: the second decline "appears to have been made in error".
- **Silences:** no account of the database, the 15 April change, the MASPER calls, the respiratory entry, the fatigue
  interval, the 10 May email, or fatigue risk management.
- **The witness list.** Four lay witnesses. It "reserves its right to amend this list depending on the case presented
  by the appellant at the hearing".
- **30 Sep:** no expert report served.
- **Effect:** the Regulator's evidence was fixed, thin and conceding. A hearing means Taylor and Reese
  cross-examined against their own emails: adopt, disavow, or stay silent.

### 4. The proposed 9A (1 Oct): the reframing exit
It rebuilt the case on their admissions:
- onus conceded;
- the earlier "descriptions of conduct" withdrawn (letter);
- causal particulars listed in Appeal Guide 4.8 form;
- every stressor tied to the treating records;
- C 5A quotes their outlines back and lists what no outline addresses.

**Effect:** their served outlines now answer a pleading that would no longer exist. To meet the 9A they would have
to amend the SOFC and redo their outlines, which means committing to a new written case while counsel is away.
Opposing leave would mean opposing a narrower pleading built on concessions, which is hard for a model litigant in
front of a Commissioner against interlocutory disputes (7 Aug).

### 5. The election and requested directions (1 Oct, direction 5): the drift exit, and the pressure point
- **Timing.** It was made "the day after" the Respondent's directions expired. Their case was fixed before it was
  answered.
- **Items 2–7 would turn each open position into a dated written obligation:**
  - **item 2:** maintain ¶27 or particularise it;
  - **item 4:** maintain each authenticity dispute with a ground, or drop it;
  - **item 5:** for each causal particular, say whether it is management action and give the facts for
    reasonableness. This is the item their outlines cannot meet.
  - **item 6:** amend the SOFC within 14 days.
- **The letter set their notified case in front of Dwyer:**
  - ¶27 identifies no management action (fact 303);
  - no medical or expert witness;
  - the three concessions;
  - "no account" of 1(b), 1(f), 1(h) and 1(k).

## Competing readings
- **Ordinary case management.** Regulators often prefer to settle directions at a conference. This fits the
  wording, but not the new party-to-party step or the clearing of documents 51 minutes earlier.
- **Preparing to fight.** Not supported: no expert application, ¶27 unamended, no opposition to leave, and the
  outlines concede.

## Falsifiers
- An application to add an expert or witnesses.
- ¶27 particularised together with opposition to leave.
- Dates offered for December or later with no approach.
- Unconditional consent to leave before the conference.

## Predictions
- Counsel's availability soon after 22 Oct.
- Any approach before or at the conference, through the agreed party-to-party step.
- The terms sought: consent orders under s 558(1)(c), no reasons, "without admission", each party bearing its own
  costs, and the leave application not pursued.
- ¶27 is not particularised unless they choose to fight.

## Discipline
- Accept nothing that releases the common-law claim.
- Respond in writing only.
- No motive language on paper.
- At the conference, the four closed questions in `REGULATOR-RESERVATION-HOW-TO-PRESS-18OCT2026.md` §5.

## Addendum (Cory's point, 18 Oct): no time sought, and no challenge to the 9A

**What meeting the 9A would take, if the Regulator meant to defend.**
- **An amended SOFC.** The May SOFC answers the April pleading's paragraphs (¶11 Stressor 1(a), ¶12, ¶16, ¶20, ¶22,
  ¶24(b), ¶27). The proposed 9A re-letters every particular and marks which are causal.
- **New outlines.** All four are tagged "SOFAC para …". None addresses 1(b), 1(f), 1(h), 1(k) or 3(e)–(h).
- **New witnesses.** The events no outline covers would need witnesses, and leave, since directions 3 and 4 have
  expired. Their 24 Sep list "reserves its right to amend … depending on the case presented by the appellant", and
  the 9A is that case in writing. They have not used the reservation.
- **An expert.** None by 30 Sep, so that needs leave.
- **Answers to item 5** for 21 causal particulars.

**What they did** (ledger R23, R26, R29, R35).
- **1 Oct:** consent to leave was asked. The letter: "does not oppose the Respondent having time to amend". Election
  item 6 offered 14 days after filing.
- **7 Oct, 09:14 (R26):** "looking over both emails … will have a response … noting your requested timeframe of
  Friday 16 October".
- **12 Oct (R29):** items 2–7 went "to the conference in lieu of ordered". That includes item 3 (leave) and item 6
  (their own time to amend). There was:
  - no consent, no opposition and no reserved position;
  - no claim of prejudice;
  - no request for time, beyond counsel's availability for a date.
- **16 Oct (R35):** Tab 32 answered; leave left unanswered past the date they noted themselves.
- **Overall:** 17 days with the 9A in hand, and no objection to any paragraph of it.

**Reading** (strong).
- A party preparing to defend asks for time to rebuild, or at least reserves its position on leave. That costs one
  line.
- A party preparing to concede needs neither, because consent orders make the 9A moot.
- "Discuss at the conference" keeps leave unresolved: no order granting it, and the 9A is never filed if they settle.
  That is B5 of the 12 Oct read.
- They cannot challenge a pleading built on their own admissions without challenging the admissions, and that needs
  r 49(3) leave.
- **Alternatives:**
  - *Officer limits* explain the form, but not the missing one-line reservation, or the date that was noted and then
    passed.
  - *Saving opposition for counsel* is possible, but weak: no prejudice has been claimed, the 14 days are already on
    offer, and Dwyer is averse to interlocutory fights.
- **Falsifier:** after 22 Oct, counsel opposes leave and asks for time, witnesses and an expert.

**Use.**
- Nothing by email.
- At the conference, ask one closed question on items 3 and 6: "Does the Respondent consent to leave? If not, on what
  ground? If it does, will it amend its statement, outlines or witness list, and in what time?"
- **Consent:** their served case answers a superseded pleading.
- **Opposition:** it needs a ground, and none has been put.
- **Time:** they are choosing to fight after the directions expired.
