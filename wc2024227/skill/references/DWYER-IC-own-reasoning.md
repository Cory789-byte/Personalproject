## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ McCOOL — TWO PRINCIPLES IN HIS OWN WORDS

### 1.1 He treats the WCRA as beneficial legislation
> **[68]** *"I am particularly conscious that **the legislation in respect of claims of this nature is
> intended to confer a benefit and it ought to be applied in that way**."* — footnoted to
> **s 194(3) WCRA**.

⇒ **His stated approach to the Act is beneficial construction, with a statutory anchor.** He said it
unprompted, in an ex tempore decision, as the final reason for allowing the appeal.

### 1.2 ⭐⭐⭐ AN EVIDENTIARY VACUUM DOES NOT ACTIVATE AN EXCLUSION — IT DEFEATS IT
The exclusion in issue was s 36 (substantial interruption to a journey). There was **no evidence at
all** explaining a five-to-six hour gap. He held:

> **[65]** *"there is simply **no evidence to explain the delay at all**… **The delay alone is not
> enough for the exclusion to apply**, especially when there is at least one theory consistent with
> a legitimate reason for the interruption."*
> **[66]** *"even though I consider this theory is plausible, **I cannot confidently make a finding
> to that effect on the balance of probabilities**. However, **the absence of an explanation for a
> substantial interruption… should not, of itself, be a barrier to the claim succeeding**."*
> **[67]** *"I am **not satisfied**… that I can conclude that there was a substantial interruption.
> In those circumstances I consider that **there is insufficient evidence available in this matter
> to activate the exclusions** found in s 36 of the Act."*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Three separate formulations of the same principle: the party relying on an exclusion needs
material to activate it. Silence does not do the work. And he will allow a claim even where he
cannot positively find the favourable theory proved.**

## 2. HOW HE FINDS FACTS — McCOOL [25]–[28]

| Method | His words |
|---|---|
| **Reasons from why a person would remember** | *"I consider it more likely that he would recall it **because that was his purpose in calling** Mrs McCool, i.e. to check on the welfare of Mr McCool. The information… **would have been an important fact and one that he is likely to have remembered**."* |
| ⭐⭐ **Weights independent witnesses heavily** | *"I consider **Mr Wilson, as an independent witness, would be very unlikely to be mistaken** about such a specific detail in his recollection."* |
| **Looks for corroboration across record types** | the SMS messages *"while not entirely informative, their contents are **consistent with**"* the oral account |
| **Distinguishes absence of evidence from evidence of absence** | *"there is **no submission** that Mr McCool deviated… **no suggestion or evidence** that Mr McCool engaged in any activity unrelated to his employment"* |

⇒ ⭐⭐ **Independent witnesses matter to him, and he reasons about why a witness would or would not
recall a thing.** ⚠ **Ms Jeffrey, Mr Harrison-Jones and Ms Conaghan are independent witnesses**, and
**Mr Harrison-Jones is a former employee** with nothing to gain.

## 5. ⭐⭐ THE APPLICATION TO s 32(5) — AND ITS LIMIT

**s 32(5) is an exclusion.** So is s 36. In McCool, faced with an exclusion and **no material to
activate it**, Dwyer IC held the exclusion was not made out and allowed the appeal — **expressly
declining to treat the absence of an explanation as fatal to the claimant.**

**Now set that against what the Regulator has actually pleaded:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Its **entire** s 32(5) case | **One sentence** — ¶27 — asserting *"**any** management action"* was reasonable |
| Management action identified | **None.** No act, date, decision, actor or document |
| Medical evidence identified | **None** in seven pages |
| Its own review decision | Found the rostering *"amounted to **unreasonable management action**"* and that *"employment was **a significant contributing factor**"* |

⇒ ⭐⭐ **The argument that follows is the strongest available on the exclusion, and it is his own
reasoning turned round:** *an exclusion requires material to activate it; the Respondent has
identified no management action, no evidence and no witness for it; and its own review officer found
the opposite.*

⛔⛔ **BUT: this is an argument by analogy, not an authority.** McCool is s 36, not s 32(5); the two
exclusions differ in structure and in how the onus is described in the authorities. **It must be
advanced as *"the Commission has approached exclusions in this way"*, never as *"McCool decides
this"*** — and only after the s 32(5) onus position is verified from the cases that actually govern
it.

## 6. WHAT THIS ADDS TO THE READ OF 7 AUGUST

1. ⭐ **His remarks about uncontradicted evidence were not throwaway courtesies to a litigant in
   person.** They are how he has actually decided a contested appeal, in writing, in his own words.
2. ⭐⭐ **His disposition on exclusions is favourable and evidenced** — and the Regulator's exclusion
   case is the thinnest part of its pleading.
3. ⭐ **His approach to the Act is beneficial**, on a statutory footing he cited himself.
4. ⚠ **The one thing he terminates proceedings for is non-cooperation** — which sharpens why the
   compliance shown since 7 August matters, and why nothing should ever be served late without
   first seeking a variation.
5. ⚠ **And the caution that survives all of it:** McCool was a **journey claim on a documentary
   vacuum**. This is a **multi-stressor psychological claim with an admitted breach**. The
   dispositions rhyme; the sections do not.
