# A PSYCHOSOCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK WITH WORKER INPUT
## What the law requires, how it is actually run, and what to ask Metro South Health

> Built 31 July 2026. **Every provision quoted verbatim from the in-force text filed in this repo.**
> **ATT24** WHS Regulation 2011 (Qld), current 29 March 2026 — downloaded and filed this session ·
> **ATT17** WHS Act 2011 (Qld) · **ATT05** Psychosocial Code of Practice 2022 · **ATT02** EB12.
>
> **The point of this document:** MSH's stated position is that it *cannot assess the risk*. The
> assessment is **mandatory**, the method is **prescribed**, and **worker input is a statutory
> element of it**. Asking for it is not a challenge — it is asking them to do the thing they say
> they are unable to do.

---

# PART 1 — IT IS NOT OPTIONAL

## 1.1 The duty
> **WHS Regulation 2011 s 55C(1):** "A person conducting a business or undertaking **must manage
> psychosocial risks under part 3.1**."

**Mandatory. No discretion. No threshold.** Not "may", not "should", not "where practicable".

## 1.2 What counts as a psychosocial hazard — and it covers this case exactly
> **s 55A:** "A psychosocial hazard is a hazard that— (a) arises from, or relates to— (i) **the
> design or management of work**; or (ii) a work environment; or (iii) plant at a workplace; or
> (iv) **workplace interactions or behaviours**; and (b) **may cause psychological harm**."

⇒ *"Unresolved workplace matters involving line management"* — the ECC's stressor (4) — is
**squarely** within s 55A(a)(i) and (iv).

## 1.3 The matters MSH is REQUIRED to consider
> **s 55D(2):** "In determining the control measures to implement, the person… **must have regard to
> all relevant matters, including**—
> (a) the **duration**, frequency or severity of the exposure of workers… to psychosocial hazards;
> (b) **how the psychosocial hazards may interact or combine**;
> (c) the design of work, including **job demands** and tasks;
> (d) the **systems of work, including how work is managed, organised and supported**;
> (e) the design and layout, and environmental conditions, of the workplace…;
> (h) **workplace interactions or behaviours**;
> (i) the **information, training, instruction and supervision** provided to workers."

⭐ **(d) and (h) are the case.** How work is *managed* and *supported*, and workplace *interactions*,
are matters MSH **must** have regard to. **(a) duration** captures a five-week exclusion.

## 1.4 The prescribed method — Part 3.1
| Reg | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **34** | "must **identify reasonably foreseeable hazards** that could give rise to risks" |
| **35** | "**eliminate** risks… so far as is reasonably practicable; and if not reasonably practicable to eliminate—**minimise** those risks" |
| **36** | **Hierarchy of control:** substitute → isolate the hazard → engineering → **then** administrative → **then** PPE |
| **37** | control measures must be **maintained** — fit for purpose, suitable for the nature and duration of the work |
| **38** | must **review and revise** controls — including **38(2)(c)** when *"a new relevant hazard or risk is identified"* and **38(2)(d)** when *"the results of consultation… indicate that a review is necessary"* |

⭐⭐ **Reg 36 is decisive on the exclusion.** The hierarchy requires **isolating the hazard** —
not the worker. Removing the person exposed is not a listed control at all. And administrative
controls come **only after** substitution, isolation and engineering have been considered.

## 1.5 ⭐⭐ WORKER INPUT IS A STATUTORY ELEMENT — NOT A COURTESY
> **WHS Act s 47(1):** "The person conducting a business or undertaking **must**, so far as is
> reasonably practicable, **consult**… with **workers**… who are, or are likely to be, **directly
> affected by a matter relating to work health or safety**. **Maximum penalty—200 penalty units.**"

> **s 48(1):** "Consultation under this division **requires**—
> (a) that **relevant information about the matter is shared with workers**; and
> (b) that workers be given **a reasonable opportunity**— (i) **to express their views** and to raise
> work health or safety issues…; and (ii) ⭐ **to contribute to the decision-making process**…; and
> (c) that **the views of workers are taken into account**…; and
> (d) that the workers consulted are **advised of the outcome of the consultation in a timely way**."

**Four mandatory elements. All four are enforceable. Section 47 carries a penalty.**
> **s 48(2)–(3):** consultation **must involve** the health and safety representative, or a
> representative the worker asks for.
> **s 48(6)(a):** but a representative need **not** be given access to *"personal or medical
> information concerning a worker"* — ⭐ **so worker participation and medical privacy coexist.**

# PART 2 — HOW IT IS ACTUALLY RUN

**Six steps. Each maps to a provision. Worker input is built into every one of them, because s 48
requires it.**

## STEP 1 — SCOPE AND CONVENE  *(s 47; s 48(2)–(3))*
- Define the unit assessed: **Switchboard Services, Logan Hospital**, all shifts including night and
  on-call.
- Convene: the **PCBU representative** (a manager **outside** the directorate whose conduct is in
  issue), the **HSR** if one exists, the **union representative** if requested, **Injury Management**,
  and **the affected worker**.
- ⚠️ **Nobody whose own conduct is a candidate hazard sits on the assessing group.** Consistent with
  **Code of Conduct cl 1.2**.
- Record who was invited, who attended, and who declined.

## STEP 2 — IDENTIFY HAZARDS  *(reg 34; s 48(1)(a)–(b))*
Sources, in order:
1. **Worker input** — structured, confidential, and *before* any management view is circulated
2. Existing records: incident reports, complaints, PIDs, grievances, injury and claims data
3. Absence, turnover and roster data
4. The **Code of Practice 2022** hazard list, worked through item by item

**Instrument:** short structured interviews plus an anonymous written return. Every worker in the
unit, not a sample. ⭐ **Anonymity matters where the hazard is management conduct** — otherwise the
instrument measures fear, not risk.

## STEP 3 — ASSESS RISK  *(reg 35; s 55D(2))*
For each hazard, record against **the s 55D(2) matters by name**:
| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration, frequency, severity** of exposure | s 55D(2)(a) |
| **Interaction and combination** of hazards | s 55D(2)(b) |
| **Job demands and task design** | s 55D(2)(c) |
| ⭐ **Systems of work — how work is managed, organised and supported** | s 55D(2)(d) |
| Workplace design, layout, environment | s 55D(2)(e) |
| ⭐ **Workplace interactions and behaviours** | s 55D(2)(h) |
| **Information, training, instruction, supervision** | s 55D(2)(i) |

⚠️ **A risk-matrix score is not an assessment.** The regulation requires *regard to the matters*.
Each must appear in the document by name, with reasoning.

## STEP 4 — CONTROL, IN THE PRESCRIBED ORDER  *(reg 36)*
| Order | Control | Applied here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Substitute** the hazard | change the process, the workflow, the allocation of the task |
| 2 | ⭐ **Isolate the hazard** | separate the **source** — e.g. reporting line, decision-maker, complaint routing |
| 3 | **Engineering** | systems, escalation routing, call handling |
| 4 | **Administrative** — *only after 1–3* | procedures, training, supervision |
| 5 | **PPE** — last | rarely applicable to psychosocial risk |

⭐⭐ **"Isolating the hazard from any person exposed to it" (reg 36(3)(b)) means isolating the
HAZARD. Removing the worker is nowhere in the hierarchy.**

## STEP 5 — DOCUMENT AND REPORT BACK  *(s 48(1)(c)–(d))*
- Record **the views of workers and how they were taken into account** — s 48(1)(c)
- **Advise those consulted of the outcome in a timely way** — s 48(1)(d)
- ⚠️ **A consultation that is never reported back is not consultation.** It is data collection.

## STEP 6 — REVIEW  *(regs 37–38)*
Set a review date. Review **earlier** if any **reg 38(2)** trigger occurs, expressly including:
- **38(2)(c)** a new relevant hazard is identified
- **38(2)(d)** the results of consultation indicate a review is necessary
- **38(2)(e)** an HSR requests it

> **Framing: the clinicians need it to answer the Health Service's own questions.** Neutral,
> procedural, no accusation. **Refusal is the answer.**

**Draft:**

> To enable my treating practitioners to answer questions 1(d) and 3 of the Request for Medical
> Information — which ask whether identified stressors present a foreseeable risk, and what controls
> are medically necessary — I ask the Health Service to provide the following:
>
> 1. The **psychosocial risk assessment** conducted in respect of the AO3 Switchboard Services role
>    and work unit, and any **hazard register** for that unit.

>    record of how the **hierarchy of control at s 36 of the WHS Regulation 2011** was applied.
> 3. The record required by **s 55D(2)** of the matters had regard to in determining those controls.
> 4. ⭐ **The date on which the last psychosocial risk assessment for Switchboard Services was
>    conducted, and the date of the last such assessment that included input from the workers in
>    that unit** — together with the record of that consultation under **s 48(1)(c) and (d)** of the
>    WHS Act, being the workers' views, how they were taken into account, and how the outcome was
>    communicated.

>    having occurred since.
> 6. Confirmation whether a **health and safety representative** is in place for the work unit.
>
> If any of the above has not been undertaken, I ask that the Health Service confirm that, so that
> my practitioners may proceed on an accurate understanding of the controls in place.

# PART 4 — WHAT EACH ANSWER DOES

| If MSH… | Then |
|---|---|
| **Produces an assessment** | ⭐ It is disclosable, testable, and **a specialist can evaluate it clinically** — which is exactly what Q1(d) invites |
| **Produces one with no worker input** | **s 47/48 breach on the face of it.** Consultation is mandatory and carries a penalty |
| **Produces one that never considered the s 55D(2)(d) and (h) matters** | The determination of controls did not have regard to matters the regulation **requires** |
| **Cannot identify when the last worker-input assessment occurred** | ⭐⭐ **The strongest outcome.** It confirms in writing that the mandatory process has not been run — and *"we do not have sufficient information to assess whether they present psychosocial hazards"* is answered: **the information does not exist because the assessment was never done** |
| **Refuses to answer** | A refusal to state whether a mandatory assessment exists, made to a worker excluded on the basis of an unassessed risk |

## ⭐ AND THE CENTRAL POINT, WHICH IS THE WHOLE CASE IN ONE LINE
MSH's stated reason for the exclusion is that it **cannot assess the psychosocial risk**.
**Assessing the psychosocial risk is a mandatory duty it owes — and the method requires the worker's
participation.**

⇒ **The Health Service excluded a worker because it had not performed a duty which, had it performed
it, would have required that worker's input.** The inability relied upon was self-created.

⇒ And **reg 36** answers the exclusion directly: **the hierarchy of control requires isolating the
hazard. Removing the worker is not a control measure known to the regulation.**

## ⚠️ HOW TO USE THIS
- **Attach it or don't** — but **ask the Part 3 questions**. The framework's value is that it makes
  the questions obviously reasonable and obviously answerable.
- **Never frame it as an allegation of breach.** Frame it as: *my clinicians need to know what
  controls exist.* Let the absence speak.
- **The "when was the last one with worker input" question is the sharpest.** It is a single date. A
  date, an "unknown", or a silence — all three help.
- ❌ **Do not put a penalty provision in the letter.** Citing "200 penalty units" at HR reads as a
  threat and forfeits the register. **Know it; don't write it.**
