## The four questions, answered

**1 · Will they settle?** ⇒ **about one third, and almost none of it now.** Two Calderbanks have
already been rejected and the without-prejudice position was "unchanged" on 14 August — ⚠ both
*before* the 8 September admissions, so the position has not been tested against the record they
now hold. ⭐ **The forcing point is not 30 September. It is the listing.** Settlement pressure is
generated by having to prepare Taylor and Reese to be cross-examined on 298 admitted facts, and
that work does not become real until a date exists.

⚠ **The structural drag on settlement:** what is at stake in this appeal is **statutory
compensation, not damages**. Conceding it concedes that the injury is compensable, with downstream
consequences. A statutory respondent defends that on principle far longer than a commercial party
would.

**2 · Will they call Taylor and Reese?** ⇒ **92%.** They conferenced **Reese on 8 July 2025** and
**Taylor on 10 July 2025** and disclosed the product at items 37–39 and 41 of their own List of
Documents. With 298 facts admitted, only characterisation is live, and characterisation requires
the decision-makers. And the Registry letter of 9 September has already foreclosed the quiet exit.

**3 · Will it be documents alone, with no witness against him?** ⇒ **about 6%, and he made it that
low himself.** ⭐ The paragraph in the covering letter to the Registrar — *"If the Respondent's list
filed by 30 September 2026 does not name them, the Appellant will seek attendance notices and will
write to the Commission"* — converted the Regulator's cheapest defensive option into a costly one.

⛔ **But understand why that scenario was ever dangerous, because it explains the whole case.**
Under **Guide 7.3 and *Prizeman***, the onus is on the Appellant, and **an absence of evidence about
management action is resolved in favour of it being reasonable**. A Respondent that calls nobody
can submit that the Commission cannot know the reasons, so the onus is not discharged.

⭐⭐ **Two things answer that, and both are already built.** First, the ~40 admitted **negatives** at
Part B.5 convert "absence of evidence" into "admitted absence of allegation". Second, and better:
**the Regulator's own review officer already found the rostering of 17–18 March 2024 to be
unreasonable management action, on the documents, and the Respondent has admitted that the decision
records it (fact 260).** A decision-maker looking at this material without witnesses has already
reached that conclusion once.

**4 · Will he be cross-examined on this fact set?** ⇒ **97% if it runs — but not on the facts.**
The documents, dates, times, senders and words are admitted. There is nothing there to cross-examine
on. The exposure is in three places only:

| Target | Why |
|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **His own earlier words** | Review Decision 69983 records him asserting *corruption*, a *"plot to obtain additional income"*, *"misuse of government funds"*, and that the Manager *"used her authority to withhold your payments maliciously"*. The decision is an admitted document. Counsel will put those to him to show the account is coloured — and thereby attack the reliability of the uncorroborated evidence at Stressor 3(e) |
| **Stressor 3(e)** | sixteen hours awake, four hours travel, four hours sleep — entirely his own account, and the heart of the case |
| **Causation** | the pre-existing history-list entries of 26/10/2022 ADHD and Anxiety; the February 2025 report's *"not for medico-legal use"* footer; and ⚠ the attendance letter of 8 September 2025 (34 occasions) |

⇒ **The cross-examination will not be about what happened. It will be about him.** That is the
direct consequence of admitting the documentary record — and it is the trade that was made
deliberately.
