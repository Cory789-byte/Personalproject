## 2. WHAT THE BENCH SAID ABOUT THE HEALTH SERVICE

There is **no criticism of Metro South Health anywhere in the hearing**. The nearest approach is
withdrawn mid-sentence. What there is, is this — **57:45 to 58:20**:

> "**and you may be right about all of that** — you don't have to go too far in any public sector or
> any organisation really — **I shouldn't beat up the public sector** — but in any organisation to
> find failures to comply with legislation, failures to comply with protocol, failures to comply with
> policy — **it happens every single day in every single workplace**. It's a question of whether or
> not there's anything material that arises from any of that… and it's only a problem **if somebody
> gets sacked or somebody gets killed** or somebody, you know what I mean? And it's only then when
> these things get **a light shone on them and they become relevant**."

⭐⭐⭐ **This cuts both ways, and the second way is the stronger.** He is saying non-compliance is
ubiquitous and immaterial — *until something serious happens, at which point it becomes relevant.*
That is precisely the link the Appellant was making at 27:28 and again at 29:17, and which was cut
off on both occasions (§22, §26.6). **The bench supplied the test that makes the patient-safety
material relevant, thirty minutes after declining to hear it.** The Appellant's case is that the
non-compliance was in an emergency communications function — which is the one setting where "somebody
gets killed" is the risk being run.

Elsewhere the health service is referred to neutrally, as a non-party:

- **18:31** — "the health service is not a party to these proceedings. They're not party to this.
  They're not involved in these proceedings apart from the consequences of the application you file
  today."
- **~31:00** — "I'd imagine the health service will probably want to engage [—] law and they'll bring
  their lawyers along and I'll allow that… then there'll be cost consequences… **a big sideshow,
  probably bigger than the hearing at the end of the day** and if you want it, you can have it."
  (⚠ the firm name transcribes as "aged ground law" — plainly a mishearing; not guessed here.)
