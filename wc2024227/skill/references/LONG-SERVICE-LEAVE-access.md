## 1. ⭐ THE ENTITLEMENT — AWARD CLAUSE 22, VERBATIM

> **22(a)** *"Long service leave, **including for casual employees**, is provided for in Division 9
> of the QES. Clause 22(b) supplements the QES."*
>
> **22(b)** *"In lieu of the provisions of sections 95(2)(a) and (b) of the Act, all employees who
> complete **10 years' continuous service** are entitled to long service leave at the rate of
> **1.3 weeks on full pay for each year of continuous service** and a proportionate amount for an
> incomplete year of service."*
>
> ⭐⭐ **22(c)** *"Employees who have completed **7 years' continuous service are entitled to TAKE
> long service leave on full pay or half pay**."*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Accrual runs from year one at 1.3 weeks per year. ACCESS opens at SEVEN years.**

⭐ **Casual service counts** — cl 22(a) says so expressly. That matters: the October 2020 master
roster (D8) records him as **Casual, payroll 388372**.

## 3. ⛔ THE PANDEMIC ROUTE IS CLOSED — DO NOT RELY ON IT
The Health Pandemic Directive provides *"early access to pro-rata long service leave, **regardless
of length of service**, when all other paid leave accruals have been exhausted"* — and Directive
13.5 disapplies **both** the seven-year minimum **and** the minimum period per absence.

⛔ **That operates only during a declared health pandemic.** It is not a general route and must not
be cited as one.

## 6. ⛔⛔ WHY THIS IS A LAST RESORT, NOT A FIRST ONE

### 6.1 It may damage the wages claim
The Petering email asserts he is **ready, willing and able** to work within the 3 July restrictions
and has been since 3 July, and asks the union to take up the non-payment **as a matter in its own
right**.

⚠ **An employee on approved long service leave is on leave, not held out.** If he applies for and
takes LSL, the Health Service can say he was on approved leave rather than excluded and unpaid.
⇒ **That cuts directly across the strongest thing he has on the employment track.**

### 6.2 ⭐⭐⭐ IT REPEATS THE EXACT TRANSACTION PLEADED IN THE APPEAL
> **Form 9A, Stressor 3(c):** Ms Taylor *"misapplied a superseded 2020 casual agreement to deny
> statutory paid fatigue leave, **compelling the Appellant to exhaust his own accrued personal
> leave** on 19 March 2024. This effectively **shifted the financial detriment of the Employer's WHS
> breach onto the injured worker**."*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Taking long service leave to survive a hold-out is the same transaction: the employer's
failure, funded out of his own accruals.** And LSL is the **most valuable accrual he has** — 1.3
weeks per year, permanently depleted once taken.

### 6.3 And it sits oddly with what he has already put in dispute
His email of 28 August records that *"the leave debited since 3 July 2026"* **remains unresolved and
in dispute.** ⇒ Contesting leave being applied while volunteering more of it needs to be handled
carefully or not at all.

## 7. ⭐ THE SEQUENCE

| Order | Step |
|---|---|
| **1** | **The AVAC and the wages claim.** If the AVAC is submitted and pay flows, no accrual is touched. This is what the Petering email is for |
| **2** | **The service statement, the balance, C38 and the Directive.** ⭐ **Do this NOW anyway** — knowing the position costs nothing and commits to nothing |
| **3** | **Only if 1 fails:** apply, and apply **expressly under protest** |

### ⭐⭐ IF IT IS TAKEN, TAKE IT UNDER PROTEST — IN WRITING, IN THE APPLICATION
Words to the effect of:

> *"I apply for long service leave **only because I have received no wages since 13 July 2026 and no
> AVAC has been submitted**. I remain ready, willing and able to work within the restrictions
> recorded in the Employee Capability Checklist of 3 July 2026. **This application is made under
> protest and without prejudice to my claim for wages for the period I have been held out**, and I
> reserve the right to seek reinstatement of the leave taken."*

⇒ ⭐ **That single paragraph preserves everything.** Without it, taking the leave looks like accepting
the position.
