## 4. WHAT THAT MEANS — AND IT IS NOT AN ACCUSATION

⛔ **Do not argue impropriety.** Rule 49(2) entitles a party to dispute without giving any reason,
and declining to admit a copy you cannot verify is the ordinary and proper use of it. Saying
otherwise is a *Briginshaw* allegation against a professional officer, it is motive-reasoning, and
it is the one way to lose this point.

**Argue the consequence instead, which is stronger and costs nothing:**

1. A dispute as to authenticity is, on its face, an assertion that a document is not what it
   purports to be. **The Respondent has not asserted that of any of the fourteen**, has identified
   no discrepancy in any of them, and has produced no competing copy.
2. What the pattern shows is that the dispute is **about the absence of a corroborating copy, not
   about the documents**. That is a state of the Respondent's own knowledge, not a defect in the
   Appellant's evidence.
3. ⭐ And the contents are already admitted. For **thirteen of the fourteen**, the Form 24 admits
   what the document says — Tab 6 at facts 49 and 163; Tab 20 at facts 263–268; Tab 30 at facts
   71–72; Tab 21 at fact 224; Tabs 22–23 at facts 167–176; Tab 1 at facts 1–13. **The Respondent
   admits the words and disputes the copy.**
4. So at the hearing the dispute has nothing in it to put. Either the Respondent puts a positive
   case that a document is not genuine — which **must** be put to the Appellant in
   cross-examination under *Browne v Dunn*, and there is nothing to put it on — or it does not,
   and the Appellant's evidence that he made or received each document and kept it is
   uncontradicted. **Which is [15:40].**
