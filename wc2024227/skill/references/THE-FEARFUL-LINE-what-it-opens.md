**WC/2024/227 · 16 September 2026 · ⛔ INTERNAL ONLY. Nothing here is put to anyone.**

**The sentence, verified verbatim** (Taylor → A McNamee, HR, Fri 17 May 2024 1:20 PM, FRMS p 36):

> *"It is not unknown within the switchboard of Corys behaviour and malice towards me which is highly
> inappopropriate and **its starting to affect my ability to perform best in my role. I am currently
> experencing high anxiety interacting with this staff member and am fearful as to what his next
> actions maybe.**"*

---

## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ What it opens — the employer's own record of the breakdown, 32 days before onset

**17 May 2024 → 18 June 2024 onset = thirty-two days.**

This is **the line manager, in writing, to Human Resources**, recording that the working relationship
had reached that state. ⇒ **It is not his assertion that the relationship was the site of the stress.
It is theirs.** For s 32(1) it corroborates the state of the employment relationship at the material
time from the other side of it.

⇒ ⭐ **And it is the only document in the matter where a Metro South manager records the relationship
in those terms before the injury.**

## 2. ⭐⭐⭐ The question it forces: what did the employer then do?

A manager tells HR she is **highly anxious**, **fearful**, and that it is **affecting her ability to
perform her role**. That is a written report of a workplace relationship in breakdown, received by
HR, one month before the injury. Everything that follows is documentary and requires no adjective:

| The question | Why it is neutral |
|---|---|
| What did HR do on receiving it? | asks about their conduct, not her motive |
| Was any risk assessment made? | a process question |
| Was anything put to him? | was he ever told a concern had gone to HR? |
| Was either of them offered anything — a meeting, mediation, separation of reporting? | management action, or its absence |
| **Did his leave, roster and pay continue to route through that same manager?** | ⭐ **they did** |

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **And the answer is already on the served record.** Topic 7 of his outline states that, **on
Metro South's own statements**, no safety assessment, investigation or change of procedure followed
any complaint he made before 30 June 2024.

⇒ **So on their own documents: HR received a written report that the relationship had broken down,
and nothing was assessed for either person — while every request he had to make continued to run
through her.** ⭐⭐ **That is the pleaded mechanism — responsibility imposed, the means withheld —
confirmed at management level, proved by their document and their admissions.**

## 3. ⭐⭐ And it bears directly on s 32(5)(a) — the *way*, not the fact

The exclusion is reasonable management action **taken in a reasonable way**. ⇒ If HR held that
report from 17 May 2024 and the reporting line was left exactly as it was, **the manner of everything
done through that line afterwards is open** — the leave decisions, the AVAC, the roster.
⚠ **This is *Prizeman* territory, and it is the safe side of it**: the reality of what the employer
did, not his perception of it.

---

## 4. ⚠⚠ The cost, priced honestly — this cuts both ways and the other edge is sharp

⛔ **Their mirror use of the same paragraph is the strongest version of their case:** the relationship
broke down **because of his conduct**; her fear is evidence of it; and the management action that
followed was reasonable *because* they were managing a situation he created.

Three specific costs:

1. ⚠⚠ **"Fearful" is the most sympathetic sentence any Metro South witness has written in this
   matter.** Put it in play and a decision-writer may reproduce it beside his own injury. **A named
   person saying she was frightened at work reads as a human being in distress**, whatever its
   sourcing.
2. ⚠ **The same email narrates 13, 14 and 15 May** — three consecutive days of calling in and
   telling operators he did not want to speak to his manager. ⭐ The answer is documentary (he had
   asked the Director for a break from her), **but the email states the conduct plainly and in
   sequence, and the SOFC already pleads it at ¶ 16(b)(i).**
3. ⚠ **It was solicited** — *"as requested"*. ⇒ **Do not run it as though she volunteered an attack.**
   The June 2023 email was unprompted; this one was asked for by HR.

⇒ ⭐ **Net: it is worth crossing only on the document's own internal weaknesses and on what the
employer did next.** Those are set out at §2 and at
`TAYLOR-17MAY-EMAIL-verified-and-what-it-does.md` §§3–5 — **double hearsay she identifies herself,
unnamed and unparticularised, contradicted four hours earlier by her own team email, and contradicted
by two switchboard witnesses already served.**

⛔ **Never on the four-day gap from 13 May.** That is PID-track, it opens **s 32(5)(b)**, and it
raises his own ***Briginshaw*** bar.
