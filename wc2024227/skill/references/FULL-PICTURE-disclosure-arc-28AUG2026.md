## BOTTOM LINE

**The Form 29 has been read as a failure. It was not.** It failed as a *compliance application* —
and it succeeded as an *evidence-gathering exercise*, which is the thing that mattered. It
extracted six categories of documents now sitting in Annexure A, **four admissions at Chief
Executive level that the protective systems the appeal complains about did not exist**, and one
**named, conceded, unproduced document** that is still recoverable by the cheapest route the
Commission has offered.

**P(the Form 24/25 delivers a materially admitted documentary record) ≈ 57%** *(80% CI 51–63%;
full red–blue span 44–71%)* on the sufficient target. On the maximal target — every tab including
the MSH-sourced ones — **≈36%** *(30–41%)*. **The gap between those two numbers is the MSH-produced
tabs, and they are the least necessary part of the case.**

## 4. PREDICTION

| Target | P | 80% CI |
|---|---|---|
| **Sufficient** — service + own-disclosure tabs + the four collisions | **57%** | 51–63% |
| **Maximal** — every tab including MSH-sourced | **36%** | 30–41% |

**What drives it:** the four collisions are the highest-leverage block (P rises to 80% when they
hold). The MSH-production tabs are the weakest and the least load-bearing.

**Bimodality — two worlds gated on one fork:** whether the Regulator answers substantively or seeks
an extension and then answers minimally.
- **World A (~60%)** — substantive response, heavy admission of its own paper, not-admits on the
  appellant-held and MSH-sourced tabs. **The case is then run on an agreed documentary spine.**
- **World B (~40%)** — extension, then a broad *"does not admit"* on anything not self-evidently
  its own. **The case is then run on the four collisions alone** — which still works, because each
  is proved from a document the Regulator cannot disown.

⚠ **In neither world does the appeal turn on further disclosure.** That is the real finding.

## 6. ⚠ VERIFICATION AND OPEN ITEMS

### 6.1 ⛔ A CORRECTION THE ARC EXPOSES — THE PID TIMING
> **MSH, Item 20:** *"the reference cited in the Notice being 'PID24-ESU-1130' **commenced in
> November 2024**, which falls outside the specified search period of 13 May 2024 to 31 May 2024."*

The complaint was made **13 May 2024**. The ESU determination was **24 December 2024**. The
reference, on MSH's account, **commenced November 2024**.

⇒ ⚠ **The pleading's *"within 48 hours of the PID being lodged"* overstates.** What was lodged on
13 May was a **complaint**. It supports the Regulator's ¶16(a) — *"Ms Reese was unaware that the
appellant had lodged a PID"* — because on this chronology **nobody could have known in May that it
was a PID.**

⭐ **The fix costs nothing and removes the vulnerability entirely: plead and lead the proximity as
complaint-to-direction, not PID-to-direction.** The interval is identical; the attack disappears.
**The Regulator admitted paragraph 21 in the very words *"48 hours after the Appellant lodged the
PID complaint"* — "complaint" is already in the admitted text.**

### 6.2 ⚠ THE ACCESS-LOG METHOD WAS ALREADY DEAD
> **MSH, Item 9:** *"The switchboard office **does not have its own access-swipe access**."*

At the mention [13:38] the Appellant proposed proving erratic presence by building-access records.
**MSH had said two months earlier that no such records exist for that office.** ⇒ Erratic presence
rests on **his own observation and that of Ms Jeffrey, Mr Harrison-Jones and Ms Conaghan** — which
is what the outlines now do, and is what Dwyer said would suffice.
