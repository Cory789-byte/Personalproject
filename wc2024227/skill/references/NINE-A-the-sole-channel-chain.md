> (characterisation). Every link below is an admitted fact.

## What ¶267 does in this position
Without it, link 6 is "Ms Taylor took six days" — a proposition about a person, met by
"she was busy / it was reasonable", requiring cross-examination. With it, the pleading is about
a **system the employer itself certified**: every road — correction, escalation, after-hours
contact, complaint — ran through one officer, whose availability was unstated on the employer's
own admitted record; and the six days is the **measured performance of that system**, not an
allegation about the officer. The CE's letter supplies the word that does the work: *solely*.
The Appellant never has to say "single point of failure" — the employer said it to the
Commissioner, and ¶267 is admitted.

## The paragraph, ready for the builder (contention 4, first limb; and the B-part narrative)
> On the employer's own statement to the Commission, every employee complaint concerning
> Switchboard operational errors was "made directly to the Line Manager of Switch Board and
> managed solely via email or verbally with the complainant" {{267}}. The reports of emergency
> calls reaching the wrong medical teams therefore travelled the only channel the employer says
> existed — to the officer who, with one other, held the means of correcting the directory
> {{39,40,43,44}}, who had notified the department that the two of them "had added themselves"
> to the after-hours contact {{49}}, and whose office hours are not alleged to have been stated
> to the staff at any time between 23 August 2023 and 17 May 2024 {{73}}. The first reply to
> those reports was sent five days, eighteen hours and fourteen minutes after the first of them
> {{64}}, and no consequential change to operating procedures followed {{266}}. That is not a
> contention about any officer. It is the operation of the only system the employer says it had.
