# What Dwyer said at the mention about the Regulator's witnesses
> 13 September 2026. Read from `documents/transcripts/TRANSCRIPT_diarised_prosody.md`
> (mention of 7 August 2026). Timestamps as diarised.
> ⚠ **A mention is not a ruling.** These are indications given while explaining process to a
> self-represented appellant. And under Appeal Guide 5.1 the Member who chairs a conference
> **will not be the Member who hears the appeal** — so the understanding recorded here may sit with
> the conference, not with the decision-maker.

## ⭐⭐⭐ The direct one — 34:43

Reading Stressor 1(c) from the original Form 9A (the communication book entry):

> **"You're not going to need documents for that, are you? That's just going to be, you're going to
> make that assertion in your evidence and *I would have thought the regulator will call Ms Taylor
> to give her side of the story and you'll get to cross-examine Ms Taylor.*"**

**The Commissioner said, on the record, that he would expect the Regulator to call Ms Taylor — and
that the Appellant would cross-examine her.** Unprompted.

⚠ Note the context: he was reading the **original** pleading, including *"unilaterally removed and
destroyed"* and *"hostile and verbally aggressive reprimand"* — characterisation since removed. His
point was that such an allegation needs **oral evidence, not documents**.

## The earlier, softer ones

**~15:00** — after telling the Appellant his observation of erratic presence is admissible testimony:
> "If the regulator wants to dispute that, they can dispute it. **No doubt they may have Ms Taylor
> on their witness list or not.**"

**18:31** — on why non-party disclosure is a step beyond the ordinary:
> "the health service is **not a party** to these proceedings… **The starting point is to ask the
> regulator to produce those documents.** … the regulator whilst not representing the health service
> **will have access to the health service. That necessarily will need to call potentially people who
> work for the health service as witnesses in the case, potentially. Maybe not, but maybe.**"

⭐ This is the passage the request letter of 9 September 2026 relies on, and it reflects it
accurately.

## ⭐⭐ The tell that matters most — the uncontradicted-evidence principle, given twice

**~15:00:**
> "It's often thrown out there as an appellant that **you have the onus of proving your case, and
> that's true, but the regulator does have to contradict things that you say. It's not the same
> thing as an onus, but if you say something and it's not contradicted, then the likelihood is
> it's not guaranteed, but there's a likelihood that it'll be accepted.**"

**49:53:**
> "Once you've made the assertion, **the regulator's got to respond to it. They've got to address
> it. They'll address it in cross-examination. They may produce documents that they seek to rely on
> to contradict it. They've got to disclose those documents to you.**"

⇒ **This is the bench's own answer to the *Prizeman* problem** identified in the scenario model.
*Prizeman* says absence of evidence about management action resolves in favour of reasonableness.
Dwyer's gloss is narrower and more practical: **the onus stays with the Appellant, but evidence
that is not contradicted is likely to be accepted.** That is the basis on which a documentary case
plus his own testimony can carry, and it is why the ~40 admitted negatives at Part B.5 matter.

## And what he expected the Regulator to do about documents — 32:43

> "if I was presiding on the matter I'd say Miss Matheson have you got any emails of that type and
> they'll say we asked but none were provided to us and then I've got to decide whether or not I
> accept your evidence or not… but more often than not, if you alleged that there were emails that
> were causative of your stress, **I would have thought the regulator would be all over that** and
> they'd say, right, well let's have a look at them… And then they'd get examples of them and
> **they would find their way into evidence.**"

⇒ He expected the Regulator to chase the material itself rather than let an assertion stand
untested. ⭐ Measured against that expectation, a Respondent that on 8 September 2026 **admitted 298
paragraphs and denied none** has done something closer to the opposite.

## ⚠ One observation that cuts against the current strategy — 32:43

> "**it's more common than not that appellants will give evidence in these proceedings without
> relying heavily on documentary case**, not suggesting that you should or shouldn't."

The case as now built is overwhelmingly documentary. That is a **departure from the norm he
described** — a deliberate and, on balance, a good one. But it means the oral evidence at Stressor
3(e) and the Appellant's credit carry more weight in the mix than is usual, not less. See the
cross-examination exposure at `SCENARIO-MODEL-13SEP2026.md`.
