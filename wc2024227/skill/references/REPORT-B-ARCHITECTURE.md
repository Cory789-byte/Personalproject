# WHAT A COMPREHENSIVE REPORT B LOOKS LIKE — architecture, not conclusions
> 8 August 2026. For briefing the psychiatrist, and for evaluating what comes back.
> ⛔⛔ **This document describes STRUCTURE and STANDARDS. It does not suggest conclusions, and
> nothing in it may be put to the doctor as a desired answer.** A report that reads as instructed
> is worth less than no report (`THE-FATIGUE-SEQUENCE.md` §10.4).

---

# PART 1 — ⭐⭐⭐ THE ONE STRUCTURAL FEATURE THAT MATTERS MOST

**The report must contain, in terms:**
> *"I am instructed to assume the facts set out in the statement of Mr Shepherd dated [X], and in
> the documents listed at [Y]."*

⭐⭐⭐ **This separates the two jobs. HE proves the facts — the role, the load, the roster, the
break, the complaints, the responses. The DOCTOR opines on assumed facts.**

⛔ **If the factual description of the workplace lives only inside the psychiatrist's report, it is
hearsay, it is attacked there, and the opinion falls with it.** Two witnesses, two jobs.

# PART 2 — THE SECTIONS OF A COMPREHENSIVE REPORT

| # | Section | What makes it strong here |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Instructions** — who instructed, the questions asked | ⭐ State the **s 32(1)** question accurately: *whether employment was **a significant** contributing factor.* ⛔ Never "major" |
| **2** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Sources of information** — every document reviewed, every person seen, dates and duration of each examination | ⛔⛔ **The most attacked part of any report.** *"You were not given X"* is the first cross-examination question. **A short list is a vulnerability; a complete list is armour** |
| **3** | **Background** — personal, educational, occupational, medical, **psychiatric**, family | ⛔ **This is where the 26 October 2022 entries must be addressed** (Part 4) |
| **4** | **Occupational history** — the role and its demands | Code Blue / MET coordination; ⭐ **criticality is ADMITTED (¶8)** so it is assumed fact, not contest |
| **5** | ⭐ **The account of events** — recorded expressly as *the patient's account*, distinguished from documented fact | Keeps the doctor out of fact-finding |
| **6** | **Course of symptoms** — onset, development, treatment, response | Part 3 below |
| **7** | **Mental state examination** — at each attendance | |
| **8** | **Diagnosis** — the classificatory framework, **the criteria met, and when**, plus **differentials considered and excluded** | MDD with anxious distress, **DSM-5 296.23** |
| **9** | ⭐⭐⭐ **Opinion on causation** — answering the question asked | **The gate** |
| **10** | **Prognosis, capacity, treatment** | ⚠ See Part 6 — the ECC trap |
| **11** | **Declaration** — the expert's duty, the assumptions relied on, and that the opinions are his own | |

⚠ **Length is not quality.** A comprehensive report runs 15–30 pages, but **the parts that decide
it are the sources list, the differential, and the reasoning from facts to conclusion.** A short
report that reasons beats a long one that recites.

# PART 3 — ⭐⭐ THE TEMPORAL SPINE

A strong report tracks a sequence, not a grievance:

**Baseline (16 Nov 2023 — *"no psychological illness"*, ADMITTED ¶34)** → **the course of exposure
(June 2023 – June 2024)** → ⭐⭐ **the documented complaints (8, 9, 24 April and 1 May 2024)** →
**onset (18 June 2024)** → **GP presentation (Sept 2024)** → **diagnosis (24 Oct 2024)** → **the
2026 question.**

## ⭐⭐ The gap the report must explain

**Onset is pleaded at 18 June 2024. The medical record begins in September 2024.** That
three-month gap is the Regulator's best causation point.

⭐ **A comprehensive report explains it clinically** — late presentation in people who continue
working in high-responsibility roles is a recognised clinical pattern. ⭐⭐ **That is an
observation the doctor can make. It is not an excuse the patient offers.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ And this is where the April–May material earns its place

The complaints of **8 and 24 April 2024** are contemporaneous, self-authored and
employer-received — **and they sit inside the gap.** ⛔ **Ask about them neutrally** (§10.4):
> *"What, if anything, do you make of the reports of fatigue recorded in April and May 2024, in the
> context of the diagnosis you made in October 2024?"*

# PART 4 — ⛔⛔ DIFFERENTIAL AND COMPETING CAUSES — where reports live or die

**The Regulator has pleaded:**
- **SOFC ¶8** — a pre-injury anxiety history.
- **Form 24 ¶32** — anxiety / ADHD entries from **26 October 2022**, said to be missing from
  Exhibit A5.

⛔⛔ **A report that does not address these is worthless in cross-examination. A report that names
them, weighs them, and reasons through them is very strong.**

⭐⭐ **And the statutory test is more forgiving than it looks — the doctor should be told it
accurately, because misstating it is leading and stating it correctly is not:**
- Employment need only be ***a significant* contributing factor** — **not the only, and not the
  main, cause.**
- **Competing causes need not be outweighed** (*Adams* [2015] ICQ 1 [23], adopting *Hochen*).
- ⭐ **s 32(3)(b): an *aggravation* of a pre-existing condition is itself an injury**, where
  employment is a significant contributing factor to the aggravation. ⚠ **s 32(4): it is an injury
  only to the extent of the effects of the aggravation.**

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **A report can acknowledge every other contributor and still be decisive.** The doctor does
not have to claim work was the only cause — **and a report that overclaims is easier to break than
one that concedes and still concludes.**

# PART 5 — ⛔⛔ THE COLLEAGUES: a correction, and the right architecture

> **The proposition: the psychiatrist contacts multiple work colleagues about the workplace.**

## 5.1 ⛔ DO NOT HAVE THE DOCTOR GATHER IT

**Collateral history is clinically ordinary. It is forensically dangerous when the clinician
collects lay factual material about disputed events.**

**What happens in cross-examination:**
> *You spoke to Mr X? · Did you take a statement? · Did you test what he told you? · Did you know
> he and Mr Shepherd are friends? · How did you weigh his account against the employer's? · You
> were not present for any of it?*

⇒ ⛔⛔ **The doctor becomes a witness to disputed facts — which is exactly the vulnerability the
assumed-facts structure exists to prevent (Part 1).** ⚠ **And it puts the colleagues' accounts into
the report as hearsay, where they cannot be tested and are worth little.**

⭐ **It also raises a confidentiality problem: contacting colleagues discloses that the patient is
under psychiatric care.**

## 5.2 ⭐⭐⭐ THE RIGHT ARCHITECTURE IS THE REVERSE

1. **The colleagues give their own evidence** — named on the **witness list (Guide 4.9)**, with a
   **one-page outline each (4.10)**, and **oral evidence at the hearing (7.6)**.
2. **Their accounts become assumed facts** in the letter of instruction.
3. **The doctor opines on those assumed facts.**

⇒ ⭐⭐ **The corroboration becomes admissible instead of embedded — and it is proved by the people
who witnessed it.**

## 5.3 ⭐ WHAT COLLEAGUES CAN PROVE THAT NOTHING ELSE CAN

- **The load** — that the shifts ran at the volume he describes.
- ⭐⭐ **An observed change in him across the period** — which is not his evidence and not a
  document. **This is the one thing no other source supplies.**
- **That the directives arrived as described**, and that nobody was consulted.

## 5.4 ⚠⚠ AND THE COST, WHICH IS REAL

- **They are MSH employees.** Being a witness against your employer is a serious ask, and it is
  their risk to take, not his to assume.
- ⛔ **The file already records the strategic cost of adding witnesses: it re-personalises the
  matter.** ⭐⭐ **Everything in this case has been moved from *people* to *system*. Colleague
  evidence can move it back.**

⇒ ⭐⭐ **Use colleague evidence for what only it can prove — the load, and the observed change.**
⛔ **Not to re-litigate any individual's conduct.** ⚠ And approach through the union, not directly.

# PART 6 — ⛔⛔ THE ECC TRAP: the report can accidentally destroy the pay claim

**The 3 July 2026 ECC certifies him FIT WITH ADJUSTMENTS.** The RWA pay claim runs on exactly that:
*ready, willing and able; excluded by the employer; wages owed.*

⛔⛔ **If Report B says he is totally unfit for work, it contradicts his own employment position and
undermines the pay claim** — which is currently his best near-term income route
(`FRESH-WORKCOVER-CLAIM-DECISION.md` Part 4).

⭐ **The two can sit together, but only if the report is precise:** capacity for the substantive
role **with the identified adjustments**, and any current incapacity addressed as a consequence of
the exclusion and its handling — **not as an inherent inability to work.**

⇒ ⛔ **Raise this with the doctor on Wednesday. It is a real risk and it is invisible unless
someone names it.**

# PART 7 — THE CHECKS ON WHAT COMES BACK

| ⛔ Check | Why |
|---|---|
| **No *"not for medico-legal use"* footer** | Confirm **in writing before the appointment** |
| **The s 32(1) question answered in terms** — *a significant contributing factor* | The gate. **Not "is he unwell" and not "is he fit for work"** |
| **The 2026 continuity question answered separately** | So it can be used, or not used, independently |
| **The 26 Oct 2022 entries addressed** | ⛔ Or it is met cold in cross |
| **A complete sources list** | The first thing attacked |
| **The assumed-facts declaration present** | Part 1 |
| **Differentials named and reasoned, not omitted** | Part 4 |
| **Capacity expressed consistently with the ECC** | Part 6 |
| **The opinion reasoned from the facts, not asserted** | *"Because A, B and C, in my opinion…"* — **an asserted conclusion is worth little; a reasoned one survives** |
