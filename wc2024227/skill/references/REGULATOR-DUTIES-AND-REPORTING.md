# THE WORKERS' COMPENSATION REGULATOR — STATUTORY DUTIES, FUNCTIONS AND REPORTING

**Researched 16 September 2026 direct from the Act.**
**Source:** *Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003* (Qld), current in-force consolidation,
https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2003-027 — full text pulled and
searched. All quotations below are verbatim from that consolidation. ⚠ Re-check currency before any
provision is put in a filing.

---

## 1. WHO THE REGULATOR IS — ch 7 pt 1

**s 326** — the office of the Workers' Compensation Regulator is established (1); the Governor in
Council **may appoint a public service officer** as the Regulator (2); appointed under the *Public
Sector Act 2022* (3). And:

> **s 326(4):** *"The Regulator **must act independently when making a decision under this Act** but
> otherwise is subject to direction in the person's capacity as a public service officer or an
> officer of the department."*

⭐ That is the only express *duty* language attaching to the Regulator's own conduct in ch 7, and it
is confined to **decision-making**. It is the provision behind the review decision (RD69983), not
behind its conduct as a litigant.

## 2. THE FUNCTIONS — s 327(1), verbatim and complete

> *(a)* to regulate the workers' compensation scheme;
> *(b)* **to monitor the compliance of insurers with this Act;**
> *(c)* **to monitor the performance of insurers under this Act, including the consistent application of this Act;**
> *(d)* to decide applications relating to self-insurance;
> *(e)* to approve amounts payable under an industrial instrument for the purposes of s 107B;
> *(f)* **to undertake reviews of decisions under ch 13, pt 2 and manage appeals under ch 13, pt 3;**
> *(g)* to support and oversee the efficient administration of medical assessment tribunals;
> *(h)* to undertake workplace rehabilitation and return to work accreditation activities;
> *(i)* to provide rehabilitation advisory services;
> *(j)* **to maintain a database for scheme-wide reporting;**
> *(k)* to promote education about the workers' compensation scheme;
> *(l)* to collect fees under the Act;
> *(m)* to administer grants under the Act;
> *(n)* **to conduct and defend proceedings under this Act before a court or tribunal;**
> *(o)* to perform other functions given to the Regulator under this or another Act.

s 327(2) declares proceedings under (1)(n) may be taken by or against it in the name *"the Workers'
Compensation Regulator"*.

### ⛔⛔ THE DECISIVE POINT

**(b) and (c) are about INSURERS. Not employers. Not workplaces.**

There is **no function** of monitoring an employer's conduct, inspecting a workplace, investigating a
hazard, or enforcing work health and safety. The Regulator regulates **the scheme and the insurers**.

⇒ **"The Regulator should have been onto that" is not a legal duty.** It cannot be argued as one.

**Other powers:** s 328 (all things necessary or convenient, plus all the powers of an authorised
person) · s 329 (delegation) · s 329A (scheme directions, with the Minister's approval; ins 2024) ·
s 329B (scheme registers; ins 2024).

## 3. REPORTING OBLIGATIONS — WHAT THE ACT ACTUALLY REQUIRES

### ⛔ The Regulator has NO annual report obligation under this Act

**Every "annual report" provision in the WCRA is WorkCover's, not the Regulator's** — searched
exhaustively:

| Provision | Whose |
|---|---|
| **s 404** Additional matters in statement of corporate intent — information to the Minister in quarterly and annual reports | **WorkCover** |
| **s 411** Quarterly reports | **WorkCover** |
| **s 412** Matters to be included in annual report — *"Each annual report of WorkCover must…"* | **WorkCover** |
| **s 413** Deletion of commercially sensitive matters from annual report | **WorkCover** |
| **s 414** **Board** to keep Minister informed | **WorkCover's board** |

WorkCover is a statutory body with a board and a statement of corporate intent. The Regulator is a
**public service office**. None of ss 404–414 touches it.

### What the Regulator IS required to do

| Duty | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Maintain a database for scheme-wide reporting** | **s 327(1)(j)** — a *function*, with no prescribed output, frequency or audience |
| **Give written notice of a review decision to the applicant and the decision-maker within 10 business days** | **s 546(1)** ⭐ a real, dated duty — RD69983 is its product |
| Publish the approved self-insurer levy rate **in the gazette** | s 96-area levy provision |
| Give written notice, with reasons, on self-insurance licence refusals, cancellations and compliance notices | ss 71–72, 96, and the compliance-notice provisions |

**In practice** the Regulator publishes the *Queensland workers' compensation scheme statistics*
report annually (released yearly since 2000, via WorkSafe Queensland), and its accountability
reporting runs through the **Office of Industrial Relations' departmental annual report**. Both are
**administrative practice and general public-sector accountability, not WCRA duties.**

## 4. IS THERE A DUTY TO REPORT A HAZARD, OR REFER TO WHSQ? — **NO**

### s 573 — Permissible disclosure of information

> **s 573(3):** *"The Regulator or WorkCover **may** disclose, to the chief executive of the
> department within which the *Work Health and Safety Act 2011* is administered, **statistical or
> other information that would help in the performance of its administrative functions**."*

> **s 573(1A):** the WHS chief executive **may** disclose to the Regulator or WorkCover any
> information relating to a matter under this Act.

⛔ **Both are permissive — "may", not "must"** — and s 573(3) is framed around *statistical or other
information* for *administrative functions*, not the referral of an unsafe workplace.

**There is no provision anywhere in the WCRA requiring the Regulator to report, refer or notify a
workplace hazard to Workplace Health and Safety Queensland or to anyone else.**

### The one place an OHS report IS mandatory — and it does not apply here

**ss 71 and 72** (self-insurance licensing): *"the Regulator **must ask** the chief executive of the
department within which the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 is administered to prepare an **OHS
report** about the employer."* "OHS report" is defined in sch 6 as a report on occupational health
and safety performance prepared under the WHS Act, sch 2, pt 3.

⛔ **These bite only on self-insurance licence issue/renewal. Metro South is not a self-insurer** —
**WorkCover Queensland is the insurer** (the decision of 25 July 2024, Amy Mo, Claims Representative,
WorkCover Queensland). **ss 71–72 have no application to this matter.**

### Two related cross-links, for completeness

- **s 330(2)(b):** a person appointed as an inspector under the WHS Act **is taken to be an authorised
  person appointed by the Regulator** while they hold that appointment. ⚠ Note the direction: WHS
  inspectors acquire WC powers. The Regulator does **not** acquire WHS powers.
- **s 385A:** **WorkCover** (not the Regulator) *"may fund and provide programs and incentives to
  encourage improved health and safety performance by employers"*, and must first consult the
  regulator under the WHS Act. Permissive, and it is WorkCover's.

## 5. ⭐ THE ONE PROVISION THAT IS ACTUALLY USEFUL — s 5(1)(b)

> **s 5 Workers' compensation scheme**
> *(1) This Act establishes a workers' compensation scheme for Queensland—*
> *(a) providing benefits for workers who sustain injury in their employment…; and*
> **_(b) encouraging improved health and safety performance by employers._**

This sits in the opening provisions, as a statement of what the scheme **is for**.

✅ **Legitimate use:** in closing, on purpose and construction — a psychological injury arising from a
workplace where the hazard was never assessed is squarely within what the scheme exists to address,
and s 32(5)(a) should not be read so as to defeat the object in s 5(1)(b).

⛔ **Illegitimate use:** it creates **no duty owed to him**, no cause of action, and no obligation on
the Regulator to have done anything. Do not plead it as a breach. It is a construction point and
nothing more.

## 6. THE APPEAL ARCHITECTURE — ch 13 pt 3 (settles the pinpoint discrepancy)

| Provision | Effect |
|---|---|
| **s 548A(1)** | *"An appeal body for this division is the industrial commission."* |
| **⭐ s 549(1)** | *"A claimant, worker or employer aggrieved by the decision (the **appellant**) may appeal to an appeal body against the decision of the Regulator or the insurer (the **respondent**)."* ← **the appeal right** |
| **s 550(1)(a)** | 20 business days after receiving notice of the review decision |
| **s 550(4)** | *"The appeal may be started **only by filing a written notice of appeal** with the appeal body."* ← filing mechanic |
| **s 550(4A)** | If the appeal body is the industrial commission, the notice must be filed **in the industrial registry** |
| **s 552A** | *"the industrial commission **may**, before the hearing of the matter, **call a conference** of the parties. (2) **The parties must attend the conference.**"* ← the 13 March 2026 conference |
| **⚠ s 552B** | *"A party may be represented by a lawyer at a conference called under s 552A or at the hearing of an appeal, **but only with— (a) the agreement of the parties; or (b) the appeal body's leave**."* |
| **s 553** | UCPR ch 7 pt 2 and ch 9 pt 4, and the *Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011*, apply |
| **s 557** | Appeal body may order anything necessary be supplied, or defects/errors corrected |
| **s 558(1)** | Confirm · vary · set aside and substitute · set aside and return with directions |
| **s 558(3)** | **Costs of the hearing are in the appeal body's discretion**, except as provided by regulation |

### ✅ PINPOINT RESOLVED

`CLAUDE.md` cites **s 550(4)**; `CURRENT.md` cites **s 549**. **Both describe real things, and neither
is wrong — they are different provisions.** The **appeal right is s 549**; **s 550 is procedure**, and
s 550(4) is the filing step. **On a cover sheet or in a heading, cite s 549.**

### ⚠ ACTION — s 552B and the March conference

Legal representation at a s 552A conference requires the **agreement of the parties** or the **appeal
body's leave**. Recollection is that the Regulator had a barrister present at the 13 March 2026
conference. **That is recollection, not record.** ⚠ Establish, before it is relied on for anything:
whether a lawyer appeared, whether leave was granted or agreement given, and whether it appears in
any order or listing notice. If leave was neither sought nor given, that is a matter to raise
procedurally and carefully — **not an accusation**, and not a merits point.

## 7. THE BOTTOM LINE FOR THIS APPEAL

1. **The Regulator has no duty to police employers or workplaces.** Its monitoring functions run to
   **insurers only** (s 327(1)(b), (c)). Any argument that it "should have been onto" the Switchboard
   is a practice expectation, not a legal obligation, and must not be pleaded as one.
2. **It has no mandatory duty to refer anything to WHSQ.** s 573(3) is permissive and is about
   statistical and administrative information.
3. **It has no annual report obligation under the WCRA.** All such duties are WorkCover's (ss 404–414).
4. **Its role here is s 327(1)(f) and (n)** — manage the appeal, and **conduct and defend proceedings**.
   Its obligations in that role are disclosure under the Rules and the **Queensland Model Litigant
   Principles**, which Dwyer IC invoked expressly at the mention [~59:45]: *"They have responsibilities
   as a model litigant… they can't hide stuff from you, they can't be sneaky or tricky."*
5. **Model litigant does not mean "build the appellant's case."** It means candour, disclosure, and
   not taking technical points. Measured against that standard, the Regulator's conduct on the one
   occasion it was tested — the response of 8 September 2026 — was **298 of 303 facts admitted, none
   denied, three days inside the rule 49 deadline**. That is compliant conduct and should be
   acknowledged as such, not attacked.
6. ⭐ **The duty he is looking for exists — but it is Metro South's, under the WHS Act, not the
   Regulator's.** See `HEARING-PLAN.md` Part 4A §4A.6: WHS is a yardstick of reasonableness for
   s 32(5)(a), never a charge, and never in this forum as a cause of action.
