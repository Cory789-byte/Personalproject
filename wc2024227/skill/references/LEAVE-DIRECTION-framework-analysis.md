## THE THREE POSSIBLE CHARACTERISATIONS — AND EACH ONE'S PROBLEM
Whatever occurred on 3 July is one of three things. Every one of them fails as administered.

### (A) IF IT IS A SUSPENSION → PS Act 2022 s 101 (verified)
- s 101(1): only **the chief executive** may suspend, **by notice**, and only on a
  reasonable belief that the employee is liable to discipline, or that proper and efficient
  management might be prejudiced.
- s 101(2): the notice **must state** when the suspension starts and ends, **whether the
  employee is entitled to remuneration**, and the effect of alternative employment.
- s 101(3): **before** suspending, the chief executive **must consider all reasonable
  alternatives** — the Act's own examples are *alternative duties*, *a change in location*,
  *another alternative working arrangement*.
- s 101(4): **"The employee is entitled to normal remuneration for the period of the
  suspension"**, unless suspended under (1)(a) (discipline) AND the chief executive
  considers it inappropriate having regard to the discipline believed.
- s 101(9): the chief executive must comply with the s 102 directive.
⇒ No notice. No start/end dates. No remuneration statement. No recorded consideration of
alternatives (he PROPOSED them — the certified restrictions, the return-to-work proposal).
No discipline alleged, so the unpaid exception cannot even be reached.
**If it is a suspension, remuneration was payable throughout.**

### (B) IF IT IS "LEAVE" → the leave instruments do not permit what is being done
**Recreation/annual leave — IR Act s 33 (verified):**
- s 33(1): the employee **and** employer **may agree** when leave is taken.
- s 33(3): if they cannot agree, the employer may decide **but must give at least
  8 weeks' written notice** of the starting date.
⇒ He does not agree. There is no 8 weeks' notice — there is a same-day payroll deadline.
The only other route is s 34 (an industrial instrument may require leave to be taken in
particular circumstances, "but only if the requirement is reasonable") — EB12 contains no
such requirement for this situation; cl 9.7 deals only with half-pay applications *by the
employee*.
**Sick leave — IR Act ss 40, 41 (verified):** the entitlement is conditional on the
employee notifying **"any illness that will cause the employee to be absent from work"**.
⇒ He has not notified an illness causing absence. **He was certified FIT with restrictions
on 3 July 2026 and has been ready, willing and able throughout.** Sick leave — paid or
unpaid — is not the applicable instrument for an absence the employer directed. Recording
it as sick leave misstates the reason for the absence in the employer's own records.

### (C) IF IT IS NEITHER → wages are simply payable
An employee ready, willing and able to work whose labour the employer declines to accept,
without lawful authority, remains entitled to wages. That is the foundation of the dispute's
forward-pay limb. ⚠ Pinpoint authority to be confirmed before any filing.

## THE DISPUTE-PROCEDURE OVERLAY — EB12 cl 1.11 (verified)
- cl 1.11.2(a): Stage 1 discussions **should take place within 24 hours** and the procedure
  **should not extend beyond seven days**. ⭐ Taylor's own letter: *"the 24-hour timeframe
  specified in the clause will not be achieved."*
- cl 1.11.4: **"while the dispute procedure is being followed, normal work is to continue...
  The status quo existing before the emergence of a dispute is to continue whilst the
  procedure is being followed. No party shall be prejudiced as to the final settlement by
  the continuation of work."**
  ⇒ Debiting the last 41.21 hours of leave, and recording unpaid absence, DURING the
  dispute, on the very subject matter of the dispute, is prejudice of exactly the kind the
  clause forbids.
- cl 1.11.5: **"no party shall act in a manner unreasonably or intentionally delay the
  timely resolution of a dispute."**

## THE STANDING CONTRADICTION IN THEIR OWN LETTER
Paragraph 1 calls it **"the decision that has prevented you from attending your rostered
shifts"**. Paragraph 3 asks him to treat the same period as **his leave**.
An absence cannot simultaneously be (i) caused by the employer's decision preventing
attendance, and (ii) leave taken by the employee. The letter needs both propositions and
they are mutually exclusive.

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY MISSING FROM THE RECORD (the ask, unchanged)
1. The instrument relied on to prevent attendance from 3 July 2026.
2. The identity of the decision-maker and the date of the decision.
3. Whether it is a suspension under s 101 and, if so, the notice required by s 101(2).
4. What reasonable alternatives were considered under s 101(3), and by whom.
5. The basis on which sick leave is recorded for an employee certified fit.

## POLICY / PROCEDURE LAYER
EB12 cl 1.13 preserves the Schedule 2 HR policies as terms of the Agreement — including
**E12 Individual employee grievances**, **E13 Workplace Harassment**, **F3 Access to
Employee Records** and **F4 Union Encouragement**. cl 1.12.1 routes grievances to Award
cl 7.2. Health Employment Directives and the s 102 suspension directive sit above local
practice: **payroll cut-off dates are an administrative convenience and are not a source
of power** — no instrument permits a processing deadline to override a statutory notice
period or the status quo obligation.
