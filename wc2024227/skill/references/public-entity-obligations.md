# MSH as a PUBLIC ENTITY — how the 28 July letter and its attachments bind them, and what a failure
# to apply them fairly/correctly amounts to

> Research-grounded (verified 28 Jul 2026 against EB12 source PDF, Directive 12/24, QH-POL-210,
> HRA 2019 ss 58/59 via legislation.qld.gov.au/austlii + QHRC). Strategy/union-brief use. NOT for the
> WC/64G track. Remedies section is deliberately honest about limits.

## 1. WHY "PUBLIC ENTITY" MATTERS
MSH (a Hospital and Health Service) is a **public entity** under the Human Rights Act 2019 (Qld).
That adds a PUBLIC-LAW layer on top of the industrial layer. Private employers owe the contract,
award and agreement. A public entity owes those PLUS: statutory obligations under the PS Act and
directives, its own published policies (administrative-law consistency), and HRA s 58.

## 2. THE FOUR LAYERS ENGAGED BY THE 28 JULY LETTER (strongest → weakest)

### LAYER 1 — CERTIFIED AGREEMENT (the strongest; verified)
EB12 is a certified agreement under the IR Act 2016 — an **enforceable industrial instrument**, not a
policy. Verified provisions engaged:
- **6.1** Employment Security.
- **7.1.5** parties agree to ADDRESS: (b) fatigue risk management; **(f) injured workers to have the
  opportunity to be RE-TRAINED IN ALTERNATIVE AREAS/DEPARTMENTS**; (g) injury management; (h)
  management of ill or injured employees; (j) psychosocial issues + Code 2022; (k) security for
  administrative staff in frontline positions.
- **7.1.11** appropriate FEEDBACK to employees who raise WHS matters.
- **7.2.1** support for workplace audits/psychosocial risk assessments "as requested through a HCF
  either by an employer **or a union party**."
- **9.12.1** Directive 12/24 Special Leave "applies to all employees covered by this Agreement."
ENFORCEMENT: the agreement's **dispute-settling procedure** → QIRC (conciliation/arbitration). A
certified agreement is enforceable; breach can also attract IR Act contravention proceedings/penalties.
This is the layer with real teeth and the one the UNION runs.

### LAYER 2 — DIRECTIVE + POLICY (administrative-law consistency)
- **Directive 12/24 (Special Leave)** — made under s 223 PS Act; applies to HHS employees (cl 4.1(b)(ii)).
  ⚠️ **Discretionary** (cl 6.1 "may approve... any purpose"), BUT cl 6.5 makes the CE's considerations
  **mandatory**: reason, duration, and **the impact on the employee if the leave is not approved**.
  → Failing to consider those factors is a defective exercise of discretion (a reviewable error in
  administrative-law terms), even though the outcome itself is discretionary.
- **QH-POL-210 Reasonable Adjustment** — applies to **ALL** QH/HHS employees; covers TEMPORARY
  adjustment during rehabilitation/RTW and adjustment for capacity restriction. No accepted-claim limit.
- **QH-IMP-401-5 Workplace Rehabilitation Standard** — rehab/suitable duties may be provided to
  employees WITHOUT an accepted WC claim where operationally reasonable; managers responsible for
  identifying suitable duties.
PRINCIPLE: a public authority that departs from its own published policy, without reasons and without
considering the individual case, acts unreasonably/inconsistently — the classic administrative-law
criticism. Not usually a standalone cause of action in an employment setting, but powerful in the
QIRC dispute, before a union, and as evidence of unreasonableness.

### LAYER 3 — HUMAN RIGHTS ACT 2019 (Qld) s 58 (the "public entity" layer)
**s 58(1): it is UNLAWFUL for a public entity (a) to act or make a decision in a way NOT COMPATIBLE
with human rights; or (b) in making a decision, to FAIL TO GIVE PROPER CONSIDERATION to a human right
relevant to the decision.**
Rights plausibly engaged on these facts (each subject to the s 13 proportionality limit):
- **Recognition and equality before the law (s 15)** — differential treatment connected with an
  impairment; equal protection without discrimination.
- **Privacy and reputation (s 25)** — handling/disclosure of medical information; the framing of his
  condition as a workplace "psychosocial hazard."
- **Protection from torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment (s 17)** — HIGH threshold; do
  NOT plead on these facts.
- (Property/work rights are NOT protected as such — Qld's HRA contains no free-standing right to work.
  Be accurate: the vehicle is equality + privacy, not "a right to my job.")
**s 58(5)-(6):** proper consideration means identifying the human rights that may be affected and
considering whether the decision would be compatible — practically, and without requiring legal
technicality.
→ APPLIED HERE: an indefinite, unpaid exclusion of a certified-fit employee, decided in 58 minutes,
with no consultation, no risk assessment, no timeframe, and no identified instrument, is a decision in
which there is **no evidence any human right was identified or considered at all**. That is the s 58(1)(b)
failure — and it is easier to establish than incompatibility itself.

### LAYER 4 — ANTI-DISCRIMINATION ACT 1991 (Qld)
Impairment is a protected attribute; work is a covered area; **positive duty in force since 1 July 2025**
to take reasonable and proportionate measures to eliminate discrimination. Blanket exclusion on
unparticularised assumed risk, against a treating doctor's fit-with-restrictions certificate, with no
individualised assessment, is the classic failure-to-adjust / direct-discrimination pattern. QHRC
complaint jurisdiction; QIRC for work-related complaints.

## 3. SO — IS A FAILURE TO APPLY THEM FAIRLY A "BREACH"? (honest answer)
- **Of the certified agreement** — YES, potentially a genuine, enforceable contravention. Strongest.
- **Of the directive/policies** — a failure to comply/consider; not usually a standalone action, but
  it is unlawfulness in the administrative sense and evidence of unreasonableness.
- **Of human rights (s 58)** — YES, s 58 expressly makes non-compatible conduct, and failure to give
  proper consideration, **UNLAWFUL**. But see the remedy catch below.
- **Of the AD Act** — arguable and independently actionable.

## 4. THE REMEDY CATCH (must be understood before anyone leans on the HRA)
**HRA s 59: there is NO free-standing cause of action for a s 58 breach, and NO damages.** A person may
"piggyback" the s 58 unlawfulness onto another proceeding in which they may seek relief for
unlawfulness — and may succeed on the human-rights ground even if the other ground fails — but
**s 59(3): the person is NOT entitled to be awarded damages** on the s 58 ground.
CONSEQUENCE / CORRECT USE:
1. **QHRC human-rights complaint** — the direct route (complaint about a s 58 contravention; conciliation-
   based). Available, low-cost, but conciliation, not compensation.
2. **Piggyback** — attach the s 58 argument to a proceeding that already exists (e.g. the QIRC dispute,
   or an AD Act complaint) to strengthen it.
3. **Leverage/persuasion** — for a PUBLIC HEALTH SERVICE, "you made a decision affecting an employee's
   equality and privacy rights without any evidence you considered them" is a serious internal
   compliance problem, even where damages are unavailable. It moves decision-makers.
→ So: the HRA is a **pressure and framing instrument**, and a real unlawfulness finding — but the
MONEY and the ORDERS come from the certified agreement (QIRC dispute) and, if ever needed, the AD Act
and general protections. Do NOT let anyone (including Cory) build the strategy on the HRA alone.

## 5. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE 28 JULY LETTER
The letter, without saying any of the above, has already put MSH in the position where each of the
four layers is engaged by their own answer (or non-answer):
- It cites the agreement clauses → Layer 1 engaged.
- It demands the delegate and instrument for each decision → forces a decision-maker to own the
  exercise of discretion → Layer 2 (and exposes whether cl 6.5 factors were considered).
- It asks them to identify what was considered and rejected → tests whether ANY consideration occurred
  → Layer 3 (s 58(1)(b) proper consideration).
- It records certified fitness + blanket refusal → Layer 4 evidence.
**Non-response is not neutral**: it is evidence that no considered decision exists to disclose.

## 6. DISCIPLINE
- Do NOT put the human-rights/AD Act framing into correspondence with MSH yet — the letter is
  strongest as an INSTRUMENT-COMPLIANCE demand. Rights framing is for the union brief and, if needed,
  the QHRC/AD Act route later (rule 8 sequencing).
- Never plead s 17 (cruel/inhuman/degrading) — wrong threshold, damages credibility.
- Verify any HRA section pinpoint before it enters a filing.
