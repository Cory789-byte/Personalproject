# 2. ⭐⭐⭐ FOUR RULES FOR GIVING IT

## 2.1 ⭐⭐⭐ CLOCK IT — DO NOT DESCRIBE IT
> Finished at 23:00. Handover took ___. Left the hospital at ___. Home at ___. In bed at ___. Alarm
> at ___. Left home at ___. Clocked on at 06:00.

⭐⭐⭐ **The number that falls out of that is his, it is arithmetic, and it is SMALLER THAN SEVEN.**
⛔ **"I was exhausted" can be disputed. "I was in bed for four hours and ten minutes" cannot.**

## 2.2 ⭐⭐ SAY WHAT HE DID, IN ORDER — NOT WHAT IT WAS LIKE
⭐⭐ **Use the admitted duty language**: *"I received emergency response notifications and distributed
them to the response groups according to the category of emergency."* ⇒ **That sentence is already
admitted at fact 8. It cannot be argued with.**
⛔⛔ **"It was relentless", "it was constant", "it was chaos" — a Commissioner discounts adjectives.**
⭐ **The facts do the work; the adjectives undo it.** ⛔⛔ **And Dwyer has already said what he does with
material he finds overwritten** [09:55, 21:46].

## 2.3 ⭐⭐⭐ DESCRIBE WHAT HE NOTICED, IN ORDINARY WORDS — THEN STOP
⭐⭐ **Small, concrete, first-person:** checking a code category twice · re-reading a pager number ·
how long it took to get to sleep afterwards · what he did on 19 March.
⛔⛔⛔ **NOT "I was fatigued" — that is a conclusion. NOT a diagnosis, a symptom name, or a date of
onset in clinical terms.** ⭐⭐⭐ **His own outline ¶8 commits him: *"He offers no clinical or diagnostic
opinion."* KEEP THAT PROMISE IN THE BOX.** ⇒ **It is worth more than anything he could add by
breaking it.**

## 2.4 ⭐⭐⭐ DO NOT REACH FOR THE CONSEQUENCE
⛔⛔ **He must not say the shifts caused the injury.** ⭐⭐⭐ **Fact 262 already has the REGULATOR saying
employment was a significant contributing factor, and the treating practitioner supplies the rest.**
⇒ ⛔⛔⛔ **If he draws the conclusion himself he converts evidence into argument and hands them the
cross-examination.** ⭐⭐ **Give the facts. Let their own document and his doctor close it.**
