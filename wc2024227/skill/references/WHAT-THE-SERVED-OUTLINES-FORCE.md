> call Ms Taylor to give her side of the story and you'll get to cross-examine Ms Taylor."*

Harrison-Jones's outline, **¶ 6**, is precisely that event:

> *"An occasion on which he was present in the Switchboard room… what Ms Taylor said to the

exactly the position Dwyer described twice (*"if you say something and it's not contradicted…
there's a likelihood that it'll be accepted"*).

## 5 · ⚠⚠ The risk that comes with Ms Conaghan

She is **still employed** in the department. Her name and the substance of her evidence were served
on 9 September, and the Respondent may pass both to Metro South Health in the course of preparing
its own material (see `THE-CONVERSATION-MATHESON-MUST-HAVE-WITH-MSH.md`).

⛔ **No allegation is made or implied about anyone's conduct.** But as a matter of ordinary
prudence:

1. ⭐ **Do not discuss her evidence with her.** Any suggestion of coaching would damage both of you,
   and she is on the list precisely because her account is her own.
2. **If she becomes unavailable or reluctant, do not chase it privately** — an attendance notice
   under Guide Part 6 exists for that, and the Appellant pays her expenses (Guide 10.1, IR(T) r 62).
3. **She may also be a witness the Respondent calls.** Nothing prevents it. Her outline is served,
   so both sides know what she says.
4. Note her protections if anything arises: **IR Act s 284** (participating in a Commission
   proceeding is a workplace right) and **s 306** (reverse onus). ⛔ Record only. Not to be raised
   unless something actually happens.
