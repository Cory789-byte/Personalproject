# PERSONAL LIABILITY — HUGHES AND TAYLOR: does acting for the Health Service protect them?

> All provisions verified against the in-force text downloaded 31 July 2026 and filed at
> `documents/instruments/`. **ATT22** Industrial Relations Act 2016 (current 1 Jan 2026);
> **ATT23** Public Interest Disclosure Act 2010 (current 30 Aug 2024); ATT19 PS Act 2022;
> ATT20 AD Act 1991; ATT17 WHS Act 2011.

## 0. THE SHORT ANSWER

**No.** Two different questions are being conflated:

1. **Is the conduct lawful?** Acting for the employer is irrelevant to this. The general protections
   prohibit conduct by **a person**, and deem individuals who advise or incite the employer to have
   contravened.
2. **Who pays?** Acting in an official capacity *does* matter here — **PS Act 2022 s 269** transfers
   monetary liability to the body corporate.

⇒ **What they have is an indemnity, not an immunity.** The contravention is still found, the
individual is still named, and the transfer is confined to *payment of an amount* — it does not
reach injunctions, declarations, criminal liability, or discipline. And it is **forfeited by bad
faith plus gross negligence** (s 269(3)–(4)).

## 1. THE GENERAL PROTECTIONS — IR Act 2016 (Qld) ch 8 pt 1

Metro South HHS is a State employer. The **IR Act 2016 (Qld)** applies, not the FW Act
(and **s 281(a)** expressly carves out anything to which FW Act ch 3 pt 3-1 applies).

### 1.1 The prohibition binds *a person*, not *an employer*
> **s 285(1)** "**A person** must not take adverse action against another person— (a) because the
> other person— (i) has a workplace right; or (ii) has, or has not, exercised a workplace right…
> or (b) **to prevent the exercise of a workplace right**."
> *Note— This subsection is a civil penalty provision.*

### 1.2 But the *adverse action* definition is employer-facing
> **s 282(1)** "Adverse action is taken **by an employer** against an employee if the employer—
> (b) **injures the employee in the employee's employment**; or (c) **alters the position of the
> employee to the employee's prejudice**; or (d) discriminates between the employee and other
> employees."
> **s 282(5)** includes **threatening** and **organising** to take that action.

⇒ Hughes and Taylor are not the employer. **They are not primary contraventors on this limb.**
Everything runs through the three deeming/extension provisions below. **That is the honest
architecture — do not overstate it.**

### 1.3 ⭐ s 307 — the direct route, and the one that fits these facts
> **s 307** "**A person is taken to contravene a provision of this part** if— (a) for a particular
> reason, the person **advises, encourages or incites**, or takes any action with intent to coerce,
> **another person to take action**; and (b) the action, if taken by the other person for that
> reason, would contravene the provision."

This is a **deeming** provision, not accessorial liability. It does not require the s 571(3)(c)
knowledge element. It is the precise profile of **a line manager and a Director recommending an
exclusion to the Health Service.**

Reinforced by the scope provision:
> **s 280** "this part applies to the following action— … (c) action that consists of **advising,
> encouraging or inciting**, or action taken with intent to coerce, **an employer**— (i) to take, or
> not take, particular action **in relation to another person**."

### 1.4 s 571 — accessorial liability (the FW Act s 550 equivalent)
> **s 571(2)** "**A person involved in a contravention** of a civil penalty provision **is taken to
> have contravened the provision.**"
> **s 571(3)** involved = "(a) **aided, abetted, counselled or procured**; or (b) has **induced**…
> whether by threats, promises or otherwise; or (c) has been in any way, by act or omission,
> directly or indirectly **knowingly concerned in or party to**; or (d) has **conspired with
> others**."

### 1.5 The onus is reversed — and it falls on the individual personally
> **s 306(2)** "It is **presumed** that the action was… taken for that reason or with that intent,
> **unless the person proves otherwise**."
> **s 305** "a person takes action for a particular reason if the **reasons for the action include
> that reason**."

⇒ Once the adverse action and the workplace right are established, **each individual joined must
give evidence and discharge the onus personally.** A genuine WHS concern sitting alongside a
prohibited reason does **not** save the action (s 305).

### 1.6 What can be ordered
> **s 314(1)** reinstatement; compensation; payment for remuneration lost; **continuity of
> employment**; **continuity of service**; and **(f) an interim or other injunction or any other
> order… to prevent, stop, or remedy the effects of, a contravention.**

**Schedule 3** — for **ss 285(1) and (2)**, **295(1) Discrimination** and **296(1) Domestic
violence**:
| Applicants | Tribunal | Max penalty for an **individual** |
|---|---|---|
| (a) **a person affected by the contravention** (Cory) · (b) an industrial organisation entitled to represent his interests (**Together**) · (c) an inspector | **the commission (QIRC)** | **90 penalty units** |

> **s 574(1)** the tribunal "may… order **a person** to pay a penalty".
> **s 575(a)** individual — capped at the schedule 3 figure; **(b)** corporation — **5 times** it.
> **s 576** the penalty may be ordered paid to **the State**, an organisation, **or a particular
> person** — i.e. **to Cory**.
> **s 578** civil double jeopardy — one civil penalty per course of conduct.

### 1.7 ⚠️ THE ONLY SHIELD INSIDE THE GENERAL PROTECTIONS — and it is the one they do not have
> **s 282(6)** "**Adverse action does not include action that is authorised under**— (a) this Act or
> **any other law of the State**; or (b) a law of the Commonwealth."

**This is the payoff of the citation work.** If the exclusion were authorised by an instrument,
s 282(6) would take it outside "adverse action" altogether and the whole part would fall away.

⇒ Across **five weeks, four officers and seven items of correspondence**, MSH identified **two
instruments**: HR Policy G3 (a *policy*, not a law) and WHS Act ss 17 and 19 (*duties on MSH*,
conferring no power over a worker). **Neither authorises an exclusion.**
⇒ MSH's inability to name a power is not merely an administrative-law defect. **It removes the
statutory carve-out that would otherwise be its complete answer** — and it removes it **on MSH's own
correspondence**. See `msh-citations-complete-list.md` and `what-power-did-MSH-use.md`.

### 1.8 ⭐ NEW — s 296: DOMESTIC VIOLENCE IS PROTECTED UNDER THE IR ACT
> **s 296(1)** "An employer **must not take adverse action** against a person who is an employee…
> **because someone has committed, or is committing, domestic violence against the person**."
> *Note— This subsection is a civil penalty provision.*
> **s 296(2)** "it is **not necessary** that the person have the benefit of, or be named as the
> aggrieved under" a DVO, police protection direction, police protection notice, or an application.

⇒ **This changes the March 2025 DFV leave dispute.** We confirmed DV is **not** a protected
attribute under **AD Act s 7**. It **is** protected under the IR Act — with no requirement for an
order or an application, and with the s 306 reverse onus. And via **ss 307 and 571** it reaches
individuals. **This is a materially better statutory home for the 2025 conduct than anything
previously identified.**

### 1.9 ⭐ LIMITATION — the open union question, answered
> **s 310(1)** dismissal — **21 days** (extendable only for exceptional circumstances).
> **s 310(3)** "An application relating to a contravention of this part (**other than dismissal**)
> must be made **within 6 years** after the contravention occurs."
> **s 573** civil penalty applications — also **6 years**.

⇒ **Six years for non-dismissal.** The 2025 conduct and the July 2026 exclusion are both well within
time. **There is no filing urgency**, which preserves the sequencing discipline (parallel tracks
behind the WC settlement).

## 2. WHAT ACTING FOR THE HEALTH SERVICE *DOES* DO — PS Act 2022 ch 7 pt 1

> **s 269(1)** "A **prescribed person does not incur civil liability** for engaging, or for the
> result of engaging, in **conduct in an official capacity**."
> **s 269(2)** liability attaches instead — (a) to **the State**; or (b) where the person acted as an
> employee/appointee/delegate of a **body corporate** — **to the body corporate**.
> **s 269(6)** "**engage in conduct in an official capacity** means engage in conduct as part of, or
> otherwise in connection with, a person's role… including, for example, engage in conduct **under
> or purportedly under an Act**."

### 2.1 It is confined to "civil liability" — defined as *payment of an amount*
> **s 269(6)** civil liability "means **liability of any type for the payment of an amount**…
> because of— (a) a claim based in **tort, contract or another form of action**… **including, for
> example, breach of statutory duty** or defamation; or (b) **a complaint made under a law** that
> provides a person may complain about the conduct… to an entity established under the law, **other
> than a complaint to start criminal proceedings**; or (c) an order… to pay costs."
> *Example 1: a liability because of an agreement or an order under the Anti-Discrimination Act 1991
> … requiring payment of an amount to a complainant.*

### 2.2 ⭐ WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
| Not covered | Why |
|---|---|
| **Making the conduct lawful** | s 269 transfers *who pays*. The contravention is still found; the individual is still the contravenor; s 269(2) presupposes that liability *would* have attached. |
| **Injunctions and non-monetary orders** | Confined to "**payment of an amount**". **IR Act s 314(1)(f)** injunctions, s 314(1)(d)–(e) continuity orders, and a declaration of contravention are untouched. |
| **Criminal liability** | Expressly excluded — "other than a complaint to start criminal proceedings". So **PID Act s 41** (167 penalty units / **2 years**, indictable), **AD Act s 129** victimisation (45 penalty units / 3 months), and **WHS ss 104, 107** (1,000 penalty units) are unaffected. |
| **Discipline** | PS Act ch 3 pt 8 discipline, a Code of Conduct finding, and a CCC referral are not "civil liability". |
| **Bad faith** | **s 269(3)–(4):** the State or body corporate **may recover contribution** from the person where the conduct was engaged in "(a) **other than in good faith**; **and** (b) **with gross negligence**" — amount as is just and equitable (s 269(5)). |

### 2.3 ⚠️ TWO GENUINELY OPEN POINTS — do not assert either
1. **Are HHS employees "prescribed persons"?** **s 268(1)(a)** is "a public service employee". HHS
   employees are employed under the **Hospital and Health Boards Act 2011**, not as public service
   employees. **s 268(1)(e)** — "a person who is not a public service employee but who is a member
   or employee of a **government entity that represents the State**" — probably captures them, and
   **s 269(2)(b)** expressly contemplates body-corporate employees. **Unverified. Check the HHB Act
   and the PS Act definition of *government entity* before relying on it either way.**
2. **Does s 269 reach an IR Act civil penalty?** Arguable both ways. A **s 572** application is
   arguably "another form of action" for breach of statutory duty (s 269(6)(a)) or a "complaint…
   to an entity established under the law" (s 269(6)(b)). Against that: a civil **penalty** is
   punitive rather than compensatory, and the contribution scheme in s 269(3)–(4) reads as directed
   at compensatory liability transfer. **Untested. Flag, do not assert.**

## 3. THE PARALLEL ROUTES — the legislature drafted *against* the shield

### 3.1 PID Act 2010 — reprisal
> **s 40(1)** "**A person must not cause, or attempt or conspire to cause, detriment** to another
> person because, or in the belief that— (a) the other person… has made, or intends to make, a
> public interest disclosure."
> **s 40(5)** "it is **sufficient if the unlawful ground is a substantial ground**… **even if there
> is another ground**."
> **s 41(1)** "A person must not take a reprisal." **Maximum penalty — 167 penalty units or 2 years
> imprisonment.** **s 41(2)** indictable.
> **s 42(1)** "A reprisal is **a tort** and a person who takes a reprisal **is liable in damages**."
> **s 42(2)** "Any appropriate remedy… **including exemplary damages**."
> **s 42(6)** "**The Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003 does not apply**" to those
> proceedings.
> ⭐ **s 43(1)** "**both** the public sector entity **and the employee**… are **jointly and severally
> civilly liable**… and a proceeding under section 42 may be taken **against either or both**."
> **s 43(2)** the **entity** has a reasonable-steps defence — **the employee has none**.
> **s 48** right to apply to the **industrial commission** for an injunction about a reprisal.

**MSH's best answer — and its limits:**
> **s 45(1)–(2)** "Nothing in this part is intended to prevent a manager from taking **reasonable
> management action**… **However**, a manager may take reasonable management action… **only if the
> manager's reasons for taking the action do not include** the fact that the person has made the
> public interest disclosure."
> **s 45(3)(c)** reasonable management action includes "a **reasonable** suspension of the employee
> from the employment workplace".

⇒ Exclusion from the workplace is expressly contemplated — **but only where it is *reasonable***.
Five weeks, no wages, accrued leave debited, **no risk assessment, no consultation under WHS
ss 47–49, and no instrument ever identified** goes directly to that word.

### 3.2 AD Act 1991
> **s 122** "A person must not **request or encourage** another person to contravene the Act."
> **s 123** if the other person acts on it, "**both are jointly and severally civilly liable**… and a
> proceeding under the Act may be taken **against either or both**."
> **s 133(1)** worker or agent contravenes in the course of work — "**both** the person **and the
> worker or agent**… are **jointly and severally civilly liable**… against **either or both**."
> **s 133(2)** the reasonable-steps defence is for the **respondent employer** — not the worker.
> **s 129** "A person must not **victimise** another person." **Individual — 45 penalty units or
> imprisonment for 3 months.** A **criminal** provision.
> **s 130(1)(a)(ii)–(iii)** victimisation = detriment because the complainant "in good faith,
> alleged, or intends to allege that a person committed an act that would amount to a contravention"
> or "is, has been, or intends to be, **involved in a proceeding under the Act against any person**".
> **s 131** applies even if the underlying allegation fails.

### 3.3 WHS Act 2011
> **s 105(1)(a)(iii)–(v)** discriminatory conduct includes putting a worker "**to his or her
> detriment in the engagement of the worker**", "**alters the position of a worker to the worker's
> detriment**", or "**treats a worker less favourably than other workers**". **s 105(2)** includes
> organising or threatening it.
> **s 104(1)** "A person must not engage in discriminatory conduct **for a prohibited reason**."
> **Maximum penalty — 1,000 penalty units.** *(s 104(2): the prohibited reason must be the*
> ***dominant*** *reason — a higher bar than IR Act s 305.)*
> **s 107** "A person must not **request, instruct, induce, encourage, authorise or assist** another
> person to engage in discriminatory conduct in contravention of section 104." **1,000 penalty
> units.**
> **s 27(1)** "an **officer**… **must exercise due diligence** to ensure that the person conducting
> the business or undertaking complies with that duty." — **Hughes, as Director Corporate Services,
> is likely an officer.**

## 4. THE STRESS TEST — where this is weak

| Weakness | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **They are not primary contraventors under s 282(1)** | Correct, and it must be pleaded that way. Everything runs through **s 307** (advise/encourage/incite) or **s 571** (accessorial). s 307 is the stronger route because it does not require the knowledge element. |
| **s 282(6) is a live fight, not a walkover** | MSH will argue the WHS Act *authorised* the exclusion. The answer — s 19 is a **duty**, not an authorisation, and removing the worker is an avoidance of the risk assessment rather than a control (Psychosocial Code 2022) — is strong but must be **run**, not assumed. |
| **Reasonable management action** | MSH's best defence across every route. **PID s 45(3)(c) expressly contemplates suspension.** The rebuttal is "reasonable" — no instrument, no assessment, no consultation, five weeks, unpaid. |
| **WHS s 104 requires the prohibited reason be *dominant*** | Higher bar than IR Act s 305 ("include that reason"). The IR Act route is materially easier. |
| **PS Act s 268 threshold** | Unverified for HHS employees. Cuts both ways — if they are *not* prescribed persons, they have **no** civil-liability transfer at all. |
| **Whether s 269 reaches an IR Act civil penalty** | Untested. |
| **Forensic cost** | Joining individuals converts Hughes and Taylor into witnesses with a **personal financial interest** in the outcome — which changes how their evidence in **WC/2024/227** must be treated, and is a reason to sequence carefully. It also raises the temperature and cuts against the restrained posture currently working. **s 310(3) gives six years — there is no reason to move early.** |

## 5. THE ONE-LINE ANSWER

**Acting for the Health Service does not make unlawful conduct lawful, and it does not stop a
contravention being found against them personally.** It transfers *payment* to the body corporate —
under a provision that is confined to money, expressly excludes criminal proceedings, and is
forfeited where the conduct was in bad faith and grossly negligent.

**The only thing in the general protections that would actually have protected them is s 282(6) —
action authorised under a law of the State. And the whole citation record establishes that no such
law was ever identified.**
