> unless marked SOFC. Built from `index/EVENT_LEDGER.tsv` and the served 303-fact notice.

## 0 · The answer in one paragraph

They do not defeat each other. On the admitted record Ms Reese and Ms Taylor are **one position**:
Reese routes him back to Taylor, is copied to every Taylor directive, defends Taylor's hours in
writing, and assesses the roster risk jointly with her. What defeats them is not contradiction
between the two; it is that **Reese's own words corroborate what Taylor did** (two separate
admissions of Taylor's rostering errors), **the Regulator's pleading is at odds with Reese**
("not repeated" against "a few … in past rosters"), and **the events that the two of them said
would happen between them have no document** — and where there is no document, the pleading does
not allege the event either. Calling both puts each one's admitted words to the other.

## 2 · Where they are at odds — three places, and only one is between the two of them

### 2.1 Reese corroborates the errors the pleading minimises (Reese v SOFC)
| Reese | SOFC |
|---|---|
| 7 Aug 2023: *"there was a rostering error that was accidentally made by Chloe with regards to night shifts"* (¶287) | 13 May 2026 ¶22(a): the 7-hour break *"was a result of human error and not intentional **or repeated**"* (¶226) |
| 10 May 2024, to HR: *"I acknowledge there has been **a few** rostering errors made by Chloe with regards to Cory's line **in past rosters**"* (¶220) | |

The Regulator's own witness has written, twice and eight months apart, that Taylor's rostering
errors on his line were plural. The pleading says "not repeated". **One of those has to give, and
the one in the witness's hand is the admitted document.**

### 2.2 The joint risk assessment that the pleading does not allege (Reese v SOFC)
Reese to HR, 10 May 2024: *"Chloe and I have run thought this and we say at best there would be a
rating of 11"* (¶219). SOFC, as constituted, *"does not allege that any fatigue risk assessment was
conducted in respect of the Appellant's position at any time before 30 June 2024"* (¶269). Either
the sentence describes an assessment, in which case the pleading omits it; or it does not, in
which case a Director told HR a rating that no assessment produced. **Either answer is theirs to
give, and it is Taylor's as much as Reese's — "Chloe and I".**

## 5 · Where this sits, and what it must not become

- **s 32(5)(a) only.** These are questions about whether the management action was reasonable and
  reasonably taken. They are not about intent. ⛔ Never framed as coordination, cover-up or
  "teaming" on paper; on paper it is *"Ms Reese was copied to"*, *"the List does not record"*,
  *"the statement of facts and contentions does not allege"*.
- **The absence is the case, not the inference from the absence.** The ~40 admitted negatives
  (Part B.5) already convert "no evidence" into "admitted absence of allegation". Leave the
  inference to the Commissioner. If a witness supplies the missing event orally, the question is
  why it is on no List — and that is a weight question the Commission decides.
- **Nothing here is Jones v Dunkel.** That becomes available only on an actual failure to call —
  never on assertion (working-notes l.4244). Wait for the 30 September list.
- **Feeds:** the witness-prep task; the cross-examination plan; the outline reading for 30 Sep
  (whether their outlines go to *why* — `REGULATOR-SIGNALS` §B). If their outlines supply the
  seven missing events for the first time, that is the case they will run, and each one is
  testable against the List.
