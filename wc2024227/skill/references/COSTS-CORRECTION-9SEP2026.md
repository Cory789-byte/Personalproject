# ⛔⛔ COSTS — CORRECTION. THEY FOLLOW THE EVENT IN THESE APPEALS.
> 9 September 2026. ⛔⛔ **I stated repeatedly this session that there is "no costs recovery either
> way". THAT WAS WRONG.** Source read: **QIRC Workers' Compensation Appeal Guide v2.10, Part 10**.

---

# 1. ⭐⭐⭐ THE GUIDE, VERBATIM
> *"Hearings before the Commission or the Industrial Court of Queensland can be expensive.
> Barristers, solicitors, medical experts, and witnesses are all involved, and can all incur expenses
> **which you may be required to cover if you are unsuccessful**."*

> ⭐⭐⭐ *"**In workers' compensation appeals costs generally 'follow the event'. This means that the
> unsuccessful party will usually be required to pay the costs of successful party/parties.** Costs
> include the cost of obtaining legal advice and legal representation, as well as any expenses in
> calling witnesses (flights etc.) and any outlays for expert reports and opinions. ⚠ **Costs are
> limited to the 'costs of the hearing' so may not necessarily include, for example, some costs that
> arise prior to hearing.**"*

> ⚠ *"If the matter proceeds to an **appeal to the Industrial Court**, the Court may order a party to
> pay costs… only if satisfied the party made the appeal **vexatiously or without reasonable
> cause**."*

> ⛔⛔ *"**Any costs order in workers' compensation appeals are based on regulation 132 of the
> Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Regulation 2014.**"*

---

# 2. ⛔ WHAT THIS DOES TO THE QUESTION ASKED
**"Would the totality and costs for unreasonable actions?"** — ⛔ **No. Costs here do not turn on
unreasonableness at all.**
1. ⛔ **Costs follow the EVENT, not the conduct.** Win and he is entitled; lose and he is liable.
   **Their behaviour in correspondence is not the trigger.**
2. ⚠ **The *"vexatiously or without reasonable cause"* test appears only for an appeal to the
   Industrial Court** — not for the hearing before the Commission.
3. ⚠⚠ **Pre-hearing costs may not be recoverable at all** — *"limited to the costs of the hearing"*.
   ⇒ ⭐ **That is exactly where every piece of conduct he objects to sits: the notices, the
   non-admissions, the authenticity refusal, the correspondence.** ⛔ **So the costs marker in the
   request letter has even less purchase than previously flagged. Keep it; never rely on it.**

---

# 3. ⚠⚠ AND THE EXPOSURE IS ASYMMETRIC — IT RUNS AGAINST HIM
| | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **If he wins** | he recovers **costs** — but **self-represented, he has no legal costs.** Realistically: **outlays only** — witness expenses, conduct money for two doctors at the higher expert rate, any report fee, filing costs. **Modest.** |
| ⛔⛔ **If he loses** | **the Regulator's costs of the hearing** — counsel, solicitors, their witness expenses. ⚠ **Potentially very large, against a man with no wages since 3 July 2026.** |

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Running to hearing carries a real financial tail he cannot absorb. That was not in the
picture when the 70–75% / "plan on the hearing" advice was given, and it changes the weight of it.**

---

# 4. ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT IT CHANGES
1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Settlement is worth more to him than the merits alone suggest**, because it **extinguishes a
   real and unquantified downside.** ⛔⛔ **Any deed must deal with costs expressly — each party
   bearing its own — and that term is not optional.**
2. ⭐⭐ **It also raises the Regulator's downside** (they would pay his outlays), **but far less than
   his.** ⇒ ⭐ **The asymmetry is theirs to exploit and they know it.** ⚠ **Expect any offer to be
   priced with that in mind.**
3. ⭐⭐ **It sharpens the medical point again.** The single largest driver of the 25–30% downside is
   the medical evidence — and that downside now carries a costs consequence, not just a lost appeal.
4. ⚠ **It does NOT change the merits assessment** (70–75%) or the case strategy. **It changes the
   consequence of the tail.**

---

# 5. ⛔⛔ THE HIGHEST-PRIORITY VERIFICATION TASK ON THE FILE
> **Regulation 132, Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Regulation 2014.**

⛔ **NOT YET READ.** It governs **every costs order in these appeals** and will state the **scale or
cap**. ⭐ **A capped scale could reduce the downside to a few thousand dollars; an uncapped one
could not.**
⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Read reg 132 from the authorised source before any decision about settling or running.
Nothing in §3 or §4 above should be relied on until it is read.**

⚠ **Also to confirm from source, not from this note:** whether the Commission retains a discretion,
and how reg 132 interacts with **IR Act s 545**. ⛔ **Do not cite a section number in any filing until
it has been opened.**

---

# 6. ⛔ WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE
1. ⛔ **Do not build a conduct-based costs argument.** It is not the test, the costs are likely
   pre-hearing and irrecoverable, and **running it would spend the posture of restraint the whole
   file is built on** for a small and uncertain return.
2. ⭐ **The model litigant principles remain what they are** — Guide Part 11: *"guiding principles…
   **not applied rigidly**, and they do not override any legislative requirements."* ⛔ **Not a costs
   jurisdiction. Never pleaded as a breach.**
3. ⭐⭐ **The proper use of the totality remains what it was: settlement value, and the second s 552A
   conference.**
