## PART 1 — ⭐⭐⭐ THE REFRAME
| ⛔ Weaker (and attackable) | ⭐ Stronger (and supported) |
|---|---|
| Breach 17–18 Mar 2024 → injury 18 Jun 2024 | **An unmanaged fatigue exposure running continuously across the whole period**, of which the March breach is the one instance that is **documented, admitted and quantified** |
| Invites: *"one short break in March cannot be a significant contributing factor to a June injury"* — **a three-month gap to explain** | ⭐ **There is no gap.** The exposure never stopped. The March breach is **proof of the absent control**, not the cause standing alone |

⭐⭐ **The March break's role changes from CAUSE to PROOF.** It proves four things at once:
1. the rule existed — **10-hour minimum** (Form 24 ¶3);
2. the rule was not being applied — **7 hours** (¶1), *"a rostering error that was accidentally made"* (¶5);
3. **no system existed to detect or prevent it** (CE letter, Items 4, 5, 7);
4. ⭐⭐⭐ **nothing changed afterwards** — *"there have been no 'consequential' changes to operating
   procedures over the period requested"* (CE letter, Item 3(c)).

⇒ **Point 4 is the one that converts an incident into a continuing state, and it comes from the
employer's Chief Executive.** A one-off error corrected is an event. A breach followed by no
procedural change, in a role with no fatigue risk assessment at all, is a **condition**.

## PART 3 — ⛔ THE TWO RISKS THAT MUST BE MANAGED
### 3.1 ⛔⛔ FATIGUE IS NOT THE INJURY
**Fatigue is a state and a contributing mechanism. The injury is MDD with anxious distress.** If the
report describes the injury *as* fatigue, the Regulator answers in one line: *fatigue is not a
psychiatric injury.* ⭐ **The pathway must be articulated: sustained unmanaged fatigue exposure →
contribution to / aggravation of a depressive disorder.** Never fatigue as the diagnosis.

### 3.2 ⛔⛔ "ONGOING THROUGH THE WORK" MUST NOT BECOME A COMPOSITE COURSE
**A composite course is *Delaney*, and *Delaney* is the Respondent's authority.**
⭐ This framing is **not** a composite course, and the distinction must be held explicitly:
> **One hazard — fatigue. Continuously present. Never assessed, never managed, never changed.**
Not thirteen grievances added together. **One exposure, one mechanism** — which maps exactly onto
the case's stated mechanism: *responsibility imposed, the means to discharge it withheld.* Here, a
24/7 emergency-adjacent role **imposed**, and fatigue management **withheld**. That is *Mahaffey*.
⚠ **If it is ever pleaded or put as "all of it together", it converts to *Delaney* and is lost.**

### 3.3 ⚠ KEEP THE ENTITLEMENT POINT SEPARATE
**Award cl 18.10 — fatigue leave requires overtime** — is a live technical point against the *pay*
claim (SOFC ¶24(b)). ⛔ It has **nothing to do with causation**, and conflating them has been a
repeat error. The **entitlement failure is a stressor**; the **fatigue exposure is a mechanism**.
Two arguments, never one sentence.

## PART 5 — ⛔⛔ THE ATTACK THIS THEORY MUST SURVIVE, AND THE HINGE THAT DEFEATS IT
**Their best answer is MSH's own words turned around:**
> *"Mandatory Fatigue Risk Management System training only applies to health practitioners and
> clinical assistants. The Logan Hospital Switchboard staff are non-clinical staff, and therefore
> there is no mandatory requirement…"* (CE letter, Item 4)

⇒ **The argument: if no fatigue framework APPLIED, there was no rule to breach — so the absence of
a fatigue system is not a failure, it is compliance with a system that never required one.**

### ⭐⭐⭐ THE HINGE — THE 10-HOUR OBLIGATION IS NOT AN FRMS OBLIGATION
**Form 24 ¶3 admits the minimum break of 10 hours (or 8 only by written agreement). That obligation
is Award- and agreement-based. It exists whether or not the FRMS ever applied to the Switchboard.**
⇒ ⭐ **The breach stands even if MSH is entirely right that no FRMS applied.** The two must never be
argued as one.

### ⭐⭐ AND THE ABSENCE GOES TO A DIFFERENT ELEMENT
The missing fatigue system is **not** relied on as the breach. It is relied on for **exposure and
foreseeability** — the psychiatric question, and the WHS Act ss 17/19 duty — namely that a role the
employer itself describes as continuous 24/7 shift work with emergency-code duties under strict
timeframes was carried on with **no fatigue assessment of any kind** until after the injury.
⛔ **Keep the two strands in separate sentences, always:**
| Strand | Element | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **10-hour minimum breached → 7 hours** | The **breach** | Award / agreement — Form 24 ¶¶1, 3 |
| **No fatigue assessment, ever, until after 30 Jun 2024; no procedural change after the error** | **Exposure and foreseeability** | CE letter, Items 3(c), 4, 5, 7 |

⚠ **Merging them hands MSH the "non-clinical" answer to both.** Kept apart, "non-clinical" answers
neither: it does not touch the Award minimum, and it does not make an unassessed exposure safe.
