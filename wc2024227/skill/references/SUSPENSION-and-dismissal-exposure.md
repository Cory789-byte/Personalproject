# PART 1 — ⭐⭐⭐ THE POWER IS s 101. THEY HAVE USED ITS EFFECT WITHOUT ANY OF ITS CONDITIONS.

**"Hold an employee out of the workplace" has a name in the Public Sector Act. It is
suspension — s 101.** Read what it requires.

| **s 101 requires** | **What happened** |
|---|---|
| **(1)** *"A public sector employee's **chief executive** may, **by notice**, suspend the employee from duty"* | ⛔ A **Manager** on 2 July; a **Director** on 31 July. No notice of suspension has ever issued |
| **(1)(a) or (b)** the CE reasonably believes the employee is **liable to discipline**, **or** that **"the proper and efficient management of the entity might be prejudiced"** | ⛔ The 31 July letter records the Health Service is **not aware of any performance or conduct concerns** raised for appropriate management |
| **(2)(a)** the notice **must state** *"when the suspension **starts and ends**"* | ⛔ **Five weeks, no start date, no end date.** He has asked for a date repeatedly |
| **(2)(b)** the notice **must state** *"**whether the employee is entitled to remuneration** for the period"* | ⛔ Never stated. Leave debited, then no pay from 13 July by reference to a **closed** QSuper claim |
| ⭐⭐ **(3)** *"**before** suspending the employee, the chief executive **must consider all reasonable alternatives**"* — examples given: **"alternative duties"**, a change in location, **"another alternative working arrangement"** | ⛔⛔ Interim duties were **refused "in any capacity"** on 15 July. **The statute makes alternative duties a mandatory pre-condition; MSH refused them outright** |
| ⭐⭐⭐ **(4)** *"**The employee is entitled to normal remuneration for the period of the suspension**, unless (a) the employee is suspended under subsection (1)(a) [discipline]; **and** (b) the chief executive considers it is not appropriate…"* | ⛔⛔⛔ **Unpaid since 13 July** |
| **(9)** the CE *"**must** comply with the directive made under section 102"* | ⏳ no directive identified |
| **s 131(1)(f)** | a **suspension without pay decision is appealable** |

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE FORK, AND IT HAS NO GOOD EXIT
**s 101(4) makes remuneration the default. The only exception is a s 101(1)(a) *discipline*
suspension where the CE decides otherwise.** So:

- **If it is s 101(1)(b)** — *"proper and efficient management might be prejudiced"* — then
  ⭐ **there is no exception at all and he must be paid in full.**
- **If it is s 101(1)(a)** — discipline — then MSH must reasonably believe he is **liable to
  discipline**, which its own letter of 31 July disclaims, and which has never been particularised
  to him in two years.
- **If it is neither** — then he is not suspended, and the question at §2.6 stands: **what is it?**

⇒ ⭐⭐ **On any reading of s 101 he should have been paid.** The power that fits would have given
him **a chief-executive decision-maker, a start and end date, a stated position on pay,
consideration of alternative duties, full remuneration, and an appeal right.**
**He has received none of the six.**

## ⭐ HOW IT IS PUT — AND IT IS ONE SENTENCE, WITH NO CITATION
**Stage 1 notice §4(b):**
> *"That I be told whether I am suspended from duty. If I am, I ask to be told **by whom, under what
> provision, from what date to what date, and whether I am entitled to remuneration for the
> period**. If I am not suspended, I ask to be told what my status is."*

⭐⭐ **That is s 101(1) and s 101(2) read back to them as a question, without naming the section.**
It asks for exactly what a valid suspension notice must contain.
- Answer **"yes, suspended"** → every condition above becomes live, and **s 101(4) means pay**.
- Answer **"no"** → then he is an employee not suspended, not on approved leave, willing and
  available, and not being paid.
- Answer **nothing** → five weeks becomes six, against **cl 1.11.5**.

⛔⛔ **DO NOT CITE s 101 TO THEM.** Naming it lets them issue a compliant notice tomorrow and cure
everything prospectively — with a start date of tomorrow, not 3 July. **The five weeks is only
unprotected while they have not noticed the problem.**

## 2.2 ⭐⭐ THE REQUEST ITSELF IS PROTECTIVE — THIS IS THE REASON TO SEND IT
**IR Act s 284 — meaning of workplace right. All three limbs are engaged by the package:**
- **s 284(1)(a)** — *"has a right to the benefit of, or has a role or responsibility under, an
  industrial law, **industrial instrument**"* ⇒ **cl 10.3.2** (certified agreement) and **s 27** (industrial law)
- **s 284(1)(b)** — *"is able to **start, or participate in, a process** or proceedings under an
  industrial law or industrial instrument"* ⇒ **the cl 1.11 dispute**
- **s 284(1)(c)(ii)** — *"is able to make a **complaint or inquiry**… in relation to the person's
  employment"*

**s 285 — Protection.** A person must not take adverse action *"because the other person (i) has a
workplace right; or (ii) has, or has not, exercised a workplace right; or (iii) **proposes to**…
exercise a workplace right"*, or **(b) "to prevent the exercise of a workplace right"**.

**s 282 — adverse action** by an employer is where the employer *"(a) **dismisses** the employee; or
(b) **injures the employee in the employee's employment**; or (c) **alters the position of the
employee to the employee's prejudice**; or (d) discriminates between the employee and other
employees"*.

⇒ ⭐⭐ **He does not need a dismissal.** Five weeks of unpaid exclusion is capable of being
**s 282(1)(b) and (c)** already.

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **And sending the three documents creates a dated exercise of a workplace right.**
Before today, adverse action had no anchor. **After today, everything MSH does is measured against
a documented date — and s 306 presumes the reason.**
