**pinpoint-verify against the full reasons before citing in any filing.** The

## 1. Scott v Steritech Pty Ltd [2020] QIRC 70 — THE TEMPLATE (near-identical)

Mr Scott sought 8 categories of documents from his (non-party) employer about
staffing/restructuring decisions; the substantive issue was whether the
employer's action was **"reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way."**

**Steritech's objections (same five MSH raises):** individuals not specified
(particularity); privilege; confidentiality / personal employee details; limited
HR resources; expense and inconvenience.

**The Commission's disposition — a partial production order:**
- **Lifted the stay on 6 of 8 categories**; ordered production in 14 days.
- **Varied 2 categories** to require **redaction of other employees' personal
  details** — privacy cured by redaction, NOT refusal.
- Each party its own costs.

**Principles directly usable:**
- Relevance: "directly relevant … only if it tends to prove or disprove an
  allegation" (*Xstrata Queensland Ltd v Santos Ltd*) — same standard as the 64G.
- **"The mere claim that a document … is confidential is not a valid objection to
  its production"** (*DP World Brisbane Pty Ltd v Rogers*). → defeats MSH's
  privacy/confidentiality objections (Items 9, 12, 17).
- **"Rule 64I allows applicants to pay reasonable production expenses, undermining
  inconvenience arguments."** → defeats MSH's expense objections (Items 3, 8, 10,
  19).
- Balancing: time elapsed, opportunity to propose alternatives, and genuine
  relevance outweighed the objections collectively.

**Map to WC/2024/227:** near-identical fact pattern (employee v non-party employer;
s 32(5)(a) reasonableness the matter in issue; the SAME five objections). Scott
got a **partial production order with privacy-by-redaction** — which is EXACTLY the
disposition the draft order seeks (overrule the confined items; field-confine 8/9;
own costs). Cory's position is STRONGER than Scott's: his Notice is more
particularised (the phrase-lexicon cures the particularity objection Scott had to
argue), and he has already self-narrowed and withdrawn the privacy items Scott had
to have varied.

## 2. Taylor v Clermont Coal Pty Ltd [2021] QIRC 430 — expense is a COST question, not a refusal ground

A r 64I expenses assessment after a Form 29 production. The non-party claimed
$14,525; the Registrar allowed **~$5,806 (~40%)**, and held that **legal advice is
NOT a recoverable "reasonable expense"** — only production disbursements
(searching, collating, copying, producing). → MSH's expense objection (CE
approval, eHealth engagement) is a **cost** met by r 64I at production
disbursement rates, NOT a reason to refuse; and much of what MSH frames as
"expense" (legal review) would not even be recoverable. Expense ≠ refusal.

## 3. Bunney v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2020] QIRC 210 — the WC-specific model (already in case-law doc)

Subdivision 7A applied to a Form 29 objection in a WC appeal; **partial-production
order** within 14 days; privilege upheld only on genuine sub-categories; own
costs. The WC-context template for the order sought.

## 4. THE PATTERN across the cases — what it tells the reception model

All three converge on the SAME disposition the bench-notes predicted:
- Objections of expense → met by r 64I (Scott, Taylor), not a refusal ground.
- Objections of confidentiality/privacy → cured by REDACTION/variation (Scott),
  not refusal; "mere confidentiality is not a valid objection" (DP World).
- Relevance → the "tends to prove or disprove" standard (Xstrata/Robson).
- Outcome → **PARTIAL PRODUCTION ORDER, privacy by redaction, own costs.**

**Cory's case is at the STRONGER end of this line** because he has *pre-performed*
what the Commission did in Scott: self-narrowed the windows, withdrawn the privacy
items, particularised via the phrase-lexicon, and offered the 8/9 field-confinement
regime. He is asking the Commission to grant an order he has already drafted to the
shape these authorities produce. The draft order IS the Scott/Bunney outcome,
pre-built.

## 5. The "currently employed" point (MSH Item 9) — a double-edge MSH walked into

MSH objects to Item 9: *"The Applicant is a current employee and reports directly
to the line manager … production could have an effect on the line manager."* This
HELPS Cory three ways:
1. **It admits the direct supervisory relationship** — confirming the Line Manager
   was Cory's direct supervisor, so her presence/absence and directives bear
   directly on him (the spine).
2. **Current employment is the basis of the nerve-centre evidence** — ongoing,
   first-hand operational knowledge; and a worker still IN the unremedied
   conditions (2 years on) is proof the conditions are systemic, not perception.
3. **"Effect on the line manager"** is answered by Scott/DP World: an effect on a
   third party is cured by the confidentiality/redaction regime (which Cory offers
   for 8/9), NOT a refusal ground. The counterweight — the effect on the INJURED
   current employee of being DENIED the records of his own injury — is the
   procedural-justice interest the cases say outweighs mere confidentiality.
