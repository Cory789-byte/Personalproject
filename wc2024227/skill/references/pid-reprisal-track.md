## 2. The reprisal tort — the framework (PID Act 2010 (Qld), ss 40–42; verified)
- **s 40 — reprisal:** a person takes a reprisal if they cause, or attempt/conspire to cause,
  **detriment** to another **because, or in the belief that,** the other (or someone) made or
  may make a PID. **Reasonable management action taken reasonably is NOT a reprisal**
  (the ESU letter restates this — and it is the same s 32(5)(a) battleground, which is why the
  reprisal must be proved on its own facts, not assumed).
- **s 41 — offence:** taking a reprisal is a **criminal offence** (max penalty applies).
- **s 42 — civil right (THE TORT):** *"A reprisal is a tort and a person who takes a reprisal
  is liable in damages to any person who suffers detriment as a result."* Any remedy a court
  may grant for a tort — **including exemplary damages** — may be granted. Heard in the
  **District or Supreme Court** (judge sitting without a jury if it goes to trial). The right to
  sue **does not affect any other right or remedy**. Crucially: **"The Workers' Compensation
  and Rehabilitation Act 2003 does NOT apply to proceedings for damages brought under this
  section"** — so the reprisal tort is preserved INDEPENDENTLY of the WC claim, and a WC
  settlement does not, of itself, extinguish it (but a poorly-scoped deed could — see §5).
- What must be proved: (a) a PID was made (determined — §1); (b) detriment suffered; (c) the
  detriment was caused **because of** the PID (the causal link). **The Regulator's own SOFC
  ¶16 — "Ms Reese was unaware that the appellant had lodged a PID" — is the live attack on
  limb (c)**: if the decision-maker did not know of the PID, the "because of" link fails. This
  is the central contest and the reason the reprisal is dangerous if leaned on prematurely.

## 4. LIMITATION — the action item, computed (research-grounded; NOT formal advice)
**Headline: the core limitation is OPEN; one pre-court sub-clock has likely passed. Get
specialist personal-injuries/whistleblower advice NOW and give any required notice promptly.**

- **3-year limitation (Limitation of Actions Act 1974 (Qld) s 11 — personal injury): OPEN.**
  A reprisal claim for **psychiatric injury** is a personal-injuries action; the cause of
  action accrued ~mid-2024 (reprisal 15 May 2024 / injury 18 June 2024 / termination 8 Oct
  2024). 3 years runs to roughly **May–Oct 2027.** As at June 2026 there is **~1 year+ left.**
  So the reprisal tort is **NOT time-barred** — the red-team's "possibly already fatal" is, on
  the core limitation, **too pessimistic.**
- **PIPA pre-court NOTICE (Personal Injuries Proceedings Act 2002 (Qld)) — LIKELY LATE.** If
  PIPA applies, a **Part 1 Notice of Claim** must be given within **9 months** of the incident
  (or **1 month** after first instructing a lawyer to act, whichever is earlier). 9 months from
  the 15 May 2024 reprisal ≈ **Feb 2025** (passed); from the 8 Oct 2024 termination ≈ **Jul
  2025** (passed). So the PIPA notice window has **probably already expired** — BUT late notice
  may be given with a **reasonable excuse for delay** (PIPA s 9A), and the court may authorise
  proceedings despite non-compliance. Late ≠ barred, but every month of delay weakens the
  "reasonable excuse," so this is the **live, time-sensitive sub-clock.**
- **OPEN QUESTION needing specialist advice — does PIPA even apply?** A reprisal-for-
  psychiatric-injury claim sits awkwardly: the PID Act disapplies the WCRA (s 42), but whether
  PIPA's pre-court regime governs (vs a "bare" s 42 tort, possibly a 6-year limitation for
  non-injury detriment) is genuinely arguable and **must be resolved by a specialist** before
  any notice/filing. Do not assume either way.

**Net:** the "defer the reprisal behind the WC settlement" premise is **not dead** — the
3-year clock is open. But the PIPA notice sub-clock is probably already running late, so the
correct move is: **obtain specialist advice now, give any PIPA notice promptly (with a
reasonable-excuse explanation), and stop treating "verify soon" as deferrable.**
