# UNION DELEGATE vs HSR — which role actually carries powers (verified 4 Aug 2026)
> ⛔ Strategy layer. Cory raised whether he has "powers under a union delegate".
> ⚠ FIRST FACT TO ESTABLISH: nothing in the record shows he holds either role. He is a
> union MEMBER (Together Qld; Moran background, Petering industrial officer). Member ≠
> delegate. Delegate ≠ HSR. The powers differ enormously.

## 1. UNION DELEGATE — representational, agreement-sourced
A delegate is elected/appointed by union members in a workplace. Powers come from
(a) the certified agreement's union-representation/delegate clause, and (b) IR Act
protections for industrial activity (s 284) — NOT from any statutory investigative power.
Typical content: reasonable paid time to represent members, access to facilities, notice
of changes, delegate training leave, right to be present in members' meetings.
⇒ Useful, protective, and it makes his activity plainly "industrial activity" for adverse
action purposes. It confers NO power to inspect, compel information, or issue notices.
⚠ Requires actual appointment through the union. Cannot be self-declared.

## 2. ⭐ HEALTH AND SAFETY REPRESENTATIVE (WHS Act ss 68–72, 85, 90) — the real powers
Elected by the work group. s 68 powers and functions, verified text:
- represent the workers of the work group in WHS matters;
- **monitor the measures taken by the PCBU in compliance with the Act**;
- **investigate complaints from members of the work group about WHS**;
- **inquire into anything that appears to be a risk to health or safety arising from the
  conduct of the business**;
and in exercising those functions may —
- **inspect the workplace** on reasonable notice (and immediately, without notice, after an
  incident or where there is serious risk from immediate/imminent exposure);
- accompany an inspector, or a WHS entry permit holder, during an inspection;
- be present at WHS interviews between a worker and the PCBU or an inspector (with consent);
- **request the establishment of a health and safety committee**;
- **request and receive information concerning the WHS of workers in the work group**;
- **request the assistance of a suitable entity** (this is the route to bringing in outside
  help — including a regulator's inspector);
plus, under Div 6/7: **direct unsafe work to cease**, and **issue provisional improvement
notices (PINs)**.
LIMITS (also verified): s 68(3) — no access to personal or medical information about a
worker without consent unless de-identified; s 69 — powers limited to that work group;
training is prescribed (the PCBU must allow the HSR to choose and attend training and pay
the fees — penalty for failing to facilitate), and the cease-work/PIN powers require the
prescribed training to have been completed.

## 3. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR HIM, HONESTLY
- The HSR route is the only one that carries investigative-style powers — and it is
  designed for the WORK GROUP's safety, not for running a personal dispute. Using HSR
  powers to advance his own case would be visible, criticisable, and would taint both.
- ⚠ He is currently excluded from the workplace. An excluded worker cannot practically
  discharge HSR functions, and standing for election now would read as positioning.
- ⭐ WHAT IS AVAILABLE AND CONSTRUCTIVE NOW: **request that a work group be determined and
  an HSR be elected for Switchboard Services** — a request any worker may make, which the
  PCBU must facilitate. He does not need to be the HSR. An HSR existing at all creates a
  permanent consultation channel with statutory powers, independent of him, which is
  precisely the structural fix his tabled package argues for (and it survives whatever
  happens to him personally).
- If elected later, in calmer circumstances, the powers above become available — including
  requesting information and requesting the assistance of a suitable entity.
