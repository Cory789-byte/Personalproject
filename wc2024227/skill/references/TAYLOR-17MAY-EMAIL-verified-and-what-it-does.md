## 1. What the document actually is

**From Chloe Taylor → Adriana McNamee (HR), Friday 17 May 2024, 1:20 PM**, subject
*"FW: Office Hours and Departmental Directives"*. It opens:

> *"Good afternoon Adriana, **as requested**. I would just like to give you a little background from
> the email that was sent by Cory."*

⇒ ⚠ **It was solicited by HR.** It is an account provided on request, not a volunteered complaint.
**That matters and it should not be overstated in the other direction.**

It then narrates 13, 14 and 15 May, and closes:

> *"I would just like to add I have had mutiple staff inform me that in the past not just this week,
> when Cory calls in for emergent leave he verbally tells the staff he does not want to speak to me.
> **I have had been advised from staff verbally telling me that he has told them he is out to get me**
> and for me to watch/be careful of Cory. These are just a few of the comments I have received of
> late from switchboard staff. **It is not unknown within the switchboard of Corys behaviour and
> malice towards me** which is highly inappopropriate and its starting to affect my ability to perform
> best in my role. I am currently **experencing high anxiety** interacting with this staff member and
> am **fearful as to what his next actions maybe**."*

## 2. ⛔⛔ Two corrections to the working notes, found by reading the source

**(a) The "three self-narrations" count is wrong. There are two.**
`working-notes.md` lists *"(2) 17 May 2024 account 'as requested' (after the retraction); (3) the
'out to get me' HR email (after PID knowledge reached management)"* as **two separate documents**.
⇒ ⛔ **They are the same email.** *"as requested"* is its opening line and *"out to get me"* is in its
closing paragraph. The pattern across the record is **6 June 2023 and 17 May 2024 — two, not three.**

**(b) The quotes had never been checked against the page.** Every prior use of *"out to get me"*,
*"malice"* and *"fearful as to what his next actions maybe"* in this repository traces to one line of
a working note, not to the source. ⇒ **They are now verified verbatim** — and the verification
changed the most important thing about the first of them (§3).

## 3. ⭐⭐⭐ What verification changed: "out to get me" is **double hearsay**, and she says so herself

Not *"he told me he was out to get me."* The sentence is:

> *"I have had been advised **from staff verbally telling me** that **he has told them** he is out to
> get me"*

⇒ **Unnamed staff → told her → what he allegedly told them.** No name, no date, no record, no
contemporaneous note, and *"these are just a few of the comments"* with none of them particularised.
**The manager's own sentence identifies the two layers.**

⇒ ⭐⭐ **The same is true of the "malice" line**: *"It is not unknown within the switchboard"* — an
assertion about what a group of people generally know, sourced to nobody.

| Time, 17 May 2024 | Document | What she says |
|---|---|---|
| **9:30 AM** | to Logan Switch, cc Reese and staff | *"regardless of **my start/finish times next week** you are to please contact me through the day/afterhours either via switch, office or mobile"* — and the hours: *"Otherwise my hours are from 06:30-14:30"* |

⇒ ⭐⭐ **At 9:30 she tells the whole department her start and finish times vary and sets out how to

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **And the 9:30 AM email supplied the very information he had asked for on 15 May** — the
email he was asked to retract. **His complaint was answered by her own conduct two days later, while
she was telling HR it was malice.** That is the whole of topic 6 of his outline, and it needs no
adjective.

## 6. ⚠ The honest cost of putting it in play — price this before deciding

The email is in her bundle, so it exists regardless. ⛔ **But leading it makes it central**, and it
carries adverse material that a decision-writer may reproduce:

1. ⚠ **Three consecutive days recorded — 13, 14, 15 May — of calling in and declining to speak to
   his manager.** That is the *"he declined to speak to his manager"* exposure already on the risk
   list. ⭐ The answer on the file is that he had asked the Director for a break from her, and that
   is documentary — but the email states the conduct plainly and in sequence.
2. ⚠⚠ **A manager recording that she is *"experiencing high anxiety"* and *"fearful as to what his
   next actions maybe."*** ⛔ **A tribunal reads that as a person in distress.** It is the single
   most sympathetic sentence any Metro South witness has written in this matter, and cross-examining
   on the email puts it on the page next to his own injury.
3. ⚠ **It was solicited** (*"as requested"*). ⇒ The *"she wrote an account each time her conduct was
   questioned"* pattern is **weaker here than at 6 June 2023**, because HR asked her for this one.
   **Do not run the pattern point as though both are the same. The 2023 email was unprompted; this
   one was not.**

## 7. ⛔⛔ The line that must not be crossed

The public interest disclosure was **13 May 2024**. This email is **17 May 2024**. ⛔ **Four days.**

⛔ **That adjacency is PID-track material and is not run as motive in this appeal.** Running it as
reprisal:
- opens **s 32(5)(b)** — injuries connected with the worker's *expectation or perception* of
  management action — which the Regulator has **not** pleaded;
- raises his own ***Briginshaw*** bar on a serious allegation about a named person;
- breaches discipline rules 1–3.

⇒ ⭐ **Everything useful in this document is available without motive.** The four legitimate uses:

| | Use | Rests on |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐⭐ | **The same-day contradiction** (§5) | two documents, four hours apart, both hers |
| ⭐⭐⭐ | **Double hearsay, unnamed, unparticularised, undated** (§3) | her own sentence construction |
| ⭐⭐ | **Contradicted by two people from the group she cited** (§4) | already served |
| ⭐ | **The complaint was legitimate and she answered it herself** (§5) | her 9:30 AM email |

⛔ **None of those four requires a word about why she wrote it.** The dates and the documents do the
work, and that is the architecture that survives cross-examination and the Guide.

## 8. ⭐⭐ What the Respondent understands, added to the strength assessment

She holds this email in her own bundle. Reading the served outlines against it, she now sees:

1. ⭐⭐ **Her best character material is unsourced double hearsay**, and the appellant has served
   **two witnesses from the department she attributed it to**, one of them still employed there,
   who say it did not happen.
2. ⭐⭐ **A third fork**, on top of the two already identified:
   - **call Taylor** → she is cross-examined on a solicited HR email containing double hearsay and a
     four-hour self-contradiction, with two switchboard witnesses waiting to contradict it;
   - **do not call Taylor** → the email cannot be led, the character case goes with it, and
     Harrison-Jones ¶ 6 on her manner stands uncontradicted — which is the position Dwyer described
     twice.
3. ⚠ **Her one gain from leading it**: the *"high anxiety"* and *"fearful"* lines, and the three days
   of declined contact. ⇒ **That is a real trade and she may take it.** ⭐ **Expect it to be led on
   manner and credit, not on causation** — and expect the *"fearful"* line, not the *"malice"* line,
   to be the one she uses.

⇒ **Net: the email strengthens his position, but not because it is damaging to her.** It strengthens
it because **the answer to it was already served before she could decide how to use it.**
