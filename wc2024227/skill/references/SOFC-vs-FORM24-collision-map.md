## 1. THE STRUCTURAL POINT FIRST

Her pleading is built on **"does not admit"** — which asserts nothing and merely puts him to proof.
But threaded through it are **positive assertions**, and those are a different thing: each one she
must prove.

⭐⭐⭐ **Seven pleaded positives can only be proved by an MSH witness:**

| ¶ | The assertion | Who alone can prove it |
|---|---|---|
| 13(c) | the 7 August email *"prompted a meeting with the appellant and Ms Reese on 10 August"* | Ms Reese |
| 14(f) | *"a matter of **human error by Ms Taylor** on the background of high work demands"* | Ms Taylor |
| 16(a) | *"**Ms Reese was unaware** that the appellant had lodged a PID"* | Ms Reese |
| 16(b)(i) | he *"did not comply with the processes of properly notifying"* | Ms Taylor |
| 19(c) | he *"was rostered in an equitable way over the Easter period"* | Ms Taylor |
| 20(b)–(c) | issues *"investigated"* and *"remedied in a timely manner"* | Ms Taylor / Payroll |
| 22(a) | *"human error and **not intentional or repeated**"* | Ms Taylor |

⇒ **This is the answer to "will the Regulator call Ms Taylor?"** Her own pleading is built on
assertions about Ms Taylor's state of mind, workload and intention. **A no-witness case abandons
every one of them** and leaves the Appellant's evidence uncontradicted, which Dwyer twice said
would likely be accepted.

## 6. ⚠ ONE TO VERIFY BEFORE RELYING ON IT — THE EASTER YEAR

¶19 answers the pay allegation by reference to *"the appellant did text Ms Taylor on or about
**4 April 2023**"* and *"this pay period involved the **Easter weekend of 8 and 9 April 2023**"*.

But Stressor 2 as pleaded runs **February to April 2024**, and Dwyer identified the relevant public
holidays at [52:27] as **Good Friday, Easter Sunday, Easter Monday and Anzac Day** — i.e. 29 March
to 25 April **2024**.

⇒ ⭐ **On its face ¶19 answers the wrong year.** ⚠ **Do not deploy this until the Form 9A is checked**
— it is possible the Appellant's own pleading refers to an April 2023 text, in which case ¶19 is
responsive and the point evaporates. ⚠ Note also that **¶20(d)** (*"not entitled to 'Public Holiday
not Worked' amounts and was paid a public holiday allowance instead"*) does engage the entitlement
question generally, so the 42% is not wholly unanswered even if ¶19 is misdirected.
