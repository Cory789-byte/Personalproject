# PART 2 — THE LEGAL AUTHORITY, TESTED ACT BY ACT

**Four distinct things were done. They require different authority. Only one of them has any.**

## 2.1 The information request — ✅ SUPPORTABLE
| Basis | Effect |
|---|---|
| Implied contractual power to give **reasonable and lawful directions** | The true source. Bounded by **reasonableness** |
| **G03 cl 3** | supporting information "**where necessary**, to either identify required workplace adjustments and/or assess the suitability of proposed adjustments" |
| **IP Act, QPP 3.3(a)** | sensitive information requires **consent** *and* **reasonable necessity** |
| **AD Act s 124(3)** | onus on MSH to prove the information was "reasonably required" |

⇒ **The 5 May request was properly founded.** It was necessary, proportionate, framed as support,
and Cory complied — he booked the appointment and produced the certificate.
⇒ **Three independent necessity tests apply to the 31 July request**, and each is a live question.

## 2.2 The exclusion from the workplace — ❌ NO AUTHORITY
**Cited: WHS s 19 (26 June) and, from 29–31 July, ss 17 and 19.**

> **s 19(1)** "A person conducting a business or undertaking **must ensure**, so far as is reasonably
> practicable, the health and safety of workers…"
> **s 17** a duty to ensure health and safety "requires the person to **eliminate** risks… or
> **minimise** those risks so far as is reasonably practicable."

⇒ **Both are duties imposed on MSH. Neither confers any power over a worker.** A duty to ensure
safety is not a power to exclude, and the Psychosocial Code 2022 treats psychosocial hazards as
**features of the work**, controlled by the PCBU. **Removing the worker is not a control — it is the
avoidance of the assessment.**
⇒ **G03 says the opposite.** cl 1.1: adjustments exist so employees "**continue work during their
recovery**". cl 1: managers must ensure work "**do[es] not unreasonably prevent employment for
people**".
⇒ **The reasonableness limb fails on MSH's own record.** A direction not to attend, maintained for
five weeks against a certificate of fitness, with **no risk assessment**, **no consultation under
WHS ss 47–49**, and **no answer to "confirm I'm right to attend"**, is not a reasonable direction.

## 2.3 The leave treatment — ❌ NO AUTHORITY, AND THE ONE STATUTORY LINK WAS NEVER INVOKED
Accrued leave → **sick leave no pay** (3 Jul) → **income protection no pay** (17 Jul).

> **PS Act 2022 s 105:** "The employee **must not be given sick leave** for any period during which
> the employee **fails to comply with a requirement under section 104(b)**."

⇒ **s 105 is the only statutory link in the entire division between a medical-information process
and leave.** It operates **solely** where an s 104(b) requirement to submit to examination is not
complied with. **s 104 was never invoked. s 105 supplies nothing here.**
⇒ **And Cory did not fail to comply with anything.** He booked the appointment, attended, and
produced the certificate.
⇒ **Absence caused by the employer's own direction is not the employee's leave.** Nothing cited
permits debiting it.

## 2.4 ⭐ THE POWER THAT EXISTS AND WAS NEVER USED
> **PS Act 2022 s 103** applies if — "(a) the employee **is absent from duty** **or** the chief
> executive is reasonably satisfied the employee **is not performing** the employee's duties
> satisfactorily; **and** (b) the chief executive **reasonably suspects** that the absence or
> unsatisfactory performance **is caused by** mental or physical illness or disability."
> **s 104** "The chief executive **may** (a) appoint a doctor… and (b) **require the employee to
> submit** to the medical examination."

⇒ **This is the only statutory power to compel medical examination, and it was never cited — not
once, by any of four officers, across five weeks.**
⇒ ⭐ **And the gateway cannot be met.** No performance issue has ever been put to Cory. That leaves
absence — **and the absence is MSH's own act.** An employer cannot exclude a worker and then rely on
the resulting absence as the gateway to a power over him. **s 103 is not satisfied on facts of MSH's
own making.**

## 2.5 The 31 July request itself — ❌ FOUR DEFECTS ON THE FACE

## 2.6 ⚠️ WHAT MUST BE CONCEDED — state it before it is put

2. **The 5 May request was legitimate**, and MSH offered a **consent mechanism** on 31 July —

# PART 4 — THE CONCLUSION

**The request has authority. Nothing else does.**

- **5 May:** a properly founded request for supporting information, framed as **support for a person
  at work**, complied with.
- **21 May:** he is **rostered and working** with the ECC outstanding. **The ECC was not a
  precondition to attendance, and MSH's own conduct proves it.**
- **26 June:** the same document is converted into a **bar to attendance**, and a **duty** provision
  is cited as though it were a **power**.
- **3 July:** the certificate arrives saying **fit**. Fifteen minutes later the subject is leave
  type. The question *"confirm I'm right to attend"* is **never answered**.
- **31 July:** a nine-question request to a GP, four questions of which are not medical questions,
  signed in **75 seconds** with a stored image, in a document nobody proofread, citing a **policy
  that says the opposite** and a **duty that confers no power**.

⇒ **For the information request: a policy was used, and it can carry the weight — subject to
necessity.**
⇒ **For the exclusion and the leave: nothing was used at all.** Not because the wrong instrument was
chosen, but because **no instrument exists that authorises it** — and the one that could have
(**PS Act s 104**) was never reached for, because its **s 103** gateway is not met on facts of MSH's
own making.

**That is why the bases have shifted six times. Each was an attempt to find, after the fact, an
authority for something already done.**
