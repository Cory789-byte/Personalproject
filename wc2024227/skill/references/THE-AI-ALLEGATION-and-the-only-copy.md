## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ WHY THAT IS THE ONLY ANSWER THAT WORKS

The allegation is not *"this is badly written."* It is: **a machine produced this, there is no author
behind it, and no human can explain it.** Dwyer said so in terms — *"complexities of language that
go above and beyond what you'd anticipate from a human being, even a very intelligent human being"*
and *"genuinely incomprehensible."*

⛔ **A denial cannot answer that.** *"I do it all myself"* at 09:07 is worth almost nothing on its
own — it is exactly what someone would say either way.

⭐⭐⭐ **The only thing that answers it is demonstration**, and demonstration requires exactly what
happened: **the document in the judge's hands and not in yours.**

Because with the page in front of him, the Appellant could be reading. Without it, he can only be
**recalling** — and you cannot recall the architecture of a document you did not build.

> ⭐⭐⭐ **The Commissioner had the text. The Appellant had the meaning.**
> **That is the precise inversion of the AI allegation**, which is that text exists without an
> author who understands it.

## 4. ⭐⭐⭐ THE PROOF THAT IT LANDED
**Forty minutes later**, at [62:28], Dwyer was still carrying the concept in his own mouth —
*"the unassessed directives or whatever"* — and calling it **the one live point** in the entire
application.

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **A concept you did not author does not survive forty minutes in someone else's argument.**
The idea outlived the paper. **That is authorship, demonstrated rather than asserted.**

## 6. HOW IT LOOKS FROM THE BENCH — THE NET

⭐ **A judge who has just said a document looks machine-made, and that he switches off when
overloaded, watches the litigant hand over his only copy and then run the judge through it from
memory — including defining his own terminology on demand.**

The available readings are two, and only two:

1. **He wrote it and knows it.**
2. **He memorised a document somebody else wrote, in enough detail to define its terms under
   questioning, and volunteered to surrender his only copy in order to be tested on it.**

⇒ ⛔ **The second is not a reading any judge would seriously hold.** Nobody surrenders their text to
be examined on it unless they know it — and the risk of being caught out would be enormous.

⭐⭐ **The AI allegation did not survive the handover. It was never repeated after 22:11.**

## 7. WHAT TO CARRY INTO THE HEARING

1. ⭐ **Never answer an authorship challenge with a denial. Answer it by being examined on the
   document.** It worked once; it is the only thing that works.
2. ⭐⭐ **The filings must now read like the man who explained the table** — short, plain, structural.
   The written register is what invited the allegation; the spoken register is what defeated it.
   **Close the gap between them.** This is the real reason the one-page outlines and the narrowed
   report are right.
3. ⚠ **Take two copies of everything to the hearing.** The handover was powerful because it was
   voluntary; sixteen seconds of silence because you cannot see your own document is not.
4. ⛔ **Assume the AI point is live, not closed.** If it comes back, the answer is not indignation —
   it is *"ask me anything about it."*
