# The served outlines — strength, and what the Respondent understands on reading them

**WC/2024/227 · 16 September 2026 · ⛔ INTERNAL ONLY.**
**Assessment only. Nothing here proposes a change to anything served.**

Read as Ms Matheson reads it: five documents land together — the witness list, the covering letter,
three one-page outlines, and a 43-page medical schedule keyed to tabs. **Their outlines are due
4 pm, 30 September.** Everything below is what she takes from the set, and how strong it is.

---

## 1. ⭐⭐⭐ The structural move she understands first, and likes least

The Appellant's outline says it in its own opening line:

> *"The events at topics 2 to 7 **are established by the facts admitted on 8 September 2026**; my
> evidence on them is what they were from the console and their effect on me."*

⇒ ⭐⭐ **She reads: he is not calling evidence to prove what happened. He says that is closed, and
closed by her.** Six of the ten topics are declared already proved. Oral evidence is reserved for
*what it was like* and *what it did* — not for whether it occurred.

**Why that is strong, and it is the strongest thing in the set:** it converts her own compliance
document into his proof. Every topic carries a pinpoint — `[¶¶ 17–25, 211–261, 263–271, 285]`,
`[¶¶ 39–113]`, `[¶¶ 70–88]` — so the outline is **self-auditing against her own Form 24 response**.
⇒ **She can check it, and when she checks it she finds she admitted it.** 298 of 303, none denied.

⚠ **And it reframes the hearing before she has filed anything.** The contest is no longer *did these
things happen*. It is only *did employment contribute, and was it reasonable management action*.
**That is a much smaller hearing, and it is the one he wants.**

## 2. ⭐⭐⭐ The second thing she understands: the history-taking attack is half-answered in advance

The standard attack on treating evidence is *the doctor only knows what the patient told him.*
Topic 9 closes the first half of it:

> *"The matters at paragraphs 2 to 7 above, and their effect on me as each occurred and as they
> accumulated, are the matters I reported to each doctor, **as their records show; the events those
> records name are the facts admitted on 8 September 2026** (Tabs M1 to M4)."*

⇒ ⭐⭐ **What he told the doctors is now admitted to be true.** The history in Dr Hawes's note of
1 July 2024 is not an uncorroborated complaint; the events it names are on her own admissions.
**She cannot run "he told the doctor a story" without running into her own response.**

⚠ **What survives for her, and she will see this immediately:** the admissions prove **the acts**,
not **the injury**. The bridge from *those acts occurred* to *employment was a significant
contributing factor* is still open, and it is medical. ⇒ **Her target moves from fact to causation.
The outline pushes her there deliberately.**

## 3. ⭐⭐⭐ The covering letter is the strongest document in the set — stronger than the outlines

> *"the Appellant **does not presently propose to call Ms Chloe Taylor or Ms Tammy Reese**. Their
> evidence is material to the matters pleaded, and the Appellant **proceeds on the basis that the
> Respondent will call them.** If the Respondent's list filed by 30 September 2026 does not name
> them, the Appellant will seek attendance notices and will write to the Commission at that time."*

**What she understands from those three sentences:**

1. ⭐⭐ **The burden of calling them has been placed on her, on the record, three weeks before her
   list is due.** She cannot now quietly omit them.
2. ⭐⭐ **It is pre-written for the bench.** Dwyer said at the mention *"I would have thought the
   regulator will call Ms Taylor to give her side of the story"* and, twice, that uncontradicted
   evidence is likely to be accepted. ⇒ **If she does not call Taylor, that submission writes
   itself and the bench has already supplied the words.**
3. ⚠ **And she has no alternative witness.** The Regulator employs nobody who was at Logan
   Switchboard. On the topics served — consultation, the directory, the reprimand, the roster line,
   the pay loop, the MASPER emails, the office hours — **it is Taylor and Reese, or nobody.**

⇒ **This is the highest-leverage paper in the set and it is not an outline at all.**

## 4. ⭐⭐ What the lay outlines change in her file

Before 8/9 September her file held a self-represented appellant asserting things about his own
workplace. It now holds **two independent people**, one of them **currently employed in the same
department**.

| | What she now has to price |
|---|---|
| **Mr Harrison-Jones** | a former colleague, ⭐ **an eyewitness to the one incident the bench itself identified as needing oral evidence** — what Ms Taylor said about the removed reminder, the sequence, and **the manner and volume** |
| **Ms Conaghan** | ⚠ **a serving Metro South officer** corroborating the console conditions, the inability to self-correct the directory, and the absence of consultation and ballot — **from inside the department** |

⇒ ⭐⭐ **The "difficult employee with a grievance" model does not survive a serving officer of the
employer corroborating the conditions.** That model was the cheapest way to run s 32(5)(a) and it is
now expensive.

## 5. ⭐⭐ The credit attack is blunted before she deploys it

Three paragraphs, one in each outline, all saying the same thing:

- Appellant ¶ 7: *"I never said anything hostile about Ms Taylor or any other member of staff."*
- Harrison-Jones ¶ 7: he *"did not at any time hear the Appellant say anything hostile."*
- Conaghan ¶ 13: she *"has not heard the Appellant speak of Ms Taylor, or of any other member of
  staff, in hostile terms."*
- Plus both lay witnesses on the Communication Book: **no entry criticised any person, directly or
  indirectly** (H-J ¶ 3, Conaghan ¶ 9).

⇒ **She understands that the *corruption / plot / maliciously* material recorded in Review Decision
69983 has been answered in advance, by two people who are not him.** ⚠ It does not remove the
material — he wrote it — but it converts her best credit point from *this is what he is like* into
*this is what he wrote to an insurer in 2024, and two colleagues say he never behaved that way at
work.* **That is a much weaker weapon and she will feel the difference.**

## 6. ⭐ And the pre-existing point is disclosed rather than defended

Topic 8 puts the October 2022 ADHD and anxiety history on the page itself, next to the
16 November 2023 record of *no psychological illness*, the medication position in May 2024, and the
psychiatrist referral renewed **the day after the retraction request**.

⇒ **She understands that her February 2026 row 32 denial is not a surprise he has to absorb at the
hearing.** ⚠ It is answered on the face of the same record. **The value of holding it back is gone.**

## 8. ⭐⭐ Where she will aim — in the order she will get there

1. ⭐⭐⭐ **Causation, and only causation.** The acts are admitted; the manner is corroborated; the
   credit attack is blunted. **The only place left is whether employment was *a significant
   contributing factor*, run through s 32(5)(a).** ⇒ **She will not fight about what happened.
   Everything she has goes into what it means.**
2. ⭐⭐ **The treating-evidence gap.** She now knows there is **no expert report on his side**. Her
   live question before 30 September is whether to obtain one. ⚠ **That is the one piece of
   intelligence the set hands her** — unavoidable, and required candour, but it is real. Watch her
   30 September list for a name that is not Taylor or Reese.
3. ⚠ **Topic 2, in cross.** Travel time, the rest actually available, whether he slept, the car.
   It is the longest topic and the least documentary — he is the only witness to most of it.
4. ⚠ **Topic 7's *"and what followed"*.** She reads it as a reprisal case he has not pleaded.
   ⇒ It gives her a reason to test motive, and motive is the doorway to **s 32(5)(b)**, which she
   has **not** pleaded. **That phrase is the one thing in the set that could hand her a ground she
   does not currently have.**
5. Conaghan ¶¶ 5 and 7, as a cheap credibility win on hearsay.

## 9. ⭐ What the outlines deliberately do not tell her — and why that is strength

Each closes the same way: *"This outline states the topics on which I will give oral evidence. It is
not a statement of evidence and is not verified."* The lay ones: *"not a statement of the witness and
is not signed by the witness."*

⇒ ⭐⭐ **She gets topics, not sentences.** There is nothing to pin him to, no prior inconsistent
statement to build, and no signed account to cross-examine against. **Direction 2 asked for one A4
page per lay witness and it got exactly that — compliant, and no more.**
⚠ **The cost, and she will feel it too:** neither side can value the case precisely off this
material. **It gives her no basis to settle and no basis to abandon.** ⇒ She must either call Taylor
and Reese and fight, or move.

## 10. Net — what she puts in the file note

> *The events are admitted and he says so on the page, with paragraph references. The manner is
> corroborated by a former colleague and by one of our own department's serving officers. The credit
> point is answered by both of them in advance. The pre-existing history is disclosed, not hidden.
> There is no one on our side who can speak to any of it except Ms Taylor and Ms Reese, and he has
> put on the record that he expects us to call them. **The only ground left is causation, and he has
> no expert report.***

⇒ ⭐⭐ **That is a strong set.** Its strength is not in the writing — it is that **it spends her
admissions**, and it leaves her one ground to run and three weeks to decide whether to buy a report
to run it on.

⚠ **Its two real vulnerabilities are both his own**: the length and undocumented character of
topic 2, and the four words in topic 7.
