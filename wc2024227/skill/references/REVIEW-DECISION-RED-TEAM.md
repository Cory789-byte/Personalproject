# PART 3 — THE RED TEAM: SIX ERRORS, IN ORDER OF FORCE

## ⭐⭐⭐ ERROR 1 — THE COUNTING FALLACY. Contrary to binding authority, and on the face of the page

> *"I note that since **two out of the three** causative factors amounted to reasonable management
> action taken in a reasonable way, this means your psychological injury **mainly** arose out of
> such management action."*

⛔⛔ **That is arithmetic, not evaluation — and it is the exact reasoning the Industrial Court has
rejected:**
- ***Adams* [2015] ICQ 1 at [23]**, adopting ***Q-COMP v Hochen***: **no dominant cause test**, and
  reasonable management action is **not** neutralised by outnumbering.
- ***Mahaffey* [2016] ICQ 10 at [51], [54]–[57]**: **a single unreasonable stressor with sufficient
  causal connection suffices.**

⭐⭐ **And the word *"mainly"* is a dominant-cause test in disguise.** s 32(5)(a) asks whether the
injury arose **out of** reasonable management action — **not whether it *mainly* did.**

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the strongest attack available, it needs no evidence, and it is visible on the
document.**

## ⭐⭐ ERROR 2 — *Delaney* recited, its threshold never tested

She states the test correctly: actions *"joined by **subject matter, time and personality**"* may be
evaluated globally.

⛔ **She then never asks whether these three factors are so joined. She simply aggregates them.**

| Factor | Subject | Time | Person |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Grievance handling | Aug–Nov 2023 | **Reese** |
| 3 | Pay | Feb–May 2024 | **Taylor / payroll** |
| 4 | The roster | 17–18 Mar 2024 | **Taylor** |

⇒ ⭐⭐ **Different subject matter, different periods, partly different people. The *Delaney*
precondition is arguably not met — and it was never examined.**

## ⭐⭐ ERROR 3 — she declined to decide the instrument question

> *"I find there was uncertainty… regarding whether the 8-hour agreement continued to apply.
> However, **it is not necessary for me to consider whether the employer's stance on its
> application was reasonable.**"*

⭐ On factor 4 the shortcut helped him — **7 < 8 either way, even allowing the 30-minute early
leave.** ⛔ **But it means she never decided whether applying a 2020 casual-era agreement to a 2024
full-time roster was reasonable — which is the *manner* question, and the manner question is the
case.**

⇒ ⭐⭐ **She found the outcome unreasonable without examining the conduct that produced it. That is
why the finding is a floor, not a ceiling.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ ERROR 4 — factor 4 treated as an INCIDENT, never as a SEQUENCE

> *"I find the employer **only failed to maintain a 10-hour break**… **on one occasion**… I
> therefore find this factor is able to be substantiated **only to the extent of the one
> occasion**."*

⛔⛔ **The request of 8 April, the escalation to HR of 9 April, the follow-up of 24 April and the
refusal of 1 May appear nowhere in her factor 4 analysis.** She recites that correspondence
elsewhere in the decision (pp 21–22) **and does not carry it into the reasoning.**

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **She assessed one shift. The conduct was a 44-day sequence through four decision-makers.
That is the whole reframe, and it is available because she never did it.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ ERROR 5 — the decisive fact was not before her

**Nowhere does the decision ask whether a fatigue risk framework existed, whether any risk
assessment was done, or whether the employer's own policy control was applied.**

⛔ **Because MSH's concessions did not exist until 5 June 2026** — no framework until *"after
30 June 2024"*, no risk assessment or register, *"no consequential changes."*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **The single most important fact in the case was not in front of her.** ⭐⭐ **This is the
safest and cleanest attack of all: not that she was wrong, but that she decided on a materially
incomplete record.** It requires no criticism of anyone and it is unanswerable.

## ⚠ ERROR 6 — factor 3, and a passage that needs full reading

At p 25 she records the submission *"that it was unreasonable for you to be required to fight for
your entitlements"* — and then finds the conduct **reasonable.**

⚠ **The full factor 3 reasoning has not yet been read line by line.** ⭐ But note what is admitted
in the appeal and is not analysed there: the **3 May *"IMMEDIATELY"*** instruction (¶40), the

# PART 5 — ⛔⛔ BIAS: THE HONEST ANSWER IS NO, AND PURSUING IT WOULD BE A SERIOUS MISTAKE

## 5.1 ⭐⭐⭐ It is legally irrelevant — the appeal is DE NOVO

Under **s 550(4) WCRA** the Commission determines the matter **afresh**. It is **not** reviewing
her decision for error or bias; **it is replacing it.**

⇒ ⛔ **There is no ground on which bias in the review could operate. Even if it were proved, it
would change nothing.**

## 5.2 ⭐⭐ The decision's own content is inconsistent with bias

**A partial decision-maker does not:**
- find the injury established;
- find employment **a significant contributing factor**;
- record the treating GP saying **work events were the *sole* cause**;
- and find **factor 4 unreasonable.**

**She made four findings against her own employer's ultimate position, and then misapplied an
aggregation rule.**

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **That is the signature of an error of law, not partiality.** ⚠ **And it is the truthful
reading — which matters more than whether it is convenient.**

## 5.3 ⛔ *Briginshaw* — he would be raising his own bar to win nothing

Bias is a serious allegation. It raises **the standard of persuasion**, and **the onus in this
appeal is his** (Guide 7.3). ⇒ **He would be increasing his own burden in order to establish
something that carries no remedy.**

## 5.4 ⛔⛔ AND IT POINTS AT A STATUTORY EXCLUSION

**WCRA s 32(5)(c) excludes injury arising out of *"action by the Regulator or an insurer in
connection with the worker's application for compensation."***

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **A narrative about the Regulator's handling of his claim does not merely fail — it walks
toward a provision written to exclude exactly that.** ⛔ Together with **s 32(5)(b)** (perception),
that is two exclusions opened by one argument.

## 5.5 ⛔⛔ "ON MULTIPLE TRACKS" — this is the conspiracy framing, and it is prohibited

Different decision-makers, different bodies, different statutory functions. **A pattern asserted
across unrelated institutions is exactly the framing discipline rule 1 prohibits, and it is the
fastest available route to losing credibility.**

⚠ **And there is a live collateral risk:** a reprisal complaint against Ms Matheson already exists
(**OIR Issue 4712 / ESU 4822**) and is **quarantined off the WC track**. **Alleging bias would merge
the tracks and hand them the vexatious characterisation for free.**

## 5.6 ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT REPLACES IT — and it is stronger

> **The Regulator's own delegate found that employment was a significant contributing factor, that
> Dr Hawes recorded no pre-existing condition, and that factor 4 amounted to unreasonable
> management action. The Regulator now pleads that all management action was reasonable and denies
> causation.**

**That is an inconsistency in their case, provable from their own documents, requiring no
allegation about any person.**

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **Error beats bias. Error is provable from the page. Bias requires proving a mind.**

## 7.7 ⚠ HER TWO AUTHORITIES — both answerable, and one confirms an earlier correction

**1. *Prizeman v Q-COMP* [2005] QIC 53** — *"the onus is on the worker seeking compensation to
establish that management action was unreasonable… in the absence of evidence to support that
assertion, management action must be considered reasonable."*
⭐ **Correct, and consistent with Appeal Guide 7.3.** ⭐⭐ **It also confirms the earlier pleading
error was rightly corrected** — the 9A had asserted the onus lay on the Respondent *under
Prizeman*. **Prizeman says the opposite, and the Regulator's own delegate cited it that way.**

**2. *Bowers v WorkCover Queensland* [2002] QIC 18** — *"management action need not be perfect or
above criticism and reasonable management action permits some failings, deficiencies and flaws"*;
*"blemishes… does not make the management action unreasonable."*

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **This is the blemish doctrine, and the answer is the no-system point: a blemish presupposes
a system.** MSH concedes there was **no fatigue framework at the Switchboard, no risk assessment,
and no register.** **You cannot have a blemish in a system that does not exist.**
