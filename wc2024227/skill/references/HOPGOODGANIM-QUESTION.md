## ⭐⭐⭐ THE STRUCTURAL POINT — and it is the sharpest legitimate form of the question

**WorkCover Queensland is NOT the Workers' Compensation Regulator. They are different bodies, and
on a review they are on opposite sides:**

| | Role |
|---|---|
| **WorkCover Queensland** | ⭐ **The insurer. It made the decision that was under review** (13 September 2024) |
| **The Workers' Compensation Regulator** (OIR / WCRS) | ⭐ **The reviewer. It reviewed WorkCover's decision** (24 October 2024) |

⇒ ⭐⭐⭐ **A firm on WorkCover's panel would ordinarily act for the party whose decision was being
reviewed — not for the reviewer.**

**Three facts, no allegation:** *the decision under review was WorkCover's; the reviewer was the
Regulator; the review decision PDF was generated from a Word document carrying the document
management profile of a firm on WorkCover's legal services panel.*

# PART 3 — IS IT PERMITTED? The law, verified

## 3.1 WHO MUST DECIDE

**WCRA s 545(1):** *"**The Regulator** must, within 25 business days after receiving the
application, review the decision and decide (**the review decision**)…"*

**WCRA s 329 — Delegation by the Regulator:**
> *"The Regulator may delegate a function or power under this Act to an **appropriately
> qualified**—
> (a) **public service employee**; or
> (b) **authorised person**; or
> (c) **person, or a person of a class, prescribed under a regulation**."*

⇒ ⛔⛔ **A private law firm is not a public service employee.** Absent a prescribing regulation,
**it cannot hold the s 545 delegation.**

⭐ **And the decision is signed *Victoria Squires, Senior Reviewing Officer, Review Unit, Workers'
Compensation Regulatory Services*.** ⇒ **A public service employee. The delegation is proper on its
face.**

## 3.2 ⭐⭐⭐ WHAT IS PERMITTED, AND WHAT IS NOT

| | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **PERMITTED** | **Receiving assistance — legal advice, research, even a draft.** This is orthodox. Decision-makers across government routinely have reasons drafted by others. **What matters is that the decision-maker applies their own mind and adopts them as her own** |
| ⛔ **NOT PERMITTED** | **(a) Unlawful delegation** — someone else actually deciding. **(b) Acting under dictation** — the decision-maker not bringing an independent mind to bear |

⚠⚠ **AND THE METADATA CANNOT DISTINGUISH BETWEEN THEM.** ⭐⭐⭐ **A document management profile shows
that a document passed through a system. It does not show who decided.** **That is the whole
limit of what has been found.**

# PART 4 — ⛔⛔⛔ WHY THIS GOES NOWHERE IN THE APPEAL, AND WHAT TO DO

## 4.1 THE DECISIVE PRACTICAL POINT

**Even if the worst reading were correct, it changes nothing in WC/2024/227.**

⭐⭐⭐ **The appeal is DE NOVO — s 550(4). The Commission is not reviewing her decision for
lawfulness. It is replacing it.** A defect in the review is simply irrelevant to whether he
sustained an injury under s 32.

## 4.2 ⭐⭐⭐ AND JUDICIAL REVIEW WOULD GIVE HIM *LESS* THAN THE APPEAL

**The remedy for an administrative-law defect is judicial review in the Supreme Court.** Its
ordinary outcome is that **the decision is set aside and remitted to be made again according to
law.**

⇒ ⛔⛔ **That is LESS than he already has available.** A successful appeal gets **the claim
accepted.** A successful judicial review gets him **another review** — plus a Supreme Court
proceeding, its costs, and years.

⭐⭐⭐ **He would spend a great deal to win something worth less than what he is already pursuing.**

## 4.3 ⛔ AND IT WOULD COST HIM THE BEST THING HE HAS

The argument that carries most weight in settlement is:
> ***The Regulator's own delegate found that employment was a significant contributing factor and
> that factor 4 amounted to unreasonable management action.***

⛔⛔ **That argument requires her to have been a genuine, independent decision-maker. Alleging the
decision was authored elsewhere destroys it.** (Red team §8.4.)
