# Their public holiday / pay outline v the Award, EB11 and their own documents — 25 Sep 2026

Sources opened at source today: Award (ATT03) cl 15.1(d), 15.2(b)/(d), 15.6, 18.10, 19.2, 23 (verbatim, pp 29–41); EB11 (ATT01) cl 7.1.10, 9.13.1, 9.18; employer letter 6 Sep 2024 (LoD 31); Att 3 (Grant→Taylor 3 May 2024); PP22 2023 roster (rendered); PP21 2024 v2.0 (rendered). The Squires recital of cl 23(h) is now verified against the Award itself (the 23 Aug caution is closed).

## Findings
1. **"Discretionary public holiday allowance" is not how the Award describes it.** Award cl 23(h): a 19.2(a) additional-week employee rostered off on Easter Saturday/Easter Sunday/Show Day/Labour Day "**shall** be paid an additional day's wage" (or a day in lieu, or a day added to annual leave). 23(i) excepts only those not ordinarily required to work weekends; a 24/7 continuous shift worker is not excepted. The employer's own letter of 6 Sep 2024: "paid an additional days pay due to not being rostered Easter Saturday, 30 March 2024, **in accordance with clause 23** of the … Award". The Regulator's own Review Decision recites cl 23(h) (fact 141, admitted). ⇒ Earl/Wright's "discretionary … must be approved and submitted by the line manager on an AVAC" conflicts with the Award and with MSH's 2024 letter. The AVAC is the payroll mechanism, not a condition of the entitlement.
2. **Easter Saturday 2024 (30 Mar): rostered off.** Confirmed on PP21 v2.0: the cell is blank; he was rostered on 29 Mar (Good Friday, handwritten 1500-23) and on 31 Mar (Easter Sunday 0700-1500). The 23(h) elements are met.
3. **Quantum: their end point is probably right.** The day was eventually paid, and 23(h) gives one additional day, not two. "Not separately entitled to a 'public holiday not worked' amount on top" is consistent with the Award. Concede it.
4. **Who coded it "public holiday not required"?** Earl's outline presents the coding as a processing matter. The admitted record (facts 242–245) shows the "public holiday not required" AVAC was submitted by Ms Taylor on 9 Apr 2024, after his request of 8 Apr, and on 1 May payroll was "still reviewing your entitlements regarding public holidays not required" (fact 255). The outline does not say whose entry it was.
5. **Two payroll accounts of the PP21 2024 hours problem.** Earl (2026): the excess was caused by the PH coding; the fix was recoding it to the allowance. Grant (Payroll, 3 May 2024, Att 3): "Fortnight 18.03.24 … has too many ordinary shifts for the fortnight resulting in wage reduction of 7.55hrs as **one of the shifts needs to be overtime**." Grant also writes "contracted 76hrs"; Earl writes "80 hours". Different explanations and different fixes. ⚠ Grant does not identify which shift. Ask; do not infer that it was the 18 Mar 06:00 shift.
6. **Wright's fatigue advice is half right.** "The Award's fatigue provision applies to breaks around overtime, not between two ordinary shifts": wrong as to the break. Award cl 15.1(d): "Employees must be allowed a break of not less than 10 hours between the termination of one shift and the commencement of another shift" (8 by agreement). Their own outline says the 8-hour agreement was made "under clause 15.1". Right as to money: 15.1(d) prescribes no rate; double rates arise only under cl 18.10(b) (overtime). ⇒ No unpaid entitlement for the ordinary-shift breach, but the break rule applied, and 7 hours breached it even on the 8-hour agreement (SOFC ¶22(a); Squires: "direct contradiction to the award and the 8-hour agreement"). If any PP21 shift was overtime (item 5), 18.10 comes into question — unresolved.
7. **Easter 2023: their position mostly holds.** PP22 2023 v1.0: Shepherd rostered off Fri 7 Apr and Sat 8 Apr; Sun 9 0700-1500; Mon 10 2300-0700. Earl: the Mon 10 night shift falls to Tuesday (cl 15.6/23(m)); 1 h Monday and 7 h Tuesday, so correct. Easter Monday and Good Friday are not 23(h) days. ⇒ For a 19.2(a) employee nothing more is payable for 10 Apr 2023. **Do not press the 2023 claim.** Small inconsistency: Taylor says he "worked … the Monday public holiday"; Earl says that shift was not public-holiday work. Taylor's "additional weekend shift" fits Sat 8 being blank on the published roster.
8. **EB11: their outline makes no EB11 public-holiday claim**, and EB11 has none that displaces Award cl 23 (9.18 concerns special public holidays only). EB11 bears elsewhere:
   - cl 9.13.1: "All employees … shall be provided with a roster prescribing their pattern of work. The roster shall be published at least 14 days in advance". This goes to PP21 v1.0 (no line) and the unknown date of v2.0. Reese's outline mentions "roster-display requirements" only as his 2023 complaint.
   - cl 7.1.10: "fatigue risk management is a health and safety issue". Against Wright's "rostering practice issue".
   - Award cl 15.2(d): changes within a roster by agreement, or 24 hours' notice, or double time for the next shift. Relevant only if v2.0 was a within-roster change; date unknown.

## Addendum: is the outline conflating the public holiday with the 7-hour break?
- **The hours arithmetic supports Earl.** PP21 v2.0 Shepherd line: 18, 19 (S/L), 20, 23, 24, 25 (A/L), 26 (A/L), 28, 29, 31 = 10 × 8 h = 80 h. Adding a "PH not required" 7.6 h entry for 30 Mar makes an 11th and exceeds 80. The payroll reduction of 7.55 h is about that size. ⇒ Do not argue that the reduction was caused by the 18 Mar shift. Earl's mechanism is coherent.
- **Two issues sit in one outline paragraph.** The fatigue question (was the 7-hour break a breach of Award cl 15.1(d) and a WHS matter under EB11 cl 7.1.10?) is answered in payroll terms: "rostered on ordinary hours rather than overtime"; "a rostering practice issue for the line manager rather than an unpaid entitlement". That answers whether money is owed, not whether the break was lawful or safe. Keep the two apart:
  (1) Easter Saturday: an Award cl 23(h) entitlement, paid late through the manager's AVAC coded "PH not required" — a pay matter.
  (2) 17–18 Mar: a 7-hour break against a 10-hour rule (8 by agreement), on a shift absent from the published roster — a working-conditions matter. No payment attaches, and that is not the question.
- Residual: Grant (3 May 2024) framed the PP21 fix as "one of the shifts needs to be overtime". Earl frames it as recoding the PH. Ask which fix was actually processed; the AVAC/PRN for PP21 will show it.
