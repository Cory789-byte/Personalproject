> `documents/instruments/ATT19–ATT21`. All quotations verified against the in-force text.

## 1. WHAT MSH ACTUALLY CITED

**31 July 2026, both letters:**
> "We are requesting this information in accordance with the provisions of the **Human Resources
> (HR) Policy G3: Reasonable Adjustment**, and **sections 17 and 19 of the Work Health and Safety
> Act 2011**."

**That is the entire stated basis.** Across five weeks and six positions, nothing else has ever been
identified.

---

## 2. THE MOST CHARITABLE RECONSTRUCTION OF THEIR THEORY

It is articulable, and Forrest stated it on **7 July 2026**:

> "your treating medical practitioner has advised that there are **unresolved workplace matters
> involving line management**. At this stage, the Health Service **does not have sufficient
> information to understand the nature of these matters or assess whether they present any
> psychosocial hazards or risks** that may affect your proposed return to work."

Reconstructed in full, MSH's position appears to be:

1. We hold a **WHS s 19** duty to ensure health and safety so far as reasonably practicable
2. The ECC identifies psychosocial stressors, including unresolved matters involving line management
3. We do not understand those matters, so we cannot assess the risk
4. Until we can assess it, we cannot safely permit a return
5. **G03** requires reasonable adjustment, and information is needed to identify what adjustments
6. Therefore we seek information under G03, in discharge of our s 19 duty

**That theory is coherent. It is not absurd. It should be engaged with, not dismissed.**

## 3. WHAT EACH INSTRUMENT ACTUALLY DOES

### G03 / QH-POL-210 — *supports the request; contradicts the exclusion*
| Provision | Effect |
|---|---|
| Purpose | "To **assist** employees requiring support to **meet the genuine occupational requirements** of their role" — an **enabling** policy |
| cl 1.1 | Temporary adjustments exist so employees "**continue work during their recovery**" |
| cl 1.1.1 | Support extends to employees "with **no claim or entitlement to compensation** (health management case)" |
| cl 1 | "Managers are responsible for ensuring work environments and work **do not unreasonably prevent employment for people**" |
| cl 3 | Supporting information permitted "**where necessary**, to either identify required workplace adjustments and/or assess the suitability of proposed adjustments" |
| cl 2 | Unjustifiable hardship "tested against **the whole organisation**"; "**the onus is on Queensland Health**… to prove an adjustment is unreasonable" |

⇒ **G03 can bear an information request.** It cannot bear an exclusion — it says the opposite.

### WHS ss 17 and 19 — *duties, not powers*
- **s 17**: a duty to ensure health and safety "requires the person to **eliminate** risks… or **minimise** those risks so far as is reasonably practicable"
- **s 19(1)**: a PCBU "must **ensure**, so far as is reasonably practicable, the health and safety of workers"

⇒ Both **impose obligations on MSH**. Neither confers any power over a worker. The way a PCBU
discharges s 19 in relation to a **psychosocial** hazard is to **assess and control the hazard** —
the Psychosocial Code 2022 treats hazards as features of the work, controlled by the PCBU.
**Removing the worker is not a control; it is an avoidance of the assessment.**

### ⭐ IP ACT 2009 — QPP 3.3 (NEW FINDING, verified 31 Jul 2026)
The Act now applies **Queensland Privacy Principles** (sch 3), current as at 1 July 2026 —
superseding the old IPP/NPP split. Earlier note on that question is obsolete.

> **QPP 3.3** An agency **must not collect sensitive information** about an individual unless—
> (a) **the individual consents** to the collection **and** the information is **reasonably
> necessary for, or directly related to,** 1 or more of the agency's functions or activities; or
> (b) QPP 3.4 applies [required by law; permitted general situation; permitted health situation;
> law enforcement].

**sch 5:** "sensitive information… means… **(b) health information about the individual**".
**health information** includes "the individual's **health at any time**" and "**a disability** of
the individual at any time".

⇒ **The RFMI collects sensitive information.** Absent QPP 3.4, MSH's lawful basis is **QPP 3.3(a):
consent plus reasonable necessity.**
⇒ **Cory's consent is therefore a statutory precondition, not a courtesy** — and consent may be
**scoped**. Declining option 2, and consenting question-by-question, is the operation of QPP 3.3, not
obstruction of a process.
⇒ **This is now the third independent necessity test**, alongside G03 cl 3 and **AD Act s 124(3)**
(which puts the onus on MSH to prove information was "reasonably required").

### ❌ PS ACT 2022 ch 3 pt 8 div 5 — **NEVER CITED**
The only statutory power to compel medical examination. Verified verbatim, current 1 July 2026:

> **s 103** This division applies… if— (a) the employee **is absent from duty** or the chief
> executive is reasonably satisfied the employee **is not performing** the employee's duties
> satisfactorily; **and** (b) the chief executive **reasonably suspects** that the absence or
> unsatisfactory performance **is caused by** mental or physical illness or disability.
> **s 104** The chief executive may (a) appoint a doctor… and (b) **require the employee to submit**
> to the medical examination.
> **s 105** The employee **must not be given sick leave** for any period during which the employee
> fails to comply with a requirement under section 104(b).

⇒ **MSH never invoked it.** And the gateway is problematic: no performance issue has ever been put
to Cory, which leaves absence — **and the absence is MSH's own act**.
⚠️ **s 105 is the only statutory link in the entire division between this process and leave** — and
it operates only where an s 104(b) requirement is not complied with. **Never invoked. So it supplies
no basis for the sick-leave debiting either.**

## 4. WHERE THE THEORY HOLDS, AND WHERE IT FAILS

### ✅ HOLDS
**The information request itself is defensible in principle.** Seeking medical information to assess
a psychosocial risk is a legitimate way to discharge s 19; G03 cl 3 permits it where necessary; and
QPP 3.3(a) permits it with consent. **MSH offered the consent mechanism** (options 1 and 2) —
operating the correct legal model, even if it never articulated it.

### ❌ FAILS — six ways

1. **The exclusion does not follow from anything cited.** G03 says adjustments exist so employees
   *continue work*. Neither WHS provision authorises removing a worker while information is
   gathered.
2. **They never assessed the risk.** If the theory is "we cannot assess it", the answer is to assess
   it. **WHS ss 47–49** require consultation when identifying hazards and assessing risks;
   **EB12 cl 7.2.1** supplies the mechanism. Five weeks; no assessment; no consultation.
3. ⭐ **The thing they say they do not understand is the thing they hold the file on.** "Unresolved
   workplace matters involving line management" is the PID (24-ESU-1130), the complaint about Hughes
   of 4 October 2025, the complaint of February 2026, and two years of correspondence.
   **MSH is the archive for the matter it is asking his doctor to explain.**
4. **Q3 inverts the duty** — asking a treating GP whether a workplace is safe, when s 19 puts that
   on the PCBU.
5. **Q6 applies the wrong test** under the very policy the same letter cites.
6. **Q9 asks the doctor to answer a question on which G03 cl 2 places the onus on MSH.**

---

## 5. ⭐ THE VERDICT

**MSH used G03 as the vehicle for an information request.** G03 can carry that.

**It then treated the exclusion as though it followed automatically.** Nothing it cited makes it
follow. G03 contemplates the opposite; the WHS provisions are duties; and the leave treatment has no
basis in either.

**And it never used the one power that would have authorised compulsion** — PS Act s 104 — because
the s 103 gateway is not met on facts of MSH's own making.

⇒ **So the answer to the question is split:**
- **For the request:** a policy was used, and it can bear the weight — subject to necessity under
  G03 cl 3, QPP 3.3 and AD Act s 124.
- **For the exclusion:** **nothing was used at all.** No instrument was identified in five weeks
  because none exists. The exclusion was not an exercise of a power. It was an act without one.

That is why the six shifting bases exist. **Each was an attempt to find, after the fact, an
authority for something already done.**
