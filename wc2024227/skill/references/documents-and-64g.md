# Documents & the Rule 64G Application — WC/2024/227

## Rule 64G framework (confirmed against the Rules and authority)

Subdivision 7A (rr 64A–64I), Industrial Relations (Tribunals) Rules 2011 (Qld). Key points:
- **r 64B(1):** notice may require production within 14 days of a document directly relevant to a matter in issue, in the non-party's possession, producible at hearing. **r 64B(2):** no production if another reasonably simple and inexpensive way exists — NOT engaged where records sit only with the non-party.
- **r 64C(1):** the notice must STATE the matter in issue about which the document is directly relevant. (Pleading anchor — Annexure B does this item by item.)
- **"Directly relevant" = tends to prove or disprove an allegation in issue** (Robson v REB Engineering [1997] 2 Qd R 102; the narrower standard, not train-of-inquiry). NOTE: v5 of the application strips ALL case law per Cory's instruction — the standard is stated as rule-text only.
- **r 64E:** objection within 7 days (or later with leave); grounds (a) expense/inconvenience, (b) relevance, (c) particularity, (d) privilege, (e) confidentiality, (f) effect on a person, (g) should have been served.
- **r 64F:** objection operates as an automatic STAY. The Commission cannot act until the party applies under 64G.
- **r 64G(1):** within 7 days of the objection, the party may apply for a decision. **r 64G(2):** the tribunal may make any order it considers appropriate, including lift the stay / vary the notice / set aside the notice (no "lapse" order; power non-exhaustive). **r 64G(3):** each party bears own costs unless otherwise ordered.
- **r 64I:** the party pays the non-party's reasonable expenses of production (notice within 1 month; assessment available).
- **Lead on-point authority:** Bunney v Workers' Compensation Regulator [2020] QIRC 210 (Power IC) — applied Subdivision 7A to a Form 29 objection; made a partial-production order under r 64H(1) within 14 days, upheld privilege only on genuine sub-categories, each party's own costs under r 64G(3). The template for the order to seek. (Held in reserve as the model — not cited in the no-case-law v5.)
- **Onus (substantive appeal):** Davis v Blackwood [2014] ICQ 009 — the APPELLANT bears the onus of establishing the injury, including excluding s 32(5)(a). Confirmed and corrected in v5.

## Onus — the corrected position (CRITICAL, was wrong in earlier drafts)

The Appellant bears the onus of establishing the injury within s 32 — that employment significantly contributed AND that it did not arise out of reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way (s 32(5)(a) is part of the definition, not a defence the Regulator proves). Earlier drafts said the Respondent carried the onus — WRONG, now fixed. The corrected onus is the STRONGER necessity argument: a party who must prove the character of conduct cannot be left without the records of it, held solely by the author of that conduct. Severity (degree/duration/density) goes to BOTH limbs — the same records prove the conduct was unreasonable (32(5)(a)) and significant enough to cause injury (32(1)).
