Because the response was in time, **no deemed admission arose by silence.** The 298 are
**express admissions made in a served response**, not admissions "taken to have been made
under subrule (2)".

⭐ **That makes them more secure, not less.** **r 49(3) empowers withdrawal only of "an
admission taken to have been made under subrule (2)"** — a *deemed* admission. On its face
r 49(3) gives no mechanism to withdraw these. Withdrawal would need some other footing
(general powers; s 531(3); an application to amend), and would have to be justified.

### ⭐⭐ "Not admitted" is not "disputed"

r 49(2) operates unless the party serves a notice **"disputing the facts or the authenticity
of the documents"**. The five entries read, in full, **"Not admitted"** — no ground, no
positive dispute. A non-admission puts the other side to proof; it does not dispute.

Matheson's covering letter anticipates the point:

> *"The enclosed documents indicates the Respondent's notice under rule 49(2) disputing those
> of the stated facts and documents that are not admitted, so as to avoid those facts and
> documents being taken to be admitted by operation of that subrule."*

**Assessment: the argument is available but is not a winner on its own.** r 49(2) aims at
*silence*, and a Commissioner is unlikely to deem admitted a fact the Respondent has plainly
said it will not accept. ⚠ Do not pitch it as a knockout.

⭐ **But the contrast between the two forms is telling.** In the **Form 25** the same officer,
on the same day, under the same rule, wrote **"Disputed"** against 14 documents. In the
**Form 24** she wrote **"Not admitted"**. She knew the difference between the two words and
chose different ones. That is worth putting in a letter, not in a submission.

### The five, one by one

- **¶ 154** — *"The Respondent's amended List of Documents dated 14 August 2026 does not list the
  Communication Book, nor any page or entry from it."* ⭐ **This is a fact about the
  Respondent's own document, served by it three weeks earlier, verifiable by reading it.**
  Non-admission without a ground is indefensible. **Press this first.**
- **¶¶ 228, 229, 231** — the contents of the 2024 Emergency Code Register (Tab 31), a
  third-party document. Non-admission is defensible on provenance.
- **¶ 230** — *"Six of the entries recorded in that register for 18 March 2024 bear times between
  6:00 and 14:00."* This is **arithmetic on ¶ 229**. Not admitting a counting exercise adds
  nothing and is worth putting to her.

⛔ **r 49 has no costs-of-proof sanction** (unlike UCPR r 189(4)). Any costs consequence runs
through **WCRA s 558(3)**, so build the correspondence record now.

### Does the dispute matter?

**Mostly no.** The Form 24 admissions give the facts directly; authenticity is not needed to
prove what a document says when the fact that it says it is admitted. It bites only at
**tender**, where the covering letter reserves objections — and there **IR Act s 531(2)(a)**
(the commission is not bound by the rules of evidence) plus the 54 admitted facts make any
objection very hard to sustain.

## 4. The covering letter's qualifications — all legally correct

The letter reserves that an admission is not a concession of **relevance**, **admissibility**,
any **characterisation or inference** drawn from it, or the **accuracy, reliability or weight**
of contents; that admissions as to documents are limited to **existence and wording**, not the
truth of any statement in them — *"including any statement by a treating medical practitioner,
any finding recorded in Review Decision 69983, or any statement made by an officer or employee
of Metro South"*; that the appeal is **de novo**; and that objections at tender are reserved.

**That is an accurate statement of r 49. Treat it as a map, not an obstacle.** It flags exactly
where the fight is:

- ⭐ **"any finding recorded in Review Decision 69983"** — the finding that the 17–18 March
  rostering was unreasonable management action, and that employment was a significant
  contributing factor, are expressly reserved. Both must be re-established on the hearing.
- ⭐ **"any statement by a treating medical practitioner"** — the medical is reserved in full.
  Confirms Guide 7.6.5: the authors must attend.

### What the qualification does **not** reach

1. **Existence and wording** of every admitted document — conceded in terms.
2. **Dates, times, senders, recipients** — admitted as facts.
3. **Intervals** — arithmetic on admitted times depends on no statement's truth.
4. ⭐ **The negatives** — facts about the state of the Respondent's *own* statement of facts and
   contentions and List of Documents. These are not "contents of a document" at all, and the
   truth-of-contents qualification does not touch them.

Categories 3 and 4 are where the value is concentrated, and the qualification is silent on both.
