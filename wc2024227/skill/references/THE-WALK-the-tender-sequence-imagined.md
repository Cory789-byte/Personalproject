# The walk — the documents tendered in the Commission, imagined in order
> 14 September 2026. A rehearsal narrative of the documentary spine tendered at hearing,
> station by station, with what is said and the admitted paragraph each ends on. The stations
> are the map's; the order is the outline's (conditions → what arrived → what could not be
> fixed → the pay loop → May → the injury). ⛔ Internal rehearsal document — refine into the
> actual tender sequence when the exhibit list is built (s 554).

**Station 1 — the duty. Tab 1.** "I tender the role description for my position. It requires the
occupant to participate in the emergency response process by receiving emergency response
notifications and distributing them to the appropriate response groups, strictly adhering to
protocols and timeframes, and to maintain the database so that the information held within
Switchboard Services is accurate. Contents admitted at paragraphs 1 to 13."

**Station 2 — the means removed. Tab 1A.** "Ms Stibbard's email of 18 July 2023. 'While I am
fixing up the database and all of its entries, I will be removing everyone's access to the
database.' Her stated hours: every Tuesday and second Monday. An urgent entry on a day she was
absent went to Ms Taylor. After hours, a correction was to 'wait until either Chloe or myself
are back.' The Contact and Number Changes book was removed from the room. Paragraphs 39 to 45.
The Respondent does not allege access was restored before 18 June 2024. Paragraph 55."

**Station 3 — the hours. Tab 30, Tab 25.** "Ms Taylor's email of 23 August 2023, subject
'What's Chloe's Hours?!': starting between 6 and 9, finishing between 2 and 5. Paragraphs 70 to
72. The Respondent does not allege fixed office hours were stated to the staff at any time
between that day and 17 May 2024. Paragraph 73."

**Station 4 — the same two people become the after-hours contact. Tab 6.** "15 April 2024,
12:39 pm, to the whole department, copied to Ms Reese: she and Ms Stibbard had added themselves
to after-hours on call, effective from today. Paragraphs 49 to 53."

**Station 5 — what then arrived. Tabs 8B, 8.** "The MASPER Registrar to Ms Taylor, 3 May 2024:
five occasions, including at 14:46 'MET call team called x5290 asking where MET call was located
VHUB. Switchboard could not tell them where VHUB was. Had to be redirected by MASPER.' 8 May:
four more. Nine occasions. Paragraphs 56 to 62."

**Station 6 — the six days. Tab 9 series.** "The first reply is 9 May at 9:20 am, five days,
eighteen hours and fourteen minutes after the first report. Paragraph 64. It asks the reporting
doctor to confirm her business hours, because they were 'not provided on the rosters.' At 10:15
Ms Taylor tells the department there have been 'many ongoing issues raised by the MASPER and the
medical department about calls being transferred to the wrong medical teams.' Paragraphs 65 to
67. The occasions fell while the arrangement of 15 April was in force. Paragraph 68."

**Station 7 — 'we can not help patients.' Tabs 10-11 series.** "The Integrated Respiratory
Service, 15 and 20 May, each marked of high importance: calls for another service are coming to
it, and it 'can not help patients or other clinical staff with OPD issues.' Paragraphs 89 to
104. The Appellant escalated the cause within two hours and twenty-five minutes. The reply said
the task 'was being actioned' and had been discussed 'this morning.' The List of Documents
records no document of that discussion, and no communication to that service. Paragraphs 105 to
108."

**Station 8 — the question about hours, and what followed. Tabs 9A, 9B.** "15 May, 1:15 pm: the
Appellant asks Ms Taylor to share her office hours so the department can be aware of her regular
schedule. Paragraph 74. At 6:23 pm Ms Reese replies that the email did not demonstrate the
iCARE2 value of Respect and did not comply with the Code of Conduct. Paragraph 76. On 17 May at
9:30 am Ms Taylor states her hours to the department. Paragraphs 81, 88. It is the first
statement of fixed hours on the record. Paragraph 73."

**Station 9 — the pay loop. Tabs 12 to 14.** "8 April: the request. 9 April: escalated to HR 'to
confirm policies.' 1 May: refused after 'a consultation with payroll and Human Resources.'
Twenty-three days. Paragraphs 242 to 249. 3 May: Payroll writes that the manager must submit an
AVAC for each fortnight. 13 May: Payroll directs the Appellant back to his line manager. 28 May:
he is asked to sign a validation of claims older than three months. Paragraphs 182 to 210."

**Station 10 — the register. Tab 31.** "The employer's Emergency Code Register, produced [by the
Regulator / by Metro South Health]. For 17 March 2024: a MET call at 19:16, a Code Blue to the
same bed at 19:20, four minutes later, a further MET call at 21:16, each in the final hours
before the 23:00 finish. For 18 March: a Code Grey at 8:48, MET calls, a cancelled MET call, two
neonatal MET calls at 11:35 and 13:09. Six entries between 6:00 and 14:00, inside the shift that
began at 6:00 after the rest the Commission has heard about. [Paragraphs 228 to 231 as
admitted, or proved by the register itself.]"

**Station 11 — the employer's certificate. Tab 20.** "The letter of Metro South Health to this
Commission, 5 June 2026, signed by the Chief Executive. The fatigue records 'do not exist.' The
training 'only applies to health practitioners and clinical assistants.' Fatigue assessment at
the Switchboard 'occurred after 30 June 2024.' 'There have been no consequential changes to
operating procedures over the period requested.' All employee complaints are 'made directly to
the Line Manager of Switch Board and managed solely via email or verbally with the complainant.'
And 'a spreadsheet of recorded MET calls is available for the period 17-18 March 2024.'
Paragraphs 263 to 268."

**Station 12 — the medical, on its own footing. Tabs M1 to M4.** "16 November 2023: poor sleep
with shift work, no psychological illness, mood good. 28 June 2024: 'stress at work,' 'upset by
people not following rules.' 1 July 2024: 'work stress,' 'they withhold pay at times, no
overtime, not processed, manipulate his roster so he works lates then earlies.' 24 October 2024:
major depressive disorder with anxiety state. The authors are here and will give their evidence."

**Then the Appellant closes the walk:** "Every document the Commission has just seen is either
admitted or the employer's own. The review officer who read this material found the rostering of
17 and 18 March to be unreasonable management action; the decision recording that finding is
admitted at paragraph 260. What remains for evidence is what these conditions were like from the
console, and their effect on me. I now give that evidence."

**Why the walk works:** no witness is needed for any station; every station ends on a paragraph
number; the only voices are the Appellant's and the employer's own documents; and the walk takes
half a day, not 2028. It is the case Dwyer described at the mention, built: "fewer documents
means simpler case, means easier for someone like me to understand it and not get it wrong."

## The other walk — the documents mention, if the 25th refuses or goes silent
> The fallback scene: a short mention before Dwyer IC on the outstanding documents only. His own
> offer at the August mention: *"if there is a document… that Ms Matheson says, well, yeah, we
> know it exists, but we don't think it's relevant, you can come back to me. And looking at a
> single document, I can tell you very quickly whether it should be disclosed or shouldn't be."*
> Probability this is ever needed: ~10–15%. Its value is mostly that they know it exists.

**The set-up.** One page in hand: the schedule from the 9 September letter. The opening:
"Commissioner, on 9 September I asked the Respondent, in accordance with the process you
indicated on 7 August, either to confirm the authenticity of thirteen documents whose contents
it has admitted, or to admit them under rule 49; and to obtain one document Metro South Health
has told the Commission is available. The Respondent sought until 25 September. [It has
maintained the disputes / It has not answered.] I ask for directions on the schedule, one
document at a time. It should take fifteen minutes."

**Tab 6, first, because it is the shortest.** "The email of 15 April 2024. Its contents are
admitted at paragraphs 49 and 163. The copy I served is the attachment to item 25 of the
Respondent's own amended List of Documents." — The bench, to the bar table: *"You disclosed this
document and you dispute its authenticity?"* There is no second question.

**Tab 20.** "Metro South Health's letter of 5 June 2026, addressed to you, Commissioner, on the
Commission's file. Contents admitted at paragraphs 263 to 268." — Nothing needs to be said after
that sentence at all.

**Tab 30.** "Quoted in the Respondent's own review decision, which is admitted at paragraph 70.
The copy sits in the Respondent's claim file."

**The MSH-authored balance (1, 5, 17–19, 21, 22–23, 30A).** "Each was sent to me by a Metro
South officer, on facts the Respondent has admitted. If the Respondent says any copy is not what
it appears to be, I ask that it say what is wrong with it." — This is Dwyer's 49:53 principle
pointed the other way: the dispute must be *responded to*; a bare column entry is not a case of
inauthenticity, and the bench will ask the bar table exactly that: *"What do you say is wrong
with the document?"* There is no answer on the record, because there has never been one.

**Tab 31, differently — production, not authenticity.** "Metro South told the Commission on
5 June that a spreadsheet of recorded MET calls is available for 17 and 18 March 2024 — that
statement is admitted at paragraph 268. On your indication, I asked the Respondent to obtain it.
I ask for a direction that the Respondent request and produce the March 2024 sheet of that
workbook within fourteen days, failing which I have leave to issue a notice of non-party
disclosure limited to that document." — This is the single-document look he offered: he can
tell "very quickly."

**The close.** "The costs of this step are addressed in my letter of 9 September, and I say
nothing further about them today." — One sentence, no argument; the reservation does its own
work by existing.

**What the bar table can say, and how each lands.** "We maintain the copies are from the
Appellant's records" → the bench asks what is wrong with them; silence. "We need more time" →
the bench asks what has been done since 10 September; the ledger answers. "We concede the
thirteen and will chase the register" → the mention ends in ten minutes and the only order is
the Tab 31 timetable. The third is what happens, because the first two are said in front of the
person who set the process.

**Why the scene matters without ever being played:** every path in it is visible to the
Respondent now. A refusal on the 25th buys this mention; the mention has one outcome; therefore
the refusal buys nothing but the record of it. That is the mechanism by which the letter
enforces itself.

**Addendum (Cory, 14 Sep) — the admissions are read aloud with every tab.** Identifying each
document at that mention *is* reading its admissions into the record: the admission is the
document's identification, so the reading is not colour, it is the necessary step. Tab 6 is not
"an email of 15 April"; it is "the email the Respondent admits was sent at 12:39 pm to the whole
department, copied to Ms Reese, adding the manager and the coordinator to after-hours on call,
effective from today — paragraphs 49 and 163." Tab 20 is the CE's sentences read out, one by
one, ending "and a spreadsheet of recorded MET calls is available — paragraph 268." Tab 30 is
"I am fulltime and work flex hours" in open Commission. **No objection exists to any of it** —
the words are the Respondent's own admissions, and the exercise is the one the bench directed.
So a refusal on the 25th does not keep the documents out; it **schedules a public reading of the
worst passages**, before the Member who set the process and chairs the conference, with MSH's
lawyer in the room — Dwyer's own August method (reading the pleading aloud) inverted, with the
appellant reading and everything read admitted. Maintaining the dispute converts a silent paper
concession into an oral preview of the case. That is the full price of refusal, and they can
price it themselves.
