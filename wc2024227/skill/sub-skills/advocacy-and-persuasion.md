# Sub-skill: Written advocacy & persuasion (how to build and word an argument)

Load with the parent `SKILL.md`. This sub-skill is the **craft layer** — how to frame, structure, and word an argument so a QIRC member is persuaded. It sits above `drafting.md` (which holds Cory's specific document mechanics) and applies to every WC-track document: the 64G, submissions, the SOFC, an outline of argument, even an email. Apply ALL parent discipline rules — especially **neutral/procedural tone** (rule 5) and **let Dwyer discover** (a discovered pattern is evidence; a declared one is discounted argument).

> The original 64G voice is the benchmark. Everything below is a generalisation of why that voice works, so it can be reproduced deliberately.

---

## 1. The QIRC framework for putting an argument forward

**Where argument lives.** Three vehicles, kept distinct:
- **Application (Form 4) + affidavit (Form 20)** — the affidavit carries *facts* (sworn); the Form 4 carries *argument + relief*. Never argue in the affidavit (it invites "that's submission, not evidence"). Never swear facts only in the Form 4.
- **Statement of Facts and Contentions (Form 9A)** — the pleading; contentions, not evidence, not submission.
- **Outline of argument / written submissions** — the persuasion document, filed when ordered.

**QIRC submission format (PD 2/2023, Industrial Court / adopted in practice):**
- Title and file number of the proceeding; state on whose behalf filed; **dated**; if settled by counsel, counsel's name at the foot.
- **Logically arranged, with appropriate headings.** Active-voice, short, consistent headings.
- Electronic copy must **not** contain hyperlinks.
- Filing under **PD 3/2021**: ≤30pp goes by email to the Registry in business hours; >30pp lodge in hard copy and serve every party (here: Respondent/OIR **and** MSH as affected non-party).

**The de novo frame.** The appeal is heard afresh (s 550 WCRA). The Review Officer's findings are persuasive, not binding (Form 24 ¶34 reserves relevance "for the Commission"). So argument is pitched at *what the Commission should now find*, not at impeaching the review — except to adopt the findings that help (the "unreasonable management action" finding, Form 24 ¶2).

**The self-represented outline structure** (LawRight, adapted for an interlocutory application like the 64G):
1. **What this application is about** — one short paragraph.
2. **Background** — a few facts, by reference to the affidavit (not re-sworn).
3. **Issues** — what the Commission must decide.
4. **Grounds** — one heading each; within each, *state the rule, then apply it to the facts* (separate sentences).
5. **Orders sought** — exact, in the draft order.
Keep it short; legal argument only, not evidence; make each point **once**.

## 2. The architecture of a persuasive submission

**Frame the essential issue first.** The single most important technique. A well-framed issue *is* the roadmap — it tells the member what to decide and, by its framing, half-decides it. In this matter the issue is framed not as "should MSH be put to expense" but as "should a self-executing server-side export, offered subject to nil-result discharge, be ordered, or should a bare assertion of non-existence be accepted un-sworn." The framing builds in the answer.

**Put context before detail (the overview).** Open with a short roadmap — what the application is about, who must do what, the position taken — before any detail. The reader absorbs detail far better once oriented. (In the 64G this is Part A + the opening grounds.)

**One controlling theory, repeated structurally not verbally.** Decide the one thing the document is about and make every section serve it. Here: *the records sit only in MSH's systems; the request is bounded and self-executing; produce-or-swear resolves it either way.* Don't restate the theory in words — let the structure carry it.

**Signpost and sequence.** Headings in active voice; each does one job; the sequence is itself an argument (concessions → necessity → relevance → proportionality → the answer to each objection). A member who reads only the headings should get the case.

**Make the point once.** Repetition does not add persuasion; it signals weakness. Say it once, with best facts, move on.

## 3. Bar-table persuasion technique (the craft)

These are the moves top advocates use (Guberman, *Point Made*; Australian bench guidance). Each is mapped to how this matter already uses it.

- **Lead with the conclusion, then prove it.** Topic sentence states the point; the rest earns it. Never make the reader assemble the conclusion themselves.
- **Short, flat sentences for the kill.** After a long, qualified build-up, land the decisive point in a short declarative: *"That record is held only by MSH. I cannot produce it."* Varying rhythm — long/complex then short/blunt — is what makes the blunt sentence hit.
- **Precision as aggression.** Exact figures, timestamps, PRNs, dates, quoted words do the accusing. *"Submitted four times… declined twice… approved on the same evidence."* No adjectives. The fact is the argument. (Parent rule 5.)
- **Concession as credibility (the "restraint" move).** Lead with what you do *not* press. Conceding the available certifies the necessity of what remains and makes it grantable. A barrister who gives ground on the weak points is believed on the strong ones. (Parent rule 6; the 64G withdraws Items 8, 17, 18 and offers nil-result discharge.)
- **Use the opponent's own words.** The most powerful sentence in a submission is the other side's admission quoted back. *MSH's own letter: complaints come "directly to the Line Manager… solely via email"* → the 20,006-staff burden objection collapses on its own terms. Quote, then turn.
- **Anchor every assertion to an admitted fact.** Tie each contention to a Form 24 admission or a produced document on its face. An argument resting on admitted facts is nearly un-answerable. (The Relevance Anchor Table / Annexure B does exactly this.)
- **Let the reader discover.** State the facts neutrally and in sequence; let the member draw the adverse inference. A discovered contradiction is the member's own finding and is owned; a declared one is your argument and is discounted. (Parent rule 5 — the cardinal persuasion rule for Dwyer, a close reader who ran the failed conference.)
- **Frame relief as the easy, proportionate path.** Give the decision-maker a single bounded step that resolves everything (produce-or-swear). Judges take the orderly exit. Make your order that exit.
- **Active voice, strong verbs, no nominalisations.** "MSH controls the records," not "the records are within the control of MSH." "The roster determines whether the page reaches the clinician," not "there is a determination."
- **Cut intensifiers and hedges.** "Clearly", "plainly", "it is submitted that", "very", "extremely" subtract force. The fact should carry the weight; if it needs an adverb, it isn't proven yet.

## 4. Sentence- and paragraph-level rules

- **One idea per sentence; one point per paragraph.** Open the paragraph with its point (topic sentence); the member can then skim topic sentences and still follow.
- **Subject–verb–object, early and close together.** Front the actor and the action; push qualifications to the end, not the middle.
- **Length variation is the rhythm of persuasion.** Build with a longer sentence; resolve with a short one. Three long sentences in a row lose the reader; three short ones sound hectoring.
- **Parallel structure for lists of reasons.** "It is bounded; it is self-executing; it requires no review." Parallelism reads as control and completeness.
- **Quote sparingly and exactly.** A short exact quotation (the manager's words, the letter's words) is evidence; a long block quotation is skipped. Never paraphrase what you can quote in six words.
- **Defined terms once, then used.** "the Form 29", "the Amended 9A", "MSH" — define on first use, never re-explain.
- **Numbers as numerals in argument** (250–300 calls; 7-hour break; 25-day delay) — numerals catch the eye and carry the precision.

## 5. This matter's established voice (reproduce it deliberately)

The 64G voice = four signatures. Every WC-track document should pass all four:
1. **Concession-first architecture** — what is *not* fought comes first; it licenses what is.
2. **Precision as aggression** — exact data accuses; adjectives are deleted.
3. **Keystone, not perimeter** — the 7-hour break (and now the safety-critical role) is the anchor the whole course of conduct is measured against, not the boundary of the claim.
4. **Neutral, procedural, proportionate** — the member discovers; the page never argues motive, never says "fraud", never narrates conspiracy (parent rules 1, 2, 5).

**The role-as-standard move (current):** describe the role precisely (AO3; life-safety hub; 250–300 calls) and let its criticality *raise the reasonableness bar* the Regulator must clear — without ever arguing "they were unreasonable." The description does the work; the conclusion is left for the member.

## 6. Persuasion calibrated to the reader (Dwyer)

- He is a close reader who ran the failed conference — he will find the contradictions himself if you lay the facts in order. Do not point; arrange.
- He must not feel managed. Restraint, concessions, and a proportionate single-step order read as reasonableness and earn trust on the contested points.
- He summoned Ruttan to attend 7 August — he wants the objection answered, not rubber-stamped. The submission's job is to give him the principled basis (produce-or-swear; the model-litigant expectation; the un-sworn-assertion problem) to require it.
- Keep reprisal/PID and the medical-causation fight off this paper entirely — both are real, both are reserved, neither persuades on disclosure and both risk the neutral posture.

## 7. Pre-send checklist (every WC-track argument)

- [ ] Issue framed so the framing builds in the answer?
- [ ] One-paragraph overview before any detail?
- [ ] Each heading active-voice, one job, sequence tells the story?
- [ ] Facts in the affidavit; argument in the Form 4/submission; not crossed?
- [ ] Every contention anchored to an admitted fact or produced document?
- [ ] Opponent's own words quoted and turned where available?
- [ ] Adjectives, intensifiers, hedges cut? Precision left to carry it?
- [ ] Conclusions discovered by the reader, not declared by the writer?
- [ ] Relief = one bounded, proportionate step (the easy exit)?
- [ ] Each point made once?
- [ ] Reprisal / fraud / conspiracy / medical-causation kept off the page?
- [ ] PD 2/2023 form (title, file no., dated, headings, no hyperlinks); PD 3/2021 lodgement?
