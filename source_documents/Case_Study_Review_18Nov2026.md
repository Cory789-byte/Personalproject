# Your Case Study, checked against the record

Case Study Complaint and Public-Safety Report, 14 July 2026. 18 November 2026. For you and your lawyers.

Only the two-page executive summary is in your Drive. The nine-part complaint and its schedule of records (D1 to D51) are not, so this review checks every statement in the summary. The same corrections apply wherever the full report repeats them.

## 1. The verdict

**What holds up.** The core of the Case Study is right and is on police's own records:

- You were named on the lease as Tenant 1. The order of 3 December 2024 had no exclusion and no contact condition (D5).
- The officers said on camera that you were allowed to be there and that nothing in the order had been breached by your being there.
- After the arrest an officer told the building manager you were not the leaseholder, and the building manager corrected it each time (camera, from 5:23 am).
- Support services were offered to her friend. No welfare question was put to you.
- The leasing manager's 2026 statement is contradicted by her own emails and texts of February and March 2025.
- The Information Commissioner closed review 318762 on the agreed basis that police would process a new request for the footage.
- It asks questions and alleges no intent to harm. Keep that.

**What must be corrected.** Twelve statements are wrong or go further than the record. Two of them are serious: the claim that your phone was used while you were in custody, and the claim that the dog is registered solely in your name. Police can disprove both with documents they already hold. Correct all twelve before the Commission letter goes on 20 November (section 5).

**What it changes when corrected.** The case gets stronger, not weaker. The summary says police arrested you before they confirmed the lease. The camera shows something better for you: the building manager confirmed the lease on speaker at about 2:28 am, and you were arrested at 2:35 am.

**Where it stands.** Police finalised your complaint on 10 September 2026 without contacting you. The next step is the Commission's review of how police dealt with it. The corrected Case Study goes with that letter.

## 3. What to stop doing

- **The paragraph headed "One officer at each step."** It names an officer and builds the complaint around him. From now on, describe what was said, when, and on which camera. The record carries the point without the name.
- **"Arrested before the inquiry was completed" (Head 1).** Replace it with the camera sequence in correction 2.
- **"Mobilised attendance" for her friend's report (Head 3).** Police took about six hours to arrive that night. Compare what each report led to, not how fast police came.
- **"My own dog."** Say "the dog I bought". Ownership is disputed, and the stronger point is that police were told so on the spot.

## 4. What police will raise that the summary leaves out

- **The December 2024 change-of-owner form.** Police hold it. Their court brief relies on it: her friend "produced a document detailing the defendant relinquishing ownership of the dog into the victim's name ... details and signature". The summary's "registered solely in my name" invites police to produce it. Correction 4 answers it first.
- **The new temporary order of 21 March 2025.** It explains the stop on 22 March. Correction 9 answers it.
- **What happened inside the unit on 23 February, and your messages before she left.** Leave these out of anything sent to police or the Commission while the criminal case runs. The criminal court decides them first.

## 5. Your work (D18)

The summary says a health service had confirmed your return to work for 7:00 am on 24 February 2025, and that you were in custody instead. That is accurate as written: the return was arranged for that morning and did not happen. Your work track records your actual return as 7 April 2025. Never write anywhere that you returned to work on 24 February 2025. Keep work material out of police papers, and police material out of work papers.
