# Getting the police records: plan and case check

17 November 2026. For discussion with your lawyers. The request wording in section 6 can be lodged once you have checked the facts in section 2.

## 1. The short answer

There are three ways in. Each opens different records.

1. **A right to information request to police.** Free for your own information. It reaches your address history, the job reports and their tabs, flags, letters police sent, and the job logs for visits to your mother's unit. It does not reach the log of who looked at or changed your file, and it does not reach the Ethical Standards files on your complaints.
2. **A privacy complaint, which can end in the tribunal.** This is the only route where a tribunal has made police open access records to inspection, and where apologies and compensation have been ordered over a misused address. It needs a real breach to start from.
3. **Production in a case you already have or are about to have.** Your criminal lawyers can get the bail and custody papers now. In the discrimination case, the tribunal can direct police to produce the Ethical Standards file on your 2026 complaint, because that file is what Claim B is about.

Start with route 1 for the address, and with your lawyers for the bail papers. Decide on route 2 when those come back.

**What the list left out.** These matter more than what it included.

Files on complaints that the corruption watchdog left with police are exempt. The exception for finished investigations "has consistently been found to apply to information 'about' the subject officer in the investigation, as distinguished from a complainant".
- **Access logs since Shelton.** X59 [2021] QICmr 55, I63 [2024] QICmr 34 and W64 [2025] QICmr 32. All refused. In no decision read has an applicant obtained an access log through the Commissioner since 2020.
- **Intelligence.** N64 and Queensland Police Service [2026] QICmr 50: twelve pages of "street check" reports were exempt as part of police methods for gathering intelligence. M39 and Queensland Police Service [2023] QICmr 66 concerned records of the police and health unit that assesses people who repeatedly contact public officials. Its result could not be confirmed.
- **Correcting a record.** Z18 and Queensland Police Service [2020] QICmr 8. Police lost the argument that the document was not a working record, but the correction was still refused. No police case was found where an applicant won a correction, and none about an address.

## 5. Your added point: the letters, the manager, the eviction, where you could live, the tenancy tribunal

**What it adds.** Three separate things, each with its own rule.

1. *Recording and using the address.* Before July 2025 police had to take reasonable steps to keep personal information accurate and to check it before using it. Since July 2025 the same duty applies to information police use or disclose. Police are not excused from these rules.
2. *Where the address went.* The typed application of 18 March 2025 is a court document. The other side in that proceeding receives it. Your mother's unit may have reached the people the dispute was with in that way. Her friend filed pages of the March 2025 police notice in the tenancy tribunal (stamp Q4626-25, 8 May 2025). Whether the address was on what they were given is something the request must establish.
3. *Harm to others.* The 2025 review found the same mechanism at system level: forms filled from the police database carrying addresses to people who should not have had them. ZIL is the tribunal decision on an address reaching the wrong hands.

**The limits.**

- Compensation in a privacy complaint is for the complainant's own loss. The eviction is your mother's loss. She would need her own complaint.
- Police will say the address came from what they were told at the unit on 24 February 2025.
- Nothing in your papers links the manager's notices to police. The dates are close. That is all so far.
- "Dictated where I lived" is the bail condition. That is a matter for the criminal court and your lawyers, and it is the bail papers that prove it.
- No tribunal file for the tenancy matter is in your papers beyond the stamped pages. As a party you can ask the registry for the file.
- Your account: you applied to the criminal court and it removed the address condition from your bail; you then applied to the tenancy tribunal; the old condition was then used to have your tribunal application struck out. The police statement of 18 March 2025 recites that old condition word for word, calls Unit 104 "the aggrieved's address", says you had not lived there, and gives your mother's unit as your address. Three documents prove the rest: the court order removing the condition, with its date; the strike-out application with every attachment, and the tribunal's decision and reasons, with dates; and Request 1 item 8, which asks who police gave your bail conditions to and when their records showed the change.
- In the tribunal, a party can apply for leave to appeal within 28 days of the decision, or apply to reopen on significant new evidence that was not reasonably available at the first hearing. The tribunal can extend time even after it has passed, but not where that would cause another party prejudice that costs or damages cannot remedy (QCAT Act ss 61, 138, 142, 143 and the definition of "reopening ground"). For a 2025 decision both routes are long out of time; whether a fresh application is open depends on why it was struck out. Your lawyers need the decision to answer that.

## 7. After the first release: correction and privacy complaint

**Correction. Apply now.** You do not need to wait for the release. You have had a copy of the typed statement of 18 March 2025, so you can apply to amend it now. The access Act allows this for any police document you have seen, however you got it. Since 1 July 2025 you can also ask police to correct any record that gives your mother's unit as your address. If police refuse, you can require them to attach your statement to the entry. No police correction case was found where the applicant won outright, so the attached statement is the floor.

**Privacy complaint.** Lodge one only if Request 1 shows the address was entered with no source, or was passed on. It goes to police first, who have 45 business days, then to the Information Commissioner, then to the tribunal if you ask the Commissioner to refer it.

- For what happened before July 2025 the old rules apply. The Commissioner may decline a complaint made more than 12 months after you first knew. You knew by 18 May 2025. Ask the Commissioner not to decline, on the ground that the source was only shown by the release.
- For anything police did with the address from July 2025, you have 12 months from learning of it to complain to police, and 20 business days from the Commissioner's notice to ask for referral to the tribunal.

At the tribunal, Eaves is the authority for asking that the access records be inspected in confidence. It was done through a barrister.
