# The case built from your Case Study

17 November 2026. For your lawyers. Built from the Executive Summary of 14 July 2026 and checked against the police camera transcript, your carrier's call log, the building manager's declaration and the court documents.

## 1. The answer

The case in your Case Study that is likely to succeed is the defence of the burglary charge. Five of your seven Heads are, in substance, that defence. They are decided in the Magistrates Court, not by a complaints body.

No claim against police reaches "likely" today. Three become real once that charge falls: a costs order that tests the investigation, and the two discrimination claims about the home and the dog.

Before anything is built on the Case Study, two of its statements have to be corrected. One of them is wrong by an hour, and the correction makes your best point stronger.

## 2. Two corrections first

**The "3:26 am call from my phone while I was in custody" did not happen in custody.**

- Your carrier's log is in Sydney daylight time, one hour ahead of Queensland. It lists the police-line call at 8:51 pm; your notes record her friend's text about the entry at 7:51 pm.
- The log's entry "24 Feb 03:26am, WiFi Calling, 4:00" to the building manager is therefore 2:26 to 2:30 am Queensland time.
- The officer's camera records that call. From 2:24 am the transcript has "You're on speaker". At 2:27:51 the officer greets the manager, and the call runs to about 2:30.
- You were arrested at 2:35:07.
- The log shows no call from your number after that until 26 February.

The manager's declaration gives 1:54 am and 3:26 am. Those are the log's Sydney-time figures. His two conversations with police match the two on camera: 2:28 am about the lease, and about 5:21 am about the access fobs.

Do not repeat the custody allegation. Correct it in one sentence in the next document you send to police or the Commission. The calls on 26 and 27 February are from your mother's suburb to your own contacts. If you replaced your SIM in those days, they are yours.

## 3. The case in one sentence

On 23 February 2025 you were a named tenant with a current lease, you had been told by building management the day before that you kept your right to enter and collect your belongings, you rang the police line for 37 minutes as you went in, and the officers who came said on camera that you were allowed to be there, five minutes before they arrested you.

## 4. The five things to prove, and what proves each

**1. The unit was your home in law.**

- Tenancy agreement naming you as Tenant 1, and the bond record in your name (D1, D2).
- The leasing manager confirmed on 22 February 2025 that the tenancy "had not been terminated", that no change of leaseholders "had been formally issued or processed", and that "Only an intent to vacate had been issued" (manager's declaration, para 26).
- The camera at 2:28 to 2:30 am (section 2).
- The call at about 5:21 am, where management says three times that the change to the lease had not been accepted (D37).
- The bond was refunded to you alone on 25 February 2025 (D3).

**2. No court order kept you out.**

- The order of 3 December 2024: good behaviour, and a condition about collecting property that expired on 1 January 2025 (D5).
- The camera: "technically he's allowed to be here"; "there's no contact conditions in there, so no one's done anything there"; "there's nothing on that order that's saying he can't be here".
- Her own statement describes the order as a good-behaviour condition (D9, para 3).

**3. You went openly, on advice, for your belongings, and asked police to come.**

- An entry notice was prepared on 19 February 2025, put under the door and emailed to her (manager's declaration, para 24).
- The manager told you after 22 February that you kept "the right to access the premises and retrieve his personal belongings" (para 27).
- Your carrier log: a 37-minute call to 131 444 starting 7:51 pm Queensland time.
- Her friend's statement has you on the phone to police during the encounter, and has her telling you to take the bed and the couch (D10, para 11).
- The woman in the unit was "not an authorised occupant under the lease agreement, nor an approved guest or visitor" (manager's declaration, para 25).

**4. The dog was a real dispute about ownership.**

- You paid for the dog in December 2023. A vet invoice of July 2024 and the council registration are in your name.
- On 4 February 2025 you wrote: "I also have every legal right to still contest ownership even if in your name."
- Police treated the same question as civil in December 2024, and said on 22 March 2025 "It would have been civil if you didn't get charged for stealing it" (D48).
- Against this: your messages of 16 to 19 December 2024 and your signature on the transfer. Your lawyers must see them.

**5. The officers recorded all of this before the arrest.**

- The timeline in section 2.
- The stated ground of arrest was breach of the order, 25 minutes after an officer said you were allowed to be there under it.
- The application was withdrawn and the order vacated the next day (D6). The issuing officer later wrote that it was struck out "upon my submission of no evidence from the aggrieved" (D7).
- The arresting officer's statement says "I activated my body worn camera on arrival". Only the watch-house interview has been produced from that camera for 24 February.

## 5. What the prosecution will say, and the reply

- **"He had moved out and signed a notice of intention to leave."** The manager told police on camera that the notice needed both signatures and that both remained on the lease.
- **"The manager himself says neither of them was living there."** True (para 25). The question is the right to enter, not where you slept. The same paragraph says the woman in the unit had no authority to be there.
- **"He signed the dog over and promised not to take the animals."** This is the hardest point. The answer is an honest belief in a legal right, shown by your message of 4 February and by police's own "civil" answer.
- **"He frightened a woman alone in the unit."** Her own statement has you on the phone to police and has her telling you to take the furniture. What else happened inside is for your lawyers, on the footage and her friend's videos.
- **"The order was in force."** It did not exclude you. The officers said so.
