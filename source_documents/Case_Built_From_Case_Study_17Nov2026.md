# Your case on its merits

17 November 2026, second version. For your lawyers. Every fact below comes from a police recording, a police document, the court file, your carrier's record or an independent person. Camera and interview lines are from machine transcripts. Speaker labels in them are unreliable, so each line is attributed here by what is said. Check every line against the footage.

## 1. The case

On 23 February 2025 you went to your own leased home for your belongings, with receipts, after management told you that you kept the right to go in, and you rang the police line as you went. The officers who came said you were allowed to be there, heard the building manager confirm the lease, and arrested you five minutes later for breaching an order they had said you were not breaching.

## 3. The merits, point by point

**1. It was your home.**

- The tenancy agreement names you as Tenant 1. The bond was in your name and was refunded to you alone (D1, D2, D3).
- On 22 February 2025 the leasing manager confirmed the tenancy "had not been terminated" and that "Only an intent to vacate had been issued" (manager's declaration, para 26).
- The manager said so to police on speaker at 2:28 am, and again three times after 5:23 am.
- The officers who interviewed you called her "the other leaseholder". There cannot be another leaseholder unless you are one.

**2. No order kept you out.**

- The order of 3 December 2024 required good behaviour and let her collect property by 1 January 2025 (D5).
- The officers said so at 2:10 am and 2:17 am.

**3. You went for your belongings, with receipts, and you called police.**

- Your interview: "first off I wanted to get my stuff, that's what I wanted to do ... I have receipts for everything" (00:07:05 to 00:07:19).
- "My intention is to beg for my belongings ... to get my belongings and to just escape from her grasp of control" (00:28:05 to 00:28:22).
- "I called the police. I thought you guys were coming." (00:47:00).
- An entry notice had been put under the door and emailed to her on 19 February (manager's declaration, para 24). Management told you after 22 February that you kept "the right to access the premises and retrieve his personal belongings" (para 27).
- Her friend's own statement has you on the phone to police, and has her telling you to take the bed and the couch (D10, para 11).

**4. You did not go for the dog, and who owns it was contested.**

- Your interview: "I didn't intentionally go there to grab my dog. And it can be contested because we're in a civil partnership" (00:09:12 to 00:09:14).
- You paid for the dog in December 2023. The vet invoice of July 2024 and the council registration are in your name.
- On 4 February 2025 you wrote: "I also have every legal right to still contest ownership even if in your name."
- Police called the same question civil in December 2024, and on 22 March 2025 said "It would have been civil if you didn't get charged for stealing it" (D48).

**5. You were arrested five minutes after the lease was confirmed.**

- The manager finished confirming the lease at about 2:30 am. The arrest was at 2:35:07 am.
- The ground given was breach of the order. At 2:10 am and 2:17 am the officers had said there was nothing in the order you had breached by being there.

**6. After the arrest, police told the manager the opposite of what he had told them.**

- 5:23 am: "cory's not the leaseholder". 5:26 am: "we've been given evidence tonight to say that corey is no longer on the lease". The officer had not spoken to the property manager.
- The manager corrected this each time. Police still required access to be restored for your former partner "and her friend", and said you would not be going back.
- Later that day the interviewing officers twice called her "the other leaseholder".

**7. Within a day the foundation was gone.**

- 25 February 2025: "APPLICATION IS WITHDRAWN, TPO VACATED." (D6). The issuing officer later wrote it was struck out "upon my submission of no evidence from the aggrieved" (D7).
- The stalking charge has been dropped. The prosecution has itself said the entry charge should be trespass.

**8. Police had your address, asked where you lived, then recorded your mother's home.**

- Police's December 2024 statement gave Unit 104 and your mobile number.
- Before the arrest an officer asked "Where are you living?" You: "This is my home. I've been in a car or on the street or in my mother's sofa." The officer: "So you're staying with your mum at the moment?" You: "I'm not staying with my mum. I have nowhere. I have to sleep in my car" (Harmer camera, D50, 00:17:49 to 00:18:15).
- In the interview: "I haven't moved into anywhere because that is my home" (00:21:15); "I'm homeless and I'm sleeping in a car" (00:29:43).
- Bail kept you from Unit 104. The police statement calls it "the aggrieved's address".
- The police court brief, made after the arrest, still gave "158 SCARBOROUGH ST Ste 104" and "currently residing at Unit 104", with your mobile number (D11).
- That evening your mother told an officer there was an "agreement between alexia and corey that it'd be two to three weeks while mum was here it has extended out to 10 weeks".
- 18 March 2025: the same officer's handwritten notice gave Unit 104 as your address for service. His typed statement for the same notice, under the same job number as the December statement, gave your mother's unit as your address, left your mobile number blank, and said you had not "resided at the address for some time prior to police attending".
- 21 March 2025: the court wrote to you at your mother's unit. 23 March: the first breach notice to your mother.

## 4. Points to meet

These are the four things the other side will press. Each needs your exact account, given to your lawyers.

1. **The fobs.** The manager told police he had been told the fobs were lost or stolen, and that you told him. In the interview an officer put that you gave "unilateral permission to cancel the fobs". Your answer there was that decisions about who stays in a jointly leased home are made together, and that you did not know the person.
2. **The notice dated 7 December 2024.** An officer showed you a notice "effective immediately to vacate" with your signature. Your answer: "I signed saying two to three weeks if she was scared." Management says it was never accepted.
3. **The dog papers.** Your messages of 16 to 19 December 2024 and your signature on the transfer.
4. **What happened inside the unit with her friend.** Her friend's videos and your own footage decide this.

## 5. What each body is asked to decide

- **The Magistrates Court:** whether the unit was another person's dwelling when you held the lease; whether anything was taken dishonestly when ownership was contested; and whether anything you did breached an order that had no exclusion condition.
- **The same court, on disclosure:** the arresting officer's statement says "I activated my body worn camera on arrival". Only the interview has been produced from that camera.
- **The same court, if the charges are dismissed:** costs. The court must weigh whether there was "a failure to take appropriate steps to investigate a matter coming to, or within, the knowledge" of those bringing the charge, and whether the investigation "was conducted in an appropriate way". Points 5 and 6 answer both.
- **The Human Rights Commission and the tribunal:** points 1, 2, 5 and 6 are the home claim. Point 4 is the dog claim.
- **The police information unit:** point 8 is the address.

## 6. Corrections to the Case Study

1. **The custody call.** Your carrier's log is in Sydney time. The "3:26 am" call is the 2:26 to 2:30 am speaker call, before your arrest. Withdraw that allegation. The manager's declaration carries the same one-hour shift.
2. **The lease confirmation.** It is on camera at 2:28 am. Use that, not the 1:54 am time.
3. **The interview and your address.** I said earlier the interview had nothing on your address. That was too narrow. It has no address question at 00:05:01, but at 00:21:15 and 00:29:43 you tell police you have no other home and are sleeping in a car. Cite those times.
4. **The interview time.** I first gave it as 5:48 am. The transcript places it at 5:48 pm. The custody record will settle it.
