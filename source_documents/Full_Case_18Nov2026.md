Cory Lea Shepherd and the Queensland Police Service. 18 November 2026. For your lawyers.

Every fact here comes from a police recording, a police or court document, an independent person or your own records, and the source is given. Where something rests on your account alone, it says so. ## 1. The case on one page

**What happened.**

- You and your former partner were joint tenants of Unit 104, 158 Scarborough Street, Southport. You were Tenant 1 and the bond was yours.
- On the night of 1 to 2 December 2024 people came into the unit and took your dog. You reported it. Police called the dog civil and left it with her. The next day police took a signed statement from her against you.
- On 23 February 2025 you went to the unit for your belongings, with receipts, after management told you that you kept the right to go in. You rang the police line as you went.
- The officers who came said on camera that you were allowed to be there, and heard the building manager confirm the lease on speaker. Five minutes later they arrested you for breaching an order they had said you were not breaching.
- Bail kept you out of your home. That morning police told the manager you were not the leaseholder. In your interview the same day the officer called her "the other leaseholder".
- The protection application was withdrawn the next day. Three weeks later police filed a new one that gave your mother's home as your address. The court wrote to you there. Your mother's household was served with breach notices and had to leave.
- On your account, the court later removed the address condition from your bail, and the old condition was then used to strike out your tenancy application in the tribunal.
- On 22 March 2025 police took your dog from you: "It would have been civil if you didn't get charged for stealing it". On your account it was then given to her.
- Your 2026 complaint was closed without contact after a one-day deadline.

**The five strands, and where each is decided.**

1. The charges fail on police's own records. The Magistrates Court decides this.
2. Police recorded your mother's home as your address after you told them you did not live there, and used it. A correction application decides this, then a privacy complaint if the records support one.
3. An out-of-date bail condition was used against you in the tribunal. The tribunal file and the police records decide this.
4. Police treated the same dispute differently depending on who complained. The Human Rights Commission decides this.
5. Your complaint was closed without contact. This goes to the Crime and Corruption Commission and, for the footage, the Information Commissioner.

**What you are asking for.**

- The charges dismissed, and costs.
- Your mother's home removed as your address.
- The camera footage and your records.
- A written acknowledgment that you were named on the lease and that the order did not exclude you.
- Compensation through the Human Rights Commission.
- Your mother's household left alone.

## 4. Strand 1: the charges

**The merits.**

1. **It was your home.** The lease names you as Tenant 1 and the bond was in your name (D1, D2). On 22 February the leasing manager confirmed the tenancy had not been terminated (manager's declaration, para 26). The manager said so to police on speaker at 2:28 am and again after 5:23 am. The bond was refunded to you alone on 25 February (D3). In your interview the officer called her "the other leaseholder". There cannot be another leaseholder unless you are one.
2. **No order kept you out.** The order of 3 December 2024 required good behaviour and let her collect property by 1 January 2025 (D5). At 2:10 am and 2:17 am the officers said so on camera.
3. **You went for your belongings, openly, and called police.** In your interview: "first off I wanted to get my stuff ... I have receipts for everything"; "My intention is to beg for my belongings ... to get my belongings and to just escape from her grasp of control"; "I called the police. I thought you guys were coming." An entry notice had gone to her on 19 February. Her friend's own statement has you on the phone to police, and has her telling you to take the bed and the couch (D10, para 11).
4. **You did not go for the dog, and who owned it was disputed.** In your interview: "I didn't intentionally go there to grab my dog. And it can be contested because we're in a civil partnership". You paid for the dog in December 2023, and the vet invoice and council registration are in your name. On 4 February 2025 you wrote: "I also have every legal right to still contest ownership even if in your name." Police called the question civil in December 2024 (your filed account), and on 22 March 2025 an officer said "It would have been civil if you didn't get charged for stealing it".
5. **You were arrested five minutes after the lease was confirmed.** The manager finished at about 2:30 am. The arrest was at 2:35 am, for breach of an order the officers had said you were not breaching.
6. **After the arrest police told the manager the opposite.** At 5:23 am an officer said "cory's not the leaseholder" and "we've been given evidence tonight to say that corey is no longer on the lease". The officer had not spoken to the property manager. The manager corrected this each time. The officer who said it was in the room when your interview called her "the other leaseholder".
7. **The foundation went within a day.** The application was withdrawn and the order vacated on 25 February (D6). The issuing officer later wrote that it was struck out "upon my submission of no evidence from the aggrieved" (D7). The stalking charge has been discontinued, and the prosecution has proposed trespass for the entry charge.

**Points to meet.** These are the other side's best points. Each needs your exact account, given to your lawyers.

- **The fobs.** The manager told police he had been told the fobs were lost or stolen. In the interview the officer put that you gave "unilateral permission to cancel the fobs", and used the joint tenancy to say that trying to have her friend removed was "an exercise in control over your partner".
- **The notice of 7 December 2024,** "effective immediately to vacate". Your answer in the interview: "I signed saying two to three weeks if she was scared." Management says it was never accepted and needed both signatures.
- **The manager's paragraph 25:** "neither Cory Shepherd nor Alexia Negro were residing in the apartment at that time". The question is your right to enter, not where you slept. The same paragraph says her friend was "not an authorised occupant under the lease agreement, nor an approved guest or visitor".
- **The dog papers.** Your messages of 16 to 19 December 2024, and your signature on the transfer.
- **What happened inside the unit with her friend.** Her videos and your own footage decide this.

**The missing footage.** The arresting officer's statement says "I activated my body worn camera on arrival". From that camera only the interview has been produced for 24 February. The footage police agreed to process after review 318762 has not been produced in full. Your lawyers can ask the court for a direction.

**Costs.** If the charges are dismissed in the Magistrates Court, the court may order police to pay your costs only if it is "proper". In deciding that, it must weigh whether there was "a failure to take appropriate steps to investigate a matter coming to, or within, the knowledge" of those bringing the charge, and whether the investigation "was conducted in an appropriate way" (Justices Act 1886 s 158A(2)). Points 5 and 6 answer both.

## 5. Strand 2: the address

**The chain in police's own documents.**

1. December 2024: their statement gives Unit 104 and your mobile number.
2. Before the arrest an officer asks where you are living and whether you are staying with your mum. You say Unit 104 is your home and that you are not staying with your mum and have nowhere (D50, 00:17:49 to 00:18:15). You say the same in the interview (00:21:15 and 00:29:43).
3. Bail keeps you from Unit 104. Their statement calls it "the aggrieved's address".
4. After the arrest their court brief still gives Unit 104, "currently residing at Unit 104", with your mobile number (D11).
5. 18 March 2025: the same officer who rang the manager writes Unit 104 by hand as your address for service. His typed statement for the same notice, under the same job number as December, gives your mother's unit as your address and leaves your mobile number blank. It says you had not "resided at the address for some time prior to police attending".
6. 21 March 2025: the court writes to you at your mother's unit. 23 March: the first breach notice to your mother.

**What it shows.** Police held your address, asked you where you lived and were told. Their own condition kept you out of your home. They then recorded your mother's home as yours, and described as "not having resided" an absence your mother had described to them as a temporary arrangement, and which from 24 February their own bail condition enforced.

**What police will say.** Your mother told them that evening that you had been staying with her. That does not explain recording her home as your address after you told them you were not staying there, or the same officer giving Unit 104 for service on the same day.

**The duty.** Police must take reasonable steps to keep personal information accurate, complete, up to date and not misleading, and to check it before they use it. Before 1 July 2025 this was in privacy principles 7 and 8. Since then it is in principles 10.2 and 13. Police were not excused from any of these, either before July 2025 or since.

**The steps.**

- Apply now to correct the 18 March statement and any record that gives your mother's unit as your address. The text is in Annex A.
- Ask your lawyers to give their office as your address for service on every file.
- Take your mother's address off your own records: licence, council registration, anything you file.
- Lodge Request 1 (Annex C). It asks when the entry was made, by whom and from what.
- Lodge a privacy complaint only if the release shows the entry had no source, or that the address was given to someone else.

**Your mother's household.** The eviction is her loss, so anything that compensates it must be in her name. She can:

- ask the complex manager for the notices of 23 March and 12 April 2025 and what prompted them;
- lodge her own free request to police for any police contact with her manager and any record giving her unit as your address;
- write to police asking that her home not be used to contact you.

Police must give proper consideration to the human rights of her household when they act, including the protection of families and a child's right to the protection that is in the child's best interests (Human Rights Act 2019 ss 26 and 58). A complaint about that is hers, and the baby's through a parent. It goes to police first. Events of March and April 2025 are more than one year old, which the Commission may refuse on but can accept. Any use of her address by police in the last year is inside the limit.

## 6. Strand 3: bail and the tribunal

**Your account.** You applied to the criminal court and it removed the address condition from your bail. You then applied to the tenancy tribunal, and by 17 April 2025 you told police the case was pending. The old condition was then used to have your application struck out.

**The document that probably carried it.** The police statement of 18 March 2025 recites the old condition word for word, calls Unit 104 "the aggrieved's address", says you had not lived there, and gives your mother's unit as your address. That is the material a party would attach to argue you had no standing in a tenancy dispute.

**The date that matters most.** If the court removed the condition before 18 March 2025, the police statement recited a condition that no longer existed on the day it was written. If the court removed it later, the statement was out of date when it was used in the tribunal. The court file gives the date.

**The three documents.**

1. The court order removing the condition, with its date (Annex D).
2. The tribunal file: the strike-out application and every attachment, the decision and reasons, and the dates (Annex E).
3. From police: who they gave your bail conditions to and when, and when their records showed the change (Request 1, item 8).

**What it can lead to.**

- *In the tribunal,* a party can apply for leave to appeal within 28 days of a decision, or apply to reopen on significant new evidence that was not reasonably available at the first hearing. The tribunal can extend time even after it has passed, but not where that would cause another party prejudice that costs or damages cannot remedy (QCAT Act ss 61, 138, 142 and 143). For a 2025 decision both routes are long out of time. Whether a fresh application is open depends on why it was struck out. Your lawyers need the decision.
- *Against police,* if they gave the old condition out after the court changed it, or their records still showed it, that is the accuracy duty in Strand 2.

## 7. Strand 4: equal treatment and human rights

You must prove each claim: a transitional rule keeps the old burden of proof for your complaint (Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 s 285AA). For Claim B, police must prove the one-day requirement was reasonable.

**Claim A: the home.** On 24 February 2025 police put the man named on the lease out of his home, and restored access for a woman who was named on neither the lease nor the order. They gave the building manager a reason, "cory's not the leaseholder", that the manager had told them was wrong, and that their own interviewing officer contradicted later that day. A stated reason shown to be untrue lets the tribunal look for the real one. The weak joint is the arrest: police will say a woman arrested on the same allegations would have been removed too. That stands or falls with the criminal case.

**Claim C: the dog.** In December 2024 she had the dog and you complained, with papers in hand. Police said it was civil, left the dog with her and took no statement from you, and took a statement from her the next day. In March 2025 you had the dog and she complained. Police said "regardless of what paperwork he has, we've got to seize the dog and then he's got to go through the courts to get the dog back", and took it. On your account of the August 2025 hearing, they then gave it to her without a court order. Same dispute, same dog, same police service: the man was the one sent to court both times. The weak joints are your December messages and signature, and the charge.

**Claim B: the 2026 complaint handling.** The police notice of 1 December 2024 records that you have "a mental illness and requires medication". On 18 June 2026 Ethical Standards required particulars by the next day. On 10 September 2026 it finalised your complaint without contacting you. The outcome names no right and does not mention your condition. This works only if your condition is why the deadline could not be met. If you answered in time, run this part under the Human Rights Act alone.

**Route.** One complaint to the Queensland Human Rights Commission under the Anti-Discrimination Act and the Human Rights Act. The draft is in the discrimination plan, section 11.

**Time.** Claim B is in time until 18 June 2027. Claims A and C are out of time and need good cause: a criminal case on the same events is still running; your health; you took the same events to the Commission earlier (EDR19098); and nothing is lost to police because the footage exists and you asked for it to be kept.

**When.** After 6 January 2027, when the Human Rights Act complaint about the outcome letter becomes available, and after your lawyers have read it.

**What to ask for.** A written acknowledgment that you were named on the lease, that the order did not exclude you, and that you reported the taking of the dog on 1 December 2024; correction of the police record; reassessment of your complaint by an officer outside Ethical Standards and Southport, with reasonable time to respond; an apology; compensation.
