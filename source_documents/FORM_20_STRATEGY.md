# The Form 20 — full picture and strategy

**13 August 2026**

The single most valuable document not yet in hand. This works through what it is, why it cannot hurt, every route to obtaining it, what each possible answer means, and the order to do things in.

---

## 1. The central insight — it is a free roll

**Cory was never given a copy of the Form 20.** That one fact means the document cannot damage him, whatever it turns out to say.

- If it ended **Ms Negro's** interest — he was the continuing tenant, and his entry on 23 February 2025 was entry to premises he alone held. The prosecution's first reason does not just fail, it reverses.
- If it somehow purported to end **his** interest — he was not told, so his belief that he remained a tenant was honestly held. Mistake of fact and honest claim of right both survive intact, and the failure to tell him becomes its own problem for the agency.

There is no version of this document that leaves him worse off than he is now. **Every other document in the case carries some risk. This one carries none.** That is why it should be pursued hard.

## 2. What a Form 20 is

**RTA Form 20 — "Notice ending tenancy interest".** It comes from the domestic and family violence provisions inserted into the *Residential Tenancies and Rooming Accommodation Act 2008*.

The scheme, as I understand it:

- A tenant experiencing domestic or family violence may end **their own** interest in a tenancy by giving the lessor or agent this notice with prescribed supporting evidence.
- The notice takes effect a short minimum period after it is given.
- Where there are co-tenants, **the tenancy continues for the remaining tenants.** The person leaving is released; the tenancy is not terminated.
- The leaving tenant's liability for rent ends on the effective day.
- There are **confidentiality restrictions** on the lessor or agent disclosing the notice and the supporting evidence, with penalties.
- There is a mechanism dealing with the bond consequences.
- A tenant cannot use this notice to remove **another** tenant. Removing someone else requires a tribunal order.

### 2.1 — Three things that must be verified before any of this is asserted

1. **That Form 20 is the notice ending tenancy interest**, and not some other RTA form.
2. **That the notice ends the giver's own interest**, and that the tenancy continues for the remaining tenant.
3. **What the lessor or agent is required to do afterwards** — in particular, whether they must notify the remaining tenant that a co-tenant's interest has ended, and what must be done with the bond.

Point 3 is not a detail. It is a separate line of attack in its own right (§7).

## 6. Every route to obtaining it, ranked

### Route 1 — The prosecution's own disclosure obligation ★ start here, costs nothing

The prosecution has taken a statement from the leasing manager **about this tenancy**. They cannot have done that without asking what documents exist.

So either:

- **they hold the Form 20 and have not disclosed it** — a disclosure breach on the central issue of Charge 1; or
- **they do not hold it** — meaning their witness gave them an account of how the tenancy ended without the document that ended it, which is worth establishing.

Either answer is useful, and asking costs nothing. Written request to the prosecutor, through Sibley, for all tenancy documents obtained from the agency, together with the date of the statement and any correspondence with the agency.

### Route 2 — Notice to produce / summons to the agency ★ the main route

Through Sibley. Compulsion is the correct tool because:

- the confidentiality restriction is likely to prevent voluntary disclosure, but production **required by law** is a different thing;
- it fixes provenance for evidentiary purposes;
- it removes any question of a defendant approaching a witness.

Expect an objection on confidentiality. That is normal and manageable — the court can inspect the document itself and rule on what is produced. Ask for the ruling; do not withdraw the request.

Address it to the **agency**, and consider a parallel notice to the **lessor**, who holds the file too.

### Route 3 — Cory's own RTA bond history ★ do it today, free, involves nobody

He is the bond contributor. He can request his own bond record. It shows:

- every contributor named on the bond, and any change;
- whether any refund was ever claimed or paid, to whom, and when;
- whether a **new bond** was lodged for a **new tenancy**, and on what date.

This will not produce the Form 20, but it independently establishes the tenancy position. **A bond sitting untouched in Cory's name through 2025, with no Form 4, is very hard to reconcile with "he was removed" in December 2024.**

### Route 4 — The tenant letter about his own records

The letter at §9 of `TENANCY_DOCUMENTS_STRATEGY_2026-08-13.md`, over his own name. It asks what document the agency recorded as ending **his own** interest. That is his own status, he is entitled to ask, and it requires no reference to Ms Negro or to any Form 20.

Every possible answer is useful, and it does not ask for anyone else's document.

### Route 5 — Claim the bond

He is the sole bond contributor and the tenancy has ended. Claiming his own bond is an ordinary thing for a former tenant to do, and it forces the question of who the tenants were and when their interests ended.

If it is paid — he was the tenant. If it is disputed — the dispute process produces exactly the records in issue.

*Check first whether this affects any other proceeding, and mention it to Sibley before lodging.*

### Route 6 — The court file containing her material

She filed the later lease. Anything else she filed about the tenancy is on that file and obtainable without going near the agent. Worth reviewing what else is in it.

### Route 7 — RTA dispute resolution (RTA:0055411)

Already open. A tenancy-status dispute has its own disclosure mechanics. Slower, and carries a risk of inconsistent findings across forums — **for Sibley to weigh, not to launch unilaterally.**

### Not a route

Contacting Ms Negro or her representatives. No.

## 7. The collateral point — what was the agency required to tell him?

Independent of what the Form 20 says.

If a co-tenant's interest ends, the remaining tenant's position changes materially — sole liability for rent, and a changed bond position. It is very likely the agency was required to tell him something. **He says he was told nothing and never received a copy.**

That produces three things:

1. **His honest belief is reinforced.** He cannot have been reckless about a tenancy change nobody told him about. This goes directly to the mental element in both surviving charges.
2. **The prosecution's second reason is undermined.** They say he *"clarified if he was on the lease. He was not."* If the agency knew a Form 20 had been given and accepted, and told him he was off the lease — while never showing him the document, never giving him the notice, and never dealing with his bond — then what he was told was, at best, incomplete. Const. Davies's own words: *"that's really tricked him."*
3. **The agency has a problem of its own**, which is relevant to how its later statement to police should be read.

Ask the question directly in the notice to produce: what notification was given to the remaining tenant, when, and in what form.

## 8. What to do once it is in hand

1. **Verify it against the Act** before asserting anything about its effect.
2. **Serve it on the prosecution** and invite them to review Charge 1 — expressly by reference to what they just did on Charge 2. The review that removed one charge should be applied to another where the document contradicts the stated reason for maintaining it.
3. **Put it beside the leasing manager's statement.** If the statement says he was removed by a Form 13 while a Form 20 from the other tenant sits on the file, the statement needs explaining.
4. **Use it in the human rights matter.** Police made the operative decision without ever obtaining the tenancy file, while the property manager was telling them on the phone at 1:54 a.m. that the Form 13 changed nothing.
5. **Keep the criminal and civil uses separate.** Criminal: it supports honest belief and defeats the stealing element's premise. Civil and tenancy: it goes to the bond and to the agency's conduct.

## 9. Risks, and how each is handled

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| Records destroyed or purged before production | **Preservation letter now.** This is the urgent item — agencies change systems and staff leave. Do not wait for the notice to produce. |
| Tipping off the prosecution | Take the objective records first — bond history, court file. Compel rather than request. Nothing is lost by them knowing the tenancy is in issue; they raised it themselves. |
| Confidentiality objection stalls production | Expected. Ask the court to inspect and rule. Do not withdraw. |
| The document turns out to be scenario E | Still survivable — he was never notified, so honest belief holds (§5). |
| Contact with a witness being characterised badly | Anything touching the proceeding goes through Sibley. Personal correspondence stays confined to his own tenancy records. |
| Overstating the effect before verification | Nothing about what a Form 20 does is asserted anywhere until it is checked against the Act and the document is in hand. |
