# PART I — THE HUMAN RIGHTS CASE

## I.1 — The complaint framework

**Statutory basis:** Human Rights Act 2019 (Qld) ss 58 and 65. As a public entity, QPS must (a) act compatibly with human rights (substantive limb, s 58(1)(a)) and (b) give proper consideration to relevant rights when making a decision (procedural limb, s 58(1)(b)).

**Companion frameworks (engaged in the alternative or in parallel):**
- Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 (Qld) s 159 — victimisation, where adverse treatment is causally connected to a protected complaint pathway.
- Public Interest Disclosure Act 2010 (Qld) s 40 — reprisal, where adverse agency action follows a protected disclosure.
- Tort of misfeasance in public office — *Mengel v NT* (1995) 185 CLR 307; *Three Rivers v Bank of England* [2003] 2 AC 1.
- Tort of conversion — for the deprivation of Romeo (PPRA s 691 return-of-thing duty engaged).
- Trespass to land — for the 23–24 February lockout of the lessee from his own leased premises.

## I.5 — The procedural-limb breach (HRA s 58(1)(b)) — operational policy admitted on tape

The procedural-limb breach is not inferred — it is admitted on QPS's own tape by the arresting officer on the night before the foundational order was struck out:

> **Const. Mitchell DAVIES (Reg 39627), Davies-with-Cory's-mum BWC [12:57.76 → 13:14.34], in the presence of Const. Levi BICKERY:**
> "And we have to defer to the judgment of our other police that have made that call that Alexia was in need of protection. And we don't reinvestigate that and we don't say, oh, no, we've spoken to his mum and his family and we don't think that's the case. **That's not how it works.**"
