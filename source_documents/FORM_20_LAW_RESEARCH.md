# Form 20 — the law, verified against source

**13 August 2026.** Researched against the RTA and the Act rather than recollection. Sources at the foot.

The earlier strategy note worked from an understanding of how these provisions operate. That understanding is now confirmed on the central point, with two corrections and one discovery that materially improves the position.

---

## 1. What is confirmed

**Form 20 is the "Notice ending tenancy interest (domestic and family violence)".** It sits in the domestic and family violence provisions of the *Residential Tenancies and Rooming Accommodation Act 2008*, at ss 308B and following.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who gives it | A **tenant** who believes they can no longer safely occupy the premises because of domestic and family violence |
| **Whose interest it ends** | **The interest of the tenant who gives it.** It cannot be used to end anyone else's |
| Notice period | **7 days** |
| Supporting evidence | Required — from the options in Part B of the form. In December 2024 that meant a domestic and family violence report or equivalent. *(A police protection direction only became available as supporting evidence from 1 January 2026, so it cannot have been used in December 2024.)* |
| Effect on the tenancy | **The agreement continues** for the remaining tenants, on the same terms, with the parties being the lessor and the remaining tenants — s 308E |
| Costs | The vacating tenant is not liable for costs of ending the agreement, for goods left behind, or for reletting |

**So the central proposition is right: a Form 20 given by Ms Negro ended her own interest, not Cory's, and the tenancy continued for him.**

---

## 2. Correction 1 — the interest ends only when *both* conditions are met

This is the material correction, and it must be got right.

The RTA states the position plainly:

> The vacating tenant's interest in the tenancy agreement ends **once the 7 day notice period has expired AND the tenant has vacated the premises.**

Two conditions. Notice **and** departure. Giving the form alone does not end anything.

That means the effective date of a notice given on 5 December 2024 is not automatically 12 December. It is the later of the expiry of the seven days and the day she actually vacated.

**This does not weaken the position. It splits it into two branches, and both work.**

### Branch 1 — she gave the notice and vacated

Her interest ended. From that date **Cory was the sole remaining tenant**, and the agreement continued for him on the same terms under s 308E. Her later presence, and Ms Phillips's, was as a guest.

### Branch 2 — she gave the notice but did not vacate

Her interest never ended, so the **joint tenancy continued** and Cory remained a co-tenant on 23 February 2025. Entry to premises he co-held was lawful.

There is a second consequence in this branch. She would have given a formal statutory notice, supported by prescribed domestic violence evidence, declaring that she could no longer safely occupy the premises — and then continued to occupy them, kept her mother there for around three months on Ms Phillips's account, installed a house-sitter in February, and travelled to Perth from the address. That bears directly on the genuineness of the fear on which the whole matter was built, and it sits beside the strike-out of the protection order on the issuing officer's own submission of **no evidence from the aggrieved**.

**Either way Cory was a tenant on 23 February 2025, and either way the prosecution's proposition that a Form 13 removed him is wrong** — because in branch 1 she left and he stayed, and in branch 2 nobody left at all.

**What this makes urgent:** establishing whether and when she vacated. Rent ledger, key and fob records, the new lease of 10 April 2025, her own statements about living there, and Ms Phillips's account of house-sitting for her.

---

## 3. Correction 2 — the confidentiality restriction is narrower than assumed

The restriction protects the **vacating tenant's contact details and her domestic violence circumstances**. It does not prevent — indeed it requires — the remaining tenant being told that the interest has ended.

That distinction changes the approach entirely.

| Protected | Must be disclosed to the remaining tenant |
|---|---|
| Her contact details | **That her interest in the agreement has ended** |
| The domestic violence circumstances | **That the agreement continues for him on the same terms** |
| The supporting evidence in Part B | Any bond top-up required, and the amount |

So Cory is not asking for a protected document when he asks whether a co-tenant's interest ended and when. **He is asking for information the Act obliged the agency to give him.**

---

## 4. The discovery — the agency owed Cory a written notice, and never gave it

**Section 308E(3): where a vacating tenant's interest ends and there are remaining tenants, the lessor must give each remaining tenant a written *continuing interest notice*.**

It must state:

- **(a)** that the vacating tenant's interest in the agreement **has ended**;
- **(b)** that the agreement **continues for the remaining tenant** and any others, **on the same terms**; and
- **(c)** if a rental bond top-up is required, that it is required and the amount payable.

And the timing is fixed:

> **No earlier than 7 days, and no later than 14 days, after the vacating tenant's interest ends.**

Cory says he was given nothing.

### 4.1 — Why this matters more than anything else in the tenancy analysis

If Ms Negro's interest ended, then the law required the agency to hand Cory a written notice telling him, in terms, that **he was the continuing tenant and the agreement continued for him on the same terms.**

That is the exact proposition the prosecution now denies. **The document that would have proved it was one the agency was obliged to give him and did not.**

The consequences run in every direction:

1. **His state of mind is closed off.** He cannot have been reckless about a change he was never told of, by a notice the Act required him to be given. He asked the RTA. He asked management. He held the keys, the fob and the bond. Nothing came.
2. **The prosecution's second reason collapses.** They say he *"clarified if he was on the lease. He was not."* If a continuing interest notice was owed to him and never given, then whatever he was told was not what the Act required him to be told.
3. **It is a compliance failure by the agency**, which bears on how its later statement to police should be weighed.
4. **It is a specific, identified, dated document** — or a specific, identified, dated omission. Both are provable.

### 4.2 — Its absence proves as much as its content

If the agency produces a continuing interest notice, it says on its face that Cory was the continuing tenant.

If the agency cannot produce one, then either the interest never ended — branch 2 — or it ended and Cory was never told, which is branch 1 plus a statutory breach.

**There is no third answer.**

---

## 5. The bond — a further check

Section 308F deals with **top-ups of rental bond** where a tenant's interest ends. Any top-up required must be stated in the continuing interest notice, and is payable no earlier than one month after that notice is given to all remaining tenants.

Cory's position is that the bond of $2,160 remained lodged in his name, untouched, with no refund processed.

So the questions for the RTA bond record are:

- was any change of bond contributors ever processed;
- was any top-up ever sought from him;
- was any refund ever claimed by her — the departing tenant applies using **Form 4a**;
- and was a **new bond** lodged for the tenancy commencing 10 April 2025.

A bond that never moved is consistent with an agency that never processed the departure at all.

---

## 6. What this changes in practice

### 6.1 — Cory can now ask directly, in his own right

The letter over his own name no longer needs to be indirect. He is a remaining tenant asking about a notice the Act obliged the agency to give **him**. Add to the requests at §9 of the tenancy strategy note:

> Whether any tenant gave a notice ending tenancy interest in respect of this tenancy; if so, the date the vacating tenant's interest ended; whether a continuing interest notice under s 308E was given to me as a remaining tenant, and if so a copy of it and the date it was given; and whether any rental bond top-up was required of me.

That asks for nothing protected. It asks about **his own** tenancy and about a notice he was entitled to receive.

### 6.2 — The schedule of documents gains three items

- The continuing interest notice given to any remaining tenant, and the date given.
- The lessor's response under s 308C, including whether any application was made to have the notice set aside.
- Records of when the vacating tenant actually vacated — rent ledger, key and fob returns, inspections.

### 6.3 — What to establish, in order

1. **Whether a Form 20 was given on 5 December 2024**, and accepted.
2. **Whether and when she vacated** — this decides branch 1 or branch 2.
3. **Whether a continuing interest notice was ever given to Cory** — almost certainly not.
4. **What the bond record shows.**

## 7. Still to verify

- The precise text of ss 308B–308F as in force in **December 2024**, since these provisions have been amended.
- What Part B of the Form 20 required as supporting evidence at that date, and what was actually supplied.
- Whether the lessor applied, or could still apply, to have the notice set aside under s 308C — and whether any such application was made.
- Whether the confidentiality provision carries a penalty, and its exact scope.
- The RTA form number for the continuing interest notice.

**None of this should be asserted in a filing until the sections as in force in December 2024 have been read.** The framework is now confirmed; the pinpoints are not.

---

## 8. The position in one paragraph

A Form 20 ends the interest of the tenant who gives it, and only that tenant. If Ms Negro gave one on 5 December 2024 and then vacated, Cory became the sole continuing tenant and the agency was required by s 308E to give him a written notice saying exactly that, between 7 and 14 days later. He was given nothing. If she did not vacate, her interest never ended and the joint tenancy continued, so he was a co-tenant on 23 February 2025 anyway — while having formally declared she could not safely occupy premises she went on occupying. On both branches he was a tenant that night, and on both branches the notice he was legally entitled to receive is the document that would have told him so.
