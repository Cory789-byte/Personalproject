# Conciliation brief

Cory Lea Shepherd against the State of Queensland (Queensland Police Service). For you and your lawyers. Not lodged, and not given to police or the conciliator.

## 1. The aim

Leave the conference with three things in writing: an acknowledgment of the facts police's own records show, an independent reassessment of your 2026 complaint with reasonable time to respond, and a correction of your police record. Money comes after those.

The 2026 claim is your strongest and it is within time. Start there. Use the 2024 and 2025 events as the reason the 2026 complaint mattered.

## 2. Rules for the day

- **Say nothing about what happened inside the unit on 23 February 2025.** Police are your prosecutor. If asked, say: "That is before the court. This complaint is about what police did."
- **Do not argue about 27 November 2024.** You deny her account. The complaint is about how police treated your own report.
- **What is said in the conference stays there.** It cannot be included in the Commission's referral to the tribunal (Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 s 164AA), and for the human rights part it is not admissible unless both sides agree (Human Rights Act 2019 s 86).
- **Ask who is attending for police and whether they can settle.** A State agency's representative "needs to be authorised to settle the complaint" (Commission fact sheet).
- **Tell the conciliator your needs in the pre-conference call:** breaks, a support person, your lawyer, and time to read anything put to you.
- **Sign nothing on the day** unless your lawyer has read it.

## 3. Opening statement

About two minutes. Read it if that is easier.

> I am here about three things police's own records show.
>
> First, in June 2026 police gave me one business day to particularise a complaint. Police had recorded in December 2024 that I have a mental illness. I could not meet that deadline. Three months later the complaint was closed without anyone contacting me.
>
> Second, that complaint was about one dispute over one dog that police decided twice, with opposite answers. When I reported it taken from my home, it was a civil matter and no statement was taken. When my former partner reported it, it was stolen property, and it was seized from me "regardless of what paperwork he has" and given to her without the court application police's receipt said would be made.
>
> Third, on 24 February 2025 officers said on camera that I was allowed to be in my home, the building manager confirmed I was on the lease, and police then told him I was not the leaseholder.
>
> I am not asking police to decide the criminal case here. I am asking for an acknowledgment of those facts, a fresh look at my complaint by someone independent, with fair time to respond, and my record corrected.

## 4. Three questions to put to police

Ask each one plainly and wait for the answer. Each answer helps you whichever way it goes.

1. **"Is one business day the time Ethical Standards gives every complainant to provide particulars?"** If yes, it is a practice, and police must prove it reasonable. If no, ask why it was set for you.
2. **"Who decided my complaint on 10 September 2026, and which of my human rights did that person consider?"** The outcome names none.
3. **"When the dog was released, was an application to a magistrate considered, and why was none made?"** The receipt said one would be.

## 6. What to ask for, in order

1. **Acknowledgment** that on 24 February 2025 you were a named tenant of Unit 104, that the order of 3 December 2024 did not exclude you, and that you reported the taking of the dog on 1 December 2024.
2. **Reassessment** of complaint COM-26-01249 by an officer outside Ethical Standards Command and the Southport chain of command, with at least 10 business days for any response, and written reasons.
3. **A written statement** of who set the deadline of 18 June 2026, who decided the complaint, and what each considered.
4. **Record correction:** the address recorded on 18 March 2025, and a note on your record that the order of 3 December 2024 was vacated on 25 February 2025.
5. **Procedure:** a review of how police handle disputed property between former partners, and of the time given to complainants with a recorded disability.
6. **The dog:** an application by police to a magistrate, or an undertaking not to oppose yours.
7. **Compensation.**
8. **An apology,** or an expression of regret.

## 7. Money

- The only awards found against Queensland police for discrimination are $8,000 (Hunter v State of Queensland [2015] QCAT 179 at [61]) and $10,000 (Harding v State of Queensland (QPS) [2025] QCAT 415). ## 8. Terms to insist on, and terms to refuse

**Insist on**

- A date for each thing police agree to do.
- The reassessment to be by a named position, not "an appropriate officer".
- The agreement to be in writing and signed on behalf of the State.

**Refuse**

- **A general release.** Limit any release to "the complaint made to the Queensland Human Rights Commission on [date]". It must not release the criminal proceedings or any costs application in them; any property claim about the dog; any claim under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2010; any privacy complaint; or your access to information requests.
- **Confidentiality that stops you using the facts** in the criminal case or in another complaint. Confidentiality about the amount paid is ordinary.
- **Any statement that you accept police acted lawfully.**

## 9. If it does not resolve

- When the Commission tells you the complaint cannot be resolved, you have 28 days to require it to be referred to the tribunal. Diarise the date the notice arrives (State of Queensland (QPS) v Fraser [2025] QCATA 39 at [36]–[37]).
- If the complaint is not finished within six months, either side may ask for referral (s 167).
- In the tribunal, the outline of submissions (document 17) becomes your contentions, and the evidence matrix (document 18) is the list of what to prove.
