# Annexure B — Treatment and comparison matrix

Complaint of Cory Lea Shepherd against the State of Queensland (Queensland Police Service).

The Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 asks how police treated the complainant compared with how they treated, or would have treated, a person without his attributes "in circumstances that are the same or not materially different" (s 10(1)). The circumstances include what police were responding to (Woodforth v State of Queensland [2017] QCA 100 at [29], [54]). A characteristic generally had by, or often imputed to, people with an attribute is part of the attribute, not a circumstance of the comparison (s 8; Woodforth at [53]). Numbers in brackets refer to Annexure A.

## Episode 1 — Two complaints on the same night (1 to 2 December 2024)

| | The complainant | His former partner |
|---|---|---|
| What was reported | Entry into his home by people he did not know; he was pushed and his ankle injured; the dog taken [6]–[8] | Money missing from her wallet [10] |
| Evidence offered | Ownership documents; CCTV of the entry; triple-zero call [6]–[8] | Her account |
| Police response | "Let it go"; dog ownership "a civil issue"; no statement [8] | Signed statement taken the next day [10] |
| Outcome | Nothing further | Statement on file; the complainant already named respondent [5] |
| How police tested the account | His explanation of the bruise recorded and set aside [5]; no statement taken about his report of the entry and assault [8] | Her account of having stayed elsewhere for several nights accepted without checking the building's CCTV, which shows her there [4A], [5] |

**Comparator.** A woman who reports to police that people have entered her home, pushed and injured her and taken the dog she paid for, and who offers documents and CCTV.

**What police will say.** The complainant had been named respondent at 8:30 pm that evening [5]; the entry was the aggrieved collecting her belongings; the dog was a civil dispute.

**Reply.** The people who entered were not parties to the relationship; an assault by third parties is a criminal complaint whatever the domestic violence designation. Police must investigate where domestic violence is reasonably suspected (Domestic and Family Violence Protection Act 2012 s 100), and a civil response operates "in conjunction with, not instead of, the criminal law" (s 4(2)(f)). The order made two days later let her collect only "undisputed" property [12].

**Attributes.** Impairment: police recorded and relied on her statement that his mental illness "can cause the resp to act crazy and go into rages without warning" [5]. Sex, in addition and in the alternative.

## Episode 2 — The dog in December and in March

| | December 2024 | March 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Who had the dog | She did [9] | He did [40] |
| Who complained | He did [7]–[8] | She did |
| Papers | His, offered at the station [8] | His, shown at the seizure [40] |
| Police's description | "a civil issue" [8] | "stolen property"; "It would have been civil if you didn't get charged for stealing it" [40] |
| What police did | Left the dog with her [9] | Seized the dog from him; released it to her without the court application their own receipt said would be made [40], [45] |
| Who was sent to court | Him | Him: "he's got to go through the courts" [40] |

**Police's approach in their own words.** On 24 February 2025 an officer told the complainant's friend: "when there is contested property we don't typically get involved but when we think it's stolen property we do". He accepted there was a "civil aspect" and said police had "a document that shows that he's transferred ownership" [34A]. On 2 December 2024, when the complainant reported the dog taken, the council registration was in his name and no transfer had been signed [4], [16]. Police did not treat it as stolen then.

**Comparator.** A woman who holds a dog she paid for, that is registered with the council in her name, whose former partner claims it, and who shows police her receipts.

**What police will say.** By March she was the person protected by an order; the microchip had been transferred to her in December [16]; he had been charged; the dog was returned to its registered owner (Police Powers and Responsibilities Act 2000 s 691).

**Reply.** Ownership was disputed and police knew it on the spot [40]; police's own December record has the animals split between the two names [11]; the transfer was signed while she held the dog [16]; the law gave police a route to have a magistrate decide (s 694), their receipt told him it would be used, and they did not use it. The officer said the paperwork did not matter [40].

**Attribute.** Sex.

## Episode 3 — The home (24 February 2025)

| | The complainant | Her friend |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Tenant 1 on a lease running to 10 April 2025 [2], [14], [43]; bond in his name alone [33] | On neither the lease nor the order |
| What officers said | "technically he's allowed to be here"; "no one's done anything there" [26] | — |
| Welfare | No inquiry [28] | Offered support and referral services [28] |
| The friend each brought | His friend, present at 7:50 pm and filming: asked where the complainant was; questioned about the dog that morning and again at 11:10 pm [34A]; no statement taken about what he saw; his video not viewed; on his account, no statement sought for fifteen months | Her friend: account taken that night; her photographs and documents received (court brief) |
| Outcome | Arrested 5 minutes after the lease was confirmed [29]–[30]; police told the building manager he was "not the leaseholder" and would "not be going back" [31] | Access reinstated "and her friend" [31] |

**Comparator.** A woman named as Tenant 1, whose co-tenancy the building manager has just confirmed, whom officers have said is allowed to be there.

**What police will say.** He was arrested on the friend's allegations; a woman arrested on the same allegations would have been removed too.

**Reply.** The arrest explains the removal that night. It does not explain telling the building manager, after he had confirmed the lease twice, that the complainant was not the leaseholder and would not be going back [31]; nor the welfare inquiry made of one person and not the other [28]. Within the hour the interviewing officer called her "the other leaseholder" [32].

**Attribute.** Sex (s 8: the characteristic often imputed to men in domestic disputes, of being the aggressor).

## The thread that runs through every episode

Each time police had to decide whose account to act on, they acted on hers and sent him to the courts. The decision that set this pattern was the designation of 1 December 2024 [5], made before the people entered his home. The December records (Annexure C, item 1) will show what police knew when they made it.
