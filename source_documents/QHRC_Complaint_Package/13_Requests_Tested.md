# Your information requests, tested against the Act and the cases

18 November 2026. For you and your lawyers. Document 08 has been rewritten to match this.

## 1. The short answer

Your requests will produce documents, but fewer than they ask for. Six things need changing before you lodge.

**Likely to be released.** Your own calls to police. Your own reports and what police did with them. The property record for the dog. Your custody and bail papers. Job logs and job reports, with other people's details removed.

**Likely in part.** Notebook entries. Flags on your record. Camera footage in which you appear or speak.

**Unlikely.** Your former partner's statements. Other people's calls. The domestic violence risk assessment. Ethical Standards files. Anything that reads as a log of who used the police database.

**What to change.**

1. Do not lodge everything at once. Police may add your applications together when deciding whether the work is too much (s 41(1)).
2. Say in words that you want the date and author of each entry. Without those words the request does not cover them (s 28).
3. Ask for documents, not answers. Three items were written as questions.
4. Say on the face of each request why it does not repeat RTI/54169 or RTI/56733 (s 43).
5. Take the policy documents out. With them in, the application is no longer free (s 24(5)).
6. Attach written consent from your friend, your mother and anyone else whose account you ask for.

**One change to my advice of earlier today.** I drafted a paragraph asking the Crime and Corruption Commission to ask police about any flag on your record. Hold it. A record that Ethical Standards obtains for a complaint can become exempt from access (section 5.1). Get the flag through your own request first.

Section references are to the Right to Information Act 2009 unless another Act is named. Since 1 July 2025 a request for your own information is made under that Act.

## 5. Three findings that change the plan

### 5.1 Your own complaints can put records out of reach

In 016UGU and Queensland Police Service (6 December 2013) a woman asked for the CCTV of her own arrest. She had complained about the officers. The complaint was referred to Ethical Standards, and "the CCTV footage had been obtained for the purpose of this investigation" (at [5]). The footage was exempt. The exception failed because "the investigation is ongoing" (at [23]).

For you this means three things.

- **Police may raise it against Requests A and A2.** Ethical Standards may have obtained the footage and the December job report when it dealt with CO-25-2722 and COM-26-01249.
- **You have an answer that she did not.** COM-26-01249 was finalised on 10 September 2026. Once an investigation is finalised, information "about the applicant" is not exempt (sch 3 s 10(6)). The narrow reading in Darlington and Werthenbach was about investigation documents. Footage and job records that show or record you are about you in the ordinary sense. This is untested. Put it on the face of the request (document 08, standard paragraph 5).
- **Do not start a new investigation into the same records before the requests are decided.** Keep the letter of 20 November to the question of who decided COM-26-01249. Hold the paragraph on the flag (document 12) until the flag request is decided.

### 5.2 Police can add your applications together

Police may refuse to deal with an application "or, if the agency ... is considering 2 or more access applications by the applicant, all the applications" if the work would substantially and unreasonably divert its resources (s 41(1)). Separate requests are still right, because a class refusal of one then cannot touch the others (s 40). But keep no more than two with police at a time.

Before refusing on this ground police must give you written notice and a chance to narrow the request (s 42(1)). You must answer in writing within 10 business days. If you do not, the application is treated as withdrawn (s 42(4)–(6)).

### 5.3 A repeat request must justify itself on its face

Police may refuse a later request for documents already sought if it "does not, on its face, disclose any reasonable basis for again seeking access" (s 43(1)(b)). Reasons given afterwards do not count: "The applicant is seeking to retrospectively provide a reasonable basis for making the overlapping request" (R31 at [21]). Police gave access to footage under RTI/56733, and the application behind review 318762 has been through a completed review. Both are caught (s 43(3)(b)(i), (c)(ii)).

Do not ask again here for the camera footage of 24 February and 22 March 2025. Pursue the missing segments by answering the Information Commissioner's question of 19 November 2025, and through the prosecution.

| Policy documents | Inspection and purchase | s 20(1) | You, by letter |

Two cautions for your criminal lawyers.

- The disclosure provisions apply to a "prescribed summary trial" (Criminal Code s 590AD). Ask them to confirm the provisions will still apply if the burglary charge is replaced by trespass, and to make the disclosure request before any change of charge.
- A document released under the Right to Information Act can be used for any purpose. Ask them what limits apply to material received by disclosure before any of it goes into the complaint.

Whatever is refused, apply for external review. In the decisions read, police released more once the Commissioner was involved: Isles at [6], Werthenbach at [7], Wyeth [2015] QICmr 26 at [4] and G31 at [3].
