# Is a criminal allegation made out against Ms Negro?

**13 August 2026 — assessment, not advocacy**

The question asked: on the sequence — an order permitting her only to collect undisputed items, then a Form 20 taking her own name off the lease which was accepted, then a Form 13 obtained from a man with a recent diagnosis of major depressive disorder in the middle of a crisis, framed as a two-to-three week arrangement, then permanent deprivation of his home and belongings, then an assertion of the lease without a bond, then a claim to be in danger — is a criminal allegation made out?

**Short answer: the conduct described maps onto recognisable criminal concepts, but the allegation is not comfortably made out on what can presently be proved, and pursuing it now would be a mistake. The same facts are worth considerably more in three other places.**

---

## 1. What the conduct would have to be charged as

| Offence | Fit | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| **Fraud — s 408C Criminal Code (Qld)** | The closest fit. Dishonestly gaining a benefit or advantage, or causing a detriment. The benefit is sole possession of the premises and the contents; the detriment is his housing and property | Requires **dishonesty at the time**. She can say she genuinely believed she was entitled to the unit, or genuinely felt unsafe. That is a live answer, and the Crown carries it beyond reasonable doubt |
| **Stealing / fraudulent conversion — ss 391, 398** | His goods, retained after he was locked out, with intent to permanently deprive | The goods were in premises she occupied rather than taken by her. Much of the furniture left with him in the van on 23 February. Deprivation would have to be pinned to specific items |
| **Perjury — s 123** | If a sworn statement contained knowingly false material | Requires the falsity to be material **and knowingly false**. "No evidence" at the strike-out is not the same as proven lies |
| **Attempting to pervert the course of justice — s 140** | If police were deliberately used to procure charges on a false basis | Very high threshold. Motive evidence exists — the December 2024 threat overheard by his mother — but that was a friend's statement, not hers |
| **Coercive control — s 334** | Describes the pattern well | **Not available.** The offence commenced 26 May 2025. The conduct is December 2024 to February 2025. It cannot be applied retrospectively |

**The recurring problem is the mental element.** Every one of these requires proof, to the criminal standard, of what she knew and intended at the time. The material establishes what she *did*, and the sequence is stark — but a person can behave badly, unfairly and inconsistently in the collapse of a five-year relationship without a court being satisfied beyond reasonable doubt that they were dishonest when they did it.

That is the same standard the prosecution is failing to meet against Cory. It cuts both ways.

---

## 2. Why pursuing it now would be a mistake

This matters more than the legal analysis.

**He is the defendant in a live prosecution, and he is winning on the current track.** One of three charges has just been conceded. The submissions that produced that result are documentary, restrained, and about the tenancy and the dog.

Making a criminal complaint against the complainant mid-prosecution would:

1. **Hand the "vexatious" framing back to them.** It was tried at the August 2025 hearing, and the Magistrate refused costs — which was a quiet but real win. Do not reopen it.
2. **Risk being read as pressure on a witness.** She is the complainant in a live matter. A fraud complaint against her, made by the defendant, while the matter is on foot, is capable of being characterised in a way that helps nobody.
3. **Move attention away from the ground where he is strong.** The tenancy documents and the honest-claim-of-right point are working. A counter-allegation invites everything to be re-argued as a feud between two people, which is exactly the frame that has hurt him from the beginning.
4. **Almost certainly go nowhere.** He already asked Const. Simpson the obvious question on tape — *"wouldn't she be criminally charged then for not [following] it?"* — and got *"It's gonna be going in front of the courts. I'm just here to pretty much serve this."* The asymmetry is documented. Another complaint into the same system produces another file, not a charge.

**The instinct behind the question is sound. The remedy sought is the wrong one.**

## 3. Where these same facts are worth far more

### 3.1 — As the answer to his own charges

Every element of the sequence already does work in the defence:

- The order required **her** to vacate and collect uncontested items — Const. Davies read it that way himself.
- The Form 20 ended **her** interest, not his.
- The Form 13 never operated because he never left.
- The contents were his, with receipts, and the person present told him to take the furniture.
- He was never given the continuing interest notice the Act required.

That is the case. It does not need her to be charged with anything.

### 3.2 — In the civil and tenancy claims, where the standard is achievable

This is the proper home for the conduct described, and it is a route he controls:

- **Unconscionable dealing and undue influence** — special disadvantage (diagnosed major depressive disorder, a bereavement, days after a protection notice), known to her, and advantage taken. *Commercial Bank of Australia Ltd v Amadio* (1983) 151 CLR 447. This is the natural claim for both the dog transfer and the Form 13, and the standard is the balance of probabilities.
- **Conversion and detinue** — for goods not returned.
- **The bond** — and see §4.2.
- **The tenancy claims** — through the RTA and QCAT.

Civil claims deliver something a criminal complaint never will: **the transaction set aside, and compensation.** A conviction would give him neither.

### 3.3 — As the asymmetry, in the human rights matter

This is the highest-value use, and it costs nothing.

The point is not *"she should be charged."* The point is:

> The same order was in force over both parties. One party is alleged to have breached it by entering premises he held the lease to, while the aggrieved was interstate, and has been prosecuted for eighteen months. The other party is recorded as having returned to premises the order required her to vacate, taken contested items, and remained — and no investigation followed. When the respondent asked a serving officer why, the answer was that the officer was only there to serve documents.

**That is a submission about the decision-making, not an accusation against her.** It is documented, it needs no finding about her state of mind, and it goes directly to the discrimination and procedural-limb arguments.

Keep it in that form. Framed as "charge her too," it fails and it damages the rest. Framed as asymmetric enforcement, it is one of the strongest points available.

## 4. Two concrete checks that follow from the sequence

### 4.1 — The date of the Form 13

Const. Bickery's statement records that Ms Phillips provided *"screenshots of documents stating that SHEPHERD had not resided at the address since the 7th of December 2024."*

**7 December 2024 is two days after the Form 20 of 5 December.**

If the Form 13 is dated on or about 7 December, the sequence is:

| 1 December 2024 | Protection notice issued |
| 5 December 2024 | Her Form 20 — ending her own interest — given and accepted |
| **7 December 2024** | **His Form 13 — two days later** |
| 17 December 2024 | The dog transfer signed |

Four documents in sixteen days, from a man who had just been served with a police protection notice, had recently been diagnosed with major depressive disorder, and had lost his grandmother.

That sequence is the unconscionability case, and it is provable from dates alone without anyone's state of mind being contested.

**Verify the date on the Form 13.** It is the single cheapest thing on this list.

### 4.2 — Whether her tenancy was created on his bond

He says she asserted the lease *"without even paying for a bond."*

The RTA bond record answers it:

- Was a **new bond** lodged for the tenancy commencing 10 April 2025?
- Or is that tenancy sitting on **his** $2,160, never refunded and never transferred?

If a new tenancy was created using a bond lodged in the name of a person who was not a party to it, that is a concrete, documented wrong — and unlike everything in §1, it requires no proof of anyone's intention. It is either in the record or it is not.

**This is the version of his complaint that is actually provable.**

---

## 5. The honest bottom line

What was done to him has a shape that the criminal law recognises. But the elements would have to be proved beyond reasonable doubt against a person who can say she was frightened and believed she was entitled, and the system that would have to prosecute her has already declined to look.

He does not need her convicted. He needs his charges gone, his transaction set aside, his property back, and a finding about how he was treated. All four are available, and none of them requires a criminal allegation against her.

**Keep the sequence. Use it as the defence, as the civil claim, and as the asymmetry. Do not turn it into a complaint.**
