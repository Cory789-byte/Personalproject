# Using a position of authority to advantage another person — the legal framework

**13 August 2026.** Provisions verified against source; sources at the foot.

The question: what is the legal view where a person in authority uses that position to take advantage of one person for the benefit of another?

It has a name at four levels, and they have different thresholds. The lowest threshold is the one that fits these facts best, and it is the one already engaged by the CCC matters.

---

## 1. Criminal — s 92A Criminal Code (Qld), misconduct in relation to public office

**Elements:**

- a **public officer**;
- who, **with intent to dishonestly gain a benefit for the officer or another person**, or dishonestly cause a detriment to another person;
- **deals with information** gained because of the office, **performs or fails to perform a function** of the office, or **does an act or makes an omission in abuse of the authority of the office**.

**Maximum penalty: 7 years imprisonment.**

Two features matter here.

**"or another person."** The section expressly covers using the office to benefit *someone else*. The officer does not have to gain anything.

**"Authority of office" is defined to include the trust imposed by the office and the influence relating to the office.** So it is not confined to formal powers. Using the *influence* that comes with being a police officer — to get a private party to do something they would not otherwise do — falls within it.

**The obstacle is "dishonestly."** It has to be proved that the officer acted with dishonest intent, to the criminal standard. An officer who says they believed they were protecting a person in a domestic violence matter has a genuine answer, and that answer is supported here by the recorded admission that they were deferring to the judgment of other officers rather than forming their own.

---

## 2. Corruption — s 15 Crime and Corruption Act 2001 (Qld)

**This is the best fit, and it is the definition the CCC matters already run under.**

Type A corrupt conduct is conduct that affects, or could affect, a public officer so that the performance of their functions or the exercise of their powers:

- **is not honest or is not impartial**; or
- knowingly or recklessly breaches the trust placed in the officer; or
- involves the misuse of information or material acquired through the office;

**and** would, if proved, be a criminal offence **or a disciplinary breach providing reasonable grounds for terminating the person's services.**

Three things make this materially easier than s 92A:

1. **"Not impartial" is enough.** Dishonesty is not required. Partiality — acting for one side of a dispute — satisfies the limb on its own.
2. **The benefit test was removed.** The 2019 amendments deleted the former requirement that the conduct be engaged in for the purpose of obtaining a benefit. It no longer has to be shown that anyone was trying to gain anything.
3. **A disciplinary breach is sufficient.** It does not have to amount to a crime.

**On these facts, "not impartial" is the natural description.** A civil dispute between two people in which the powers of office were exercised consistently in favour of one of them is, at minimum, a question of impartiality.

---

## 3. Civil — misfeasance in public office

The tort. *Northern Territory v Mengel* (1995) 185 CLR 307; *Three Rivers District Council v Bank of England (No 3)* [2003] 2 AC 1.

Two alternative limbs:

| Limb | What must be shown | Prospects here |
|---|---|---|
| **Targeted malice** | The officer acted with the intention of injuring the plaintiff | **Weak.** Nothing on this record shows an officer setting out to harm him personally |
| **Knowledge of invalidity or reckless indifference** | The officer knew the act was beyond power, or was recklessly indifferent to whether it was, and knew or was recklessly indifferent to the probability of harm | **Genuinely arguable** — see below |

The second limb is where the material sits, because knowledge is documented:

- **1:54 a.m.** — the property manager telephoned Constables Bickery and Harmer during the lockout to tell them a Form 13 does not terminate the lease.
- **2:00 a.m.** — the arrival briefing recorded that Cory had the key, the lease and the bond.
- **Throughout** — *"technically he's allowed to be here"*, *"we don't know who owns the place"*.
- **3:26 a.m.** — police asked the building manager to reactivate the fob, which is inconsistent with a belief that he had no interest.
- **The lockout proceeded anyway.**

Being told the act is unlawful, recording that you do not know whether it is lawful, and proceeding regardless, is capable of amounting to reckless indifference to invalidity. That is the strongest civil framing available, and unlike the criminal provisions it produces damages.

---

## 4. Administrative — improper purpose

A statutory power exercised for a purpose other than the one for which it was conferred is invalid, regardless of the officer's honesty. The relevant material is the pre-charge statement of the objective:

> *"we haven't charged him yet so yeah so he'll be young like we're gonna put him on bowel [bail] conditions not to go back there."*

If exclusion from the residence was the objective and the charge was the means of achieving it, the power was exercised for a purpose other than its own. This does not require dishonesty and it does not require malice.

## 5. Applying it to the facts — the strongest instances

Ranked by how directly each shows office being used to advantage another person.

**1. Romeo handed to Ms Negro before any court order.** Police powers used to transfer possession of disputed property to one private claimant. Admitted by QPS in open court in August 2025. The seizing officer stated the mechanism himself: *"regardless of what paperwork he has, we've got to seize the dog and then he's got to go through the courts to get the dog back."* One party got the property; the other got the burden of litigating for it.

**2. Directing the property manager, and threatening him.** Const. Davies: *"you need to reinstate those immediately because Corey's not the leaseholder… cory will not be going back to the foreseeable future"*, and *"your behaviour, like cancelling those fobs, it's been pretty unprofessional, okay? So it's obstructed police."* That is the **influence of office** — expressly within the s 92A definition — used to procure a private actor's compliance in a civil tenancy dispute, against the position that same manager had just stated, and to the benefit of the other party.

**3. The lockout itself**, after the 1:54 a.m. warning and the 2:00 a.m. briefing.

**4. The bail objective announced before the charge existed.**

**5. The exculpatory evidence packet left with Ms Phillips** — material handed to the arresting officer by an independent witness, disposed of into the custody of the opposing party's friend.

**6. Threatening the independent witness** with *"parties to offences and… possessing tainted property"*.

## 6. The honest difficulty, stated plainly

**The admission that helps the human rights case hurts the dishonesty case.**

> *"we have to defer to the judgment of our other police that have made that call… And we don't reinvestigate that… That's not how it works."*

That is powerful evidence of a failure to give proper consideration to rights, and of a system operating without error-correction. But it also supports an innocent explanation of motive: these officers were not setting out to advantage anyone, they were applying a policy of not revisiting another officer's decision.

That answer defeats **targeted malice** and makes **dishonest intent** under s 92A hard.

It does not defeat:

- **impartiality** under the CC Act — a policy of automatic deference to one side's account *is* a failure of impartiality, whatever its motive;
- **reckless indifference to invalidity** under the second limb of misfeasance — "we don't reinvestigate" is close to the definition of not caring whether the act is lawful;
- **improper purpose**, which does not depend on state of mind at all; or
- **HRA s 58**, where the whole point is that proper consideration was not given.

**So the framing should follow the evidence.** Not "they set out to help her" — which is hard and probably wrong. Rather: **the powers of office were exercised partially, without verification, against express warnings, and consistently to one party's advantage.** That is provable on the tapes.

## 7. Where each of these belongs

| Framework | Forum | Status |
|---|---|---|
| CC Act s 15 — corrupt conduct | **CCC** | Already engaged — the s 35(3) submission and CO-26-1318 |
| HRA s 58 | **QHRC** | EDR19098 |
| Misfeasance, reckless indifference limb | **Civil court** | Not commenced |
| Improper purpose | **The criminal proceeding**, and civil | Available now |
| s 92A | **Police / DPP** | Not realistically available — requires dishonest intent, and the decision belongs to the same institution |
| Police misconduct | **Internal, via CCC** | Within the existing referrals |

**The practical point:** this framework is already deployed in the right places. It does not need a new complaint. What it needs is to be argued at the right threshold — impartiality and reckless indifference rather than dishonesty — because that is where the recorded evidence actually reaches.
