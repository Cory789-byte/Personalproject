## 6. The claims

You must prove each claim on the balance of probabilities. A discrimination claim needs three things: you were treated worse than a person without the attribute was or would have been; the situations were not materially different; and the attribute was a substantial reason.

### Claim C: sex. The dog

The treatment:

1. 2 December 2024. Police declined to act on your complaint of assault and the taking of the dog, called it civil, and took no statement. The same day police took her complaint about money by signed statement, and escorted her to collect belongings.
2. 22 March 2025. Police took the dog from you. On camera: "it's been stated that the dog was taken in a [DV] breach. So regardless of what paperwork he has, we've got to seize the dog and then he's got to go through the courts to get the dog back"; "It's my dog." "It doesn't matter"; "It would have been civil if you didn't get charged for stealing it". Police then gave the dog to her without a court order.

The comparison is a real one: a woman, in the same dispute, over the same dog, with the same police service. "Civil" means police leave the dog where it is and the person without it goes to court. Police applied that rule when she had the dog. They reversed it when you had the dog. Both times the man was the one told to go to court.

Why the reason given does not explain it: the officer said the paperwork did not matter. The reason given was her allegation and the charge. Your allegation in December, made with documents in your hand, did not get a statement taken.

The weak joints:

- Your December messages (section 5). Police will say that by February the dog was hers by your own signature and promise, so the two situations were different.
- The charge. Police will say the February entry made it criminal. That stands or falls with the criminal case. The prosecution has already proposed replacing the stealing-based charge.
- Proof. What was said at the station rests on your account until the police record of that night is obtained.

The weak joint: police will say you were removed because you were arrested, and a woman arrested on the same allegations would have been removed too. If the remaining charges end in your favour, the arrest stops explaining the exclusion. If they do not, this claim fails.

## 10. Route, timing and remedies

**Route.** One complaint to the Queensland Human Rights Commission under the Anti-Discrimination Act (sections 7(a) and (h), 8, 10, 11 and 101), with a Human Rights Act complaint about the outcome letter. Section 101 covers any police function. Section 8 covers assumptions made about a group, which is how assumptions about men in domestic disputes come in. The State answers for its officers. If conciliation fails you can require referral to the tribunal. Each side usually pays its own costs. The tribunal can adopt what the criminal court finds.

**Why not the federal route.** It covers "services" only, which does not fit a seizure, an exclusion or an arrest. Using both routes lets police argue that one bars the other. The federal two-year marks (2 December 2026, 24 February 2027, 22 March 2027) can pass.

**Time.** Claim B is in time until 18 June 2027. Claims A and C are out of time and need good cause. The good cause is: a criminal case on the same events is still running; your health; you took the same events to the same Commission earlier; and nothing is lost to police because the footage exists and you asked for it to be kept. In-time and out-of-time acts can go in one complaint. The Commission may put Claims A and C on hold while the court case runs. That costs nothing.

**When.** After 6 January 2027, when the Human Rights Act complaint about the outcome becomes available, and after your lawyers have read it. Earlier if the criminal case ends first.

**What the tribunal will not decide.** Who owns the dog. Only a property claim does that. The family-law time limit for a property claim runs two years from the end of the relationship and may fall this month. Raise it with your lawyers now.

**What to ask for.** A written acknowledgment that you were named on the lease, that the order did not exclude you, and that you reported the taking of the dog on 1 December 2024; correction of the police record; reassessment of your complaint by an officer outside Ethical Standards and Southport, with reasonable time to respond; an apology; compensation.
