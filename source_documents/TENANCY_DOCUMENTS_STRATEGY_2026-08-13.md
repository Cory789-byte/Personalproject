# Getting the tenancy documents — Form 20, Form 4, and the later lease

**13 August 2026 — prepared in response to a proposed letter to Ms Arase (leasing manager)**

---

## 1. The proposed letter should not be sent

Not in that form, and not from Cory personally.

### 1.1 — She is likely the prosecution's witness

The prosecution's stated reason for rejecting the submission on Charge 1 begins: *"Statement from the leasing manager advising that your client sent a form 13 notice of intent to remove from a tenancy, this was accepted, he was removed."*

Naoko Arase is the **leasing manager**. Denis Constable is the building/property manager. The statement now relied on by the prosecution may well be hers.

A defendant in a live criminal proceeding writing directly to the prosecution's key witness on the very issue in dispute — telling her what the charges are, telling her which issues survive, and asking her to produce documents — is a serious step. Even where the motive is entirely proper, it is open to being characterised as an attempt to shape a witness's evidence.

Given the history in this matter, that risk should not be run. Correspondence with this witness should come from Sibley Lawyers on their letterhead, or the documents should be compelled. Not a personal letter.

### 1.2 — A Form 20 is a domestic and family violence document, and she probably cannot hand it over

The domestic and family violence provisions of the *Residential Tenancies and Rooming Accommodation Act 2008* carry restrictions on disclosing a notice ending tenancy interest and the evidence supporting it. A lessor or agent who receives one is constrained in what they may pass on.

So a voluntary request is likely to be **refused for good reason**, and the effect will be:

- the document is not produced;
- the prosecution learns exactly what the defence is looking for and why;
- a defendant has personally contacted the key prosecution witness, for nothing.

**The correct tool is a summons or notice to produce, not a request.** A recipient who is restricted from disclosing voluntarily can produce under compulsion. Compulsion also fixes provenance, which matters if the document is going into evidence.

*(The precise scope of the confidentiality provision should be confirmed against the Act before anything is sent. The practical point holds either way: compulsion is the reliable route.)*

## 3. The important part — what the Form 20 may actually establish

This is the reason the document matters, and it may be the single strongest fact in the case.

**Form 20 is the RTA's "Notice ending tenancy interest".** Under the domestic and family violence provisions of the RTRAA, that notice is given by the tenant who is experiencing domestic violence, and its effect is to end **that tenant's own** interest in the tenancy. The tenancy continues for the remaining tenant or tenants. It is a mechanism for the person leaving to get out without breaking the lease or carrying ongoing liability.

**If that is right, and if Ms Negro gave a Form 20 on 5 December 2024 and the agent accepted it, then the person who came off that tenancy was Ms Negro — not Cory.**

That is the exact inverse of the prosecution's case, which is that Cory "was removed" and she remained.

It also explains every other fact that has never quite fitted:

- Denis Constable to Const. Davies: *"the lease wasn't accepted by the property manager because he was not able to come off the lease **because she's not able to have the apartment with her name only on it**."*
- Denis Constable to Const. Easthope: *"Both parties must sign that form… it hasn't even started and we're not aware of it."*
- Const. Easthope's arrival briefing: *"it's his lease, his bottom and it's his key."*
- The bond of $2,160 standing **solely in Cory's name**, never refunded, with no Form 4.
- A **new sole-tenant agreement in Ms Negro's name commencing 10 April 2025** — which is the day the earlier agreement ran out. She needed a fresh agreement because she had no continuing interest in the old one.

### 3.1 — The dates now line up precisely

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 11 April 2024 | General Tenancy Agreement commences — **Cory named as Tenant 1** |
| 5 December 2024 | Ms Negro emails the agent a **Form 20**; agent replies accepting |
| **23 February 2025** | **The conduct charged.** The 11 April 2024 agreement is still running. |
| 10 April 2025 | The earlier agreement expires; **new sole-tenant agreement in Ms Negro's name commences** |

On these dates, the operative tenancy on 23 February 2025 was the agreement naming Cory as Tenant 1, and Ms Negro's own sole tenancy did not begin until seven weeks after the alleged offence.

### 3.2 — This must be verified, not asserted

Before this is put anywhere:

1. **Confirm what a Form 20 is and does**, against the Act and the RTA's own material — in particular whether the notice ends the *giver's* interest, and what the agent must then do.
2. **Obtain the actual document.** The argument depends on what the form says, who signed it, and what it purported to do.
3. **Confirm the agent's acceptance** and what was done with the bond afterwards.

If the form turns out to operate differently, this collapses. Do not build on it until the document is in hand. That is precisely why it must be obtained through a route that actually works.

## 4. The right way to get each document

Three separate channels. Only one of them involves the witness, and that one is compelled rather than requested.

### 4.1 — The bond and the Form 4: go directly to the RTA

The bond is held by the **Residential Tenancies Authority**, not by the agent. If the bond of $2,160 stands solely in Cory's name, **he is the bond contributor and can request his own bond record directly.**

That answers the Form 4 question completely, with no witness contact at all, and produces an independent third-party record showing:

- the bond number, amount, and lodgement date;
- every contributor named on it;
- whether any change of contributors was ever processed, and when;
- whether any refund was claimed or paid, to whom, and on what date;
- whether a **new bond** was lodged for a **new tenancy**, and when.

**Do this first. It costs nothing, it involves nobody, and it is objective.**

### 4.2 — The Form 20 and the later lease: summons / notice to produce

Through Sibley Lawyers, addressed to the agency rather than to an individual. Compulsion solves the confidentiality problem, fixes provenance, and removes any question of witness contact.

Suggested schedule of documents — to be settled by the solicitors:

1. The General Tenancy Agreement for Unit 104, 158 Scarborough Street, Southport commencing on or about 11 April 2024, together with any predecessor agreement, renewal, or variation.
2. Any notice ending tenancy interest (RTA Form 20) received in respect of that tenancy between 1 November 2024 and 30 April 2025, together with the covering email and any reply, acknowledgement or acceptance.
3. Any notice of intention to leave (RTA Form 13) received in respect of that tenancy in the same period, together with any reply, acknowledgement or acceptance.
4. All records of the rental bond for that tenancy, including the lodgement record, any change of bond contributors, and any refund of rental bond (RTA Form 4).
5. Any tenancy agreement for Unit 104 commencing on or about 10 April 2025, and any application, approval or reference documents relating to it.
6. All correspondence between the agency and Alexia Negro, and between the agency and Cory Shepherd, relating to the tenancy between 1 November 2024 and 30 April 2025.
7. The tenancy ledger for the premises for the same period.
8. Any statement or draft statement provided to the Queensland Police Service in relation to this tenancy, and any correspondence with police about it.

Item 8 matters. If the leasing manager gave the statement the prosecution now relies on, its date and the circumstances in which it was taken are directly relevant, given what the same agency told two officers on camera on the night.

### 4.3 — Preservation: a short letter, from the solicitors

The one piece of correspondence that is both safe and useful. It asks the recipient to **keep** material — not to hand anything over, and not to say anything. A draft is at §5.
