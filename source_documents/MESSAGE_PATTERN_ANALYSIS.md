# The message pattern — how to analyse it, and what not to attach it to

**20 October 2026**

Cory asked for the transcript of a YouTube audiobook — *"Narcissist's Playbook: The Art of Manipulation and Control"* (Stealth Psychology) — to be analysed against the WhatsApp record from 2022 to 2024, in support of the point that his own pattern was to **walk away from conflict and then text to make his point**.

The underlying point is a good one and it is worth developing. The framework is not, and it must not go near paper. This note separates the two.

---

## 1. The framework cannot be used, and using it would cost him the case

### 1.1 — It has no evidential standing

A popular-psychology audiobook on a YouTube channel is not a clinical instrument, is not peer-reviewed, is not an authority, and is not admissible. No court, tribunal or commission will receive it. The QHRC will not. A magistrate will not.

### 1.2 — The real damage is not inadmissibility, it is characterisation

**A self-represented or part-represented litigant who diagnoses the opposing party with a personality disorder from an internet checklist hands the other side the case.**

In this matter specifically:

- Charge 2 — unlawful stalking, intimidation, harassment or abuse — was laid and maintained for eighteen months on the proposition that Cory was fixated on Ms Negro. **It has just been conceded and discontinued.**
- A document analysing her against a "narcissist's playbook" is the single most effective way to make that charge look like it was right after all.
- At the August 2025 hearing QPS argued he was vexatious. The Magistrate refused costs. That finding is worth protecting.

Everything that has worked in this matter has worked because it is documentary and restrained — the 1:54 a.m. call, the 2:00 a.m. briefing, the Form 20, the bond, Yaun's email. Each of those is a record that speaks without anyone being characterised. **Diagnostic framing is the opposite, and it would contaminate all of it.**

This is the same discipline recorded elsewhere in this file: do not allege concert, use the asymmetry; let the documents speak.

### 1.3 — The distinction worth keeping

Reading about manipulation and coercive control **for his own understanding** is entirely legitimate. Five years of a relationship that ended this way needs making sense of, and he is entitled to look for an explanation.

**That is a separate activity from building a case.** Private understanding: read whatever helps. Paper, submissions, correspondence, affidavits, anything that reaches a decision-maker: nothing from that genre goes in.

---

## 2. The point he is actually making is strong, and does not need the framework

> *"my actions started walking away then texting to get my point across"*

Stated without any psychology at all, that is a direct answer to the characterisation in Ms Negro's statement that he sent *"numerous, long, abusive and controlling messages."*

### 2.1 — The behaviour described is de-escalation

Physically removing yourself from a conflict and then putting your position in writing is:

- what people are advised to do;
- **non-proximate** — it requires being somewhere else;
- **asynchronous** — the other person reads it when they choose, and can not read it;
- **non-physical** — no presence, no cornering, no blocking of exits; and
- **self-documenting** — it creates a record that can be checked.

### 2.2 — It is the opposite of the conduct the legislation targets

Coercive control, as the law describes it, is characterised by proximity, monitoring, surveillance, isolation, restriction of movement and control of resources. **Leaving the room is not on that list.** Nor is writing something down.

### 2.3 — The irony that should be put plainly

**The volume of messages exists because he chose the only channel that leaves a record.**

A person who stayed in the room and shouted would have generated no evidence at all. A person who walks out and writes generates a complete transcript of everything he said — which is then available to be quoted selectively against him.

**He is being prosecuted on the artefact of having chosen the safer channel.**

### 2.4 — And the messages were not prohibited

The order in force contained two conditions: good behaviour towards the aggrieved, and no domestic violence against the aggrieved. **There was no no-contact condition.** Ms Negro's own sworn statement recites the same two conditions and nothing more.

Communication between separating partners about property, animals, a lease and separation documents was not prohibited by anything.

## 3. What an honest analysis of the messages would actually show

This is the work worth doing, and it needs the messages, not a framework.

### 3.1 — Scope it to the period that matters

The volume figures in the older file cover roughly five years (80,286 from him, 53,224 from her). **Over a five-year relationship that is unremarkable, and arguing about it is a distraction.**

What matters is the **post-separation period — December 2024 to February 2025** — because that is the period Ms Negro's statement quotes from and the period the charges concern. Analyse that window.

*(2022–2024 is useful only as a baseline: it establishes what normal traffic between them looked like, so the later period can be measured against it rather than against nothing.)*

### 3.2 — The five things to extract

1. **Sequence.** Where a message follows a face-to-face disagreement, does the record show him having left first? Timestamps, location references, "I've gone", "I'm not doing this here". If the pattern holds, it is demonstrable rather than asserted.
2. **Subject matter.** Classify every post-separation message: property recovery, the animals, the lease, separation and civil-partnership documents, the visa, welfare of the dog, other. Ms Negro's own framing of the dispute four days before the alleged breach was *"a legal civil case that he is required to obtain a lawyer for."* If the messages are overwhelmingly about those subjects, the "abusive and controlling" characterisation is answered by content.
3. **Bilaterality.** Her statement quotes his messages and not her replies. The full threads show whether contact was two-way. This is the single most useful corrective and it requires nothing but the export.
4. **The invitation messages.** *"two to three weeks"*; *"I won't restrict you from the animals"*; *"All in your name now"*; *"He's always happy to see you"*; *"You can trust me too."* Const. Davies confirmed on his own camera that he had read them. **These are the highest-value messages in the corpus** — they go to the mental element on both surviving charges and to how the Form 13 was obtained.
5. **The dog-walking permission message.** Identified by Cory in interview, acknowledged by Ms Phillips to police, never produced. If it is in the WhatsApp export rather than only on the seized phone, **it may already be recoverable without QPS.**

### 3.3 — What is needed to do it

The WhatsApp corpus is **not in this repository.** To run this:

- the WhatsApp export files (`.txt` chat exports, or the processed corpus); and
- confirmation of the date range available.

Point 5 alone justifies pulling the export: the message that answers the predicate offence in Charge 1 may be sitting in a file he already has, rather than on a phone that has been in police custody and unexamined for twenty months.

---

## 4. Summary

The instinct is sound: his conduct ran in the direction of disengagement, and the written record exists because he chose the least confrontational way to communicate. **That point is strong, it is provable from the messages themselves, and it needs no psychological framework whatsoever.**

Attaching it to a YouTube audiobook on narcissism would take a good argument and make it look like the thing he has just been cleared of.

**Do the message analysis. Leave the framework out of the file.**
