### 19.1 ⛔ THE SENTENCE I MISSED — "we all want to get to a hearing and move past this"

This is the most settlement-relevant thing said in the room, and v5 didn't have it.

**He said *get to a hearing*. Not *resolve this*.** A member steering parties toward settlement
says something quite different — *have the parties considered a conference*, *this is a matter
that ought to resolve*. He said none of that. He said: **strip the disclosure back, get to a
hearing, move past this.**

Paired with *"Queensland Health will spend a great deal of money fighting this disclosure — it
will be a long fight"*, the message is proportionality and momentum: **stop fighting about
documents, start moving toward a determination.**

⇒ **That is a directions signal, not a settlement signal. It lowers P(consent) and, more
importantly, it moves the settlement mass to the far end of the timeline.**

### 19.2 ⭐⭐ THE SENTENCE THAT CUTS THE OTHER WAY — "Matheson would then have to displace it"

Dwyer did not merely say the appellant could give evidence. He explained a **mechanic**: rather
than obtaining Taylor's building-access export, **put it to her in cross-examination — and the
Regulator then has to displace it.**

That is the Commission explaining, in front of the Regulator's carriage officer, **how a
self-represented appellant runs a hearing that puts the practical burden of contradiction on
the Respondent.** It is not evidence and it cannot be quoted. **It is now in Matheson's head
when she next assesses prospects, and that is the only place it needs to be.**

⇒ **The strongest settlement input from the room — and it pushes against 19.1.**

### 19.3 HOW THE TWO RESOLVE

They do not cancel. They combine into a shape:

**The destination is a hearing (down for settlement) — but the hearing now looks harder for the
Regulator than they had priced it (up for settlement).**

⭐ **A case that is harder to defend, heading somewhere it will actually be determined, settles
LATE — at or near the hearing door, not in October.** The probability of resolution holds; the
distribution shifts sharply right.

### 19.4 ⚠ "THAT'S JUST MANAGEMENT ACTION" — read it twice

The bench put **the Respondent's entire s 32(5)(a) case in six words**, unprompted, at the first
substantive engagement. Two available readings:

- **(a) Merits instinct.** His starting position on the conduct is the Respondent's position.
  The answer given — *it lacked consultation and adversely affected patients* — is the correct
  answer and goes to the **"taken in a reasonable way"** limb. But the topic being *dropped* is
  not the topic being *conceded*. A member persuaded on a point tends to say so.
- **(b) Relevance probe.** He was testing whether a sought document was necessary, on a
  **disclosure** application — not stating a view on the appeal.

⚠ **Both are live; (b) is the more probable in context, but (a) cannot be excluded.**

⇒ ⛔ **This stops the hearing-prospects estimate rising on the merits.** The v5 lift to 42–45%
was earned by *the evidentiary path being open*, which is a different thing from the merits
looking good. **Hold hearing prospects at ~40–44%, causation gate unchanged.**

⭐ **This exchange is the spine of the outline of argument.** Whatever else is written, it must
answer *"that's just management action"* in its first page.
