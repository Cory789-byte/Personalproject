# Annexure E — The Forty Questions

A one-page companion to episodes 17–21, for the table during a sitting.
Audio: `audio/17_sitting-one.mp3` … `audio/21_sitting-five.mp3`.

## Running the recorder

- Quiet room. Phone charged. Notifications off. **Recorder running before the first question.**
- Say the date, the place, and who is in the room. Then have Tom say, in his own
  words, that he knows he's being recorded and what it's for.
- **One question, then silence.** Let the pauses run — the pause is where the good
  material lives. Never correct him mid-story; note it and come back.
- Follow the energy, not the list. If Q3 opens a door, go through it.
- 45–90 minutes a sitting. Five sittings.
- Objects on the table help: the framed citation, the whakapapa sheet, the land
  schedules, the block map, photographs.
- **At the end of every sitting: spell every name said that day**, and say roughly
  when and where each person was born if known.
- If a question lands wrong, skip it and mark it. Nothing here has to be answered.

## Sitting One — Tame  *(Q1–12 · episode 17)*

1. The frame on the wall — the portrait and the citation. Who framed them? Where did they hang, and who has them now?
2. The very first picture of your grandfather in your head — the earliest thing you remember?
3. What did his hands look like?
4. Did the war wound ever show — a scar, a stiffness, a way he held things?
5. Did he talk about the war? If so — when, to whom, what did he say? If never — how did the family come to know what it knows?
6. Where is the medal itself?
7. Anzac Day, reunions, the RSA, Battalion hui — did he go? Did he march?
8. Which sheds did he shear — whose farms, which districts, which gangs? Names of the men he worked with?
9. Where was the knife-hand work — which works, what was his job on the chain actually called?
10. Did he keep tallies? Any numbers the family still quotes about him?
11. What did he sound like? Te reo at home, English, both — and who did he speak each to?
12. Christmas Day 1989 — whatever you're comfortable telling about the day he died, and what the family did.

## Sitting Two — The marae and the land  *(Q13–21 · episode 18)*

13. Which marae is the whānau's? Which hapū, which iwi — and if that's for someone else to say, who should I ask, and will you take me to them?
14. The surname comes from a man — Kimi's father, Tamou. Has anyone at the marae ever named him? Who holds the deep whakapapa now?
15. What do you know of Kimi and Pare? Where did they live, what did they do, when did they die?
16. Tio Kimi — "Joe" — what's remembered of him? And the brother born in 1927 — what was his name?
17. Did the whānau talk about the Pākaraka blocks — the leases, the rent? Did money ever actually arrive, and what did it look like?
18. Did anyone in the whānau ever farm any of it, or try to?
19. What was said at home about the name Maxwell — and about the woolshed? Did the old people speak of Handley's Woolshed, of Ihaka Takarangi?
20. The name went back to Pākaraka in 2022. Was the whānau part of that? How did it land with everyone?
21. On the land files there's a Matiu Tamou holding the Rangiwaea blocks. Who is that — Harriet's father, her son Mathew, or someone else? And where is Rangiwaea?

## Sitting Three — Harriet, Maria, the seven  *(Q22–28 · episode 19)*

22. Tell me about your grandmother Harriet. Which name did she actually go by — Te Pohe, Harriet, Phillips, Matiu? Where was she from?
23. The seven children — Agnes El Hama, Linda Rebecca, Maria, Kimi, Leoni Mariana, Mathew, Rosalie. One at a time; one thing about each.
24. Tell me about your mum. Where did Maria sit among the seven, and what was she like when you were a boy?
25. How did your mum and dad meet? Where, when, what were they each doing?
26. You were born in February 1966 and your mum had just turned eighteen. What did the family ever say about that time? Who stood by them?
27. Where were you raised, exactly — year by year, house by house, as best you can walk it?
28. Did the whānau have a church — Rātana, Catholic, Anglican, something else? Where were people baptised and married?

## Sitting Four — The Balsley side  *(Q29–35 · episode 20)*

29. Which Gordon was the labourer — your dad, your grandfather, or both? What work did each actually do, and where?
30. Your grandfather Gordon died in 1972, when you were six. Do you remember him at all?
31. What did your dad say about his own growing up — Turakina, Maxwell, school, leaving school?
32. Did anyone ever say the Balsleys were Danish? Any story about where the name came from, or the old country?
33. Who is Mabel? Who else is buried at Launceston, and can anyone photograph the headstones?
34. Did the New Zealand Balsleys and the Tasmanian ones stay in touch — letters, visits, anything?
35. Sydney Balsley died in 1963. Did your dad know his grandfather? Where did Sydney die?

## Sitting Five — You, Kiel, now  *(Q36–40 · episode 21)*

36. Who is Kiel Gordon Balsley to you?
37. Does anyone know where the name Kiel came from? Was anyone thinking of the old Dane, Kele — or is that this book finding a pattern that isn't there?
38. When did you leave New Zealand, and why the Gold Coast?
39. Who is still at Whanganui now? Who holds the whakapapa sheet and the land papers, and who do I ask before anything from them goes further than the whānau?
40. What do you want this book to do — and who has to read it, and be happy with it, before anyone outside the family ever does?

**Then spell the names.**
