The Hand. Episode sixteen.

How to Run a Sitting.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

This is the beginning of Annexure E, and it is the part of the book that is not a document. It is a tool.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Forty questions, to be answered out loud, on tape.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The book says it plainly, and it is worth hearing before anything else in this episode: this is the middle of the book.

Not an appendix. The middle.

Everything you have listened to so far is the part that could be found in an archive. What follows is the part that cannot.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

So — how to run a sitting.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

First, the room.

Quiet room. Phone charged. Notifications off. Recorder running before the first question.

Say the date, the place, and who is in the room. Then have Tom say, in his own words, that he knows he is being recorded, and what it is for.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Second — and this is the one people get wrong.

One question. Then silence.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Let the pauses run. The pause is where the good material lives.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Never correct him mid-story. Note it, and come back to it.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Third. Follow the energy, not the list.

If question three opens a door, go through it — and pick the list up later.

The list is a safety net, not a script.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Fourth. Forty-five to ninety minutes a sitting. Five sittings.

And objects on the table help. The framed citation. The whakapapa sheet. The land schedules. The block map. Any photographs.

A man will tell you more with a document in his hands than he will across an empty table.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Fifth. At the end of every sitting — spell every name said that day. And say roughly when and where each person was born, if it is known.

That one habit is worth more, later, than anything else in this list.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

And last.

If a question lands wrong, skip it, and mark it.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Nothing here has to be answered.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Now — a word about the five episodes that follow, because they can be used two ways.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

You can listen to them on your own, before a sitting, to get the questions into your head — so that on the day you are not reading off a page, and you can look at him instead.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Or you can take them into the room and let them ask.

If you do that, hit pause after each question, and let the silence run for as long as it wants to. There is a short gap built in after every one, but it is deliberately not long enough. The real pause is the one you leave.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

One last thing, and it is the reason this whole apparatus exists.

The whakapapa of the Tamou line, beyond what the whānau's own sheet records, is not written in this book, and is not this book's to write.

It is held at the marae. By people.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

So these are not research questions. They are an invitation to a man to say what only he can say — and, in Sitting Two, to name the people who should be asked instead of him.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]
