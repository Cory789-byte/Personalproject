The Hand. What the Work Was Worth. Balslev, Funen, to the Gold Coast. Eighteen sixty-four to twenty twenty-six.

This is the audio edition of the second assembly.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Episode zero. How to listen.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

This is a family history about work.

It runs from the abandonment of a Danish earthwork in February eighteen sixty-four, to a man living on the Gold Coast in twenty twenty-six. And it follows two lines that had nothing in common until nineteen sixty-six, when they produced a child between them.

Both lines held land. One lost it and crossed the world. One kept the title and lost the use. Neither ever got any of it back.

What each has instead is paper proving it once held something. A Tasmanian purchase on one side. An Army recommendation form and a land determination schedule on the other. And in every case, the paper outlived the thing it described.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Now — the structure of this series, and why it is built the way it is.

The book is built on ten grounds. Each section is a place, because the argument of the book is land, and the structure should be the argument. This series follows that exactly. One episode per ground. The places are the chapters.

Balslev. Levendale. Launceston. Turakina. Pākaraka. The River. Tebaga Gap. The Shed and the Works. Nineteen sixty-six. The Gold Coast.

Then the annexures: the lines, the timeline, what is record and what is not, the searches — and last, the questions.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Before any of it, you need the grading system, because this book will not let you forget it, and neither will this recording.

There are five grades, and you will hear them spoken out loud, every time.

[[PAUSE 0.6]]

RECORDED. A document says so, and the document has been seen, or is cited by a source that has seen it.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

UNVERIFIED. It appears in a user-submitted tree, or an index whose underlying record has not been examined. Good enough to search on. Not good enough to print as fact.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

INFERRED. It follows from the evidence, but no document states it. Marked every time — including mid-sentence.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

NOT ESTABLISHED. A live question, with no answer yet.

[[PAUSE 0.5]]

BROKEN. The line stops.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

If that sounds like a lot of scaffolding for a family story — that is the point. A family history that will not tell you how it knows a thing is just a story with names in it.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Two men in this book are documented to the day.

A Dane who bought Tasmanian land in eighteen seventy-three. And a corporal of the twenty-eighth Māori Battalion who was shot through the hand on a Tunisian hill in nineteen forty-three, and who we now know was born on the twenty-seventh of June, nineteen twenty, and died on Christmas Day, nineteen eighty-nine.

Around those two men, the record has begun to fill. A whakapapa sheet. A Māori Land Court determination file. A framed citation. And where the record is still thin, the thinness is marked on the line.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Here is the promise the book makes, and this recording keeps it.

Nothing here is put into anybody's mouth. There is no invented dialogue and no invented interior life. Where a scene is described, it is described from a document, and the document is named.

That matters more in audio than it does on the page. A voice reading a story out loud can make anything sound settled. So when you hear something in this series that sounds like a novel — a man on a hill, a boy in the flax — understand that every line of it is coming off a form, a report, or a whānau's own record. Where it is not, you will be told.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

One more thing, and it is the most important thing.

The whakapapa of the Tamou line, beyond what the whānau's own sheet records, is not written in this book, and it is not this book's to write. It is held at the marae, by people.

The questions that only people can answer are gathered at the end — in Annexure E — to be answered out loud, with a recorder running.

The middle of this book is not in an archive. It is in Tom.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Which is why this series does not end with the story. It ends with five episodes of questions — forty of them — read out one at a time, so they can be listened to before a sitting, or carried into the room and used.

That is the part of this recording that is not a document. That is the part that is a tool.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

A word on what this book will not do. Flatter, or tidy.

The Balsleys were dispossessed people who became beneficiaries of dispossession. At Levendale, and again at the village that carried Maxwell's name. Nobody in these pages did the removing. Everybody in these pages ate off it.

That distinction is held all the way through, and it is never used as an exit.

And the book keeps its own errors on the page. It has been wrong four times so far, and the corrections are logged, because a family history that hides its corrections cannot be trusted about anything else.
