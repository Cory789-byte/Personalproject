The Hand. Episode four.

Launceston.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Ground three. And this is the shortest ground in the book, because it is a contradiction that has not been resolved yet.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The family's own memory places them at Launceston. In the north.

Every record found so far places them in the south. Levendale. Richmond. Cutting Grass. Oatlands.

Two hundred kilometres of discrepancy.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Now, the easy thing to do here would be to decide the records are right and the family is wrong. Most family histories do exactly that, because records feel more solid than memory.

This book does not do that. And there is a reason.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

It is not a mistake. There are Balsley graves at Launceston. And one of them is Mabel's.

Family testimony.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

So who is Mabel?

Mabel appears in no index found so far. She is not among the four children born between nineteen oh nine and nineteen twenty-two.

So she is another child of Sydney's — or she is Sydney's wife.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

And that is not a small fork. Follow it.

If Mabel is Sydney's wife, buried at Launceston, then the family has a northern half that never crossed the Tasman at all.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

So this one unnamed woman decides the shape of the whole Tasmanian side. Who she was — and whether her burial predates or postdates nineteen twelve — decides whether this is one family that moved, or one family that split.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

A photograph of the headstone settles it.

That is the entire cost. Somebody stands in a cemetery in Launceston, finds the stone, and takes a picture of it.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Until then: NOT ESTABLISHED.

It is Annexure E, question thirty-three — and you will hear it asked, in Sitting Four.
