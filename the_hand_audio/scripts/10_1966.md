The Hand. Episode ten.

Nineteen sixty-six.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Ground nine. This is where the two lines of this book meet, and it takes about ninety seconds to lay out — and then it takes the rest of the episode to say what it costs.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Thomas Sydney Balsley was born on the twenty-second of February, nineteen sixty-six.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Wind it back.

Conception around late May, nineteen sixty-five.

Gordon was eighteen. Maria was seventeen.

She had turned eighteen twenty days before the birth. He was nineteen years and eight months old on the day.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

A Pākehā labourer, and a Māori girl of seventeen. In New Zealand. In nineteen sixty-five.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

That is the period of maximum institutional pressure on young unmarried mothers in that country.

And Māori girls were not exempt from it.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

So the notable fact about Thomas Sydney Balsley is not that he was born.

It is that he stayed in the family.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Somebody made that happen. And it will have cost them something.

Who, and what, is Annexure E — questions twenty-six and twenty-seven.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

And now the column. This is where the argument of the book lands, and it is best heard as a list, so here it is as a list.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

On the Balsley side.

Kele Balsley — landholder. The best open country in his district.

Sydney Balsley — not known.

Gordon Sydney Thomas Balsley, born nineteen eleven — not known.

Gordon Sydney Thomas Balsley, born nineteen forty-six — labourer.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

And on the other side.

Whanganui Māori before eighteen forty-eight — a river, and the country either side of it.

By nineteen hundred — one third of it.

Tame Kimi Tamou — title at Pākaraka worth cents in the pound. Shearer. Knife hand. Soldier. Military Medal.

His daughter's partner — labourer.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Now hear what those last words actually mean, because they are job classifications, not adjectives.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Shearing is skilled piece-work. A good man on the board earns on his tally.

Labourer is the bottom classification on the wage board. And the first man put off when the work stops.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

And the fall happens in the generation immediately after the soldiers. On both sides at once.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Whatever the war was supposed to buy — it did not arrive.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

One last thing about the child.

He was named Thomas.

A name arriving from two directions, in two languages. Down the Danish line, inside Gordon Sydney Thomas. And across the whakapapa from Tame — his grandfather — who was on the hill.
