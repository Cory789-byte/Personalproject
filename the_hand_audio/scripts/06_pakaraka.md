The Hand. Episode six.

Pākaraka.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Ground five. The centre of the book.

The ground where the two families stood on top of each other without knowing it.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

The woolshed.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

On the twenty-seventh of November, eighteen sixty-eight, a government militia came upon a group of unarmed children of Ngā Rauru Kītahi and other Taranaki iwi, at Handley's Woolshed, near Waitōtara.

The children were from Tauranga Ika Pā at Nukumaru. The eldest was about ten.

The militia fired on them. Then rode them down, and attacked them with sabres.

Two children were killed, and others wounded.

RECORDED.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

The unit was the Kai-iwi Yeomanry Cavalry Volunteers. Raised by local farmers, when Whanganui came under threat from the Ngāti Ruanui commander Tītokowaru — who was then leading the resistance to the confiscation of Taranaki land.

Their own commanding officer, Colonel Whitmore, described them as a motley collection of horsemen aged fourteen to sixty. A perfect pack of devils. And uncontrollable.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Among the founders, two names to keep.

John Bryce — who thirteen years later led the invasion of Parihaka.

And George Maxwell — a young Scotsman, off the Agra in eighteen fifty-two, farming north of Whanganui.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

One of the children was Ihaka Takarangi. Nine years old.

Shot through the thigh. Fallen into flax. A trooper slashing at his head.

He threw up his hands.

He survived by playing dead.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

His whānau say more than two died that day, and that it was a massacre.

His portrait hangs at Pākaraka Pā. And in it, his fingers are severed.

RECORDED — from Ngāti Maika.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Maxwell was killed in a skirmish weeks later.

And when a settlement was surveyed near the woolshed two years on, it was named Maxwelltown, in his memory.

In nineteen twenty-seven, it was shortened to Maxwell.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

The Balsleys arrive.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

From Turakina, the family moved to Maxwell. Family testimony.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

Now — the book stops here and says the thing plainly, so that nobody has to wonder whether it is being avoided.

There is no evidence any Balsley was in New Zealand in eighteen sixty-eight. They were in Tasmania. Kele had not yet lost his farm.

Nobody in these pages fired a carbine at a child.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

But they took up residence in a memorial to the man who did. And the memorial was the address on their letters.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

It is the same distinction as Levendale, held the same way, and not used as an exit.

A family with no land goes where land is cheap. And land is cheap where somebody else has been removed from it. And the removal is never the newcomer's doing — and is always the reason he can afford the ground.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

And now the thing nobody expected.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Sitting in a Māori Land Court determination file — Aotea District — searched and valued to the cent by a clerk, on the twenty-ninth of June, nineteen eighty-two, in the estate of Harriet Te Pohe Matiu, Mrs T. K. Tamou:

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

Pākaraka one-A-one. Pākaraka one-A-two-A. Pākaraka one-A-two-B. Pākaraka one-A-two-B-three.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

The Tamou whānau were the Māori owners of the Pākaraka blocks.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

So: the village the Balsleys settled in. The militiaman's name on it. And the land title underneath it.

And the two families that would produce a child together in nineteen sixty-six.

All one ground.

RECORDED.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Now — what owning it actually amounted to. This is from the schedule, and the numbers are the argument.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Pākaraka one-A-two-B-three. Eighty-four hectares. Valued at a hundred and seventy-one thousand, five hundred dollars. Leased to D. J. Blair — twenty-one years from the first of October, nineteen seventy-seven, at three thousand one hundred and twenty-seven dollars a year. No right of renewal. No compensation for improvements.

Her interest: nought point one seven eight six shares, of eight point five five nine. Three thousand, five hundred and seventy-eight dollars and sixty-seven cents.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Pākaraka one-A-two-A. Fifty-seven hectares. Valued at a hundred and twenty-seven thousand dollars. Leased to A. E. Wilson and B. V. Pearce — fifteen years from the thirtieth of June, nineteen sixty-eight, at one thousand one hundred and twenty-four dollars and twenty-six cents a year. No compensation. No right of renewal.

Her interest: four thousand, six hundred and forty-six dollars and seventy-seven cents.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Pukehika. Nine hundred and seventy-three hectares, held in one thousand two hundred and forty-nine shares.

Her interest: one point three three three three of them.

One hundred and seventy-six dollars and thirteen cents.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Kaipo A thirteen. Six hundred and seven square metres. Carrying a rating lien of twenty-one dollars and seventy-four cents.

Her interest: fourteen dollars and twenty-eight cents.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

And then the rest of the schedule.

Shares in the Ātihau-Whanganui Incorporation. Shares in Parininihi ki Waitōtara — from Parininihi to Waitōtara. The confiscated coast itself.

Araukuku one-A and one-B — sold to the Māori Trustee. Paratupu one-A — sold to the Māori Trustee, the twenty-sixth of August, nineteen seventy-one.

And on a second file, under the name Matiu Tamou: Rangiwaea four-F-sixteen, four-B-one, four-B-three and four-B-two-A. Two hundred and seventy, seventy-nine, and twenty-two hectares. Leased to R. D. Stout, until the thirty-first of May, nineteen ninety-one.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Those numbers are the Native Land Court, finished.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Here is the mechanism, and it is worth understanding, because it explains the whole of this side of the family.

The court converted collectively held country into individual shares. Shares descend to every child. Every generation multiplies the shareholders and divides the share.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

After a century, you arrive at this. A nine-hundred-hectare block, held in twelve hundred and forty-nine parts. A woman's inheritance in it worth a hundred and seventy-six dollars. And her interest in another block worth fourteen dollars and twenty-eight cents.

While the farmable paddocks are worked by Blair, Wilson, Pearce and Stout — on long leases.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Nobody stole this.

It is all lawful. All recorded. All signed.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

The name comes back.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

In twenty twenty-one, Ngāti Maika — hapū of Ngā Rauru Kītahi — applied to the New Zealand Geographic Board to restore the name.

More than five hundred submissions. Upheld in February twenty twenty-two, and confirmed by the Minister. The Board's chairman said none of the reasons against outweighed the case for righting the wrong.

The first Pākaraka signs went up — and were flattened within a fortnight. And were replaced.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

A hundred and fifty-four years after a trooper took a boy's fingers off, the boy's people got the name of the place back.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The Balsleys were long gone from it by then.

And one of their descendants had a Whanganui grandmother whose estate held the ground.
