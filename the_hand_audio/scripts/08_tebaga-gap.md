The Hand. Episode eight.

Tebaga Gap.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Ground seven. This is the ground that gave the book its title, and this episode does three things: the campaign, the paperwork, and then the coincidence the book refuses to make an argument out of.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

By March nineteen forty-three, the North African campaign had moved into Tunisia. And the Axis armies were dug into the Mareth Line.

The Long Range Desert Group found a way around it.

Montgomery used it.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The newly formed New Zealand Corps — fourteen and a half thousand men of the second New Zealand Division, including the Māori Battalion, with six thousand British and six thousand Free French — went on a wide left hook inland, approaching Tebaga Gap by the nineteenth of March.

The assault to open it was code-named Supercharge Two.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

On the twenty-fifth of March, the eve of it, Five Brigade took over the line facing the eastern end. The Māori Battalion relieved Twenty-Six Battalion.

Its objectives: Point two-oh-nine, and the foothill below.

RECORDED.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

You have already heard the two days themselves, in the prologue.

Hikurangi taken and held on the twenty-sixth. Point two-oh-nine surrendered at seventeen hundred on the twenty-seventh. Over two hundred prisoners. The Battalion's hundred casualties. Ngārimu dead, and given the Victoria Cross.

And Corporal Tame Tamou. Twenty-two. Section commander on both days. Directing his light machine guns. Firing a captured Spandau. Shot through the hand, and carrying on.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Now — the signatures. Because this is the part that is easy to skim on a page, and should not be.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The immediate Military Medal recommendation went up fast. Brigade on the third of April. Division by the sixth. Army by the seventeenth.

Under five names, in ascending order.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

Bennett — the Battalion.

Kippenberger — the Brigade.

Freyberg — Second New Zealand Expeditionary Force.

Montgomery — Eighth Army.

Wilson — Commander-in-Chief, Middle East.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

The entire command chain of the Eighth Army put its signature to a piece of paper about a shearer.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The medal was awarded. That last part is family testimony — and the Gazette wording is on the search list. Worth having, not because there is any doubt, but because a family is entitled to hold what the Crown actually published.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

One point of vocabulary, for anyone who ever reads the form itself.

IMMEDIATE is not a description of speed.

It is a category. An award for a specific named act — as against a periodic award in the honours list.

His was immediate because it was for two days, on one hill.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

And now: two hands.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

In November eighteen sixty-eight, on the Nukumaru flats, a nine-year-old named Ihaka Takarangi put his hands up to stop a cavalry sabre, and lost his fingers doing it.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

In March nineteen forty-three, on a hill in Tunisia, a corporal named Tame Tamou put his hand on a captured German machine gun, and had it shot through.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Seventy-five years, two countries and two wars apart.

Between the two events, the Balsleys lived in the village named after the man who ordered his horse at the children. And afterwards, this family's son married into the people the boy came from.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

And here the book stops itself, deliberately, and this recording stops with it.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

This book does not claim those two facts mean anything.

They are not connected by cause, and nothing follows from setting them together.

They are both simply true. Both documented. And both happened to people whose descendants now sit at the same table.
