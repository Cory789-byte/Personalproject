[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Ground eight. What he came home to.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The Battalion's men returned through nineteen forty-five and nineteen forty-six.

And he married Harriet Te Pohe Matiu — daughter of Matiu — recorded elsewhere as Phillips, and as Te Pohe Manu.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Their children, in the order the whakapapa sheet gives them:

Agnes El Hama. Linda Rebecca. Maria. Kimi. Leoni Mariana. Mathew. Rosalie.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

A son named Kimi, for his father.

A son named Mathew — Matiu — for hers.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The same habit as Sydney, Thomas and Gordon on the other side of this family, in another language.

When there is nothing else to give a child, you give him the name of the man before him.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Maria was born on the second of February, nineteen forty-eight. Conceived around May nineteen forty-seven — well over a year after the war. Nothing to explain.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

And he worked as a shearer, and a knife hand.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Now, those are not two careers. They are one year.

[[PAUSE 0.8]]

Shearing runs the summer — roughly November to February. The killing season fills the other half.

A man who does both has arranged his life so that there is work in every month. And moves between two places to get it.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

A seasonal round.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

The same shape the Portmairremener kept on the Prosser — coast in winter, marshes from August — worked now for wages, on other people's sheep, by a man whose people's land had been converted into shares too small to live on.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

He held title at Pākaraka.

The paddocks were let to somebody else, on a twenty-one-year lease.

So he went shearing. And in the off season, he took a knife to a chain.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

And here is the fact the book will not go past.

He was shot through the hand in nineteen forty-three. And then spent his working life in the two occupations least forgiving of a damaged hand.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Whether it healed clean. Whether it cost him tallies. Whether he chose those trades in spite of it, or because they were the only ones open.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

That is not in any archive.

And this book will not guess.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

It is Annexure E — questions three, four, eight, nine and ten.

And it is the reason the recorder exists.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

One grade before we close, because it applies to everything you just heard about his trades.

UNVERIFIED. The occupations rest on family testimony.

His attestation papers record occupation on enlistment. And the personnel file — service file thirty-nine thousand six hundred and thirty-nine, requestable by next of kin — will confirm the knife hand, or correct it.
