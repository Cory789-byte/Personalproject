The Hand. Episode five.

Turakina.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

Ground four. And this one can be dated, without a single shipping record — because four children were born in two countries, and the births bracket the crossing.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Nineteen oh nine. William John Balsley, born at Cutting Grass, Tasmania.

Nineteen eleven. Gordon Sydney Thomas Balsley, born at Levendale, Tasmania.

Nineteen thirteen. Maisie Florence Balsley, born in New Zealand.

Nineteen twenty-two. Bryce Peter Balsley, born in New Zealand.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

All UNVERIFIED as records. INFERRED as siblings.

But the pattern is hard to read any other way.

The family crossed the Tasman between nineteen eleven and nineteen thirteen. And Gordon Sydney Thomas Balsley, born at Levendale in nineteen eleven, was carried out of Tasmania as an infant — and did not come back.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

They landed at Turakina. Family testimony.

Turakina is in the Rangitīkei, on the river of that name, south-east of Whanganui.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

And here the book does something careful, so listen to the distinction it draws.

Turakina is Crown purchase country. Not confiscation country.

The great Rangitīkei transactions of the eighteen sixties were contested at the time, and have been contested since. A different instrument than Taranaki.

Same result.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

That is the second time you have heard this pattern, and it is the third time it happens in this book.

At Levendale, land was available because people had been removed by war and by removals. At Turakina, land was available because of purchases people argued about then and argue about now. Different mechanism. Same outcome — ground becomes available, and a family with nothing can afford to stand on it.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

What the Balsleys held at Turakina, if anything, and on what tenure: NOT ESTABLISHED.

[[PAUSE 1.5]]

Now — the two Gordons. And this is a warning about method, placed deliberately in the middle of the book.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

There are two men named Gordon Sydney Thomas Balsley.

One born in nineteen eleven at Levendale, died nineteen seventy-two.

One born the eighteenth of June, nineteen forty-six.

Almost certainly father and son. Named identically.

[[PAUSE 1.0]]

Which means every record bearing that name must have its date checked before it is attached to a man.

And the family's account — that "Gordon Balsley was a labourer" — currently attaches to both at once.

Which of them it describes, or whether it describes both, is Annexure E, question twenty-nine.

[[PAUSE 1.2]]

This is how family histories go wrong. Two men, one name, and a single fact that quietly lands on the wrong one and then gets repeated for fifty years.

It is flagged here so that this one does not.
